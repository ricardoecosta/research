### I1. Grass-fed beef shares without owning a ranch

- **Customer:** Consumers who want to know where their meat comes from and buy in bulk to save money
- **Problem:** Beef prices are at record highs and buyers distrust grocery store beef
- **Offer:** Quarter, half or whole cow shares, or shipped cuts, grass-fed or grain-finished
- **Business model:** Bulk beef sales (shares) and packaged e-commerce
- **Pricing:** Per pound / per share. range by grade USD 8-20 per lb; quarter cow USD 12 per lb; USD 1500 per quarter cow; USD 6000 per whole cow About 40% net margin claimed. Book slaughter dates months in advance.
- **Acquisition:** other: Not detailed; plan to ship nationwide via his e-commerce business; earlier backyard version sold to local buyers.
- **Delivery:** Rancher raises the cattle on a grazing lease; owner earmarks and brands animals, sends them to a slaughterhouse at optimal size, packages and ships via e-commerce/fulfilment.
- **Unit economics (claims):** About 40% net margin; Quarter cow about 125 lb at about $12/lb = about $1,500; whole cow about $6,000
- **Tools:** Facebook Marketplace
- **Startup cost:** Not stated; no land needed
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — Beef box and meat share businesses exist in the UK; needs FSA-approved abattoir and cutting plant, local authority food registration, labelling and traceability rules; chilled delivery is costly.
- **Risks:** Slaughterhouse capacity bottlenecks; Food safety and cold-chain compliance; Cattle price volatility; Commodity competition (ButcherBox, Snake River Farms)
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Inbound content and paid ads can be agent-run; local buyers via opted-in lists. Cold SMS/DMs to consumers are not allowed in the UK.); onboarding 4/5 (Online order and deposit forms.); delivery 1/5 (Cattle sourcing, slaughter scheduling and cold-chain shipping are physical and need human coordination.); billing 5/5 (Online checkout and deposits.); support 3/5 (Agent support for order questions; quality complaints need humans.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Use Claude to pick apart a land appraisal** (finance)
1. Upload the seller's appraisal and screenshots of comparable sales to Claude
2. Have it flag mismatched comps (60-acre comps used for a 231-acre ranch; larger parcels are cheaper per acre)
3. Use it to draft negotiation replies
4. Get your own appraisal before closing
> "Claude alone, my $20 and this is not an ad for Claude" — Chris Koerner (~10:21)

**Win a discount through the seller relationship** (sales)
1. Meet the seller in person and tour the property with him
2. Show you will keep the land rural (no neighbourhood or data centre)
3. Honour handshake commitments such as keeping a tenant at below-market rent
> "just by nature of who you are as a person means you can get a discount below fair market value" — Chris Koerner (~14:14)

**Rebate agent commission as negotiating leverage** (finance)
1. Get a real estate licence (about $1,000 and tens of hours of study)
2. Represent yourself or have a family member do it
3. Give back part of the commission at closing to bring the price down
> "we essentially gave back 2% of her 3% commission at closing" — Chris Koerner (~15:47)

**Beef shares supply chain** (operations)
1. Agree with a rancher to buy and earmark/brand specific cattle
2. Let them grow to the optimal size instead of selling at 500-600 lb
3. Book slaughterhouse dates months in advance
4. Package and sell quarters/halves/wholes or ship cuts via e-commerce
> "Be sure you get a date months in advance because they get very busy" — Chris Koerner (~19:35)

**Rent-to-own land to de-risk a land business** (finance)
1. Find unloved land on Zillow (floodplain, highway frontage, long on market)
2. Lease it with an option to buy
3. Put portable/removable assets on it (storage units)
4. Buy the land once the business is proven; walk away and resell assets if not
> "he rents it until he's proved the model. Once he has a successful business, he buys it" — Chris Koerner (~26:51)

**Own equipment instead of hiring contractors** (operations)
1. Buy used dozers and dump trucks at auctions or on Facebook Marketplace
2. Hire operators at $20-50/hour instead of $200-500/hour contractors
3. Resell the equipment when finished

**Use farm credit lenders for land** (finance)
1. Use an ag or farm and ranch bank that only lends on large tracts
2. Expect about 15% down, 20-year amortisation, 5-year ARM
> "I think it was really important to go through a farm credit lender because this is what they know" — Chris Koerner (~14:28)

## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: Buy land in the path of growth as an asymmetric bet: worst case you enjoy it, best case it appreciates.
- *niche_selection*: Land near rail trails turned state parks has historically outperformed; look for public infrastructure catalysts.
- *offer_design*: You don't need to be vertically integrated: big beef brands buy from farmers rather than owning ranches.
- *niche_selection*: Commodity markets are not winner-take-all; many sellers can succeed.
- *scaling*: Learnings compound like money: skills from one YouTube channel transfer to a new niche channel.
- *validation_testing*: Prove a land-based business on leased land with an option to buy before committing capital.
- *automation_ai*: Missing public databases (like farm ponds) are data business opportunities now that GIS and AI agents exist.
- *automation_ai*: A cheap AI subscription can save large sums in negotiations by checking professional work like appraisals.
- *mindset_execution*: Hard, physical chores build work ethic in kids.

## Warnings and mistakes

- A lapse in agricultural tax exemption lets the county claw back back taxes, potentially hundreds of thousands of dollars.
- Appraisals using comps of much smaller parcels overstate per-acre value.
- Floodplain land is worth about a third and you can't build permanent structures on it.
- Hunting leases carry liability and aren't very profitable.
- North Texas land prices went parabolic 2020-2024 and have been dropping; timing is uncertain.
- Septic near rivers is regulated, complicating riverside rentals.
- Don't make enemies with neighbours or tenants when you buy rural property; relationships are the deal pipeline.
