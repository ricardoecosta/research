## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Aaron Edwards, founder of DocsBot, an AI customer support agent SaaS that passed $1M ARR in 3.5 years with >90% gross margin, ~$130 ARPU and ~3% revenue churn. After several failed AI image products, he built DocsBot from a problem at his employer, launched via build-in-public on Twitter, and now sells mostly through agencies that charge small businesses about $2,000-$3,000 setup and $500/month. He describes an automated pipeline: find businesses with docs but no chatbot (BuiltWith, job posts), auto-build a demo bot, and send AI monthly reports. Key takeaway: the opportunity is implementing AI agents for businesses that won't do it themselves.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Automated AI-agent sales pipeline** (acquisition)
1. Use ChatGPT/Claude ($20/month) with tools to find businesses with a knowledge base/docs but no chatbot
2. Use BuiltWith to list sites using basic widgets like Help Scout
3. Scrape job sites/LinkedIn for companies hiring support agents (budget ready)
4. Find decision-maker contact via Tregt (API router to Apollo etc., ~1c/lead)
5. Auto-build a bot on their docs via DocsBot MCP and send a demo link
6. Charge ~$2,000 setup + $500/month
> "Go find businesses, manufacturers that are doing this and that have a knowledge base on their website, but no chatbot" — Aaron Edwards (~32:36)

**Automated monthly value report** (operations)
1. Each month have AI analyse the client's bot logs and stats
2. Generate branded PDF report and email it
3. Flag unanswered questions and recommend new support docs
> "For that 500 bucks a month, it's doing it for you" — Chris Koerner (~0:20)

**10x value pricing** (pricing)
1. Estimate tickets/month and minutes per ticket
2. Assume conservative deflection (50%)
3. Show savings ~10x the fee
> "you want to provide 10 times the value that you charge" — Chris Koerner (~20:28)

**Paid-first instead of free trial** (pricing)
1. Require payment upfront with refund guarantee
2. Filters for serious customers and saves founder time
> "you pay us and show us you're serious. We'll give you a refund if it doesn't work for you" — Aaron Edwards (~13:25)

**Waitlist + survey MVP** (product)
1. Share concept with your network
2. Waitlist form plus survey on core features
3. Build the minimum feature set in two weeks

**Conference attention hack** (acquisition)
1. Skip $25k booth
2. Build $50 talking shoulder robots (AI-designed 3D print) connected to your voice agent as live demo
3. Walk the floor

**Solve your employer's problem first** (acquisition)
1. Build a proof of concept for your current employer
2. Make them first customer while transitioning out

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Try many cheap experiments while employed; the ninth stuck.
- *customer_acquisition*: Build in public: one repost from a stranger can launch a product.
- *pricing*: Charge more than you think; offer high tiers and enterprise contact.
- *niche_selection*: Bad-fit customers (solo devs) churn and DIY; target companies with support teams.
- *offer_design*: SMBs won't learn new software; the opportunity is implementation services on top of AI.
- *customer_acquisition*: Companies hiring support staff already have budget and ticket load.
- *customer_acquisition*: Spend more per prospect on unique outreach when deal value is high.
- *mindset_execution*: Lifestyle bootstrapping gives freedom (6 weeks vacation) but slower growth; he'd reinvest more in ads.
- *operations*: Prove value monthly to justify retainers.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Consumers are hard to sell to; TV and magazine coverage produced zero sales of the custom book.
- Viral free products don't mean revenue.
- Self-serve SaaS demand from tech-savvy customers has dried up; need service/implementers.
- Underpricing: his son wanted to charge $500/month for $100k/year value.
- Cheap outreach (cold email, LinkedIn DMs) is increasingly ineffective.
- Fads die; AI profile pictures revenue disappeared.
