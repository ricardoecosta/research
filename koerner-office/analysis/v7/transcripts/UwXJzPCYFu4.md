## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build proof on your own channels before selling the service** (acquisition)
1. Choose a platform and post consistently about the work and your journey.
2. Use your own account to test formats and show prospective clients what you can do.
3. Let prospects see the work and do their own due diligence before approaching you.
> "our whole like I guess philosophy is first we do it for ourselves and then we prove it and then we do it for clients." — Stanley Henry (~2:10)

**Build an initial professional network through frequent in-person meetings** (acquisition)
1. After returning to a market where you lack a network, post about the journey and meet as many relevant people as possible.
2. Stanley held five to six coffee meetings a day and attended networking events for six months.
3. Keep in touch and respond when people ask for help with the skill you have been demonstrating.
> "So for 6 months straight, I just like had five to six coffee meetings with people every day." — Stanley Henry (~6:16)

**Test a new platform by hiring platform-native talent** (operations)
1. When TikTok looked promising, Stanley hired a creator with a large TikTok following.
2. Ask the creator to make content for the company account while learning what works on the platform.
3. Use the results and platform knowledge to decide whether to expand.
> "Let's learn how this works. Try make us content as our brand and then we'll try to learn what's working on the platform" — Stanley Henry (~12:03)

**Give a new internal content creator a measurable production target** (operations)
1. Hire an internal creator whose full-time job is making content.
2. Set a target of five videos a day and give the person six months to learn.
3. Ask for a weekly report covering what they did, learned, and will start, stop, and continue.
4. Review progress and add specialist guidance only after the creator has tested enough content.
> "every week you need to tell me what you did, what you learned, what you going to start, stop, continue." — Stanley Henry (~28:11)

**Make a local business recognisable to its target area** (acquisition)
1. Make the location clear in the content, including naming the town or area served.
2. Publish consistently so platforms can test the content with a local audience.
3. Judge results by local reach and the credibility or calls it creates, not just viral view counts.
> "if you mention I'm the number one, you know, plumbing business in West Palm Beach, then like it's going to show to people in West Palm Beach." — David Heacock (~30:19)

**Let an organic series establish audience interest before adding sales calls to action** (offer_design)
1. Start with one repeatable series designed to entertain or create a feeling associated with the brand.
2. Keep the early format consistent and allow it time to find an audience.
3. After the characters and format have established interest, add service or product content and calls to action.
4. Put paid distribution behind content that already shows audience interest.
> "the first three month engagement is just get the show to be loved and once we've done that and we prove it then we can look at the next tier of stuff" — Stanley Henry (~42:30)

**Keep early creative tests simple** (product)
1. Start with the core story and use the simplest edit that communicates it.
2. Avoid adding hooks, effects, and editing tricks before the basic story has been tested.
3. Use early results to decide which extra elements are worth adding.
> "The videos that we're putting live on your channel uh this afternoon are as the simplest possible edit we can do." — Stanley Henry (~50:25)

**Use content that performs organically as the basis for paid campaigns** (acquisition)
1. Post content about the work and products.
2. Identify a post that receives strong comments or other engagement.
3. Use that post in a paid campaign and increase spend when sales are working.
> "one that goes well, we do the mini chat automation and one that gets lots of comments, we just put money behind it" — Stanley Henry (~71:13)

**Launch education to an audience you have already built** (validation_testing)
1. Use an existing audience to test whether people will buy a practical product based on your company's work.
2. Begin with a webinar and digital products that are quick to launch.
3. Use early customer acquisition results to decide whether to scale the offer and target new markets.
> "We just thought it's easy to spin up. We can test if our audience actually buys from us." — Stanley Henry (~72:16)

**Use AI to turn conversations into proposals and internal briefs** (operations)
1. Record client or prospect calls.
2. Feed the transcript into the agency's AI context system.
3. Have the system prepare a proposal or creative pitch so the team can spend more time discussing ideas.
4. Use AI to prompt staff with the briefs and tasks needed for its work.
> "that phone call can be recorded and then the AI can just spit out the proposal straight afterwards" — Stanley Henry (~16:10)

**Add a new channel or content series one at a time** (scaling)
1. Choose one new platform, channel, or series.
2. Put the team's attention and resources into getting that one working.
3. Expand to another audience or series after the first has a stable format.
> "every time we're going to go into a new platform and a new channel, let's just only do that. put all our effort into that" — Stanley Henry (~57:19)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Demonstrate your service on your own channels so prospects can see evidence of your work before they buy.
- *offer_design*: For social content, a real and relatable story is more durable than a short-lived hook or editing trick.
- *validation_testing*: Test with enough volume to learn, but keep the process simple so you can see what is working.
- *mindset_execution*: Treat organic social as a long-term channel and give a new format time to find its audience.
- *offer_design*: Build an entertaining series first; introduce product messages and calls to action after people care about the format.
- *automation_ai*: AI can take over coordination and routine document work, but the agency keeps humans in the creative work that needs high quality.
- *niche_selection*: Create separate channels or series for different audience segments instead of forcing every audience into one format.
- *scaling*: Focus resources on one new platform or series at a time, then expand after it works.
- *finance_unit_economics*: An agency can build skills, relationships, and cash flow that later support a more scalable product business.
- *validation_testing*: A business audience can be a low-friction place to test whether customers will pay for educational products.
- *automation_ai*: When using AI in a company, capture useful business context from real conversations and documents, then make it available to the team.
- *offer_design*: Make content easy to understand in the first moments because people scroll quickly and have little patience for complicated setups.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Organic content usually takes time to work. Stanley says some small businesses give up after only two weeks, while he recommends giving it three to six months.
- Paid spend cannot reliably rescue content that people do not want to watch; weak content can become expensive to distribute.
- Trying too many platforms or series at once can split attention and leave some work without the resources it needs.
- The agency still relies on people for strong creative production; Stanley says AI editing is not yet good enough for the quality his business needs.
- Social content built from cheap tricks, excessive editing, or overly complicated execution can lose the story and feel inauthentic.
- Agency work can generate cash flow but is difficult to scale and can have cash-flow challenges.
