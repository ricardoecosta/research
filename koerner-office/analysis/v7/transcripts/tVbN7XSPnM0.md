## Tactics and playbook steps

**Turn existing expertise into a low-friction digital product** (product)
1. Review existing recordings, notes, and files for material that solves a customer problem.
2. Package the useful material in a simple digital format, such as a Google Drive folder.
3. Test subject lines with A/B emails to relevant newsletter segments.
4. Deliver the link directly after purchase.
> "I ended up just selling a link to the Google Drive. That's it, not like a community with moderators" — Chris Koerner (~4:04)

**Test content formats and keep separate research and posting accounts** (acquisition)
1. Test formats such as carousels, infographics, picture posts, and talking-head videos.
2. Track both positive platform signals and whether you enjoy making the content.
3. Use a separate research account to train the feed toward your niche and collect content examples.
4. Keep the posting account focused; use a separate personal account for unrelated viewing.
> "I have like a consumption Instagram account for research. And then I have a posting Instagram account from posting" — Chris Koerner (~11:32)

**Use a hook and withhold the promised payoff until the end** (content)
1. Open with a visual or verbal hook that identifies the audience and promises a specific payoff.
2. Explain the situation and what you tried without revealing the key answer immediately.
3. Show evidence or a result, then deliver what the opening promised.
4. Use a call to action that fits the platform and the offer.
> "And the whole time they're like, what is the thing? What, why are you hiding the idea?" — Chris Koerner (~19:06)

**Ask followers what they need before building a product** (validation_testing)
1. Use an Instagram story poll to ask what problem followers have.
2. Follow up with another poll or question about the leading problem.
3. Repeat for several days to learn the specific product or solution they want.
4. Test the offer before making a large product investment.
> "You don't have to guess what they want. So then when you have a big launch a month later" — Chris Koerner (~15:22)

**Send social viewers to one simple email landing page** (acquisition)
1. Use a single focused landing page instead of a link page with many choices.
2. Ask for an email address first; collect additional details later if needed.
3. Use a clear lead magnet that is more valuable than the short video preview.
4. Send a short welcome sequence, then one relevant, valuable email each week.
> "The more there is for them to click on the landing page, the more likely they are to get distracted and to never convert at all." — Chris Koerner (~38:05)

**Build a comment-to-lead workflow** (operations)
1. Connect the Instagram account in GoHighLevel through Facebook integrations.
2. Choose the specific post and use a trigger phrase with a contains-phrase filter.
3. Track first-level comments only.
4. Like the comment and rotate several public replies to reduce repetitive-looking responses.
5. DM a clickable button to the landing page, then capture the email and deliver the lead magnet.
> "you want to be sure it's just contains and not exact match." — Chris Koerner (~44:10)

**Keep a simple lead workflow and add tags** (operations)
1. Start with the trigger and one useful response instead of adding unnecessary complexity.
2. Tag each contact by the lead magnet or topic they requested.
3. Use tags later to send relevant offers to the right subset of contacts.
4. Build a thank-you page and a short delivery email, with a backup link on the page.
> "If you have 30 different PDF lead magnets over the course of a year, and you tag all of those people that buy it as PDF" — Chris Koerner (~50:35)

**Demonstrate the voice agent as part of a qualified lead journey** (sales)
1. Collect a phone number only when needed and include the required consent language.
2. Set an appropriate delay and condition in the workflow before an outbound call.
3. Train the voice agent for a narrow job, such as demonstrating itself to a business owner.
4. Invite interested prospects to a follow-up call with a real person.
5. Check applicable phone and marketing rules before using automated calls.
> "your express goal is to get them on a call with me, a real human to further answer questions" — Chris Koerner (~79:57)

**Reuse a voice-agent template across clients** (operations)
1. Start from a marketplace template.
2. Replace the business name, voice, goals, availability, and knowledge base.
3. Connect the client's calendar and notifications.
4. Review call transcripts and update the prompt when the agent fails on repeated questions.
5. Duplicate a working configuration for the next client, then test the client-specific version.
> "you literally just duplicate it. You just click that. Okay. Then you swap out the big and same with your new client." — Chris Koerner (~78:03)

**Let AI improve a rough prompt and iterate on the prototype** (product)
1. Write the idea in plain language, even if the prompt is rough.
2. Ask a separate AI tool to improve the prompt for the specific builder.
3. Build a first version and test it with dummy data.
4. Show the builder a screenshot and describe the specific change needed.
5. Do not treat a working prototype as proof that a regulated product is production-ready.
> "the shortcut is to ask the AI, what to ask the AI." — Chris Koerner (~82:53)

**Keep testing growth and offer variations** (validation_testing)
1. Test different lead magnets, formats, offers, and workflow variations.
2. Keep successful versions running while testing new ones.
3. Make small changes and monitor what happens.
4. Do not assume a growth tactic will work indefinitely.
> "Test all of it. Like, never ever ever stop testing." — Chris Koerner (~89:31)

**Publish and learn through execution** (mindset)
1. Build a small version of the offer.
2. Publish it and observe real customer behavior.
3. Use the results to improve instead of spending too long researching or waiting for a mentor.
> "You just have to ship the thing. You got to press publish." — Chris Koerner (~91:14)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Treat content as repeated experiments: publish, learn from the signals, and keep improving rather than expecting one post to work.
- *customer_acquisition*: Optimize short-form content for retention and a promised payoff, not just views or likes.
- *niche_selection*: Keep an account focused on the audience it has built; test a different niche with a new account if needed.
- *offer_design*: Ask an audience about its problems before investing in a product.
- *niche_selection*: A small, loyal audience can be more valuable than a large, weakly engaged one.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use a focused landing page with a single clear next step to reduce friction.
- *operations*: Own an email relationship because a social platform can remove access to an audience.
- *legal_risk*: Use audience engagement to earn permission for a response; keep automation compliant with platform rules and applicable law.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI to get a first version made, then test it with real inputs and correct specific problems.
- *mindset_execution*: Publishing and selling teach faster than extended planning and research.
- *validation_testing*: Keep successful experiments running while testing new variations; a tactic's performance may not last.

## Warnings and mistakes

- A platform ban can remove a major revenue source overnight; build an email list and landing-page path that you control.
- Repeated identical automated replies or unsolicited DMs can trigger spam filters or account restrictions. The speakers advise varied replies and an explicit comment-based opt-in.
- The presenters are not compliance experts; phone collection and automated calls have additional regulatory requirements. Do not treat this demonstration as legal advice.
- Chris warns that bypassing A2P verification can lead to a $5,000 per-number-called fine, and says verification should not be skipped.
- Do not add phone-number collection casually; the transcript says consent language is needed and describes additional phone-related regulation.
- Do not assume a large audience will buy a product. Ask the audience what it needs and validate product-audience fit.
- Do not put too many choices on the landing page; extra clicks and fields can reduce conversion.
- Do not assume an AI-generated prototype is safe or accurate enough to replace accounting software; Chris says a real QuickBooks alternative is complex and involves regulation.
- A simple app may not be worth a high price if others can quickly build the same thing; the offer must over-deliver.
- Cross-posting tools may underperform native posting on some platforms, according to Chris's own observation.
- Growth hacks can stop working; keep testing new ideas while monitoring existing winners.
- Chris warns against spending too long researching, planning, and looking for mentors instead of publishing and learning from real execution.
