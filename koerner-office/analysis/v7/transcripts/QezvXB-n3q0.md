## Tactics and playbook steps

**Lock the location before buying equipment** (validation_testing)
1. Choose a machine that fits a site with existing demand.
2. Count traffic or check usage before purchasing.
3. Get the host agreement in writing and confirm site access, utilities, and permits.
4. Buy the machine only after the location is secured.
> "Confirm the traffic first, sign the lease first, buy the machine last." — Unnamed narrator (~4:30)

**Use a simple ATM host pitch** (sales)
1. Scout cash-heavy businesses that are busy and have no ATM.
2. Offer the store a machine at no upfront cost.
3. Tell the owner that you handle the machine, cash, and paperwork.
4. Offer a monthly share of fees or another clear host payment.
> "No upfront cost to you. That is it." — Unnamed narrator (~27:11)

**Scout, compare, pitch** (acquisition)
1. Count which businesses already have an ATM and which busy businesses do not.
2. Compare two processors' offers.
3. Pitch the best empty wall you found.
4. Start with one placement before building a route.
> "Scout, compare, pitch. Three moves. None of them cost him a dollar." — Unnamed narrator (~27:02)

**Use three filters for ATM sites** (validation_testing)
1. Check for real cash traffic.
2. Make sure the host benefits from the placement.
3. Choose a site close enough to refill on an easy route.
4. Reject a location that misses any of the three filters.
> "Traffic, a happy host, an easy loop." — Unnamed narrator (~34:00) [quote not matched verbatim]

**Review a weak ATM placement after real usage** (operations)
1. Let the machine run for 30 days before judging the placement.
2. Compare actual withdrawal activity with refill and travel effort.
3. If the site is weak, renegotiate the split or move the machine.
> "Never judge a placement before 30 days of real numbers." — Unnamed narrator (~31:13)

**Start with hosting, then consider machine ownership** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Use a processor-supplied machine and cash at the first site if available.
2. Learn how location traffic and fee splits work.
3. Move toward owning machines after the economics are understood.
> "Start light, then own more. That is the ladder." — Unnamed narrator (~30:12)

**Check physical requirements before committing** (operations)
1. For a dog wash, confirm the hair trap and sewer connection.
2. For a laundromat, confirm water, sewer, and gas capacity.
3. For pizza vending, check food approval.
4. For a car wash, check zoning and water-discharge requirements.
> "Confirm the drain, then buy the tub." — Unnamed narrator (~8:01)

**Build a route from placements, not one perfect machine** (scaling)
1. Use the first placement to learn the permit, site-pitch, and vendor process.
2. Reuse that learning for the next location.
3. Add machines only after the first location has demonstrated demand.
> "The stack is the business, not the single box." — Unnamed narrator (~9:44)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Validate the location before buying equipment; traffic and a written host agreement are central to the economics.
- *niche_selection*: For location-based machines, the doorway and customer flow matter more than the machine itself.
- *offer_design*: A placement deal should give the host a reason to keep the machine available and performing.
- *operations*: A route works best when sites are close enough to service without turning refills into long trips.
- *validation_testing*: Use real operating data to evaluate a placement rather than relying on a traffic guess.
- *operations*: A low-performing placement can be moved or renegotiated; the operator should not become attached to the equipment.
- *scaling*: A first placement can teach the operator about permits, the sales pitch, and vendors before they add more locations.
- *operations*: Unattended equipment still needs physical work such as refilling, utility checks, repairs, and compliance oversight.
- *finance_unit_economics*: The narrator's route examples suggest that several modest placements may produce more stable income than relying on one machine.
- *validation_testing*: Before spending money, estimate how much daily use a site needs and identify who controls the location.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not buy a machine before confirming the site and getting an agreement; the narrator describes an unplaced machine as an expensive paperweight.
- ATM placements can fail when the location has too little traffic to cover refill trips.
- For a dog wash, obtain approval for the hair trap and sewer connection before installation.
- For a laundromat, verify water, sewer, and gas capacity before signing the lease.
- For a car wash, check water-reclaim and discharge permits, storm-water rules, and zoning before closing.
- Crypto ATM and cash ATM compliance requirements in the transcript are US-specific and should not be treated as UK guidance.
- The transcript labels itself educational and says it is not financial advice.
