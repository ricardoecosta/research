## Tactics and playbook steps

**Premium preservative-free sourcing with simple recipe** (product)
1. Use pure ribeye with no additives, cornstarch or gum
2. Buy imported frozen beef (Uruguay, Australia, Chile, New Zealand) for quality control
3. Temper frozen meat for a day before slicing
4. Keep the recipe unchanged
> "if you won't feed it to your family, you shouldn't sell it out the window." — Frankie (~13:18)

**Grow revenue via add-ons and combos, not menu sprawl** (pricing)
1. Avoid adding many menu items
2. Offer add-ons and combo meals (e.g. chicken fingers and fries with soda)
3. Discount items with the lowest cost, such as drinks
> "I think it's add-ons that are the way to go. Or a combination meals." — Frankie (~37:14)

**Find a meat supplier as a newcomer** (operations)
1. Ask the local chamber of commerce for distributor introductions
2. Call cheesesteak owners in other states, who share ideas
3. Ask them to refer a distributor in your area
4. Shop at Restaurant Depot for better prices on basics

**Use celebrity and media proximity as marketing** (acquisition)
1. Photograph visiting celebrities and hang pictures on the wall
2. Allow film shoots on premises
3. Brand yourself as the original/king

## Lessons

- *operations*: Consistency of product is a durable moat: not changing the recipe is the main reason given for 96 years in business.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Restaurant economics: with 35%+ food cost, labour, insurance and utilities, even a landmark with about $3.9M in eight months nets about 18% before tax.
- *exit_acquisition*: Single-location food businesses are tough to sell without a successor or scalable system, regardless of brand fame.
- *customer_acquisition*: Free media exposure (films, celebrities, visitors) can amplify demand far beyond local advertising.
- *offer_design*: Do not add menu items just to fill slow seasons; add-ons and combos raise revenue with less complexity.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Commodity input costs can triple in a few years, so price flexibility and supplier relationships matter.
- *niche_selection*: Peers in the same trade in other regions will often share supplier and operating knowledge since they aren't competitors.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Weather and external shocks can swing sales by 25-30%, so reserves matter.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Restaurant margins are very low; Frankie advises people to stay out of the business unless they love it.
- Post-COVID, 100-200 new cheesesteak sellers sprang up within a mile and a half, increasing competition.
- Domestic beef had bone fragments, creating lawsuit and broken-teeth risk.
- Single-location businesses are hard to sell; private equity isn't interested, and there is no successor.
- Tariffs, fuel, labour, insurance and utilities are all pushing costs up.
