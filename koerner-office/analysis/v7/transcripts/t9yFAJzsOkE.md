### I1. Keyword-led single-feature iOS app portfolio (ASO-first)

- **Customer:** App Store users searching for a specific utility or feature, mostly iOS
- **Problem:** People want a simple tool for one task (converting JPEG to PDF, paraphrasing text, green noise sounds) and search the App Store for it
- **Offer:** A narrow single-purpose app whose name is the exact target keyword, with a hard paywall and free trial
- **Business model:** In-app subscriptions (weekly/yearly) or one-time purchases via App Store
- **Pricing:** Subscription, mostly weekly for one-time-use utilities and yearly for frequent-use apps; copy competitor pricing. Paraphrase app most popular plan USD 6.99 per week; Copied from Endel-type pricing USD 60 per year; Original frequency generator one-time fee USD 2.99 one-off; Yearly plan with 7-day trial on Paraphrase USD 50 per year Look at the top in-app purchases in Sensor Tower and copy the leader's most popular plan.
- **Acquisition:** app_store: ASO: target keywords with traffic score about 30 and few competitors with the keyword in the title; put keyword in app name and first screenshot; ratings prompt for reviews.; paid_search: Apple Search Ads exact-match keywords and Max Conversions campaigns at $10-20/day as plan C; Google Ads for cheaper Android downloads.; seo_organic_search: Google search for 'app' keywords also drives some downloads.
- **Delivery:** Software delivered via App Store; first version built with Claude Code in two to three days; Apple handles payments and refunds.
- **Unit economics (claims):** JPEG to PDF app about $500/month; Paraphrase app about $2,600/month, expected $5k; Green noise app $200-300/month; A different app at $35,000 MRR with 12% trial start and 60% trial-to-paid; About 40% of launched apps make money (10 launches for 4 winners)
- **Tools:** ASO Mobile, Sensor Tower, Keywords Everywhere, Claude Code, ChatGPT, Apple Search Ads, Facebook Ads Library
- **Startup cost:** Not stated beyond the Apple developer account; ad spend optional ($10-20/day). Personal developer account can be used initially.
- **Time to first revenue:** JPEG to PDF app took 2-3 days to ship; a new app made $1,600 in its first month
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — App Store works the same in the UK. UK developers need an Apple developer account, VAT/tax handling and a privacy policy; individual accounts are possible but UK trader-status disclosure applies.
- **Risks:** Apple review inconsistency over hard paywalls; Users can use ChatGPT instead; Roughly 60% of launched apps fail; Keywords may not stick; Apple scrutiny of unusual download and review spikes; Apple takes 15-30%
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 4/5 (ASO and in-store ads are inbound and software-driven; keyword research and metadata tuning can be agent-run with occasional approval.); onboarding 5/5 (Self-serve in-app onboarding and paywall with no human involved.); delivery 4/5 (Software product; Claude Code can build and maintain, with occasional human review of App Store submissions.); billing 5/5 (Apple handles subscriptions, billing and refunds.); support 4/5 (Low volume (1-2 refund emails a week); templated reply directing users to Apple.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Unbundle a big app's secondary keyword** (acquisition)
1. Pull top-grossing apps on Sensor Tower
2. Find keywords they don't target, or that appear in their ads
3. Check ASO Mobile for traffic score about 30
4. Check search results for few apps with keyword in title and weak ratings
5. Build a one-feature app named after the keyword
> "you unbuddle the big app and then you go, all right, what does the competition look like?" — Steve (~11:08)

**Copy competitor pricing and plan type** (pricing)
1. Open the competitor in Sensor Tower
2. Check the most popular in-app purchase
3. Copy price and weekly or yearly format
4. Use weekly with 3-day trial for one-time-use apps, yearly with 7-day trial for daily-use apps

**Paywall conversion tweaks** (product)
1. Use hard paywall in onboarding
2. Use the word 'Continue' instead of 'Subscribe'
3. Add 'No payment now' under the call to action
4. Show all plans in the same yearly price format
5. Use the word 'free' for premium features
> "30% increase in conversions with the word continue versus subscribe" — Steve (~38:20)

**Fast MVP launch** (product)
1. Prompt Claude Code for a single-feature app
2. Ship in 2-3 days even without a paywall or nice screenshots
3. Add privacy policy and terms
4. Launch on iOS under a personal account
5. Add paywall once downloads show up

**Ratings prompt and ASO push** (acquisition)
1. Add the built-in iOS ratings prompt (about 1% of downloads leave reviews)
2. Ask a little later, e.g. day two
3. Optimise keyword and title
4. Optionally run a lifetime-deal push

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Distribution is the hard part, not building; pick ideas where discovery is already built in through search demand.
- *validation_testing*: Validate demand by confirming an existing competitor makes money and people search for the keyword.
- *niche_selection*: One narrow problem per app; more niche raises willingness to pay.
- *pricing*: Copy proven pricing instead of inventing your own.
- *mindset_execution*: Treat it as a portfolio: expect around 60% of launches to fail and ship many.
- *operations*: Don't over-engineer legal structure or branding early; start with a personal account and change later.
- *automation_ai*: Low support load because Apple handles payments and refunds makes apps highly passive (about 1-2 hours a week for one app).
- *exit_acquisition*: Leave growth headroom in an app you want to sell; buyers pay for potential.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Apple reviewers are inconsistent about hard paywalls and forced the team to add an X to close, halving sales.
- Many launched apps fail because keywords don't stick; the hit rate is about 40%.
- Android users reach more people but pay less, so launch on iOS first.
- Apple emailed developers over abnormal download and review spikes from the lifetime offer.
