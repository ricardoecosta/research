## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build compact routes for seasonal sprinkler work** (operations)
1. Target homeowners with irrigation systems in the same neighborhood.
2. Offer both spring startup and fall winterization, then bundle them into an annual plan.
3. Use an air-compressor rental to test demand before buying equipment.
4. Schedule several nearby properties in one evening or Saturday morning.
5. Train technicians to run additional routes after the owner is booked.

**Sell holiday lights through visible local proof** (acquisition)
1. Supply the lights so the operator can charge more for the full install.
2. Consult after dark so homeowners can see the lighting effect.
3. Use door-to-door sales, flyers, yard signs and neighborhood social groups.
4. Post before-and-after photos in Meta ads.
5. Hire installation crews when demand exceeds the owner's weekend capacity.

**Make pet-waste service easy to verify** (product)
1. Offer a recurring weekly yard visit.
2. Take a before-and-after photo so the customer can see the completed work.
3. Promote in neighborhood and dog-owner groups and ask satisfied clients for referrals.
4. Pay independent contractors a cut of each completed job as the route grows.

**Develop knife-sharpening skill before selling restaurant service** (operations)
1. Start with a basic sharpening kit and safe transport case.
2. Build residential experience before handling expensive chef's knives.
3. Approach restaurants during the quiet period between lunch and dinner.
4. Build recurring restaurant routes and train another sharpener only after the process is reliable.

**Sell dumpster-pad cleaning through property managers** (sales)
1. Target property managers who oversee several restaurants, plazas or apartment complexes.
2. Offer an initial clean to bring neglected pads back to baseline.
3. Convert the property to a quarterly or monthly schedule.
4. Capture and haul off wastewater rather than blasting grease into storm drains.
5. Hire technicians for established routes while the owner sells to more managers.

**Run cleaning as a dispatch and management operation** (operations)
1. Recruit independent cleaners who already have supplies and a car.
2. Send cleaners to booked customer jobs and retain a stated 40% to 50% share of client payments.
3. Use online booking and automated texts to reduce manual scheduling.
4. Hire a virtual assistant to answer phones and help recruit cleaners.
5. Convert first-time deep cleans into weekly, every-other-week or monthly recurring bookings.

**Use dusk demonstrations to sell landscape lighting** (sales)
1. Show the homeowner the design at dusk, when the lighting effect is visible.
2. Partner with landscapers and pool companies for referrals and consider a referral kickback.
3. Use before-and-after photos in Meta ads.
4. Schedule installations for weekends.
5. Hire an installation crew so the owner can focus on design, quotes and project sales.

**Build drone-inspection referrals before scaling** (acquisition)
1. Complete the required training and legal steps before taking paid flights.
2. Develop referral relationships with roofers, insurance agents, solar installers, contractors and property managers.
3. Treat training-program revenue projections as projections rather than typical results.
4. Add qualified pilots and pursue commercial contracts after the service has a track record.

**Win a property-manager contract for litter cleanup** (sales)
1. Sell to a property manager or owner rather than approaching individual storefronts.
2. Schedule work before opening or after closing when the lot is empty.
3. Use before-and-after results to build testimonials.
4. Run Facebook ads once there is proof of completed work.
5. Build routes and hire workers as additional properties are added.

**Sell apartment valet trash as a property-wide service** (sales)
1. Pitch the leasing office, owner or property manager rather than individual residents.
2. Explain the property-level unit economics and how resident billing differs from the operator's fee.
3. Build credibility because the sales decision covers many units at once.
4. After winning the first property, hire an evening crew and add routes property by property.

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: A recurring service can become easier to forecast when a one-off job is converted into a weekly, monthly or seasonal plan.
- *niche_selection*: A service that does not require the customer to be present can fit around an evening or weekend schedule.
- *offer_design*: A clear before-and-after result can help customers judge whether a local service was completed.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: A business that hires people to perform the core work can give the owner more room to focus on sales and operations, but still requires customer and worker support.
- *scaling*: When physical work gets busy, the owner can shift toward quoting, routing and selling by hiring crews or technicians.
- *customer_acquisition*: Some businesses require a property-level decision-maker, so the sales process may involve one larger contract rather than many small customer decisions.
- *validation_testing*: Validate the local market before spending money, especially when the video relies on public data or projections.
- *customer_acquisition*: A low startup cost does not guarantee easy customer acquisition; a low barrier to entry can make credibility and sales the main challenge.
- *operations*: Estimate the work schedule as well as revenue: seasonal or after-hours fit can be useful, but the owner should consider support that may happen during the day.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The video says sprinkler startup and winterization work is seasonal, concentrated in two short windows.
- Holiday-light work is concentrated in a 10 to 12 week season and may leave the operator dormant outside it.
- Do not sharpen expensive chef's knives after only one tutorial; the work requires skill.
- Dumpster-pad wastewater must be captured and hauled away; discharging grease into a storm drain can violate rules and lead to large fines.
- Remote cleaning can require the owner to support customers and cleaners during normal work hours early on.
- Check local rules before landscape-lighting work; Johnny says low-voltage requirements vary by state.
- Drone work requires training and legal compliance. Johnny warns against buying a drone and immediately starting paid work, and says fines can be substantial.
- The drone program's weekly revenue figure is its own projection, not a documented average across pilots.
- Apartment valet trash has a difficult first sale because the buyer must approve service across many units, and the low barrier to entry can make credibility important.
- The speaker says his financial examples are for education, are not promises, and should be checked against the local market.
- Johnny promotes Home Service Academy training and one-on-one help for remote cleaning, which is a promotional conflict.
