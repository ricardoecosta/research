2. Choose an offer that can be sold to people you already understand or can reach through that advantage.
> "Anytime anyone starts a business, you need to go to your unfair advantage. Everyone has an unfair advantage." — Chris Koerner (~3:09)

**Randomize the list before an email price test** (validation_testing)
1. Export active subscribers and put the addresses in Google Sheets.
2. Add a RAND formula, sort the list by the random values, then split the randomized rows into groups.
3. Keep the sample below 10% of the full list so the initial lower-price test does not reach too many subscribers.
4. Tag and segment each group, and exclude unsubscribed subscribers.
> "So, I want to perfectly randomize this list because what I'm about to do is what's called an AB test or a split test." — Chris Koerner (~6:05)

**Test price with the same email and different payment links** (pricing)
1. Create the same one-time offer at $47 and $97.
2. Send identical emails to randomized groups, changing only the payment link and price.
3. Compare the revenue and conversion results, and allow time for higher-priced purchases to arrive.
4. Repeat tests when results are uncertain before sending the selected offer to the rest of the list.
> "If I send both of those identical emails, it's the same email, same subject, same wording, same everything. The only thing that's different is the payment link with the price, right?" — Chris Koerner (~13:05)

**Reduce checkout friction** (sales)
1. Use a direct payment link and avoid asking for information already held in the subscriber list.
2. Do not add optional fields unless the value of collecting that data justifies the conversion cost.
3. Check that the payment link is one-time rather than recurring and test the link before sending.
> "The more friction you add to the checkout process, the more your conversion rate will drop." — Chris Koerner (~14:26)

**Build the presale offer from existing assets** (offer_design)
1. Inventory training videos, spreadsheets, documents, and guides that already exist.
2. Describe the outcome the material supports and bundle it as a practical library.
3. State what future material and live sessions buyers will receive, and make the one-time lifetime terms clear.
> "I have a treasure trove of videos, spreadsheets, and docs in my Google Drive account that has never been published." — Chris Koerner (~21:31)

**Segment and send the test campaigns** (operations)
1. Import each randomized CSV group into the newsletter platform and add a separate tag.
2. Build dynamic segments from the tags and filter to active subscribers.
3. Duplicate the email so the copy stays consistent, change the link and price, then send each version to its assigned group.
4. Exclude prior test groups from later campaigns to avoid sending the same offer repeatedly.
> "We want to AB test the entire email which I think you can do in Beehive as well but I haven't learned it yet so I'm not trying to learn it right friction momentum time kills all deals etc." — Chris Koerner (~17:03)

**State the price in the email** (sales)
1. Compare a price-disclosed email with one that hides the price, using separate payment links to track sales.
2. Koerner reports that the disclosed-price version converted better in his test and chose to show the price in later emails.
> "So the lesson, tell them the price in the email." — Chris Koerner (~40:46)

**Organize delivery after validating sales** (operations)
1. Start with an existing file folder and a payment redirect to deliver the material.
2. After purchases arrive, organize the materials into clear folders in a community platform.
3. Invite buyers and provide a short walkthrough video.
> "So, we basically downloaded all of the Google Drive content and uploaded it here uh into separate folders." — Chris Koerner (~38:46)

**Use a small list to test, then scale the winning email** (customer_acquisition)
1. Use a small randomized sample to test price, copy length, and payment destination.
2. Track each payment link and email campaign separately.
3. Send the best-performing version to subscribers who have not received the earlier tests.
> "After a few more AB tests, I'm going to know exactly what people need. And then I'll send the right price with the right messaging to everyone on my newsletter." — Chris Koerner (~31:59)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Look for a relevant advantage you already have, such as an audience, work experience, or knowledge of a market.
- *mindset_execution*: Get an offer in front of customers quickly; Koerner argues that branding work and extra planning can slow momentum.
- *validation_testing*: Run a simple, controlled test instead of overcomplicating early validation.
- *pricing*: Test prices with comparable groups and consider revenue per subscriber, not only how many people buy.
- *other*: Avoid hiding a price when a test suggests buyers respond better to seeing it before clicking.
- *customer_acquisition*: Trust from an existing audience can make it easier to sell a new offer, but that trust depends on delivering what was promised.
- *customer_acquisition*: A small audience can still support sales when the offer fits its needs; the host uses a second-hand example and a hypothetical projection.
- *offer_design*: Repurpose existing material into a clear product before investing in new content production.
- *offer_design*: Stand behind a course when the seller believes it delivers the value promised, and keep the offer aligned with that value.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Build more than one source of income because platform decisions can remove a business's main revenue source.
