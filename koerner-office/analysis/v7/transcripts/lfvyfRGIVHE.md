## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Matt (Jackson, Michigan), a former high school math teacher who started flipping couches, appliances and church pews on Facebook Marketplace/Craigslist, then moved to riding and zero-turn lawnmowers. ## Tactics and playbook steps

**Win on speed and lock the deal** (acquisition)
1. Monitor listings and respond within minutes
2. Get the seller on the phone, not text
3. Pay a deposit via Venmo/PayPal to secure the deal
4. Ask them to confirm their word is good
> "give them a deposit on Venmo, PayPal, any of those sorts of things, and that will absolutely secure the deal." — Matt (~8:45)

**Outsource driving before negotiating** (operations)
1. Do the first 5-10 deals yourself
2. Negotiate and agree a price by phone first
3. Send a low-skill driver at $20-25/hr (more if their truck)
4. Build a call list of 4-5 backups
5. Have the driver use an inspection sheet and call you to renegotiate if needed

**Negotiate: make the seller name the number** (sales)
1. Build rapport by talking up the machine
2. Ask what the lowest price they'd consider is
3. If they won't say, offer an unrealistically low number
4. Stay silent after their number
5. Deliver or other perks only after price
> "He who speaks first loses." — Matt (~21:51)

**Source from dealers who dislike used stock** (acquisition)
1. Call local dealers asking if they have used units
2. Find ones not advertising inventory well
3. Buy their best units, ask what else they have
4. Become a regular contact for trade-ins
> "There are dealers that just hate dealing with used stuff." — Matt (~29:46)

**Test, don't fix** (operations)
1. Choose items that can be fully tested (blades on, drives forward and back)
2. Buy near perfect condition at low price
3. Sell at market within a week

**Be transparent about being a buyer-seller** (sales)
1. Don't hide that you trade
2. Use wording like a dealer/trade buyer instead of 'flip'
3. Follow up with past sellers for repeat deals and referrals

## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: The edge in flipping is buying below market, not repairing; the value is in the buy price.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Outsource the lowest-skill, most time-consuming task (driving) first and keep negotiation for yourself until it is systemised.
- *niche_selection*: Higher-ticket items take the same time and skill but yield bigger margins, so move up the price ladder (couch to mower to equipment).
- *customer_acquisition*: One reliable supplier relationship (a dealer) can provide volume leverage rather than hunting deals one by one.
- *pricing*: Take 80% of the profit and move stock fast rather than maximising each sale.
- *validation_testing*: Entering a new market, a first purchase is a near no-brainer win because you know the price was off against your outside view; learn by doing.
- *operations*: Pay people market rates and use a trusted network; your time is worth more than DIY tasks.
- *mindset_execution*: The niche matters less than picking something with real demand and sticking with it for years.
- *scaling*: Business evolves: a side flip became a profit-share programme and then a dealership via opportunistic real estate.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Speed matters: a good deal is gone minutes later, and without a deposit another buyer can offer more.
- Flipping items needing repair or doing the fixing yourself lowers hourly return and prevents scaling.
- Trailers must be hitched with safety chains; Matt's first trailer detached on a highway.
- Sold appliance with a faulty heating element made him doubt the work; avoid selling untested goods.
- Don't spend your time hauling; eight hours a day in the car is unsustainable.
- Pricing above market (Koerner's mower listed at $3,500 for weeks) means it won't sell.
