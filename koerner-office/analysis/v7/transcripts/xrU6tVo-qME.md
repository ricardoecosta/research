## Tactics and playbook steps

**Choose a bid you can understand and deliver** (validation_testing)
1. Read the solicitation and its attachments before committing to a bid.
2. Prioritize service contracts when starting; Natalie says they were easier to explain to a service provider than product orders.
3. Confirm that a subcontractor can do the work and provide the required information before submitting.
> "My very first step on what I do is look for something that I understand what they're looking for." — Natalie (~6:12)

**Get a subcontractor quote before bidding** (operations)
1. Search for providers in the contract's location and service category.
2. Ask for a quote and confirm willingness to work before submitting the proposal.
3. Do not rely on a supplier who is slow to respond or reluctant to take the work.
4. Check reviews, website, prior work and apparent credibility; seek a contingency provider after winning.
> "I always get a quote first, right? And get somebody that's willing to work before submit, right?" — Natalie (~8:58)

**Use a broad business name** (product)
1. Choose a generic company name so the name does not imply that the business only serves one trade.
2. Still match the proposal to the solicitation's requirements and provide the required subcontractor information.
> "I always tell people to choose a generic name, so you can go for any type of proposal." — Natalie (~5:36)

**Check past award pricing before setting a bid** (pricing)
1. Search USAspending.gov for prior contracts, contractors, agency spend and relevant industry codes.
2. Compare the prior contract and scope with the current solicitation.
3. Account for inflation and the current scope when setting a price; do not assume the government will accept a high markup.
> "Nowadays, when I'm pricing things, I'm able to check past pricing. I'm able to look up to see, you know, hey, what did the government pay for something like this before?" — Natalie (~12:54)

**Organize bid work across the week** (operations)
1. Set aside a block to find opportunities.
2. Prepare scopes of work to send to potential subcontractors.
3. Contact vendors, collect required information and review opportunities again before submission.
> "Monday, I'm looking for opportunity. I'm gonna spend two hours just looking for as many opportunities I can." — Natalie (~40:33)

**Use AI to screen solicitations and prepare proposals** (automation_ai)
1. Create a project in Claude or ChatGPT for a solicitation.
2. Attach the solicitation and its supporting documents.
3. Use a prepared prompt to identify whether the opportunity is worth pursuing and extract submission requirements.
4. Use AI to help draft the proposal, then check all instructions and facts yourself.
> "I make a project. I think you can do it on chat, JBT. Also, go on the project, attach all the attachments." — Natalie (~25:31)

**Follow the evaluation criteria exactly** (sales)
1. Read each solicitation's evaluation criteria.
2. Check whether it requires local presence, past performance, a particular price approach or limits on subcontracting.
3. Skip opportunities whose mandatory conditions cannot be met.
> "On every single contract, they give you an evaluation criteria. On every single contract, they tell you this is what they're looking for." — Natalie (~19:04)

**Build a niche after learning the process** (niche_selection)
1. Use early bids to learn the submission process and test what is understandable and deliverable.
2. As experience and past performance build, focus on industries where the business and partners have experience.
3. Use qualifying partner past performance when the solicitation permits it.
> "But once you start building your business and government contracting, it's definitely good to niche down to a specific industry." — Natalie (~24:23)

**Keep reliable subcontractors and a backup** (hiring_outsourcing)
1. Look for responsiveness, strong communication, credibility and a real need for the work.
2. Review the provider's reviews, website and previous work.
3. Find a contingency subcontractor after a contract is awarded.
4. Raise scope or performance problems with the government early and propose a solution.
> "Communication has to be impeccable. We have to be on a safe page at all times." — Natalie (~35:58)

**Treat bid losses as part of the process** (mindset)
1. Expect to lose some bids.
2. Keep submitting well-qualified proposals and improve as you learn the process.
3. Balance persistence with checking that a subcontractor can actually deliver.
> "You have to understand you're not going to win every single one." — Natalie (~32:01)

**Add real coordination value as the prime** (legal)
1. Review the host's clarification on self-performance and subcontracting limits for the relevant contract type.
2. Perform the required share of work where applicable.
3. Coordinate the project, provide strategy or other real value and document that work.
4. Ask a PTAC or APEX Accelerator office for free advice on the proposal and local requirements.
> "If you don't do anything whatsoever, no strategy, consulting, coordination, etc. It's just called a pass-through scheme, which is very illegal." — Chris Koerner (~47:36)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Start with opportunities you can understand and subcontractors who can deliver them.
- *operations*: Confirm supply and price before bidding; winning without a delivery partner can turn an award into a problem.
- *pricing*: Use past award data to inform pricing, while accounting for the current scope and inflation.
- *customer_acquisition*: Follow the solicitation's evaluation criteria and submission instructions precisely.
- *mindset_execution*: Expect losses and improve the process rather than treating each unsuccessful bid as proof the model cannot work.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Reliable partners and strong communication are central to keeping a subcontracted contract running smoothly.
- *automation_ai*: AI can reduce the time needed to understand long solicitations and draft a response, but the bid still needs informed review.
- *niche_selection*: After learning the process, concentrating on a niche can build useful past performance and help target larger contracts.
- *legal_risk*: The prime needs to provide real value and understand applicable performance and subcontracting limits.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Separate contract revenue from profit, and include travel, delivery costs and payment timing when pricing.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The host's post-interview clarification says some contracts require the prime to perform at least 50% of the work, with an exception, and says certain subcontracting arrangements have a 50% cap. Check the solicitation and applicable rules.
- The host says a prime that contributes no strategy, consulting or coordination may be operating an illegal pass-through scheme.
- A prime's subcontractor is not responsible to the government in place of the prime; failures and contract performance problems can land on the prime.
- Do not submit a bid before confirming that a subcontractor is willing and able to do the work.
- Missed requirements and incorrect proposal formatting can disqualify a bid even when the bidder can perform the work.
- In the UK, software-led subcontractor outreach must comply with PECR and calling rules; do not assume US cold-contact practices transfer.
