## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate visible demand before committing** (validation_testing)
1. Notice an event concept on TikTok and check the original operator's account and website.
2. Look for evidence of demand such as high-view videos and sold-out scheduled events.
3. Compare the concept with existing bingo or trivia nights that already draw people.
> "I checked their website and they had 10 events next month and half of them were sold out already." — Guest (name not stated) (~1:41)

**Run a small test event at a venue you control** (validation_testing)
1. Use an existing venue to test logistics and audience response.
2. Charge a low introductory ticket price and include a simple extra such as food.
3. Treat the first event as a test of setup, hosting, and customer reactions, even if it loses money.
> "Yeah. It was like, okay, let's see if this actually can work. Let's see how the logistics work. Let's make sure Steph's comfortable." — Guest (name not stated) (~4:00)

**Piggyback on venue promotion** (acquisition)
1. Approach venues that already host trivia or bingo and have enough seating.
2. Offer an event on a quieter night so the venue can earn from food and drink sales.
3. Agree a no-fee arrangement in which the event operators handle the work and marketing.
4. Ask the venue to share event content with its existing audience.
> "We do all the work. We market it and then we just hopefully they want to share it with their people, but otherwise we collect the money." — Guest (name not stated) (~7:30)

**Use organic social posts and community groups** (acquisition)
1. Post short event videos organically on TikTok and Instagram.
2. Use personal accounts to seed early reach, then make content from actual events.
3. Create Facebook Events for each date.
4. Reach out to local plant groups with an event that supports the community rather than only advertising the business.
> "And just do videos. Tik Tok, Instagram has worked amazingly well for us." — Guest (name not stated) (~16:33)

**Presell dates and use extra boards to increase event revenue** (pricing)
1. List event dates weeks or months ahead and collect ticket revenue before buying event supplies.
2. Offer additional bingo boards at the event; the guest reports selling around 30 extra boards at a 50-person event.
3. Use observed no-shows to decide whether to allow a limited number of oversold tickets or walk-ins.
> "And the events sell you know weeks months in advance which is great for our cash flow." — Guest (name not stated) (~12:43)

**Keep the initial kit small and use venue equipment** (operations)
1. Buy only the core supplies needed for the event, including plants and printed bingo materials.
2. Ask whether the venue already has a speaker and microphone before buying more equipment.
3. Use an existing venue or host at home to avoid venue hire costs.
> "And even like half the venues we go to have speakers and microphone there to do trivia. You just buy plants and print some papers." — Guest (name not stated) (~13:33)

**Use hosts to reduce event-day pressure** (hiring)
1. Run early events yourselves while learning setup and venue logistics.
2. Hire hosts to help run events when the workload or event size makes it worthwhile.
3. Compare the reduction in stress with the lower profit after host pay.
> "So, like I don't mind making a little bit less to make it easier." — Guest (name not stated) (~12:11)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Look for evidence that customers already attend a related activity before building a new event format.
- *validation_testing*: Test the logistics with a small event before scaling; a first event can be useful even if it loses money.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use venue partners' existing audiences and give them a reason to promote the event.
- *niche_selection*: A niche event can appeal to people seeking a social activity even when they are not enthusiasts of the topic.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Preselling events can help fund supplies and improve cash flow because ticket sales arrive before the event date.
- *other*: Use venue downtime to create value for both sides: the organizer earns ticket revenue and the venue may sell more food and drinks.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: A profitable operation can still involve substantial hands-on work, and hiring help trades some margin for lower stress.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use organic content to test demand; the guest says ticket sales moved with TikTok views despite a small following.
- *scaling*: A repeatable event concept may support other themes or locations, but those extensions still need their own validation and operating plan.
