## Tactics and playbook steps

**Rehire score (stay filter) exercise** (hiring)
1. Write down every team member's name on one sheet
2. Next to each, answer yes/no: if they quit and reapplied next week, would I hire them again?
3. For each yes, ask if you treat them well enough that they'd say the same about you
4. For each no, schedule one honest, respectful conversation within a couple of weeks
> "If this person quit tomorrow and reapplied next week, knowing everything I know about them right now, would I hire them again?" — Narrator (~17:10)

**Hire for integrity, intelligence, energy with a paid trial shift** (hiring)
1. Screen for high integrity, high intelligence and very high energy; any weakness disqualifies
2. Ask about a time they did the right thing when nobody was watching
3. Run a single paid trial shift rather than relying on interviews
4. Define the outcomes the role must deliver before writing the listing
> "High integrity, high intelligence, very high energy. All three present [music] or it's a no." — Narrator (~7:26)

**Walton walk on one competitor** (product)
1. Pick the competitor that scares you most (shop, website, channel, listing)
2. Spend 20 minutes looking for the one thing they do better than you
3. Write it down in one sentence
4. Copy the principle (not logo/name/words) and ship a better version within the week
5. Repeat the watching continuously, even when winning
> "Spend 20 minutes going through it with one single goal. Find the one thing they do better than you, not 10 things." — Narrator (~36:26)

**One unbreakable promise** (operations)
1. Choose a promise simple enough to keep on your worst day (e.g. free 30-day recheck, redo any room)
2. Display it where customers see it (wall, receipt, door)
3. Never break it to save a few dollars
4. Make things right before the customer asks
> "Pick one promise so simple you could keep it on your worst day." — Narrator (~42:29)

**Three-day follow-up calls** (operations)
1. Log name, car/job and date in a notebook for every job
2. Three days later, make about five calls in a slow hour
3. Ask only whether everything is working okay, no upsell
4. Catch problems before they become bad reviews
> "A simple follow-up call 3 days after every job just to ask if the car is running right. No upsell, no pitch" — Narrator (~51:14)

**Underpromise and overdeliver on quotes** (pricing)
1. Quote longer timelines or higher prices than you expect to need
2. Finish early or charge less than quoted
3. Show the worn part and talk customers out of non-urgent work to build trust
> "Promise less than you can do, then beat your own quote every time." — Narrator (~49:29)

## Lessons

- *hiring_outsourcing*: Retention is a deliberate system: pay, scheduling, recognition and honest conversations all act as filters on who stays.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Hire for integrity, intelligence and energy over resume experience; skills can be taught but integrity cannot.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Hire slowly and address poor fit early and respectfully; vague standards hurt everyone.
- *scaling*: You can beat a better-funded competitor by systematically copying proven ideas and executing better, not by inventing something new.
- *validation_testing*: A bigger competitor's experiments are free research; observe them continuously, especially when you are winning.
- *customer_acquisition*: Customer churn is usually a trust problem rather than a price problem; retention is far cheaper than acquisition.
- *pricing*: Fair pricing and talking customers out of unnecessary spend builds lifetime trust worth more than the margin forgone.
- *customer_acquisition*: Redirect marketing spend toward existing customers (service, follow-up, guarantees) so they drive referrals.
- *mindset_execution*: Pick one small action tonight (a list, a walk, a written promise); simple consistent execution beats clever one-off campaigns.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Keeping a poor-fit employee too long damages good staff and customers; act early and kindly rather than hoping it improves.
- Hiring and firing rules vary by jurisdiction; check local employment law before acting. Content is not legal advice.
- Copy principles, not property: do not take competitors' logos, names or exact wording.
- Padding bills or grabbing extra margin teaches customers not to trust you and costs repeat business.
