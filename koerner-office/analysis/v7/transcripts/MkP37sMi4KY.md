## Tactics and playbook steps

**Out-show the boss by arriving early** (mindset)
1. Identify what time the senior decision-maker arrives (she found the MD came in at 6:30)
2. Arrive before them and leave after them
3. Let performance plus visible effort get noticed, which led to opportunities others did not get
> "I didn't wait for someone to notice my work on its own. I engineered the moment where it couldn't be missed." — Codie Sanchez (~8:43)

**Negotiate pay tied to outcomes** (pricing)
1. Negotiate based on growth or revenue you drive, not on hours worked
2. Seek bonuses and commissions on deals so most pay is results-linked
3. Always ask; also negotiate with vendors instead of accepting the first number
> "I got there by negotiating my worth based on how much growth or revenue I drove the company." — Codie Sanchez (~6:41)

**Go in person to get access** (acquisition)
1. Fly to the employer's HQ for a role rather than only applying online
2. Prepare and deliver a live presentation
3. Pay your own way to meet partners across cities if needed
4. Stay in contact after a rejection; a contact later circled back with an offer
> "The world will not hand you access. You have to go out and get it yourself loudly, uninvited if you have to." — Codie Sanchez (~6:18)

**Publicly share proof of your work** (acquisition)
1. Show your building progress openly instead of staying quiet
2. Treat sharing results as 'proof' for the people deciding whether to trust you
> "all that is called proof. It's the most important word if you want to sell things to people." — Codie Sanchez (~10:00)

**A-player hiring matrix** (hiring)
1. Look for a history of winning with receipts, not just credentials
2. Prefer people who have done hard things and stuck with them long enough to get good
3. Hire a little slower, fire quickly when wrong
4. Re-evaluate who counts as an A player as the company grows
> "I look for a history of winning. I don't want just credentials. I want receipts." — Codie Sanchez (~18:55)

**Classify decisions as one-way or two-way doors** (operations)
1. Decide fast on reversible (two-way door) choices
2. Be careful on irreversible (one-way door) choices
3. Treat a fast wrong answer as cheaper than a slow right one
> "a fast, wrong answer is almost always cheaper than a slow right one" — Codie Sanchez (~17:01)

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: Opportunity and access come from proximity to high performers; surround yourself with people who have already broken through.
- *pricing*: Be paid for outcomes rather than effort; nobody cares how hard you work, only what you deliver.
- *pricing*: Ask for what you want and negotiate; accepting the first offer leaves money on the table.
- *customer_acquisition*: Visibility matters: unseen work is functionally the same as work that never happened, so promote yourself and your business.
- *mindset_execution*: Move fast on reversible decisions; indecision has a daily cost and you learn nothing from it.
- *other*: Keep emotions managed at work to be trusted with harder, higher-paid responsibility.
- *mindset_execution*: Build in intense seasons: early-stage building rarely allows work-life balance, and the trade is temporary.
- *niche_selection*: Owning a business is the only route to wealth that can be engineered rather than hoped for, as opposed to inheriting, marrying money, being a Fortune 500 exec or running an asset manager.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Team quality drives growth: hire only A players, expect turnover as the company scales, and re-define the A-player profile at each stage.
- *other*: Relocating to work in unfamiliar places builds more capability than short tourist-style travel (her view, controversial and anecdotal).
- *customer_acquisition*: Her argument against remote work (in-person builds pull, trust and opportunity) is opinion; a solo agent-run founder may weigh it differently, but in-person networking is a valid access channel.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Friends and family may discourage risk because they want you safe, even though risk is where the money is.
- Calling your company a family promises unconditional belonging you cannot honour when you need to fire or review people.
- Keeping the wrong person during a growth stretch is costly; fire quickly.
- Waiting to be discovered lets a competitor with a worse product and a louder voice take your money.
