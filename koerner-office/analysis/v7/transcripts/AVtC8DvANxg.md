## Tactics and playbook steps

**Use AI for market research on ideas** (product)
1. Ask an AI assistant to do market research on your idea
2. Ask whether the idea would sell, e.g. on Reddit
3. Look for niches the large AI labs are unlikely to serve well
> "Get your AI to go do some market research." — Robert Scoble (~29:08)

**Test new tech personally before judging it** (mindset)
1. Try the product or demo yourself (e.g. a free Apple Store Vision Pro demo)
2. Track tools via curated lists to stay current

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: New technology is resisted at first by most people, so early adoption of disruptive tools is where advantage lies.
- *validation_testing*: Customers often reject what they have not experienced; watch behaviour after exposure (e.g. Google staff all looking at phones within three rides) rather than stated preferences.
- *niche_selection*: Data and user scale create flywheels in AI: more users make the system smarter, favouring incumbents, so small players should avoid head-on competition.
- *mindset_execution*: Constraints drive creativity and entrepreneurship, as in Israel's startup density.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI itself as a research assistant and idea validator before building.
- *niche_selection*: Early adoption of new hardware often comes through enterprise use cases before consumers.
- *scaling*: The AI boom is larger and faster than the dot-com era, with funding at a far higher scale, so timing and speed matter.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Brain-computer interfaces that write to the brain raise free-will and manipulation concerns.
- AGI-era systems with autonomy and control of infrastructure such as the power grid could cause serious harm.
- Customer research can mislead on future products because customers cannot imagine what does not exist yet.
