## Tactics and playbook steps

**Pick a niche by checking ad competition** (acquisition)
1. Ask Claude for underserved niches where people search for software but competition is weak
2. Shortlist niches (pet grooming, pressure washing, gutter cleaning, tattoo booking)
3. Check which competitors exist and whether they bid on Google Ads for the keywords
4. Choose the niche with weak products and almost no bidders, so cost per click stays low
> "Find an underserved niche where people are searching for software, but the competition is weak." — Tim Richard (~1:06)

**AI-driven competitor feature gap build** (product)
1. Have Claude scrape and analyse every competitor (4-5): features, pricing, positioning, strengths, weaknesses
2. Combine all features into one list and add differentiators
3. Have Claude produce a screen-by-screen product plan
4. Get a single build prompt and paste it into Claude Code
5. Iterate on bugs; ask it to explain things simply when stuck
6. Use Claude design for branding, logos and colours
> "Claude scraped and analyzed all the major tattoo shop booking softwares out there." — Tim Richard (~3:09)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Choose a niche where ad competition is thin, since low cost per click is what makes paid acquisition profitable.
- *validation_testing*: Check whether big incumbents already cover a niche under a broader product and bid on its keywords before committing.
- *automation_ai*: AI can compress product research, planning and a first build of a software product into days for a non-technical founder.
- *customer_acquisition*: A measurable acquisition plan (paid ads) suits a founder who does not want to do outreach or become an influencer.
- *validation_testing*: Building is the easy part; the real test is whether customers sign up and ads pay back, which this video has not tested yet.
- *niche_selection*: Domain familiarity in a boring industry helps judge whether existing software is poor.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Competing with big software firms that bid on the same keywords is hard because of their large budgets.
- Making the product does not guarantee money; the opportunities are opinions and results are not guaranteed.
