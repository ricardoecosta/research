## Tactics and playbook steps

**Audit a neighbourhood before spending** (validation_testing)
1. Walk or drive through neighbourhoods and record the types of mailbox damage you see.
2. Write down exact addresses and look for at least 20 opportunities within a 15-minute drive.
3. Talk to homeowners and try to sell the work while the need is visible.
4. Do not buy tools until a first job is secured.
> "You're not just doing research. You're going to sell to these people." — Chris Koerner (~19:02)

**Use early jobs to create proof** (offer_design)
1. Do your own mailbox first if needed and photograph the process.
2. Take before-and-after photos and videos of every paid job.
3. Use early jobs to gather reviews and build a portfolio.
4. Store job photos in Google Drive for future promotional material.
> "Do it for yourself first just to document the process and to be your own case study." — Reddit commenter (~4:41)

**Start with an accessible price, then raise it with evidence** (pricing)
1. Quote early jobs below the rate you eventually want while building case studies and reviews.
2. Track actual materials and labour so later prices reflect real job costs.
3. Raise prices after you have photos and customer proof.
> "you just want to get some case studies. You want to get some fivestar reviews. You want to get some traction." — Chris Koerner (~19:38)

**Get discovered inside neighbourhood groups** (acquisition)
1. Join relevant neighbourhood Facebook groups and Nextdoor.
2. Watch for posts asking for help and reply quickly with a useful answer.
3. Share work and helpful information before making direct promotional posts.
4. Use before-and-after pictures to show the result.
> "We're talking about you kind of lurking in the shadows, waiting for someone else to post so you can jump on it as soon as possible." — Chris Koerner (~15:14)

**Test targeted local advertising** (acquisition)
1. Select older neighbourhoods where damaged mailboxes are common.
2. Run Facebook or Nextdoor ads only in those areas.
3. Test 10–20 marketing angles and keep the ones that perform best.
4. Time direct mail after major snow or wind storms and include before-and-after photos.
> "I guarantee you if you test like 10 to 20 marketing angles, you're going to find one or two that command all of your time because they work so well." — Chris Koerner (~16:35)

**Build referral routes through adjacent trades** (partnerships)
1. Ask handymen whether they take mailbox jobs and offer to pay for referrals.
2. Approach landscapers who visit many homes and ask them to refer visibly damaged mailboxes.
3. Build relationships with realtors who advise sellers on home improvements.
> "I call this piggybacking." — Chris Koerner (~23:32)

**Keep the business name broad enough to allow expansion** (scaling)
1. Start in the mailbox niche, but avoid a name that prevents customers from understanding adjacent services.
2. Add related services such as pressure washing or exterior painting only if mailbox demand is too small for the target business size.
> "Don't pigeon hole your business with the name." — Chris Koerner (~22:27)

**Expand capacity with equipment, crews, and nearby territories** (scaling)
1. Start with a post-hole digger and reinvest in a motorized auger to speed up jobs.
2. Add second and third crews and serve nearby cities if demand supports it.
3. Seek referral partnerships with handymen and landscapers.
> "You want to reinvest in better tools. Start with a post hole digger. Upgrade to an augur." — Chris Koerner (~21:19)

**Offer custom products that can be shipped** (product)
1. Make, buy, or import distinctive mailbox designs rather than competing only on standard local installations.
2. Test products that are visually appealing and suited to photos and social sharing.
3. Use a storefront and targeted social ads to test demand for particular designs.
> "You can buy them from a third party vendor, but you're going to be the guy known for the weird, wacky mailboxes." — Chris Koerner (~10:48)

**Qualify HOA work against the sales effort** (sales)
1. Compare expected HOA job value with the extra effort required to win and deliver the contract.
2. Meet property managers and attend HOA meetings to build relationships.
3. Explore recurring maintenance or replacement work after winning a community.
> "Sell to one to sell to many." — Chris Koerner (~13:01)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Look for customers whose need is already visible; targeting a broken mailbox can make prospecting more focused than selling a general service.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use visible evidence of completed work, such as before-and-after photos, to build trust and create reusable marketing material.
- *validation_testing*: Test customer willingness to pay before investing heavily in tools, branding, or business setup.
- *pricing*: Early traction and reviews may matter more than maximizing price on the first few jobs.
- *customer_acquisition*: Neighbourhood marketing works best when it contributes value and responds to a real request instead of indiscriminately promoting.
- *validation_testing*: Use targeted experiments to find the audience and marketing message that produces results.
- *scaling*: Keep the business name broad enough to allow sensible adjacent services later.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Compare the value of a larger contract with the extra effort needed to win and fulfil it.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use local partners who already visit relevant properties to find opportunities and referrals.
- *mindset_execution*: Choose a business based on workable problems and customer demand, rather than status or how impressive the idea sounds.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The host says not to place flyers inside mailboxes, describing it as illegal in the US. Check UK postal and direct-marketing rules before using a similar tactic.
- Validate real willingness to pay before buying tools or formalizing the business. If direct prospects consistently prefer DIY or an existing handyman, the host recommends moving on or gathering a larger sample.
- The host says jobs can involve insurance, unhappy customers, and free rework.
- HOA contracts have longer sales cycles and can be difficult to manage.
- The host warns that spreadsheet projections can look attractive while real operations remain difficult.
- In the UK, follow PECR rules for unsolicited electronic marketing to individuals and sole traders; screen calls against TPS/CTPS where required.
- Do not assume the host's gross-margin, conversion, demand, or market-size claims are independently verified.
- The host promotes his own newsletter and podcast during the video, creating a promotional conflict.
