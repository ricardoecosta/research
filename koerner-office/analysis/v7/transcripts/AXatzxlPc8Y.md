## Tactics and playbook steps

**Micro-problem filter (5 questions, pass at least 4)** (product)
1. Who exactly has it (job title or situation)?
2. How often does it occur (weekly or better)?
3. What does it cost them now (time, money, embarrassment, missed sales)?
4. Are they already paying something to handle it?
5. Can one feature fix the core?
6. Fill in: 'I want to build an app that does X to solve Y problem for these users'
> "If your fix needs 10 features, it's not a micro app. You need to shrink it" — Host (~7:10)

**Proof checklist (need 3 or more)** (acquisition)
1. Find paid competitors in app stores and Product Hunt; check reviews and ratings
2. Search Reddit, Facebook groups and niche forums for unprompted complaints
3. Read competitors' one-star and three-star reviews for gaps
4. Google '[competitor] alternative' to find people looking to switch
5. Look for homemade workarounds like spreadsheets or templates
6. Copy exact customer quotes into a doc unedited for use on the sales page
> "evidence cost somebody something" — Host (~8:25)

**Switch conversation interviews** (sales)
1. Find 5 to 10 people matching the buyer persona in the communities where complaints appeared
2. Ask questions rather than pitch, and follow group rules
3. Ask about the past: 'Walk me through the last time this happened'
4. Ask what they use now, what they hate, what they tried that failed, and what would make them switch
5. Never ask 'Would you use my app?'
6. Listen for heat and existing spend
> "The question that's missing is, "Would you use my app?" Please never ask that." — Host (~13:07)

**Smallest paid promise test** (product)
1. Write one sentence: 'I help this person do this one thing without this pain for this price'
2. Option 1 pre-sale: one-page site, mock-up, price, Stripe buy button, ship date and money-back guarantee
3. Option 2 do-it-by-hand: deliver the result manually for a small fee for a few weeks
4. Option 3 specific waitlist with founding price
5. Write the pass mark on a sticky note before starting
6. After 2 weeks, build if the mark is hit; otherwise change one variable (price, persona, feature) and retest or drop it
> "If nobody will pay you to do it by hand, they probably won't pay for your software that does it, either." — Host (~15:44)

**15-minute competitor review mining** (acquisition)
1. Pick your app idea
2. Find the biggest existing competitor
3. Read 20 one-star and three-star reviews
4. Copy lines that make you think 'I can fix that'

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: AI made building cheap but customer attention and your evenings are still scarce, so validate before building.
- *niche_selection*: Existing paying competitors are evidence of a market, but you still need a specific reason for someone to switch.
- *validation_testing*: Prefer evidence that cost people something (money, time, effort) over compliments or opinions.
- *mindset_execution*: Pre-commit to a numeric pass/fail threshold and deadline before running a test.
- *validation_testing*: Ask about past behaviour, not future intentions, to get truthful customer answers.
- *offer_design*: Shrink offers to one feature for one specific person; vague 'help small businesses' ideas are too big.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI as a brainstorming and sparring partner, but it cannot replace real customers.
- *mindset_execution*: Walking away after a two-week test is a cheap success, not failure; log what you learned each time.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use customers' unedited words from complaints and reviews to write sales copy.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Proving a market exists is not the same as proving anyone will buy from you; you need a reason to switch.
- Do not ask 'would you use my app?'; people say yes because it costs them nothing.
- Zero competitors and zero complaints usually means an empty road for a reason, not an open lane.
- Set the pass mark before testing, or you will grade on a curve and move the goalposts.
- Honour the refund if a pre-sale app does not ship; reputation cannot be rebuilt by AI.
- Treat YouTubers' income claims as their story, not your forecast; avoid gaming votes or buying fake downloads.
- Do not pitch in communities that ban promotion; ask questions instead.
