### I1. Tariff sourcing agent / alternative-factory finder for Shopify stores

- **Customer:** US ecommerce brands (e.g. Shopify Plus stores) and companies importing from tariff-hit countries
- **Problem:** Tariffs make sourcing from China and other affected countries costlier; brands need alternative manufacturers
- **Offer:** Find better/alternative factories within 24 hours for three of the brand's products; paid only if the brand uses the supplier; or act as sourcing agent for a percentage of imports
- **Business model:** Success-based finder fee or broker percentage of imports
- **Pricing:** Success fee / percentage of imports.  Only charge if the client ends up using the supplier found; broker's fee or finder fee as a percentage of everything imported; no figures stated
- **Acquisition:** cold_email: Scrape Shopify Plus stores with owner/exec contacts from BuiltWith (or have an Upwork VA do it for $20-50); email offering to find alternative manufacturers; list their three best sellers found via the /collections/all?sort_by=best-selling URL suffix; only do the work after they reply.
- **Delivery:** Research on ImportYeti and Jungle Scout to find factories supplying similar products in less-affected countries, order samples to compare quality, and report alternatives to the client
- **Unit economics (claims):** Upwork VA can scrape a list of Shopify stores for $20-50; 95%+ of cold emails get no response
- **Tools:** ImportYeti, Jungle Scout, BuiltWith, Upwork, Shopify
- **Startup cost:** Low: ImportYeti account plus $20-50 for list scraping
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — Tariff framing is US-specific, but UK brands also seek supplier diversification. Cold email must target limited companies. ImportYeti covers US import data so UK brand coverage is weaker.
- **Risks:** Success-fee model means unpaid research effort; Clients may bypass you once they have the factory; Brands may not disclose actual purchases to verify fee
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 2/5 (Lists and emails can be automated, but cold email to Shopify owners who are sole traders/individuals is restricted under UK PECR; targeting limited companies is allowed. Needs care.); onboarding 3/5 (Agent can handle replies and collect the three products.); delivery 2/5 (Agents can search ImportYeti-type data and shortlist factories, but supplier vetting and sample ordering need a human.); billing 3/5 (Success-fee invoicing needs tracking of orders but is largely automatable.); support 3/5 (Agent handles routine questions; negotiation escalates to a human.)
> "I'll find you better factories within 24 hours. Tell me three products of yours that you'd like a better manufacturer for" — Chris Koerner (~5:37)
> "act as a sourcing agent for them and make a percentage of everything they import as a broker's fee or a finder fee" — Chris Koerner (~4:25)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Find winning products then reverse-engineer their factories** (product)
1. Pick a niche such as baby goods or kitchenware
2. Use Jungle Scout to find Amazon products with high sales and demand exceeding supply, checking recent trends
3. Search ImportYeti for the same or similar manufacturers
4. Look for manufacturers in countries less affected by tariffs
5. Order samples from them and from the incumbent to compare quality
> "spend a couple hours one night finding random niche products that are selling like crazy" — Chris Koerner (~3:16)

**Cold-email Shopify stores with their best sellers, work only after reply** (acquisition)
1. Export Shopify Plus store list with owner contacts from BuiltWith or pay an Upwork VA $20-50
2. Add the best-selling sort suffix to each store URL to see top products
3. Email stating you know their top three products and will find three alternative manufacturers at no cost
4. Only do the work once they reply, charge only if they use a supplier you find
> "you don't get to work until they respond to you" — Chris Koerner (~6:56)

**Use import lead time as an early signal** (product)
1. Monitor ImportYeti for new product shipments
2. Use the 1-3 month lead time before products reach the US to anticipate launches

**Buy distressed business with downside protection** (finance)
1. Learn about tariff impacts to gain an information advantage
2. Negotiate seller financing
3. Structure deals so the seller can reclaim the business or real estate if you default

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Do not wait to do the work for cold prospects: validate interest with a low-effort email first, since most never respond.
- *offer_design*: Success-fee offers reduce buyer risk and can raise reply rates, at the cost of unpaid effort.
- *pricing*: Niche audiences of buyers/executives can command ad rates far above audience size.
- *automation_ai*: Software and digital businesses are insulated from physical tariffs and AI tools lower the build cost.
- *niche_selection*: Gaps left by AI builders (unfinished projects) create service opportunities.
- *mindset_execution*: Market predictions are unreliable; avoid panic decisions.
- *exit_acquisition*: Information asymmetry plus seller financing lets buyers cover downside when acquiring in a downturn.
- *niche_selection*: Crowded markets can still be worth entering if the industry is early in its long-term growth.
- *customer_acquisition*: Simple expertise built through a few hours of AI research plus public content can position you as an expert when demand outstrips supply.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Staffing/offshore recruiting is crowded; placement revenue isn't recurring so you're only as good as your last deal.
- Buying businesses amid panic is a bet: if you are wrong about the economy you likely lose, so structure downside protection.
- Avoid starting an ecommerce business reliant on imported goods right now.
- Host admits he panic-sold stocks at the start of COVID; don't panic-sell.
