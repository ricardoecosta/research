## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Rachel, who with her husband runs a restaurant specials-board ad business in Missoula and Knoxville: they give venues a free dry-erase sandwich board and sell six ad slots to local businesses at 600 USD a year each (3,600 USD per board, ~400 USD cost). They found hosts with copy-paste Instagram DMs and filled boards using guided owner referrals (12-20 names, ~6 closes). The real money is the upsell: about half of 63 advertisers bought reputation management (~400 USD/month), websites (1,000-6,000 USD) or AI receptionists via a GoHighLevel agency. They report ~50,000 USD board revenue in six months and 10,000-15,000 USD/month overall at ~85% margin; the takeaway is a cheap trusted foot-in-the-door offer feeding a higher-value service ladder.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Host-first, then advertisers** (acquisition)
1. Find a high-foot-traffic host (restaurants first, then coffee shops, gyms, breweries; avoid dentists/physio waiting rooms)
2. Send short copy-paste Instagram DMs with a photo, stressing it is free
3. Book a visit and bring the physical board; sign a 1-year visibility agreement that transfers if the venue is sold
4. Only then pitch advertisers, naming the venue
5. Print only once all six slots are paid
> "you want to start by finding the host location so you can tell the advertisers where it's going to go" — Rachel (~7:08)

**Guided referral prompts** (sales)
1. Ask 'is there anyone you don't want on the board?' with a joke
2. Then ask who does their insurance, who their attorney is, who they called when HVAC broke, which realtor eats there weekly
3. Leave with 12-20 names and phone them, naming the owner
4. Expect about 6 closes, filling a board in days
> "who do you know or like who does your insurance? Who handles your insurance? Who's your attorney?" — Rachel (~12:16)

**Sell reach, not ROI** (sales)
1. Ask the host for rough monthly customers
2. Tell advertisers they will be in front of about that many people every month
3. Frame it as branding: 'I see you everywhere in town'
4. Encourage advertisers to share photos on social
> "this is truly about branding. This is about just being everywhere at a low cost" — Rachel (~20:44)

**Micro-commitment upsell ladder** (sales)
1. Get paid for the board and place it
2. Email a photo of the live board
3. Run a GoHighLevel prospecting report; skip prospects scored at 20-30% likelihood unless they miss calls
4. Walk through citations and unanswered reviews; offer reputation management
5. Then websites, then AI chatbot/receptionist once they get more customers
> "you offer them the free thing and then the $50 thing and then the $500 thing and then the $5,000 thing" — Chris Koerner (~45:58)

**Automated review engine** (operations)
1. Connect GHL to the client's invoicing
2. On payment, send review requests 3 times by email and 3 by text
3. Reply to reviews to improve Google ranking
4. Post 5-star reviews to social and add a chat widget
5. Clone via GHL snapshot for each new client
> "it's set on an automation to go out three times via email and three times via text because they're more likely to respond to a text" — Rachel (~40:21)

**Themed boards by venue audience** (product)
1. Pick a venue with a specific audience (e.g. Humane Society)
2. Sell slots to matching advertisers (vets, groomers, pet supply)
3. Donate part of revenue to the host cause
> "we're launching two boards and from that money we're actually donating to the humane society" — Rachel (~54:21)

**Measure host social reach with an agent** (sales)
1. Track host venues' Instagram posts showing the board
2. Log views to a spreadsheet
3. Use the extra impressions to improve close rate or prices
> "anytime they post a picture of that board, you know, it could log that to a spreadsheet" — Chris Koerner (~17:16)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Put ads where people already look: a specials board by the host stand gets attention without new tech.
- *customer_acquisition*: Specific referral prompts unlock names that a broad 'who do you know?' never does.
- *offer_design*: A simple, familiar product is an easier first sale than AI and earns the trust needed to upsell AI later.
- *customer_acquisition*: Someone who has already given you money without regret trusts you far more than any cold emailer.
- *pricing*: Collect annual payment up front when the daily cost is tiny; it removes churn and admin.
- *niche_selection*: Finance/insurance/realtor advertisers buy brand awareness easily; home-service trades are harder for ads but easiest for review/website upsells.
- *automation_ai*: Software with flat pricing (GHL snapshots) makes each extra client almost pure margin.
- *customer_acquisition*: Local identity in outreach matters more in an AI-spam world.
- *mindset_execution*: Combinations of channel, industry, pricing and distribution create millions of untaken variations; 'saturated' is usually an excuse.
- *scaling*: Have the 'what else do you do?' answer ready so a side hustle can grow into an agency.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Board revenue is collected up front and is not recurring until renewals; you must keep placing new boards.
- Cold walk-ins went badly; appointments booked via DM close far better.
- Facebook blocks rapid cold messages; Instagram was more permissive (and in the UK PECR restricts cold DMs to sole traders).
- Avoid low-traffic hosts like dentist or physiotherapy waiting rooms.
- Don't open with AI; business owners are wary of it and saturated with AI pitches.
- Churn is still unknown since no board has completed a year.
- Episode contains promotions: GoHighLevel affiliate links and the host's Playmakers AI community.
