## Tactics and playbook steps

**Launch a service with a simple live demonstration** (validation_testing)
1. Buy or borrow a drone capable of producing useful thermal footage.
2. Film a simple demonstration on your own property, such as showing a thermal view and then identifying the subject with a zoom camera.
3. Display the footage at an event attended by likely customers.
4. Bring business cards and small takeaways; Mike says he ran out of both.
> "Your like foray into the whole industry was to just take a low stakes video of your own property of some deer. That's it. Go to an expo with your target audience right there" — Chris Koerner (~5:34)

**Use customer feedback to set an initial price** (pricing)
1. Ask potential customers what they expect the service to cost.
2. Treat feedback carefully when respondents may not be the buyer, but use specific comparisons to understand the value they see.
3. Mike initially considered $250 to $350, heard that the service was worth more, and later set a higher price with a different fee if no deer was found.
> "And so I took all that info that they were giving me that I was like, Geez, maybe I start high, right? I can always come down." — Mike (~6:18)

**Place simple flyers where the target customer already goes** (acquisition)
1. Print a flyer with the equipment, thermal image, and corresponding zoomed image.
2. Ask local bow shops and rifle ranges if you can display it.
3. Use the flyer to explain an unfamiliar service visually.
> "I printed off these brochures, like a a flyer, and I went to the local bow shops, uh local rifle ranges, that type of thing." — Mike (~10:11)

**Talk to the regulator and local enforcement before launching** (legal)
1. Ask the relevant state department how the intended drone use fits current rules.
2. Explain the service's purpose and boundaries.
3. Meet local game wardens and demonstrate the equipment.
4. Check the current written regulations rather than relying only on an informal conversation.
> "I did talk to the the Ohio Department of Natural Resources head guy and told him what I planned on doing" — Mike (~11:17)

**Build a niche directory around a service audience** (product)
1. Let visitors enter a zip code and see nearby operators.
2. Give each operator a clear contact action.
3. Charge listed operators an annual subscription.
4. Use traffic and contact tracking to show operators the audience they receive.
> "It gives you a drop down of who's close by and then they just click on contact pilot and then it'll text them the phone number so we can track exactly how many people go to the website" — Mike (~16:05)

**Win farmers by demonstrating the result on their own crop** (sales)
1. Bring the trailer and equipment to the farm.
2. Ask permission to demonstrate with water over part of the crop.
3. Show the farmer the droplets on the plant and explain where the drone can spray.
4. Follow up on fields where conventional equipment has trouble reaching or operating.
> "I take my rig, I go to his farm, I introduce myself, and I say, Hey, can I just show you how this works?" — Mike (~34:28)

**Use agricultural co-ops as an introduction point** (acquisition)
1. Identify co-ops where local farmers buy seed and chemicals.
2. Visit with the drone and trailer visible.
3. Explain the service and show that the equipment is real and ready to work.
> "I went to co-ops like where the farmers buy their seed and chemical from. So I showed up there with my drones and my trailer doing exactly what you said." — Mike (~41:10)

**Keep the product recommendation with the farmer** (operations)
1. Ask the farmer which chemical and application they want.
2. Apply the farmer's selected product rather than recommending a product outside your expertise.
3. Avoid having a poor product outcome become confused with the performance of the drone service.
> "So, I'll just do what you you want me to spray." — Mike (~39:31)

**Prepare licensing before buying into agricultural spraying** (legal)
1. Obtain the remote pilot certificate and the required state commercial pesticide applicator license.
2. Contact the equipment seller about the remaining paperwork for heavy-lift drone operations.
3. Ask how any temporary operating program works and confirm its eligibility and terms.
> "Those are the two two main things you need to get started. Then my team will help you with everything else." — Mike (~60:27)

**Protect time and delegate in deliberate seasons** (mindset)
1. Set family and personal commitments as priorities on the calendar.
2. Batch email and text replies rather than responding to every notification immediately.
3. If a busy period requires extra work or travel, decide when that period ends and plan time off afterward.
4. Delegate tasks when the quality trade-off is acceptable.
> "But you just have to know, you have to put an expiration date on it. Like this is my season and I'm going to have we're going to take a month off and go travel as a family for that season" — Chris Koerner (~74:20)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Show the service in action when customers do not yet understand what it does; Mike's thermal footage drew attention at an expo.
- *pricing*: Price feedback can be more useful when it comes from people who understand the alternative but are unlikely to be biased as buyers.
- *offer_design*: A new service can be more valuable than the established alternative if it avoids a costly or inconvenient side effect.
- *scaling*: A focused directory can reinforce the service that created it: content brings attention, the directory routes demand, and listings monetize the audience.
- *customer_acquisition*: For unfamiliar equipment-based services, a physical demonstration can make the value clear faster than a business card or online listing.
- *operations*: Keep responsibility for the service distinct from responsibility for a customer's inputs; Mike applies what the farmer chooses rather than recommending chemicals.
- *automation_ai*: Automated flight can reduce piloting effort, but a physical service still needs human supervision and repeated handling of equipment.
- *niche_selection*: Do not assume the first operator in a category is the one who makes it mainstream; public demonstrations and content can grow demand for an entire niche.
- *mindset_execution*: Protect time for family and personal commitments by setting boundaries and treating intense work as a season with an end date.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Delegate when the time saved is worth the possible reduction in quality; the trade-off should be a deliberate choice.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not rely only on informal legal advice for drone deer recovery. Mike says the rules were initially unclear, and he later met local game wardens and watched the regulations change.
- Do not use a drone to locate a deer so that it can then be shot. Mike says the official he contacted supported recovery only, not using the drone to find and shoot the deer.
- Chris's idea of flying a drone at a farmer's property without permission was challenged by Mike, who said he needed permission to fly over the land.
- Mike says he waited too long to trademark Drone Deer Recovery; the phrase became generic and similarly named operators caused customer confusion.
- Mike says he does not recommend farmers' chemicals because a poor result could lead them to blame both the product and the drone application.
- The guest says heavy-lift spraying requires multiple licenses and FAA paperwork, and describes that process as difficult and slow.
- Mike says some hunters may take marginal shots because they expect a drone to find the deer, creating an ethical concern.
- The host's AI employee promotion for HighLevel and promotion of TKOowners.com are promotional segments, not evidence about the guest's drone business.
