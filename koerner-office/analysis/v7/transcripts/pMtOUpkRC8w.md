## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate with a small paid-ad test** (validation_testing)
1. Create several distinct product or creative concepts.
2. Run ads and compare clicks and conversions.
3. Use broad targeting where the platform can optimise delivery.
4. Stop spending on weaker concepts and follow the results.
5. Treat early results as evidence to investigate, not proof of profit.
> "Go test eight designs on ads. Spend $100 behind each one. Which one's getting you the clicks and the conversions and then kill the other nine." (~20:48)

**Use offline reactions to test apparel** (validation_testing)
1. Make sample versions of different slogans.
2. Ask friends or family to wear them in ordinary settings.
3. Notice which designs prompt unsolicited compliments or questions.
4. Compare those reactions with online ad results before scaling.
> "you print out a hundred of them on a thousand different t-shirts and you pass those out or you just friends and family like, 'Hey, will you wear these around and tell me what you think?'" (~18:12)

**Use identity and resonance in product positioning** (offer_design)
1. Choose a message that a specific audience wants to express.
2. Make the product visibly communicate that message.
3. Test whether people wear, share or talk about it.
4. Use the response to refine the design and product range.
> "And selling a product that they resonate with, which is literally what brand really is, is like, do I resonate with this thing enough to put it on my body?" (~16:59)

**Offer a pay-per-use option for AI tools** (pricing)
1. Identify users who want occasional access but do not want another subscription.
2. Charge for individual use or let customers reload a small credit balance.
3. Check API costs against likely usage before setting the price.
4. Set safeguards against repeated or unusually expensive requests.
> "I'm going to have to look at my token usage and make sure economically it makes sense." (~3:29)

**Use a free product distribution test** (customer_acquisition)
1. Make a simple, recognisable product around a message.
2. Distribute samples in places where the intended audience gathers.
3. Observe whether recipients share the product or message.
4. Use the response to decide whether to build a larger brand.
> "And he's just thinking like, I just want to spread awareness. I want to build a brand, and then I'll I'll worry about making money." (~11:59)

**Use a local free-pizza ad to test demand** (validation_testing)
1. Target people within a defined local radius.
2. Offer a free pizza in exchange for a phone number.
3. Calculate the cost per lead and assess whether the shop can convert those leads into profitable visits.
4. Get appropriate consent before using contact details for marketing.
> "I'm just going to launch some ads real quick and say, get a free pizza. And I'm just going to do a geo targeted 10 mile radius around me." (~25:05)

**Start with a broad, familiar market** (niche_selection)
1. Do not reject a large market just because many competitors exist.
2. Start with a simple offer in an area where many people already spend money.
3. Use product tests to find a specific audience or angle.
4. Let evidence guide the next product rather than trying to predict the final business in advance.
> "If your gut reaction is that market is oversaturated, that's the market you need to go to because that's the big freaking market." (~0:25)

**Build a simple AI interface around an existing model** (product)
1. Choose a customer context, such as cooking or personal training.
2. Make the interface and name immediately understandable to that audience.
3. Use an existing model through an API rather than assuming a custom model is required.
4. Test whether the context and experience create enough value to retain users.
> "What happens if you don't wrap it? What happens if you just put it out as like, yo, this is your AI assistant?" (~4:44)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: People may pay for convenience or accountability even when they could do a task themselves.
- *niche_selection*: A large market can be attractive despite competition; test a clear angle rather than rejecting it as saturated.
- *validation_testing*: Move from private speculation to experiments that produce observable customer behaviour.
- *customer_acquisition*: Creative can help identify the audience: people who do not relate to an ad are less likely to click it.
- *mindset_execution*: An idea can change as a founder learns; the first product can be a way to learn advertising and customer demand.
- *offer_design*: A product can sell because customers identify with its message, not only because of its functional features.
- *validation_testing*: A single standout product can matter more than a broad catalogue, so measure which product earns the strongest response.
- *mindset_execution*: Small, simple tests can create useful evidence faster than repeatedly debating an idea.
- *customer_acquisition*: Free or cost-price distribution can be a deliberate way to build awareness, but the right approach depends on the product and context.
- *niche_selection*: Before choosing a marketplace model, account for the challenge of attracting both buyers and sellers.
