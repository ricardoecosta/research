## Tactics and playbook steps

**Start with an item you already own** (validation_testing)
1. Find an item at home that fits one of the services.
2. Look up what three shops charge to repair or restore it.
3. Practise on your own item before accepting customer property.
> "Find one box in your own house. Old tapes, a drifting controller, a pair of sneakers, a stack of shirts." — Unnamed narrator (~17:48)

**Document incoming customer property** (operations)
1. Count items when the parcel arrives.
2. Photograph the box and each item before work.
3. Track the job and return it with tracked shipping.
> "Count the tapes, photograph the box the day it arrives, and ship it backtracked." — Unnamed narrator (~4:23)

**Quote after inspecting the actual item** (pricing)
1. Ask for photos and a repair list as an initial intake step where useful.
2. Do not commit to a restoration price from a photo alone.
3. Inspect the item, document its condition and send the estimate for approval before starting.
> "He wrote it on a card above the desk. No quote until it's in my hands." — Unnamed narrator (~11:00)

**Price tape work by footage** (pricing)
1. Track the recorded duration of each tape.
2. Set a minimum charge.
3. Explain that a long recording and a short recording are different amounts of work.
> "The pro tip: price by the hour of footage, not by the tape. A 2-hour tape and a 10-minute tape are not the same job." — Unnamed narrator (~4:33)

**Use referrals to expand beyond the first customers** (acquisition)
1. Start with people who already know you.
2. Deliver carefully and ask satisfied customers to pass the service to friends or family.
3. Build a local profile and community presence so people outside the initial network can find the service.
> "A handful of boxes from people who already know you, then referrals, then strangers who found you online." — Unnamed narrator (~3:34)

**Use before-and-after work as proof** (acquisition)
1. Photograph a repaired or cleaned item before and after.
2. Post the results in relevant communities or on Instagram.
3. Build a gallery of completed work that demonstrates quality.
> "A dirty pair next to a clean pair is the whole advertisement." — Unnamed narrator (~9:53)

**Practise controller repairs in stages** (operations)
1. Repair your own controller first.
2. Practise on a friend's controller.
3. Take a few jobs from a local group at a flat price before offering mail-in service.
> "A realistic start is fixing your own controller, then a friend's, then a few from a local group at a flat price." — Unnamed narrator (~8:17)

**Price quilts by shirt block and take a deposit** (pricing)
1. Count shirt blocks before quoting.
2. Set a per-block price so the quote rises with the number of shirts.
3. Take a deposit and include return shipping in the quote.
> "The pro tip, price by the shirt block, not by the quilt." — Unnamed narrator (~16:04)

**Validate demand with competitor prices** (validation_testing)
1. Choose one item from home.
2. Look up prices from three repair or restoration shops.
3. Write the prices down before committing to equipment or a service offer.
> "Look up what three shops would charge to fix it and write the numbers down." — Unnamed narrator (~17:55)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: A service can be easier to sell when the customer already owns the item and cares about its history; the value is often preservation rather than replacement cost.
- *niche_selection*: Mail-in fulfilment lets a specialist serve customers beyond the local area from a small home workspace.
- *operations*: Build trust with documented intake, approved estimates and careful returns when customers send unique possessions.
- *pricing*: Price work by the measure that drives labour, such as footage or shirt blocks, rather than using one flat price for unlike jobs.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use before-and-after examples as proof of quality and as a repeatable source of inbound interest.
- *validation_testing*: Start with practice on your own item, then use early jobs to improve the process before taking more valuable customer property.
- *niche_selection*: A small specialist can find enough demand by serving a niche across a wide area, even when one town has few potential customers.
- *validation_testing*: Test a business idea with existing household items and competitor prices before spending on equipment.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The narrator says the video is educational, not financial advice, and tells viewers to check the rules where they live before starting.
- Do not quote a job from photos alone when item condition or recording length could change the work; inspect and document the actual items first.
- Mail-in work can involve irreplaceable family property. Count, photograph, label and track items so they are not lost or mixed up.
- A tape can take substantially longer than expected if it is long-play; the narrator says Harry absorbed the extra work after quoting from a photo.
- For sneaker restoration, confirm the final price after inspection and photograph the shoes from all sides as soon as the parcel is opened.
- T-shirt quilt work risks damaging the only copies of sentimental shirts; count and photograph them on arrival and handle the parcel carefully.
