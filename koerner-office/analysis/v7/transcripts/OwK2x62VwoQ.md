## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Marcus Lemonis (The Profit, now The Fixer on Fox; Camping World; Bed Bath & Beyond) in New York. Marcus says he lost about $50 million personally investing in businesses on the shows, has funded over 100 small businesses of which fewer than 10 worked out, and now gives no-strings money (e.g. $55k) instead of taking equity. He talks openly about depression, anxiety, work as self-medication, regret about not having children and about doing TV, and his push to try uncomfortable things like anchoring live news. Advice for beginners: pick one thing, take calculated finite risks, start with small side hustles and never bet the house. His next-decade idea is an 'everything home' platform with homes recorded on blockchain via Bed Bath & Beyond and tZERO.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Trade equity for honest time expectations** (finance)
1. When someone offers 10% for 10 hours a week, counter with 1% for 30 minutes a week
2. State up front that involvement tapers: a week together, then twice a month, then monthly, then quarterly
3. Or take no equity and give capital/advice informally, accepting repayment if offered
> "let's do 1% of the business and 30 minutes a week" — Chris Koerner (~6:40)

**Signals of founders worth backing** (mindset)
1. They ask a lot of questions
2. They communicate without being chased and send information you did not ask for
3. They are overly transparent
4. They want a relationship
> "They send me information that I didn't ask for. They are overly transparent" — Marcus Lemonis (~13:17)

**Fast hiring decisions** (hiring)
1. Decide on a hire within 5-10 minutes of interview
2. Skip multiple interview rounds
3. Cut bait within 30 days if it does not work
> "My interviews with employees are I know within 5 to 10 minutes. But I'm willing to cut bait within 30 days" — Chris Koerner (~51:53)

**Wing walker's code for quitting your job** (finance)
1. Start with low-risk side hustles while keeping income
2. Stack to two or three side hustles
3. Leap only when cash flow is proven; never use your 401k or a second mortgage
> "never let go of one railing until you're already holding on to the next" — Chris Koerner (~69:27)

**Go in curious, not over-researched** (sales)
1. Visit businesses without prior research so you ask the obvious questions
2. Avoid reciting AI-generated stats to partners or investors
3. Make the maths simple for others
> "I want entrepreneurs to go into businesses that they're interested in, that they want to invest in, more curious than knowledgeable" — Marcus Lemonis (~30:00)

## Lessons

- *hiring_outsourcing*: Self-aware 'shiny object' entrepreneurs should hire or partner way up to fill their gaps rather than force themselves to focus.
- *automation_ai*: Business is a people business, but AI makes process matter more over the next decade for pricing, sourcing, marketing and conversion.
- *mindset_execution*: Try things that make you wildly uncomfortable; being a fish out of water is the best curriculum.
- *scaling*: A founder's success is measured by how the business does after they leave: foundation, people and culture.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Treat employees as more important than customers so they treat customers well.
- *validation_testing*: Start with small, low-risk side hustles and take finite, calculated risks.
- *customer_acquisition*: Showing vulnerability and being bad at things on camera makes content relatable.
- *mindset_execution*: Mass rejection (door knocking, cold calling) builds the resilience entrepreneurs need.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Protect yourself from impulsive spending, e.g. giving up direct access to your checkbook.
- *mindset_execution*: Business ownership is not freedom but optionality; the regret is in not trying.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Taking equity in many small businesses accumulated stakes that ended up worth little.
- Romanticising a new investment and spending lots of time early creates expectations you cannot keep, leading to conflict.
- Time kills deals: when a counterpart stalls, momentum is lost for good.
- Do not bet your 401k or take a second mortgage on a first venture.
- Over-reliance on AI research makes founders sound scripted, and the information is often wrong or slanted.
- Doing business with close friends and family often damages relationships.
- Undocumented non-equity investments created tax and audit confusion with the IRS.
- TV fame cost Marcus a large share of his life and time; he is not sure he would do it again.
