### I1. Commercial parking lot sweeping

- **Customer:** Shopping centres, apartment complexes, HOAs and their property managers
- **Problem:** Parking lots must be kept clean and presentable.
- **Offer:** Scheduled parking lot sweeping and cleaning, usually 1-5am.
- **Business model:** Recurring monthly service contracts
- **Pricing:** Monthly contract per site. Multi-site contract income USD 10000-20000 per month Per-site price not stated.
- **Acquisition:** other: Landing contracts with property managers is described as the hard part; method not specified.
- **Delivery:** Owner or staff ride a used ride-on sweeper at night; or start by hand with trash bags.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Used ride-on sweeper $8-15k; $10-20k/month with multi-site contracts; Parking Lot Pros over $2M/year
- **Tools:** Used ride-on sweeper
- **Startup cost:** $8,000-15,000 for a used ride-on sweeper; much less if starting by hand
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Works in the UK, where it is called car park sweeping; contracts are with facilities managers; the sweeper costs and permits differ.
- **Risks:** Unsocial night hours; Hard to win first accounts; Equipment capital cost
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 1/5 (Landing contracts with property managers is relationship-led; some B2B outreach to limited companies is possible but mostly human.); onboarding 2/5 (Contract and site-set-up can be templated, but a site visit is likely.); delivery 0/5 (Physical sweeping at night requires a person and machine.); billing 4/5 (Monthly recurring invoicing is easily automated.); support 2/5 (Complaints about missed sweeps need human response.)
> "it's all commercial accounts. Why I love commercial accounts when you're talking about starting a service-based business like this one, commercial is going to be much stickier" — Johnny Robinson (~2:05)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Door-knock restaurants and do first jobs free for testimonials** (acquisition)
1. Knock on about 100 restaurant doors
2. Ask if they are unhappy with current provider (price, no-shows)
3. Offer a free first service to earn case studies if you have no proof
4. Use the testimonial in later pitches
5. Move customers to monthly or quarterly agreements
> "just do it for free for them, so you can get a couple quick case studies or testimonials" — Johnny Robinson (~5:54)

**Fleet-account cold email for mobile services** (acquisition)
1. List local rental, logistics and office-based companies
2. Send cold emails offering an on-site service deal
3. Build a few fleet contracts plus a residential route

**Rent-and-re-rent dumpsters to start lean** (operations)
1. Rent a dumpster from an existing provider
2. Re-rent at a markup to test demand
3. Buy a used dumpster ($2-5k) once proven

**Reinvest every dollar to scale a machine route** (finance)
1. Place first machine with a revenue split near the entrance
2. Use profits to buy the next machine
3. Repeat until the route is large

**Subcontract cleaners on a revenue split** (operations)
1. Partner with independent cleaners who bring equipment and vehicles
2. Pay them 40-50% of the booking
3. Vet cleaners to maximise recurring conversion

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Favour customers who are legally required to buy the service, since demand is non-discretionary.
- *offer_design*: Commercial contracts are stickier than residential and produce recurring revenue.
- *finance_unit_economics*: High switching costs make revenue sticky and retention easy.
- *niche_selection*: Unglamorous niches have less competition than trendy online businesses.
- *mindset_execution*: Start small with whatever equipment you have and reinvest to upgrade.
- *scaling*: Fleet or multi-site accounts concentrate repeat work and justify the sales effort.
- *mindset_execution*: Most businesses reduce to taking payments and recruiting/staffing, so pick one and start.
- *other*: Company-size claims for named examples are unverified and none of this is agent-run: nearly all ideas are physical delivery.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Host disclaims that results are not guaranteed and that market and execution vary; do your own homework.
- Grease trap, medical waste and similar businesses require certifications and licences before starting.
- Landing the first commercial accounts is the hard part of parking lot sweeping and medical waste.
