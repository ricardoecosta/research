## Summary

Chris Koerner demonstrates building a niche CRM micro-app for independent pool service operators using only AI, with no coding. He uses Claude to pick a growing niche, then compares Claude Code, Replit and ChatGPT on the same prompt: Claude Code built it in about 2 minutes and was best, Replit took about 16 minutes, ChatGPT was fast but gave code without a preview. He then runs a back-of-envelope market sizing (79,000 US pool businesses, 1,000 customers at $20-$100/month) and outlines customer-finding methods: Facebook groups for feedback, Gummy Search for Reddit pain points, Claude deep research, Google Trends, scraping and cold outreach, and Facebook custom-audience ads. Key takeaway: niche vertical SaaS is cheap to prototype with AI, and validation and distribution are the real work.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Use AI to pick a rising, fragmented niche** (product)
1. Prompt Claude for 10 niches in local/home services growing fast, backed by hard data
2. Reject niches likely to be concentrated among a few big firms
3. Prefer industries with many solo operators
4. Pick one and sanity-check with Google Trends
> "I want an industry that's highly fragmented with a lot of solo operators." — Chris Koerner (~1:37)

**Prompt-build a niche MVP and compare tools** (product)
1. Write one prompt: dead simple, niche-specific, fewer fields than generic CRMs, 'I have no coding experience, show me the UI'
2. Paste the same prompt into Claude Code, Replit and ChatGPT and time each
3. Ask for fixes by follow-up prompt (e.g. make records editable)
4. Tell each tool the others are competing to push quality
> "You always want to make the apps jealous of each other or competitive with each other." — Chris Koerner (~5:35)

**Validate and seed customers via Facebook groups** (acquisition)
1. Search 'pool service' in Facebook groups and filter by near me
2. Favour private, moderated, active groups; skip big spammy ones and groups with no posts
3. Join and ask what the perfect CRM would do, then implement the feedback
4. At launch DM helpers offering free use in return for feedback, reviews, referrals and case studies
5. Later convert them to paying
> "oftentimes smaller is better because it's easier for the managers to wrap their arms around it." — Chris Koerner (~10:43)

**Mine pain points with Gummy Search and deep research** (acquisition)
1. Use Gummy Search to pull Reddit pain points and desired features
2. Run Claude deep research on what operators hate, love and cannot live without in current software
3. Feed findings into product features and ad copy to cut acquisition cost

**Scrape and target the full niche list** (acquisition)
1. Scrape all pool service businesses with Outscraper
2. Offer 90 days free or free until first five customers
3. Upload the list to Facebook Ads as a custom audience to show ads only to those owners
> "Scrape all 79,000, upload them to your Facebook ad campaign as a custom audience" — Chris Koerner (~14:17)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Big incumbents do not remove opportunity; niche vertical software for fragmented industries is still open.
- *niche_selection*: Pick niches that are growing and fragmented with many solo operators, not concentrated or shrinking ones.
- *automation_ai*: AI coding tools differ greatly in speed and non-coder friendliness; test the same prompt across several and choose by preview quality and iteration speed.
- *finance_unit_economics*: A conservative price plus small market share gives a simple revenue model, but this math is hypothetical until conversion is proven.
- *validation_testing*: Co-design with community members and give early users free access in exchange for reviews, referrals and case studies.
- *validation_testing*: Use AI research and Reddit mining to find pain points before building and reuse them in ad messaging.
- *offer_design*: Domain-specific defaults (pool type, gate code, service day) make software feel purpose-built versus generic CRMs.
- *customer_acquisition*: The build is now the cheap part; distribution and getting paying customers is the real work.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Large Facebook groups are often low quality and spammy; inactive groups make your post stand out badly.
- The host admits he lacks domain knowledge of pool service, so features may miss real needs; validation with operators is required.
- The first AI output was not editable; expect follow-up prompting and check functionality, not just looks.
