## Summary

Spencer Haws of Niche Pursuits explains why he stopped posting on YouTube for a year and why he is returning. His channel served as a funnel (brand, email list, podcast) to sell his software, Long Tail Pro and later Link Whisper, which he sold for $2.7M in January 2025 after six years. He no longer needs the money, bought an e-commerce business with his brother, and discloses a benign pituitary tumour that explained his low motivation. Key takeaway: content channels are distribution for a product; evergreen videos compound.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Content-to-software funnel** (acquisition)
1. Publish educational YouTube videos for brand awareness
2. Push viewers to email list and podcast
3. Sell your own software to the warmed audience
4. Field inbound acquisition interest once the product is large

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: A YouTube channel's primary purpose is usually brand awareness for a product, not ad revenue.
- *customer_acquisition*: An evergreen video library keeps compounding views and subscribers even after you stop posting.
- *exit_acquisition*: A content audience generates inbound acquirers for the product; multiple buyers approached him.
- *offer_design*: Without a product to sell, a content channel loses its business purpose; tie content to an offer.
- *mindset_execution*: Retired founders often come back within a year; he lasted about 10 months.
- *mindset_execution*: Enjoying the creative process sustains content creation beyond money.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Persistent low motivation may have a medical cause; he had a benign pituitary tumour lowering testosterone, found via blood tests and MRI.
- Running multiple businesses at once for years meant juggling a lot; he chose to pause after the exit.
