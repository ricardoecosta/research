## Summary

Chris Koerner tours a concrete-leveling (polyurethane foam lifting) operation in Houston with Jeff, a W-2 employee and father who started 90 days earlier and subcontracts all the physical work. Jeff claims almost $20k revenue, 30-40% gross margins and a $2,500 average job. He gets leads via Facebook ads, Nextdoor, Google, referrals, a Replit-built site with chatbots and HighLevel texting, and quotes jobs remotely from photos. Koerner shows the maths that about 36 leads a month and about 5 quoting hours a week could yield $10k/month profit. Key takeaway: a drop-servicing model, where you sell, quote and coordinate while insured subcontractors do the work, can run part-time, though the claims are early-stage and self-reported.

### I1. Concrete leveling drop-servicing (subcontracted foam lifting)

- **Customer:** Homeowners (trip hazards, aesthetics, avoiding replacement); also HOA managers, property managers, new home builders and retail property owners
- **Problem:** Sunken or uneven concrete slabs (sidewalks, driveways, parking lots) are trip hazards and ugly; replacement costs roughly 10x more
- **Offer:** Polyurethane foam concrete leveling at about a third of the cost of new concrete, with a quality guarantee, excellent communication and a free power wash on residential jobs
- **Business model:** Per-job fee; markup over subcontractor cost; 50% deposit, balance on completion
- **Pricing:** Quote per job based on estimated foam volume; minimum project around $1,000. average job size USD 2500 per job; minimum small job quote when subbing USD 1000 per job; cost of subcontractor on a small job USD 600-700 per job; cost of free power wash USD 100-150 per job Avoids bidding wars; because he subs out he is more expensive than owner-operators and may close fewer jobs. Conservative foam estimates are built into quotes. Travel time must be factored in (first job at an hour outside town earned no margin).
- **Acquisition:** paid_social: Facebook ad campaign; first lead within 1-2 hours of launch at about $4.60 per lead, converting to a $2,500 job; turns campaigns on and off; organic_social_content: Nextdoor organic posts work well; seo_organic_search: Google search organic plus inbound website leads captured by forms and chatbots; referrals_word_of_mouth: Customer referrals; cold_sms_dm: Outbound texting campaigns thanking HOA managers and property managers who oversee multiple properties; slow trickle of volume. UK PECR would restrict this for individuals and sole traders.
- **Delivery:** Insured subcontractors with their own foam-lifting trailers do the work. Jeff quotes remotely from photos and phone calls, books jobs never in the same week, takes a 50% deposit, and requires subs to send photos and check-ins. A power washer subbed out 2-3 days later is baked into the quote and upsold for backyards at $100.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Almost $20k revenue in 90 days, mostly in the last 60; 30-40% gross margin per job; Average job $2,500 with about $1,000 profit; Small job: sub costs $600-700, charge at least $1,000; Close rate about 33% on qualified quotes; Own-equipment entry about $120k for a 20ft trailer, or $60-70k for a van; Ongoing costs under about $500-600/month
- **Tools:** HighLevel (CRM, texting, AI chatbot), Replit (website), Facebook Ads, Nextdoor, Google search, Custom software
- **Startup cost:** Near zero when subcontracting: CRM plus Replit under about $500-600/month; website built at $0. Owning equipment would cost $60-70k (van) to $120k (20ft trailer).
- **Time to first revenue:** First job sold June 2025, about 2 hours after launching the Facebook campaign; roughly 90 days to almost $20k revenue.
- **UK applicability [E]:** 2/5 — The model of selling and subcontracting a trade works anywhere, but UK concrete is mostly poured on lower-volume sites and has less slab settling than Texas. Foam lifting exists in the UK, but the market is smaller, and PECR limits outbound texting. A UK version would need subcontractors with public liability insurance and a different demand profile.
- **Risks:** Unexpected voids can multiply costs (a $1,500 job became $5,000); Under-estimating foam is the quickest way to lose money; Subcontractor quality or reliability, since the business depends entirely on subs; Higher price than owner-operators lowers close rate; Remote quoting lowers win rate to about 25%; Saturated markets; a large owned-equipment path requires heavy capex; Results are only 90 days old and self-reported
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Paid Facebook ads, SEO, a landing page and chatbots can run with agents. Nextdoor organic and the outbound texting are weaker and PECR-restricted in the UK.); onboarding 3/5 (HighLevel automations text new leads instantly, a chatbot qualifies them and photos are collected remotely; agents can draft quotes, but a human or phone call still helps close.); delivery 1/5 (Physical foam lifting by subcontractors. Dispatch, scheduling and photo check-ins can be tooled, but a human must find, vet and manage subs and estimate foam volumes.); billing 4/5 (50% deposit and balance on completion can be invoiced and chased by software with light approval.); support 3/5 (Chatbots and automated texts handle inquiries and updates; disputes and on-site problems need a human.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Fast Facebook lead ads plus instant text follow-up** (acquisition)
1. Launch a Facebook ad campaign for the service and area
2. Use HighLevel to text each lead right after the form is submitted
3. Ask if they are free to connect, then call regardless of reply
4. Respond to leads immediately with no downtime
> "it goes a long way if you get a text right after you complete a form, and then ask if they're free to connect, and then call them regardless of what they say." — Jeff (~22:52)

**Remote photo-based quoting** (sales)
1. Ask for photographs instead of driving to site
2. Ask educated questions about trees, water lines and sprinklers
3. Quote conservatively on foam volume
4. Accept a lower win rate (about 25%) in exchange for 5-10 quotes in the time of one site visit
> "I can crank out five or 10 quotes, you know, the time it takes me to drive an hour outside of town" — Jeff (~17:09)

**Free power wash differentiator baked into price** (pricing)
1. Include a free power wash in the quote for residential jobs
2. Sub out the power wash 2-3 days after leveling for about $100-150
3. Text the owner offering sidewalks free and a backyard for $100
4. Use the upsell to cover the cost
> "you level your driveway with us, we'll power wash your driveway for free." — Jeff (~14:13)

**Subcontractor control and deposit terms** (operations)
1. Never book jobs in the same week
2. Take 50% deposit on sale and the rest on completion
3. Require subs to hold $1-2M insurance
4. Require pictures and check-ins and a firm arrival time
> "each sub has to have at least a million dollars of insurance, one to two million dollars" — Jeff (~23:07)

**Recruit plateaued solo operators** (acquisition)
1. Find solo providers who have hit a revenue ceiling
2. Offer to send them jobs above that ceiling
3. Keep your margin in the middle
> "I can get you the next level without you having to do any additional work." — Chris Koerner (~2:38)

**Work backwards from profit target to leads** (finance)
1. Divide the $10k target by about $800 profit per job to get 12 jobs
2. At a 33% close rate, quote 36 jobs
3. Target 35-40 leads per month at about 1 hour per quote

**Sell fear/liability for commercial contracts** (sales)
1. Use trip-and-fall lawsuit facts
2. Position leveling as prevention
3. Sell annualized plans
> "If you're playing into the fear side of it as opposed to like the aesthetic side of it, I think you can have some success there." — Chris Koerner (~19:03)

## Lessons

- *operations*: Selling and coordinating a trade service while subcontracting the physical work lets someone with a full-time job run it, since the owner's work is leads, quotes and subcontractor oversight.
- *customer_acquisition*: Fast lead response (text immediately, then call) is a cheap competitive edge in local services.
- *validation_testing*: Trading a lower win rate for much higher quote volume can increase total revenue; test the maths.
- *offer_design*: Differentiate on communication and guaranteed quality, not price, to avoid bidding wars.
- *offer_design*: Bake small freebies, like a power wash, into the quote so they raise perceived value for little cost.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Back-solve profit targets into leads, quotes and hours before committing.
- *niche_selection*: Choose a validated, old industry and bring modern marketing and systems rather than inventing a new market.
- *scaling*: Recurring contracts increase revenue predictability and justify buying assets.
- *legal_risk*: Make subcontractors carry their own insurance and prove work with photos and check-ins to protect yourself.
- *customer_acquisition*: Selling the problem (liability, pain) works better than selling aesthetics in B2B.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Under-calculating foam is the quickest way to lose money on a job.
- Factor driving time and distance into cost; his first job lost its margin because it was an hour outside town.
- Hidden voids, such as a cracked sewer creating a flowing river under a driveway, can triple cost.
- Do not let the gun clog; without the gun you cannot work.
- Avoid bidding wars; those customers tend to be more of a pain over time.
- Rolling a truck for a small job strains margin after fuel and travel time.
