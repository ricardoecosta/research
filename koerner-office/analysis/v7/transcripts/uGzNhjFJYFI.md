## Tactics and playbook steps

**Write sales copy in a natural speaking voice** (offer_design)
1. Capture how the founder naturally explains the offer in conversation.
2. Write down the founder's words as close to verbatim as possible.
3. Use that personal note as the lead copy on the sales page or in an ad.
> "He was just like, "Dude, I want Hampton is like and he just went on his rant and it was kind of epic."" — Neville (~2:08)

**Structure a pitch with Attention, Interest, Desire, Action** (sales)
1. Get attention with a clear hook.
2. Build interest with facts, use cases, and testimonials.
3. Show how the customer's life or work improves if they buy.
4. Tell them what to do next.
> "Attention, interest, desire, action. That's what every single AppSumo email was based on." — Neville (~13:36)

**Turn overlooked product uses into sales bullets** (product)
1. Ask buyers how they actually use the product.
2. Find customer groups beyond the obvious target audience.
3. Turn those uses into concrete examples and bullet points in the sales pitch.
> "That's a freaking bullet point to put in a sales pitch somewhere that plumbing companies use these." — Neville (~16:24)

**Send one short, one medium, then one long cold email** (acquisition)
1. Start with a very short message that sounds like a person wrote it.
2. Follow with a medium version that adds the main proof and offer.
3. Send a longer version last to cover the remaining reasons to respond.
4. Keep UK outreach within the applicable marketing rules, including consent requirements for individuals and sole traders.
> "I say sending out one short one, one medium one, one long one to everyone." — Neville (~30:30)

**Balance useful content with sales emails** (customer_acquisition)
1. Include something useful, entertaining, or educational in regular sales emails.
2. Use Neville's suggested 70% content and 30% sales split as a starting point.
3. Choose a frequency the team can sustain with useful material.
> "If you are willing to do 70% content and 30% sales, you could email them unlimited and they never get mad." — Neville (~31:08)

**Test simple email formats** (validation_testing)
1. Test a plain text email with a short subject line and a simple link.
2. Try a second format with a headline, a product picture, and another short section repeated down the email.
3. Compare clicks and sales against the current format.
> "And then I'm basically just going to copy this over and over and over." — Neville (~22:18)

**Use customer feedback to detect quality problems** (operations)
1. Do not treat silence as proof that customers like the product or content.
2. Look for specific feedback that identifies a fixable issue.
3. Use recurring customer replies and engagement as signals about long-term quality.
> "For writing really really good copy what you notice is like over the long run people will still reply to newsletters still read it still be influenced by it" — Neville (~5:51)

**Preserve distinctive human details in creative work** (product)
1. Keep the informal phrasing, rough drawings, or visual quirks that customers respond to.
2. Use AI to assist with production, but check that the result still sounds and looks like the business.
3. Compare the polished version with the more personal version.
> "going back to like goofy to where it's just like this is so stupid AI wouldn't have made it" — Neville (~10:04)

**Give ChatGPT a reusable project brief** (automation)
1. Create a ChatGPT Project for the business.
2. Upload the website, blog, images, PDF decks, and real case studies.
3. Tell it to reuse the company's stories, numbers, and results when drafting emails.
4. Review the facts and copy before sending.
> "Upload all your images, all your PDF decks, all that kind of stuff." — Neville (~44:14)

**Keep running a proven ad while its economics work** (finance)
1. Track the cost to run the ad and the return it produces.
2. Continue the ad while the return remains above the cost.
3. Stop or revise it when the economics no longer make sense.
> "You spend $1,000, you make a,000 plus one, you keep you keep doing it until it makes under that." — Chris Koerner (~37:46)

**Use a product story rather than an arbitrary word count** (content)
1. Start with a specific, emotionally credible story connected to the product or customer.
2. Make the copy as long as the story needs, but remove unnecessary repetition.
3. Judge the result by reader response rather than length alone.
> "I think you wrote a really heartfelt story, right?" — Neville (~40:43)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Use the customer's real language and a founder's natural speaking voice; clear plain English can be more persuasive than polished formal prose.
- *niche_selection*: Explain concrete product uses and unexpected customer groups so buyers can see how an offer fits their own situation.
- *other*: Use Attention, Interest, Desire, Action as a starting structure for a sales pitch, then adapt it to the format.
- *customer_acquisition*: An email list is not automatically valuable; measure whether subscribers read, reply, and remain engaged.
- *customer_acquisition*: Give recurring sales emails useful or entertaining material so the relationship is not only a sequence of sales pitches.
- *other*: A compelling story matters more than hitting a particular word count; long copy works when readers want to know what happens next.
- *automation_ai*: Keep distinctive human touches in creative work; tools can make production easier but may also remove the qualities that make a brand memorable.
- *automation_ai*: Put a company's real stories, case studies, and numbers into AI writing projects, then check the output before using it.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Treat proven marketing as a testable system: continue the approach while its economics hold, and change it when they do not.

## Warnings and mistakes

- A quiet email list can hide poor engagement: people may ignore or unsubscribe without explaining why.
- Over-polishing creative work or making it sound like generic AI output can remove the personality that customers value.
- Email frequency alone is not a useful rule; repeatedly sending only sales pitches can annoy subscribers.
- The coat-of-arms mail-order account is second-hand, and Neville says it is only Gary Halbert's side of the story; he also recounts a mail-fraud case and complaints.
- The AppSumo profit result is a reported anecdote without test details or independent verification.
- In the UK, unsolicited marketing emails, SMS, WhatsApp messages, or DMs to individuals and sole traders require consent; cold email to limited companies is allowed subject to the applicable rules.
