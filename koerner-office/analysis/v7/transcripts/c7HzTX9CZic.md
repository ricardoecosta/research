## Tactics and playbook steps

**Deep study of a few understandable companies, then wait for price** (finance)
1. Pick only a few companies you can understand.
2. Study them very deeply: read annual reports and keep detailed written notes over months.
3. Keep watching the company and wait until the price is cheap enough.
4. Buy on the day the opportunity appears; otherwise do nothing.
> "I study a few things that I can understand. And I study them very deeply. I keep very detailed notes and then I just wait for an opportunity." — Sam Parr (~3:18)

**Pre-mortem before a big decision** (mindset)
1. Before starting, write down every way it could fail (Dorsey wrote a list of 140 reasons Square would fail).
2. Use the list to design around those failure modes.
3. Judge decisions on the quality of the inputs or framework, not just the outcome (avoid 'resulting').

**Sort decisions into reversible vs irreversible** (mindset)
1. Classify each decision as reversible or not.
2. Do not agonise over reversible ones.
3. Spend thinking time only on the irreversible ones.

**Rehearse the feared event in granular detail** (mindset)
1. Recreate the real conditions in advance (same clothes, same meal, same environment), as George St-Pierre's coaches did.
2. Before a hard conversation such as a firing or negotiation, walk through the room and the motions beforehand.
3. Use exposure practice for phobias, such as short subway trips.

**Define the game you are playing** (mindset)
1. Ask yourself where you want to be in five years.
2. Name the game you are actually playing right now.
3. Let that game decide what you want and who you need to be, then ignore other games' metrics.

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: Few, high-conviction decisions can beat constant activity; the reported investor averaged about 2.9 decisions a year and had three years with none.
- *niche_selection*: Stay inside your circle of competence and be willing to say no to good opportunities you don't understand.
- *validation_testing*: Write ideas down through pre-mortems and journals, and evaluate decisions on process rather than outcome.
- *mindset_execution*: Triage decisions by reversibility; don't agonise over reversible ones.
- *scaling*: Investing rewards avoiding losses, while entrepreneurship rewards a few huge wins, so many mistakes are acceptable if the big bets pay off.
- *other*: Strong leaders seek input widely from smart people, including less accomplished ones, and use the answers to gauge who gives high-signal advice.
- *mindset_execution*: Motion beats waiting for direction: start building quickly after a setback, since direction becomes clearer once you are in motion.
- *mindset_execution*: When a business is struggling, stack small life wins (routine, exercise, tidy space) rather than waiting for outcomes to change.
- *mindset_execution*: Feared events are often worse in anticipation; rehearsal, acceptance and leaning into the feeling reduce fear.
- *other*: Know the game you are playing; different games demand different traits and most people never define theirs.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Mistaking movement for progress: constant activity and agonising over small decisions can crowd out the few decisions that matter.
- Don't judge decisions by outcomes alone (resulting); judge the input or framework.
- Letting a failing business bleed into your daily life and routines (no trash can, air mattress) lowered productivity; fix routines first.
- Beware the Dunning-Kruger overconfidence of early learners; the best admit how little they know.
