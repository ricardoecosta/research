## Tactics and playbook steps

**Compare debt relief providers side by side** (validation_testing)
1. Get quotes from at least two companies.
2. Compare total estimated cost, including all fees.
3. Compare the estimated program timeline.
4. Judge how comfortable you feel with the company during the consultation.
> "Honestly, I'd recommend getting quotes from at least two of these companies and comparing them side by side." — Casper Capital host (~19:17)

**Check the contract and creditor-specific track record** (legal)
1. Read the contract carefully.
2. Ask about the company's settlement track record with your specific creditors.
3. Get the terms in writing.
4. Treat a pushy sales process as a reason for extra caution.
> "Read your contract carefully, ask about their settlement track record with your specific creditors, and be wary if the sales process feels pushy." — Casper Capital host (~19:08)

**Check whether settlement fits the customer's situation** (validation_testing)
1. Check that the debt is unsecured and meets the provider's minimum.
2. Assess whether the customer can sustain monthly deposits through the full program.
3. Consider whether the customer is already behind or likely to fall behind.
4. Compare settlement with consolidation or a nonprofit debt management program where appropriate.
> "That's why debt relief only works for people who can still afford consistent monthly payments, even if those payments feel tight." — Casper Capital host (~5:50)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Debt settlement is presented for unsecured debts; it does not work for mortgages, car loans, or federal student loans.
- *finance_unit_economics*: A debt solution should fit the customer's ability to keep making payments for the full program.
- *offer_design*: Compare settlement with alternatives instead of assuming settlement is best for everyone.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Evaluate the total fee, credit impact, lawsuit risk, possible tax consequences, and settlement uncertainty together.
- *pricing*: A lower quoted fee does not settle the question; compare the full cost, estimated timeline, and customer experience.
- *legal_risk*: Get contract terms in writing and ask about outcomes with the customer's specific creditors.
- *legal_risk*: The host says settlement providers should not charge upfront fees and warns against big promises.
- *validation_testing*: Provider review scores and complaint counts are useful comparison inputs, but the video does not independently verify them.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Debt settlement requires intentionally stopping creditor payments, which can damage credit.
- Creditors may sue after payments stop; the host says a successful lawsuit may lead to wage collection or a claim against property.
- Forgiven debt may have US tax consequences.
- Some creditors may refuse to negotiate, leaving debt unresolved.
- Quitting before completing the program can leave the customer with credit damage and a worse financial position.
- The host reports customer complaints alleging persistent sales calls and representatives mischaracterizing settlement as a loan.
- The host advises avoiding companies that make big promises or charge upfront fees.
- US provider fees, ratings, availability, credit references, and tax discussion do not establish suitability for a UK customer.
