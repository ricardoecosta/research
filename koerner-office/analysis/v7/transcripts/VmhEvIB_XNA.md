## Tactics and playbook steps

**Research local demand before bidding** (validation_testing)
1. Choose the resale venue first, such as eBay, Whatnot, Poshmark or Facebook Marketplace.
2. Search the category and inspect sold listings rather than relying on active asking prices.
3. Check what top sellers list and what appears to sell in the local market.
4. Use an incognito browser session when researching competitor listings, as Shannon suggests.
> "The thing is is trying to find what sells in your market. That's the first one." — Shannon Gene (~2:37)

**Match inventory to an unfair advantage** (niche_selection)
1. List practical advantages such as storage, a warehouse, a barn, helpers, repair skills or a useful network.
2. Choose products that fit those advantages; avoid bulky appliances if there is no space or moving help.
3. Use product knowledge to focus on a niche where other bidders may have less expertise.
> "And then you match those products to your what your advantages are." — Shannon Gene (~4:44)

**Use image and listing research to investigate obscure lots** (validation_testing)
1. Screenshot unclear auction photos and use image research as a starting point.
2. Treat AI identification and price estimates as leads, not proof.
3. Call the auction contact to confirm brand, completeness and condition.
4. Avoid public questions that expose useful research to competing bidders.
> "The great thing about Gov Deals is you can contact these people. You can ask a question." — Shannon Gene (~12:57)

**Start with low-cost items and build experience** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Start with free or cheap local goods such as couches or books.
2. Make small mistakes and learn what sells before committing to expensive lots.
3. Reinvest as the seller gains knowledge and can evaluate inventory more quickly.
> "Start small, make some mistakes, figure it out. What do you like to sell?" — Shannon Gene (~30:53)

**Create visual listing content that shows the work** (customer_acquisition)
1. Film time-lapse cleaning or preparation with a phone.
2. Show visible restoration or condition improvements in the listing.
3. Use the video to stop local marketplace users while they scroll.
> "You're going to show them doing that as well. And that is the first thing people are going to see when they're scrolling through Facebook Marketplace." — Shannon Gene (~27:06)

**Use local listings to clear a consistent furniture lot** (sales)
1. Count the inventory and create separate listings for repeatable item types.
2. Set a low, clear price and include the pickup address and collection hours.
3. Say that an item is available while it remains listed and avoid spending time replying to repetitive availability messages.
4. Offer a room set as a bundle where useful, and state whether prices are firm.
> "If you're seeing it here, it's in stock right now. If you message me, is this still available? I will not respond to you." — Chris Koerner (~18:50)

**Check manifests, photos and freight before buying** (operations)
1. Get the manifest and calculate the delivered cost per unit.
2. Compare freight cost with the auction bid before deciding whether the lot is viable.
3. Inspect item photos for poor stacking, exposed surfaces and likely damage.
4. Prefer items protected in their boxes when possible.
> "You want to always have a manifest. They tell you exactly what you're going to get." — Shannon Gene (~33:18)

**Use low-margin staples to bring customers into a store** (customer_acquisition)
1. Stock frequently needed goods that local customers have to travel to buy.
2. Price them near a familiar retailer's price to encourage visits.
3. Use the visit to expose shoppers to the rest of the store's changing inventory.
> "He's like, 'I sell it the same price Costco does.' And I just bring it in, bring it in, bring it in. It gets them in the store." — Shannon Gene (~39:56)

**Make a home-based liquidation operation welcome locally** (operations)
1. Offer neighbours first access to discounted goods.
2. Let local families buy items so the business is seen as useful to the community.
3. Manage delivery times and noise so nearby residents are not surprised by trucks.
> "So everybody gets a deal in your neighborhood. Everybody gets a $100 Blackstone." — Shannon Gene (~43:14)

**Sell liquidation as a treasure hunt** (sales)
1. Show buyers the unusual deal and invite interest rather than using pressure-heavy sales language.
2. Offer a clear discount on recognizable products.
3. Build repeat local buyers by letting them know more stock may arrive.
> "It's like, wow, I got all this stuff. It's amazing. would you I have these Blackstone grills I could sell for half price." — Shannon Gene (~42:50)

**Avoid emotional bids in efficient markets** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Estimate realizable resale value rather than relying on a high reference price.
2. Subtract buyer fees and likely selling costs before setting a maximum bid.
3. Walk away if the spread depends on winning at an unrealistic price.
> "someone's going to be bidding with emotion, which is a big mistake." — Shannon Gene (~36:40)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Research actual sold prices in the market where the seller plans to list; active asking prices alone do not prove demand.
- *mindset_execution*: Start with a small, low-cost deal to learn the work and identify which categories suit you.
- *niche_selection*: Choose products that fit existing advantages such as space, physical help, repair skills or industry contacts.
- *niche_selection*: Overlooked, unglamorous categories may attract less competition than obvious popular products.
- *finance_unit_economics*: An auction discount is not enough by itself: include freight, condition, repair, storage and the resale market in the bid decision.
- *scaling*: A useful business can begin as a side project and grow by reinvesting in inventory and systems.
- *mindset_execution*: A seller's personal reason for building the business can sustain effort when the work seems mundane.
- *offer_design*: A seller can create value by solving the inventory-clearance problem for a supplier, not only by finding a cheap item.
- *customer_acquisition*: Specialist connections can make an unfamiliar niche more approachable, but still verify the inventory and economics.
- *customer_acquisition*: A clear, visual demonstration of cleaning or repair can help a local listing earn attention.
- *finance_unit_economics*: A low-margin product can be useful as a traffic driver, but the retailer must understand its role in the broader economics.
- *automation_ai*: The examples depend on physical labour, local pickup and inventory handling; automation can support research and listings but cannot run the core work unattended.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Research first and buy second. Shannon says buyers sometimes acquire goods and only then ask what to do with them.
- Do not treat a speculative large auction lot as a gamble; calculate risks and learn product values before bidding.
- Broken or heavy equipment can create losses through repair, freight and handling costs.
- Do not bid on gold emotionally; fees and a liquid market can erase the apparent spread.
- Avoid products where many components can fail unless the seller has a reason and capability to handle repairs; the host says he generally avoids electronics.
- Poor listing information creates uncertainty. Verify identity, condition and completeness directly with the auction contact rather than relying only on AI.
- Heavy products require suitable storage, transport and help; select inventory that fits available space and labour.
- The transcript's pressure-washer starting cost is inconsistent: one passage says $1,500 and another says $1,000.
- The host's furniture profit example assumes quick sell-through and omits some costs such as transport, storage, labour and unsold inventory.
- A home-based resale operation should account for neighbours and delivery activity; the guest suggests involving neighbours so they support the operation.
- The guest's advice to collect buyer emails and keep in touch needs consent and compliant data handling.
