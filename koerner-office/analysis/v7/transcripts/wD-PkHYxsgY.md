## Summary

Chris Koerner and regular guest Sam discuss one-person businesses that can reach $20K/month profit without employees. Sam describes testing an AI-written divorce book ($29 ebook, $17 bump, $300 back-end) sold via Facebook ads at roughly break-even CAC, then printing 300 physical copies until Facebook restricted the ads as 'personal hardship' targeting. Chris adds the idea of monetising the book buyers by referring them to divorce lawyers for pay-per-lead or consulting/referral fees, and extends it to niche legal books (DUI, car accident, etc.). The key takeaway is to test quickly rather than over-research, start with the simple pay-per-lead model, and that $20K/month solo is far easier than scaling to a million-dollar team business.

### I1. Divorce self-help book funnel (ebook + bump + back-end)

- **Customer:** People going through or facing a divorce (US)
- **Problem:** Divorce is expensive, emotional and people dislike their divorce attorneys; little non-lawyer material targets them
- **Offer:** $29 ebook 'How to Win Your Divorce', $17 negotiation training toolkit bump, $300 Financial Protection Protocol back-end; later a 200-page printed copy
- **Business model:** One-off product sales (ebook/print book) plus order bump and back-end programme; possible lead resale
- **Pricing:** Low-ticket front end with bump and back-end upsell. Front-end book USD 29 per ebook; Negotiation training toolkit bump USD 17 one-off; Financial Protection Protocol back-end USD 300 one-off AOV around $42 with bump; about 70% of buyers took the bump per Sam
- **Acquisition:** paid_social: Facebook/Meta ads, broad targeting, automatic placements, optimised for purchases using a pixel on the thank-you page; about $100/day; first week $500-700 spend. Ads later blocked from purchase optimisation as 'personal hardship' targeting.; organic_social_content: Idea of theme pages about divorce or crazy divorce stories to move inventory for free (not tested).
- **Delivery:** Ebook delivered digitally (zero COGS); physical books printed (300 copies at about $6 each, about $8.50 landed with shipping) and shipped to customers; book written with ChatGPT then about 5 hours of refinement.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Ebook $29, CAC about $40 at first, then about $40-45 CAC at break-even with AOV about $42; About $5-6K in sales, roughly break-even; 300 printed books cost about $6 each, about $8.50 shipped; Spent about $3,000 on printed books
- **Tools:** ChatGPT, Perspective, ClickFunnels, Facebook Ads / Meta pixel, Printivity (print-on-demand), Canva
- **Startup cost:** About $500-700 initial ads plus about $3,000 for printed books; ebook version near zero
- **Time to first revenue:** First week of ads (sales within days)
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — Funnel works in UK but divorce law, lawyer market, pricing and Meta ad restrictions on personal hardship differ; the 50% divorce stat is US-framed (UK roughly 40%+).
- **Risks:** Facebook classified ads as targeting personal hardship, blocking purchase optimisation; Low-quality AI-written content (V1 was 'AI slop'); Break-even CAC leaves no margin without upsells or lead resale; Unsold physical inventory in the garage; Giving legal-adjacent advice
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Paid Meta ads with creative refresh are largely automatable, but needs human creative and policy-compliance handling; ad category restrictions are a risk.); onboarding 5/5 (Funnel checkout with bump and upsell is automated software.); delivery 4/5 (Ebook is instant; print-on-demand fulfilment can be automated, though Sam printed stock himself.); billing 5/5 (Checkout processor handles payment automatically.); support 3/5 (Order and shipping queries can be handled by an agent with a human check; a VA suggested for email.)

### I2. Divorce lead generation sold to lawyers

- **Customer:** Divorce attorneys (and other personal-injury/legal practices)
- **Problem:** Lawyers pay very high costs per lead on Google and are burned out on poor-quality leads
- **Offer:** Pre-qualified leads from people who bought a niche book, delivered to lawyers at a fixed price per lead or per signed contract
- **Business model:** Pay per lead, per signed client, or percentage of legal fees (framed as consulting fee)
- **Pricing:** Pay per lead, per signed contract, or revenue share. Illustrative pay-per-lead USD 100 per lead; Illustrative USD 1000 per signed contract; Illustrative if close rate is 16% USD 1200 per signed contract; Illustrative later price USD 150 per lead Chris suggests 30% of fees collected (fees between $7K and $23K, attorney about $270/hr); a quarter of invoices is an alternative; all hypothetical
- **Acquisition:** cold_call: Phone two or three lawyers beforehand to agree terms, then build relationships and share close data to set pricing for other lawyers.; paid_social: Book funnel ads act as the lead source, aiming for break-even on the book so leads cost nearly nothing.
- **Delivery:** Refer or send buyer details to lawyers; track which leads close by staying close to 2-3 lawyers; or just sell leads upfront and not worry about closing.
- **Unit economics (claims):** 3% take rate on 300 back-end puts you at $900/day profit (Sam's hypothetical); 100 leads, 8 close at $1,000 = $8K; Lead costs could drop to about $10 net if book revenue offsets CAC; Legal Google clicks can cost up to $5,000 per lead (trucking accident)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Fast test of a book funnel** (acquisition)
1. Spot a big painful market and check keywords briefly
2. Have ChatGPT write the book
3. Build a two-step funnel on Perspective
4. Run broad Meta ads, automatic placements, about $100/day, optimise for purchases with a pixel on the thank-you page
5. Read signal from CAC versus price
6. Move to ClickFunnels for bump and upsell offers
> "I just want purchases." And then I just kind of let it ride." — Sam (~4:51)

**Raise AOV with bump and back-end** (pricing)
1. Add a $17 bump at checkout before payment
2. Add a $300 back-end programme
3. Aim to bring AOV near CAC so ads break even

**Improve conversion by upgrading to a physical book** (product)
1. Rewrite the AI draft with about 5 hours of work
2. Order 300 print copies
3. Market it as a real shipped book to lift conversion

**Start lead gen with the simplest pay-per-lead deal** (sales)
1. Sell leads upfront at a low price (e.g. $100) to avoid babysitting lawyers
2. Build relationships with 2-3 lawyers who share close data
3. After about 3 months use data to price for lawyers you do not know
4. Raise prices or move to revenue share once close rates are proven

**Benchmark against your own past performance** (mindset)
1. Ignore industry averages
2. Compare cost per click and conversion to your own history
3. Ask whether you can make your own math work

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Test with a quick funnel instead of over-researching; first-party data beats industry averages.
- *mindset_execution*: Running one funnel teaches copywriting, landing pages and ads, skills reusable across any market.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Break-even customer acquisition via a low-ticket product, with profit from a back-end or lead sale, is a strong model.
- *scaling*: Choose the lower-touch, lower-profit version first so you do not burn out before learning.
- *offer_design*: Sell the same lead to the buyer, not the vulnerable consumer: monetising lawyers with money to spend.
- *scaling*: Going from a $20K/month solo business to a million-dollar team business adds disproportionate work, stress and people for modest extra profit.
- *customer_acquisition*: Pay-per-lead is an arbitrage between wholesale and retail lead cost, and a front-end product can subsidise lead cost.
- *mindset_execution*: Shrink goals to a quarter million a year solo rather than chasing yachts; doubling income and halving stress.
- *mindset_execution*: Execution matters more than ideas; people given a good idea often do nothing.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Facebook may classify divorce-related ads as targeting personal hardship and block purchase optimisation, which hit Sam right after spending $3,000 on books.
- Certain US states require you to be a lawyer to accept referral fees; structure as consulting.
- Doing too much too early in lead gen (high touch revenue share) can cause burnout and failure; start simple.
- Revenue-share deals risk lawyers not paying.
