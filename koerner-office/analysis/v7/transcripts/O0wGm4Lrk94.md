## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: Plan personal spending against after-tax income: a $250k month is roughly $125k after tax.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Keep committed monthly overhead low so you never put what you have built at risk; prefer controllable variable spend.
- *mindset_execution*: Lifestyle creep is something to stay conscious of rather than fear; each upgrade becomes a recurring obligation.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Expensive assets can be justified on time saved when the business requires frequent travel to hard-to-reach places, especially if bought fractionally with cash.
- *mindset_execution*: Recent wealth argues for caution: he has only been making money for a short time, so he holds back on big purchases like a $5M house.

## Warnings and mistakes

- High fixed personal overhead becomes dangerous when business income is volatile; a couple of bad months or a big investment need can create stress.
- Lifestyle upgrades compound: one partner's purchase (e.g. a personal trainer) leads the other to match it, adding recurring commitments.
- Losing the guilt or caution about spending is the point where things get dangerous.
