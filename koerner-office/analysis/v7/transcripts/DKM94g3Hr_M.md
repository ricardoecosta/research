## Tactics and playbook steps

**Run equal-stake model portfolios** (validation_testing)
1. Choose several AI models and give each a separate, equal starting allocation.
2. Keep the accounts or portfolios distinct and label each by model.
3. Treat the result as an experiment with a defined end point rather than changing the comparison period after seeing returns.
> "So, I decided to give five different models $1,000 of real money." — Brandon Doyle (~1:08)

**Prompt for concrete recommendations** (operations)
1. Ask each model for a real trade recommendation rather than a theoretical example.
2. State the permitted risk level and that the model should make a recommendation.
3. Ask for the reasoning behind each suggested trade so the operator can understand the thesis.
> "I want real recommendations. Grock they were fine with it right away which is just kind of funny" — Brandon Doyle (~3:06)

**Use a recurring portfolio review** (operations)
1. On the weekly review, screenshot each model's holdings and load the screenshots into the chats.
2. Ask whether the model wants to buy, sell, or hold for the next trading session.
3. Keep a record of portfolio totals so performance can be compared over time.
> "And then every Saturday what I do is I screenshot all five of their holdings and then I load all the screenshots into each model." — Brandon Doyle (~6:44)

**Limit holdings disclosure to reduce copying** (validation_testing)
1. Share portfolio totals more often than exact competing holdings.
2. Disclose the other models' holdings only occasionally if the test is meant to reduce copying.
3. Track whether disclosure changes later recommendations.
> "So, every week I show them the totals of the others and I remind them of their holdings and I say, "What do you want me to change?" And then once a month I show them the holdings of all the others as well." — Brandon Doyle (~10:35)

**Require a thesis and a reason to exit** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Ask the model why it wants to buy an asset.
2. Record what event or condition would make the original thesis fail.
3. Set a time horizon or milestone for positions whose outcome depends on a specific event.
4. Use the sell recommendation as a prompt to reassess the same thesis in the personal portfolio.
> "And usually I don't think like I don't think that hard sometimes when I buy stuff in mine because I might buy whatever I might buy." — Brandon Doyle (~27:42)

**Separate a thesis-based test from a general model contest** (validation_testing)
1. Keep the starting allocations and model comparison consistent.
2. Restrict a separate test to a stated selection thesis, such as network effects and founder leadership.
3. Score the thesis inputs and compare the resulting portfolio with a benchmark.
> "Same thing. Here's $1,000, five models. Compete against each other. Don't be afraid to be risky, but only buy knuff stocks." — Chris Koerner (~31:41)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: A test needs enough real stakes to keep the operator engaged, but the amount at risk should still be affordable to lose.
- *automation_ai*: Models may default to hedged or theoretical answers; state the task and decision boundaries clearly when asking for useful recommendations.
- *operations*: Use a consistent review routine and comparable inputs when evaluating several approaches.
- *finance_unit_economics*: When a competition rewards catching up, a losing participant can respond by taking more risk instead of reassessing the strategy.
- *validation_testing*: Write down why an investment is being made and what would invalidate the thesis; event-driven investments also need a time horizon.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Buying can feel easier than selling. A systematic sell rule can help counter the urge to hold a position just because it is doing well.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Do not judge an investing approach only by visible wins; people often share gains more readily than losses.
- *validation_testing*: A backtest can make a selection thesis easier to inspect, but the host's discussion does not show enough data to establish that the thesis will keep outperforming.
- *automation_ai*: Using AI-generated reasoning can challenge an investor's assumptions, but it can also bias personal decisions toward positions that performed well in the experiment.

## Warnings and mistakes

- A competition prompt that rewards winning and permits any level of risk can encourage a losing model to take increasingly speculative positions.
- The guest's leveraged ETF examples include a 2x Solana product that fell sharply and 3x products whose losses would also be magnified.
- The guest reports strong returns over a limited period and expects further gains, but the interview does not independently verify performance or establish that it will continue.
- Showing competing holdings can create copycat recommendations and contaminate the comparison.
- The guest changes to newer model versions during the experiment, so results do not compare a fixed set of models over an unchanged period.
- The host and guest state that the discussion is not financial advice.
- The host includes paid promotions for HighLevel and Playmakers, an AI agency community he says he built; these create a promotional conflict for business-related claims elsewhere in the video.
