## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate demand with a narrow starter inventory** (validation_testing)
1. Start with a small selection of items that address a clear local gap.
2. Use a storage space already available, if possible.
3. Post the items and contact likely buyers before committing to a much larger inventory order.
4. The guest said she initially ordered about four chair styles with 20 of each.
> "I think it was probably four styles, 20 of each chair." — Tyau (~1:46)

**Reach event planners with a visual menu** (customer_acquisition)
1. Find local event planners online.
2. Follow their social accounts and introduce the business.
3. Send photos of available items and a clear price list or menu.
4. Build relationships with planners who serve the target customer.
> "And I just started following them on social media, putting together pictures of our items, sending them a price list, like a menu" — Tyau (~5:27)

**Create portfolio content from real events** (customer_acquisition)
1. Record unboxing and early inventory posts.
2. Attend an early event in person and capture the setup and party, with appropriate permission.
3. Collaborate with planners and venues on styled shoots.
4. Encourage vendors and customers to tag the rental business so their audiences can discover it.
> "I made sure that I was on site, recording the process, recording the party, you know, and put that out there." — Tyau (~5:49)

**Use a descriptive business name and SEO** (customer_acquisition)
1. Choose wording customers are likely to search for when they need the service.
2. Build a website that shows the inventory and can capture bookings.
3. Invest in SEO to bring searchers to the site.
> "So that was really helpful with getting, just leads again to our website directly and our website being able to capture the bookings" — Tyau (~20:48)

**Expand inventory from requests and themed collections** (product)
1. Track what customers ask for and prioritize additions rather than buying everything at once.
2. Plan collections around party themes, trending characters, and color palettes.
3. Use upcoming bookings and sufficient lead time to source missing items.
4. List incoming inventory online before it arrives when the booking lead time allows.
> "So we were really listening to the clients. So at this point we have tables and chairs and then the clients are asking us for other things." — Tyau (~24:58)

**Package more of the party through one supplier** (offer_design)
1. Add complementary play equipment and activities based on what customers ask for.
2. Offer packages so parents can coordinate fewer vendors and deliveries.
3. Keep a lower-friction option such as customer pickup for clients who want to transport items themselves.
> "These parents are busy and they just, they really want to just deal with, if possible, one person." — Tyau (~27:14)

**Set deposits and collect the balance before the event** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Take a 50% deposit to reserve items.
2. Collect full payment one week before the event.
> "Our payment terms are 50% deposit for to reserve the items, and then one week before the event, they paid the 100% upfront." — Tyau (~38:15)

**Build a team to keep the owner out of daily operations** (hiring_outsourcing)
1. Put a manager and drivers in place for delivery operations.
2. Assign customer inquiries, administration, and social media to staff.
3. Keep the owner focused on ownership and decisions while retaining a full-time job.
> "I have a full time job. I have a team in place who is managing the business essentially and then I'm basically, I'm the owner." — Tyau (~0:14)

**Vet franchise operators** (hiring_outsourcing)
1. Look for operators who are eager to start and have resources.
2. Vet prospective partners before entering a franchise relationship.
> "we're just vetting and making sure that we're getting into business with the right types of people." — Tyau (~41:29)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Research a sector closely enough to notice a clear local gap before investing.
- *validation_testing*: Start with a manageable inventory, then take calculated risks as real customer demand appears.
- *offer_design*: Distinctive design and a clear point of view can help a business stand out in a service that might otherwise seem interchangeable.
- *validation_testing*: Use early customer feedback and requests to decide which products to add next.
- *customer_acquisition*: Strong event execution can lead to referrals from guests and vendors who see the product in use.
- *customer_acquisition*: Content can keep a business in mind long before someone is ready to buy.
- *offer_design*: Combine complementary offers when that makes the customer's job easier and reduces coordination.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: A business can remain a side hustle if the owner builds a team and assigns day-to-day responsibilities.
- *operations*: Build operational capacity with care: hiring the right people can be difficult even when delegation gives the owner time back.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Consider pre-selling or listing incoming inventory when bookings come far enough ahead to reduce the risk of buying too early.
