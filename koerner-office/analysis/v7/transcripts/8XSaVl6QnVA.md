## Tactics and playbook steps

**Recruit cleaners through local groups** (hiring)
1. Post 'looking for a good cleaner, any recommendations?' in local Facebook/Nextdoor groups
2. Expect 10-30 replies
3. Message each: we're a cleaning company looking for another good cleaner; you were recommended
4. Filter: 1+ year professional experience, own transport, own supplies, takes ownership
5. Build a roster of 2-3 reliable cleaners
> "Hey, I'm looking for a good cleaner. Does anyone have a recommendation?" — Sergio (~10:26)

**Secret test clean** (hiring)
1. Send the cleaner to a friend/family home (partial clean ~3 hours, pay ~$100)
2. Set expectations and that the customer belongs to the company
3. Have the friend rate quality and service
4. Have the friend ask 'how can I book again?'
5. Pass only if the cleaner says to book through the company
> "If the cleaner says, "Oh, just call the company, they can get you on the book." Boom, your cleaner passed the test" — Sergio (~13:20)

**Grand opening neighbour post** (acquisition)
1. Post as a neighbour introducing yourself with a family/dog photo
2. Mention you have a cleaning business, not a hard sell
3. DM everyone who likes or comments to thank them
4. Offer $50-100 or 50% off the first clean in exchange for telling the group and leaving a review

**Convert one-time to recurring within 10 minutes** (sales)
1. Call the customer 5-10 minutes after the clean finishes
2. Confirm it went well
3. Say most people go every other week or monthly; ask if they'd like that
> "it's super important that you call them right after the clean was done, like within 5 to 10 minutes" — Sergio (~15:43)

**Set up the business in a weekend** (operations)
1. Pick a city of 50k+ people
2. Simple website, Google Business Profile, separate business phone number
3. Hire first cleaners within 1-2 weeks
4. Aim for revenue in 30 days

## Lessons

- *scaling*: Leverage comes from owning customers and systems, not labour.
- *niche_selection*: Pick services with existing demand, recurring purchase, and contractor-friendly delivery.
- *niche_selection*: Boring industries have less serious competition; local operators lack marketing and systems.
- *customer_acquisition*: Recurring customers compound; convert one-time buyers at peak emotion.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Hire on experience, transport, supplies and ownership; most applicants fail.
- *pricing*: Discount the first job heavily when new to buy leeway and reviews.
- *mindset_execution*: To make the first dollar you only need a contact method, a good worker and a customer.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Service businesses where you do the labour cap your income at your hours.
- Cleaners may try to poach customers; test for it before hiring.
- Bad cleans will happen; send someone back and fix it.
- Hiring people who make excuses brings headaches.
- In the UK, cold DMs/SMS marketing to consumers needs consent under PECR, and 'independent' cleaners may legally be workers.
