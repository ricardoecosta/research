## Summary

David Heacock interviews Jim Baloga, CEO and owner of three US proprietary schools (Porter and Chester Institute, YTI Career Institute, College of Court Reporting) that train skilled trades, healthcare and court reporting students. Baloga explains how he bought the court reporting school as a small platform to avoid a large US Department of Education letter of credit, then used it to acquire the larger schools he had run for about 20 years. He covers tuition, outcomes, regulatory barriers to entry, upfront cash collection, low acquisition multiples and a roll-up plan. The host's takeaway is that deep industry experience reveals opportunities, and that a trade skill is a low-risk base for entrepreneurship.

### I1. Accredited trade and healthcare school (Porter and Chester / YTI)

- **Customer:** School leavers, career changers and displaced workers (e.g. a 35-year-old ex-paralegal) wanting a skilled trade or healthcare career.
- **Problem:** Shortage of skilled tradespeople and mismatch between available jobs and trained talent; families want careers that AI will not replace.
- **Offer:** About one-year programmes with lectures plus hands-on industry-modeled labs, career services, job fairs and employer partnerships. Mantra: graduate on Friday, go to work on Monday.
- **Business model:** Tuition per programme, about 80% paid via federal sources (Pell grants, direct loans, veterans benefits, state grants).
- **Pricing:** Tuition per programme. Typical tuition USD 25000-35000 per year (programme) Some employer-sponsored programmes cost the student nothing (e.g. 10-week welding programme for a nuclear sub contractor).
- **Acquisition:** other: Online marketing plus in-person referrals feed an admissions process; about 5-6,000 prospective students a year, about half enrol.; referrals_word_of_mouth: Students often start after seeing a friend's parent run a trade business; campus visits encouraged.; partnerships: Employer partnerships and in-person career fairs with local contractors, hospitals and care facilities.
- **Delivery:** In-person campuses of 25,000-60,000 sq ft with labs (e.g. two-storey stick house for wiring), qualified instructors, curriculum, student finance and career services teams. About 350-400 employees, two-thirds in education.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Tuition $25-35k per year per student; About 80% of tuition revenue from federal sources; Revenue CAGR 6-7%, EBITDA CAGR about 10%; Tuition collected upfront at start cycles about every 10 weeks
- **Tools:** Power BI, CRMs, student information systems, Federal student aid programmes
- **Startup cost:** Not stated; accreditation takes at least two years and a change of ownership may require a letter of credit of 25% of prior-year Title IV receipts.
- **Time to first revenue:** At least two years to become accredited for new entrants.
- **UK applicability [E]:** 1/5 — Depends on US federal student aid and US accreditation. UK equivalents (FE colleges, apprenticeship providers, Ofsted, funding rules) differ entirely and are heavily regulated.
- **Risks:** Heavy regulatory exposure; one compliance foot-fault can be fatal; Dependence on federal funding policy; Birth-rate cycles drive enrolment; Student dropout and graduation/employment benchmark requirements (e.g. 55% graduation, 70% employed); Capital-intensive leased campuses
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Online marketing and lead gen can be largely automated, but admissions and in-person referrals need humans.); onboarding 1/5 (Admissions, financial aid and enrolment involve counsellors and regulated paperwork.); delivery 0/5 (Hands-on lab teaching by instructors on physical campuses.); billing 3/5 (Student finance and aid disbursement can be systematised but are regulated and need staff.); support 1/5 (Career services, job fairs and student retention are human-led.)

### I2. Acquire under-managed regulated schools and professionalise them (roll-up)

- **Customer:** Aging school owners and operators looking to exit.
- **Problem:** Many proprietary schools are under-managed or mismanaged; buyers face a letter of credit equal to 25% of prior-year Title IV receipts on a change of control.
- **Offer:** Professional management and systems for acquired schools; Baloga bought the small College of Court Reporting first, then used it as a platform to buy the larger Porter and Chester ('David bought Goliath').
- **Business model:** Acquire at 3-7x earnings, improve operations, grow revenue and EBITDA, use seller financing and cash flow post close.
- **Pricing:** Acquisition multiples. Typical multiples in the sector  3-7 x earnings (multiple) Multiples were around 10x in 2000-2010. Lenders comfortable with 3-4x debt.
- **Acquisition:** networking_events: Deals sourced via industry relationships and private equity contacts (Thoma Bravo/Thoma Cressey).
- **Delivery:** Operator takes over, installs professional management, systems (CRM, student information) and compliance team.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Court reporting school up over 50% since takeover; Multiples 3-7x; Debt financing 3-4x
- **Tools:** Seller financing, Private equity backing, Letters of credit
- **Startup cost:** Nominal purchase for the small platform; letter of credit on a $50M Title IV school would be about $12.5M.
- **Time to first revenue:** About 5 years to find the regulatory pathway (talks from about 2017, closed July 2024).
- **UK applicability [E]:** 1/5 — Structure is tied to US Department of Education rules; UK equivalent would be buying small training providers, a different regulatory regime.
- **Risks:** Capital-intensive letters of credit; Regulatory approval of change of control; Cultural fit in acquisitions; Needs deep operating experience
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 0/5 (Deal sourcing relies on relationships and negotiation.); onboarding 0/5 (Integration of acquired schools is human-led.); delivery 1/5 (Operational turnaround is management work.); billing 3/5 (Finance functions are systematised.); support 1/5 (People-centric business.)
> "look for, um, under-managed, um, solid companies with good educational programming that could be turned around and professionalized" — Jim Baloga (~6:07)
> "I basically ended up structuring a transaction where David I bought David, and then David bought Goliath" — Jim Baloga (~7:38)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Small-platform acquisition to dodge letter of credit** (finance)
1. Identify a small, under-managed accredited school with a low Title IV revenue base
2. Buy it first so the letter of credit is small
3. Use it as the acquiring platform for the larger target
4. Back with PE relationship and structure nominal purchase price
> "David I bought David, and then David bought Goliath in a way that facilitated a unique transaction" — Jim Baloga (~7:42)

**Employer-linked career pipeline** (operations)
1. Run in-person career fairs with local electrical, plumbing, HVAC firms and hospitals
2. Prepare students with resumes and interviews
3. Let employers hire on the spot

**Deal structuring with low upfront capital** (finance)
1. Use seller financing to stretch payments
2. Target underperforming or undermanaged schools
3. Pay using post-close cash flow
4. Keep debt at 3-4x

**Cash-flow-positive tuition cycle** (finance)
1. Run start cycles about every 10 weeks
2. Collect tuition upfront each cycle
3. Negotiate trade credit terms for near-negative working capital

**Campus real estate standardisation** (operations)
1. At lease renewal pick a high-traffic, highly visible site
2. Prefer rectangular or square footprints
3. Overlay a proven campus design

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Deep time in an industry lets you spot opportunities outsiders cannot; the best path is often to work in a field for years before acquiring.
- *niche_selection*: Regulatory barriers to entry protect incumbents and create moats for skilled operators.
- *validation_testing*: Test fast, fail fast, adjust fast, even in a regulated environment; the right structure took 3-5 years of trying.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Getting paid upfront gives a strong cash conversion cycle and can fund growth.
- *mindset_execution*: A trade skill de-risks entrepreneurship: worst case you return to working the trade, and student debt cannot be wiped by bankruptcy.
- *exit_acquisition*: Structure acquisitions to use post-close cash flow and seller financing instead of outside capital.
- *operations*: Invest in mentoring and a flat functional structure so the CEO is not essential day to day.
- *niche_selection*: AI-resistant, physical trades are attracting career changers and taking share from traditional higher education.

## Warnings and mistakes

- New entrants to regulated education face accreditation taking at least two years and compliance errors can be fatal.
- Changes in control can require large letters of credit that tie up dead capital for about 3 years.
- Over-leveraging in the 2000s boom drew regulatory attention and multiples collapsed.
- Boom-bust enrolment tied to birth rates.
