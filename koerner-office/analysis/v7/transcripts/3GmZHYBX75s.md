### I1. Custom pet portrait print-on-demand store

- **Customer:** Pet owners and gift buyers (about 60% gifts: memorials, new puppies, birthdays)
- **Problem:** Want a personalised keepsake of a pet
- **Offer:** Hand-drawn pet portraits in several styles, up to five pets, frames, backgrounds, rush queue and other upsells; an automated 'vogue' style needs no artist
- **Business model:** One-off product sales (DTC Shopify and Etsy)
- **Pricing:** Per product with upsells. AOV on own store USD 90 per order; AOV on Etsy USD 65 per order; two-pet framed portrait USD 105 per item; red oak frame upgrade (costs $8) USD 10 per upsell Free shipping absorbed; gross margins 60-70% overall, 40-50% on framed portraits.
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: Etsy listings and Etsy ads (pay-per-click, $1,000/day budget but often only $100/day spent outside Q4).; paid_social: Basic Facebook ads from month two; scaled down once Etsy worked.; organic_social_content: Launch via personal Facebook posts, Instagram engagement pods and paying pet pages ($20-50) for posts; Pinterest worked well.; existing_audience: Email list of about 24,000 with pruning flows.
- **Delivery:** Overseas commissioned artists (80 at peak, mostly Philippines) draw portraits; print-on-demand/screen-printing partners print, frame and ship for a flat rate; one employee grades files for the automated style.
- **Unit economics (claims):** First month ~$1,000, month two $3-5k, months 3-10 $10-20k/month; Over $800k revenue in Nov-Dec 2020; first full year just over $1M; 65-70% of revenue in Nov-Dec; Gross margin 60-70%; net ~20-30%; AOV rose 20% after checkout redesign
- **Tools:** Shopify, Etsy, Canva, Fiverr, Upwork, Facebook ads, Pinterest, Print-on-demand partners
- **Startup cost:** About $30-40 (Shopify plan) built over one weekend
- **Time to first revenue:** Launch week (November 2019); ~$1,000 first month
- **UK applicability [E]:** 5/5 — UK POD printers and Etsy UK make it identical; strong UK pet gifting market.
- **Risks:** Extreme Q4 seasonality; AI-generated $10 digital files commoditising Etsy; Ad platform dependence; Low repeat purchase
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 4/5 (Etsy search/ads and Meta ads run inbound; creative refresh can be agent-made.); onboarding 5/5 (Self-serve checkout with photo upload and options.); delivery 3/5 (AI image models could replace artists (they already automate one style); printing/shipping is outsourced POD; human QA of files.); billing 5/5 (Store checkout.); support 3/5 (AI agents can handle revision back-and-forth; some edge cases need humans.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Weekend print-on-demand launch** (product)
1. Spot a product style working for a competitor (Renaissance pet portraits) and adapt it
2. Build a Shopify store over a weekend with YouTube tutorials and Canva
3. Outsource art via Fiverr/Upwork and fulfilment to print-on-demand
4. Launch with personal social posts around Black Friday
5. Add Facebook ads in month two and Etsy in month three
> "I knew that I could start up for close to nothing" — Spencer (~0:00)

**Multi-step customiser checkout to raise AOV** (pricing)
1. Step 1 choose style and number of pets (up to 5)
2. Step 2 frame, size, background with paid upgrades rounded up (e.g. $8 cost frame sold for $10)
3. Offer a paid skip-the-queue rush option
4. Final step photo upload, name, font, halo, artist notes
5. Result: AOV up 20%

**Creative-volume Facebook ads** (acquisition)
1. Use broad targeting / CBO and let Meta find the audience
2. Film lots of simple employee videos with products (10 seconds to 10 minutes)
3. Put spend behind winners
4. Replace creative when performance dips after 1-2 weeks
5. Optionally test many AI-avatar UGC ads with HeyGen
> "You need more creative. You need to test more things." — Chris Koerner (~24:14)

**Survey price vs human-made preference** (pricing)
1. Show customers a $90 human-drawn and a $65 AI version side by side
2. Ask which they'd buy
3. Decide whether the human-art differentiator is worth the margin

**Live auction to drive store traffic** (acquisition)
1. Prepare prints of the most common breeds in ~20 styles
2. Auction them on TikTok Live/Whatnot
3. Use the show to send viewers to the website
> "And do an auction like for a eight by 12 or whatever for the drawing style." — Chris Koerner (~32:14)

**Spy on competitors in Meta Ad Library** (acquisition)
1. Regularly review competitor ads (e.g. Aston Willow) in the Meta Ad Library
2. Copy what works and test it

## Lessons

- *operations*: Print-on-demand plus commissioned offshore labour lets you start for almost nothing and stay cash-flow positive without inventory.
- *scaling*: Giftable products convert best around Black Friday and Christmas; plan cash and capacity for that peak.
- *finance_unit_economics*: To scale with paid ads in e-commerce you need roughly 60-70% gross margins.
- *customer_acquisition*: On modern Meta ads, creative volume and freshness matter more than targeting.
- *validation_testing*: Test assumptions about what customers value (human vs AI art) directly instead of assuming.
- *pricing*: Add-on upsells and a guided customiser lift order value substantially.
- *customer_acquisition*: Early movers in new platform categories (TikTok Live auctions) get outsized algorithmic distribution.
- *automation_ai*: AI image models now make offering cheap automated product variants and lead-gen visuals practical.
- *mindset_execution*: Entrepreneurs are constant students; YouTube can teach most tools within a week.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Etsy ads can't guarantee spend or be targeted granularly, limiting scale outside Q4.
- Heavy seasonality: growth that relies on holiday sales or friends and family is not a sustainable traffic source.
- Sudden order surges can overwhelm operations; they had to turn off the Etsy shop in Q4 2020.
- Old Instagram growth tactics (engagement pods, paid shout-outs on big pages) no longer work.
- Being dogmatic about hand-made differentiation may leave money on the table if customers just want cheaper.
- Inventory-based products (sewn goods) brought sourcing and manufacturing headaches versus print-on-demand.
