## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Jazz, who runs Winnipeg Digest, a local events/community newsletter (about 35,000 email subscribers plus about 73,000 social followers) started in April 2024. Jazz explains audience growth via Facebook ads, annual exclusive sponsorships priced at about $12,000 per year, survey-based audience data, and a VA plus Claude/ChatGPT content workflow. He is now vertically integrating by selling Christmas light installation to newsletter readers, with plans for permanent lights and a fractional-CMO-for-equity model. The key takeaway is that a local newsletter is a cheap audience asset to build, sponsor sales are the hard part, and the audience can be monetised further through owned businesses.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Sell annual exclusive sponsorships** (pricing)
1. Do not sell one-off sponsorships
2. Offer exclusivity by category (one HVAC company per year)
3. Price at about $12,000 for 12 monthly ads
4. Pitch it as a monopoly over the local audience
> "you want to sell annual sponsorships on a exclusivity basis." — Jazz (~24:46)

**Sell sponsors on lifetime value** (sales)
1. Ask the business what a customer is worth
2. Compare to the ad spend, e.g. $5,000 customer vs $10,000 spend
3. Say you only need to find them four customers out of 35,000 readers
4. Target high-ticket or recurring categories like dentists, HVAC, plumbing, roofing
> "I just need to get you four customers for you to double your um return on ad spend with us" — Jazz (~28:02)

**Signup survey for audience data** (product)
1. Send new subscribers straight to a short Beehiiv survey (8 questions)
2. Ask neighbourhood, home ownership, buying or selling plans, income
3. Use results like '75% homeowners' in sponsor pitches

**Use Instagram inbound leads to sell newsletter ads** (acquisition)
1. Wait for marketing coordinators to ask for an Instagram feature
2. Jump on a call and sell the newsletter
3. Convert to annual ad dollars

**Ramp publishing frequency** (operations)
1. Start weekly
2. Move to twice weekly then three times a week within a month when content overflows
3. Stop where content quality stays consistent

**Short urgency-based subject lines** (acquisition)
1. Keep under five words and 40 characters
2. Include urgency such as 'this weekend'
3. Example: new Winnipeg Costco location

**Facebook ad creative for newsletter signups** (acquisition)
1. 15-second video with local monument or drone footage
2. Headline: struggling to find things to do in [city]
3. Subline: [Newsletter] sends you 50+ events every week
4. Test video against image

**Exclusive giveaway to build a leads list** (acquisition)
1. Run a giveaway of the product through the newsletter
2. Collect entries (about 300)
3. Call entrants offering $150 off for entering
4. Add a deadline discount

**In-home design consult sales process** (sales)
1. Book a design consult
2. Sit with the customer, show before and after mockup
3. Walk through a booklet and anchor on a high price (e.g. $3,000)
4. Remove items down to about $1,500
5. Offer a time-limited discount if booked today

**Pre-sell permanent lights during the Christmas season** (pricing)
1. Offer $500 off permanent lights next year to Christmas light customers
2. Pull revenue forward from three years to two

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: The audience is easy to build; the hard part is selling advertisers on a new advertising concept. Plan the sponsor sales process early.
- *niche_selection*: First-mover advantage in an underserved local market lowers acquisition costs and creates a monopoly position.
- *pricing*: Exclusivity and annual terms are the product; sell the monopoly over a local audience rather than impressions.
- *scaling*: Own audience lets you vertically integrate and capture far more value than third-party ad networks; ad-only monetisation caps out.
- *validation_testing*: Test a new vertical in a short, low-commitment window (10 weeks) so it can be dropped if it fails.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Learn the work by doing it yourself before outsourcing so you can manage labour properly.
- *automation_ai*: AI plus VAs can cut daily writing from about 8 hours to a small review step, letting the founder focus on sponsors.
- *exit_acquisition*: Detach the founder's face from the brand if you want the business to be a sellable asset.
- *mindset_execution*: A time-compounding business where audience grows with the years is attractive because it is hard to lose.
- *offer_design*: Choose adjacent businesses that use your existing strength (marketing) and your audience's demographics (women making home decisions).

## Warnings and mistakes

- Don't clean your newsletter list automatically; tracking can show real readers as unopened and cleaning removes them.
- Don't try to close the sale at the first consult without a structured presentation; early quotes converted poorly.
- Avoid accepting casinos or clubs as advertisers if it conflicts with the audience vibe.
- Markets under 100-150k are too small and over a million are too spread out for one newsletter.
- Horizontal expansion lowers CPMs because national brands pay far less than local advertisers.
- Don't use ad networks like Beehiiv boosts for local newsletters; it harms reader experience and trust.
