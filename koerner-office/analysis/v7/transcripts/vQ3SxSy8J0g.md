## Tactics and playbook steps

**Learn company problems from inside the organization** (customer_acquisition)
1. Consider working at a company to see how its teams operate.
2. Notice where employees struggle, where spreadsheets are used, and where work slips through the cracks.
3. Use those observed pain points to identify possible AI services or tools.
> "If you're on a meeting that could have been an email, that's an opportunity. If somebody let something slip through the cracks, that's an opportunity." — Nick (~38:22)

**Use a short AI update to open executive conversations** (sales)
1. Identify publicly traded companies and find executive contact information from public sources.
2. Write a concise, relevant message that offers a short briefing on practical AI changes.
3. Expect rejection and improve the pitch based on responses.
4. Use the first meeting to demonstrate a concrete workflow rather than just describe AI.
> "If you can get on their calendar and just have like what I had with that executive team, like within a couple of minutes, they're going to be like, "Oh, I get it. I get it."" — Nick (~39:34)

**Treat an AI demo as a prototype, not proof of demand** (validation_testing)
1. Build a small demo around one real workflow.
2. Show it to people who do that work and ask what the process currently costs them.
3. Use their feedback to decide whether to refine, sell, or abandon the idea.
> "And so, from this, like I I showed some of the people at my company like, "Hey, this is pretty cool."" — Nick (~20:58)

**Keep a human review stage in AI-generated earnings scripts** (operations)
1. Collect the company strategy and current reporting data.
2. Generate an initial draft from prior transcripts and the new data.
3. Workshop the recommendations, refine the script, and export only after people review it.
> "The thing that people miss here is they think AI just does everything. It doesn't. It's a good help but like you still have to work shop things." — Nick (~23:17)

**Create a clear taxonomy before building AI retrieval** (operations)
1. Inventory where files are stored and identify inconsistent folder and naming conventions.
2. Agree on a hierarchy and common naming structure.
3. Use AI to categorize files, then have an experienced person resolve ambiguous cases.
4. Prepare the resulting data for search and retrieval.
> "Now, clean data would be clear taxonomy, which a taxonomy would just be a hierarchical way for you to to uh identify information." — Nick (~27:07)

**Start with meeting capture and follow-up** (automation_ai)
1. Record meetings with appropriate participant notice.
2. Transcribe and summarize the meeting.
3. Save the transcript and summary in a searchable location.
4. Extract commitments and create reminders for agreed follow-up.
> "But if you just record meetings, document what was said, and then put it in a place where you can go and get back to later or build something that reminds you" — Nick (~36:29)

**Use multiple models to review plans and specifications** (operations)
1. Ask one model to turn an idea into a product requirements document.
2. Send the document to other models for an adversarial review.
3. Revise the specification through several review rounds.
4. Repeat the review for the implementation plan before building.
> "And I go through like four rounds of that. And after those four rounds, I've got something that's pretty good." — Nick (~45:41)

**Test a physical product idea where customers already are** (validation_testing)
1. Take the printer to school and offer on-demand printing.
2. Try specific products such as team shirts, customized spikes, tumblers, phone cases, and stickers.
3. Ask which products and colors customers prefer and what they will pay.
4. Follow customer demand instead of committing early to one product.
> "How much will people pay? What colors do they like? Do they want tumblers? Do they want their phone cases engraved? Stickers, whatever." — Chris Koerner (~5:29)

**Use new technology for rapid testing** (validation_testing)
1. Treat a new tool as a way to test many possible offers.
2. Run small experiments and observe actual demand.
3. Keep options open until customers show where interest is strongest.
> "That's what you should do with this. And leave your options open and then just chase the energy." — Chris Koerner (~5:36)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Learn AI by applying it to real customer or workplace problems; experimentation alone is not a business.
- *validation_testing*: Keep testing possible uses and follow demonstrated customer demand instead of marrying the first idea.
- *mindset_execution*: A job can be a way to learn a company's real problems and build expertise before starting a business.
- *automation_ai*: Start with a clear task, training material, and oversight plan before handing work to an agent.
- *operations*: Clean, structured data is a prerequisite for useful AI retrieval and analysis.
- *automation_ai*: Start with simple, useful workflows such as meeting summaries and follow-up before building elaborate agents.
- *operations*: Use AI as a first-draft and analysis aid while keeping people involved in judgment-heavy work.
- *customer_acquisition*: A practical demonstration can make a new technology understandable to leaders who have not tried it.
- *automation_ai*: Use multiple AI models to challenge a plan and expose gaps before implementation.
- *customer_acquisition*: Build an audience you can reach directly because social media access can disappear.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not assume AI can deliver a finished, reliable result without human review; Nick says messaging still needs to be workshopped and refined.
- A powerful agent is not useful unless the user has a plan for what work it should do and how it will be supervised.
- Poorly organized or inconsistent source data can prevent AI from finding and using the right information.
- Locally hosting an agent is a security choice Nick says he prefers; he says hosting it on the web scares him.
- AI classification leaves ambiguous files that still need an experienced person to decide where they belong.
- The suggested cold email approach needs UK legal checks: marketing emails to sole traders generally require consent, and cold calls must be screened against TPS/CTPS.
- Treat the printer idea as unvalidated and test demand, prices, and product preferences before committing.
- The host's Facebook account and page were banned, illustrating that social platform access and audiences can disappear.
