## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Jamison, a former private-equity professional who launched West Coast Decks in Southern California with no trade experience. He closed $60,000 of jobs in month one using paid lead services, and says he reached just under $1M in year one and now does $4-8M a year. The video is a tactical playbook for launching a high-ticket home service business: pick a low-decision-count, high-order-value niche, invest in branding early, respond to leads in seconds, hire a well-paid VA, cultivate referrals from adjacent trades via social media, and bid at 50% gross margin (2x COGS).

### I1. West Coast Decks (premium deck building)

- **Customer:** Southern California homeowners wanting new decks or deck repair; also referrals via landscapers, roofers and other trades
- **Problem:** Homeowners want a deck built or repaired by a trustworthy, professional contractor rather than a handyman; the market is short of good providers
- **Offer:** Design and installation of decks (wood or composite, railings, optional lighting and outdoor kitchens), sold in a short one-meeting close
- **Business model:** Per-job project sales, bid at about 2x cost of service (50% gross margin)
- **Pricing:** Project-based quote; cost of labor and materials x2. typical deck job cited USD 50000 per job; large complex deck USD 500000 per job Early pricing was admittedly too low, which helped close the first jobs and gather social proof.
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: Paid lead services at launch (Angie's List, HomeAdvisor; today Thumbtack, TaskRabbit, Angie). Deck repair leads about $60 and deck build leads about $120. Respond within 30 seconds, site visit within a day, proposal within another day.; organic_social_content: Posts daily on Instagram and Facebook plus an email newsletter; consistent branding and profiles on Google, Yelp, Houzz, BBB and Chamber of Commerce.; referrals_word_of_mouth: Instagram-driven referrals from other trades (mostly landscapers, roofers) said to produce about $400k in year one and $600k in year two; also meets lumber yards and supplier sales reps in person who send leads.; paid_search: No ads for the first 3 years; PPC, Google LSA and Meta ads are a 2026 initiative.
- **Delivery:** Installed physically by crews; California in-house versus subcontractors is tricky, he says either works if managed well. Installs about half a million sq ft of decking per year.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Closed $60k in 3 jobs in month one for about $1,500 of lead spend; Bid at 50% gross margin (2x COGS) to reach about 15% net; Year one revenue just under $1M; now $4-8M per year
- **Tools:** Angie, HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack, TaskRabbit, Google LSA, Pinterest, Upwork, Fiverr, Instagram, Facebook, Yelp
- **Startup cost:** $15,000 set aside as working capital; branding under about $3,000
- **Time to first revenue:** First month ($60k closed within about 3 days of leads)
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — Home-improvement decking works in the UK, but lead platforms differ (Checkatrade, MyBuilder, Bark), the climate and regulations differ, and he would need real trade crews.
- **Risks:** Asymmetric downside: costs only compress after the bid; Customer expectation mismatches; Pricing too low early; Key-man risk until about $7-8M revenue; Need to respond instantly to leads
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 2/5 (Lead-marketplace purchases, ads and social posting can be tooled, but supplier/trade relationship building and rapid human responses drive results.); onboarding 1/5 (Site visits and quote meetings are in person; design decisions made with the client.); delivery 0/5 (Physical construction work by crews.); billing 3/5 (Invoicing and deposits can be handled by software, with change-order enforcement by humans.); support 1/5 (Homeowner expectation management needs a human.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Cheap crowdsourced branding kit** (product)
1. Browse Pinterest, Behance or Dribbble for brands you like, even from other industries
2. Post an Upwork or Fiverr job: $200 for 20 design concepts, hire about 5 designers (about $1,000, around 100 concepts)
3. Pick the 3 favourite concepts
4. Hire a designer for a full kit (logo, typography, colours, uniforms) for $500-750
5. Total under about $3,000
> "for $200, I want 20 design concepts for my brand" — Jamison (~8:43)

**Name = service area + service** (acquisition)
1. Name the business [Place] + [Service], e.g. Dallas Decks
2. Check the .com is available before committing
3. Avoid owner-name branding
> "The simple equation for what you name your business is your service area and your service." — Jamison (~6:30)

**Speed to lead** (sales)
1. Respond to every lead within 30 seconds (5-minute hard limit)
2. Do a qualifying call or site visit immediately, site visit within a day
3. Deliver the proposal within another day
4. Keep phone ringer on and answer
> "need to respond to a lead within 30 seconds. We have a like a 5-minute rule upon pain of death" — Jamison (~13:49)

**Bootstrap social proof** (acquisition)
1. Ask friends, neighbours, former co-workers and online followers for reviews
2. Set up Google, Yelp, Houzz, BBB, Chamber of Commerce profiles with identical name, phone and address
3. Use lead-gen platforms to get first jobs and real reviews

**Referral ecosystem from adjacent trades and suppliers** (acquisition)
1. Post daily on Instagram and Facebook so roofers, landscapers etc. see your work
2. Visit lumber yards and supplier sales reps in person and ask how you can help each other
3. Hire a VA to support the posting and admin
> "they will send you leads. They will send you business." — Jamison (~22:31)

**Price at 2x cost and hold the line** (pricing)
1. Measure labour and material cost, multiply by 2
2. Push price as high as possible at the bid stage
3. Enforce subcontractor agreements, tight labour budgets and supplier terms
4. Charge for change orders and stick to scope
> "bid at 50% gross, which means 2x your COGS" — Jamison (~32:22)

**Overpay your first VA offshore** (hiring)
1. Pay about $12-15 per hour to a VA in Central/South America or the Philippines rather than the lowest rate
2. This buys an experienced person who can work without an established system
> "pay 12 or 15, which feels like a lot" — Jamison (~19:50)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Choose a niche with high average order value and few client decisions to reduce room for error.
- *offer_design*: Professional branding early can substitute for missing track record when trust matters.
- *customer_acquisition*: Speed of response is the most important sales advantage in local services.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use paid lead marketplaces to get first jobs and reviews, then shift to organic referral and later paid ads.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Price at 2x cost; confusing margin and markup is common and fatal.
- *customer_acquisition*: Targeting adjacent trades and suppliers for referrals is cheap and compounding.
- *mindset_execution*: Going all-in with commitment (contractual obligations, burned boats) drove execution.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Well-run trade businesses should net about 15%; more suggests underinvestment, less suggests a problem.
- *scaling*: Key-man risk persists until a management team exists, typically around $7-8M revenue.

## Warnings and mistakes

- If you cannot respond to leads immediately, do not do a home service business.
- Don't rely only on word of mouth; advertising works.
- Construction and service have asymmetric downside; jobs that lose money sink the business unless priced at 50% gross margin.
- Underpricing early can help close jobs but is a loss leader.
- Scaling from about $3M to $8M is a 'death zone' where you need leadership you cannot yet afford.
