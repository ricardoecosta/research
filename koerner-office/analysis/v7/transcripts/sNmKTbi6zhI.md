## Tactics and playbook steps

**Make sales the foundation** (customer_acquisition)
1. Start with direct conversations with potential customers.
2. Expect discomfort and awkward situations as part of selling.
3. Follow up persistently, even when it feels uncomfortable.
> "You got to go talk to people. You got to be uncomfortable. You got to put yourself in awkward situations. You have to follow up to the point of annoyance." — Chris Koerner (~4:35)

**Prefer labor-intensive outreach to an untested shortcut** (customer_acquisition)
1. Compare the results of software-sent cold emails with messages written by hand.
2. Track deliverability and response outcomes before scaling the automated approach.
3. Treat the speaker's preference for manual email as an anecdote, not a measured result.
> "it's not as effective as literally just typing out cold emails to people by hand." — Chris Koerner (~9:25)

**Run small tests and revisit old winners** (validation_testing)
1. Test one change at a time, such as a subject line or offer.
2. Keep a stable, effective version while testing alternatives.
3. Revisit old tests when the audience or conditions have changed.
> "And so to this day, I do that, but I haven't tested it in over a year." — Chris Koerner (~12:43)

**Look for high-leverage customers** (customer_acquisition)
1. Compare the work needed to sign a small account with the work needed for a larger account.
2. When a larger customer is within reach, estimate the added locations and overhead before prioritizing it.
3. Do not assume large customers can be sourced on demand; Chris says there are power laws at play.
> "And it's taking just as much work, if not less, to sign that customer than it's taken to sign our customers with single locations." — Chris Koerner (~13:35)

**Review content performance and focus on outliers** (operations)
1. Track which videos drive followers rather than judging only by total publishing volume.
2. Identify the small group of videos responsible for most audience growth.
3. Study the outliers for repeatable themes while recognizing that results may not generalize.
> "I've put out about 600 short form videos over the last year, and 90% of my followers have come from 2% of those." — Chris Koerner (~14:05)

**Balance optimization with preserving a working baseline** (operations)
1. Record the settings and results of a stable campaign before changing it.
2. Test changes in a controlled way instead of changing multiple variables at once.
3. Stop testing when the cost of further experimentation outweighs likely improvement.
> "You can, it is very easy to overoptimize anything." — Chris Koerner (~11:32)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Build a business on sales and real value rather than a polished appearance or a large team without revenue.
- *mindset_execution*: Do the uncomfortable customer-facing work and follow up persistently.
- *customer_acquisition*: Shortcuts can deliver quick results, but their effectiveness may fade; durable practices often require more effort.
- *validation_testing*: Measure performance, preserve a working baseline, and keep testing when conditions change.
- *operations*: Avoid both analysis paralysis and assuming that a once-successful tactic will keep working forever.
- *scaling*: A small share of projects or content may account for most outcomes, so inspect the outliers and learn from them.
- *mindset_execution*: Look toward the path you want to follow instead of fixating on obstacles.
- *operations*: Notice hidden sources of drag in a business, including site speed, company culture, or an overlooked setting.
- *mindset_execution*: Choose to help repair other people's mistakes rather than respond first with blame, and show gratitude for help that goes unseen.
