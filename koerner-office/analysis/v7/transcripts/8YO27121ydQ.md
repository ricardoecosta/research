### I1. Garage furniture flipping

- **Customer:** Local buyers: people moving, first apartments, new homeowners
- **Problem:** Want attractive affordable furniture
- **Offer:** Refinished solid-wood dressers, tables, nightstands, credenzas
- **Business model:** Buy low, refinish, resell per piece
- **Pricing:** per piece. tall boy dresser USD 325-425 per piece; long dresser USD 475-525 per piece Rule: buy at half of retail or less; skip pieces that won't clear double the all-in cost.
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: Daylight driveway photos listed on Facebook Marketplace; relist weekend mornings.; facebook_groups_communities: Source via 20+ local buy-and-sell groups and curb finds.
- **Delivery:** Owner refinishes each piece (2-3 hours basic) in the garage.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Buy $40-120, sell $325-525; Nightstands net $40-110, dressers $100-250, dining sets $120-320, credenzas $200-450; 5-10 pieces/week = $2-5k/month
- **Tools:** Facebook Marketplace, Penny Hoarder, Commonplace
- **Startup cost:** $100-150; then $20-60 per piece for paint and hardware
- **Time to first revenue:** First flip; e.g. $65 table to $300
- **UK applicability [E]:** 5/5 — Facebook Marketplace and furniture upcycling are popular in the UK.
- **Risks:** Pieces may not sell; seasonal slowdown in winter; Labour-intensive, does not scale; Particle board pieces sell poorly
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Listings can be drafted and posted by agents; sourcing needs a human eye.); onboarding 3/5 (Buyer messaging can be agent-handled.); delivery 0/5 (Physical refinishing.); billing 3/5 (Cash/payment at pickup.); support 2/5 (Pickup coordination.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Furniture flip buy rule** (pricing)
1. Only buy solid wood
2. Total paint, hardware and hours before buying
3. Walk away unless sale clears double the all-in cost
4. Buy at half of retail or less
5. Join 20+ local buy-and-sell groups
6. Photograph in daylight on driveway; relist weekend mornings

**Restaurant knife account demo** (acquisition)
1. Practise on own knives 2 weeks
2. Visit restaurants at ~3pm quiet hours
3. Slice paper in the air with a sharpened knife
4. Batch 10-12 knives per session
5. Set a pickup minimum

**Drop-off ticket system** (operations)
1. Numbered ticket for every drop-off with name, phone, count and pickup date
2. Rule: no ticket, no tool touches it
> "No ticket, no tool touches it" — Narrator (~10:11)

**Boat wrap walkaround before quoting** (operations)
1. Measure the boat
2. Check support frame to avoid pooled water callbacks
3. Price per foot

**Pick a garage business with three filters** (mindset)
1. Fits your bay and power
2. Busy season starts within 60 days
3. First 3 customers reachable where buyers already look (Marketplace, Nextdoor, marina board)
4. Measure bay, count outlets, check 3 local shops' prices

**Cross-sell seasonal services** (sales)
1. Wrap boat in October
2. Winterise mower and wake snowblower same visit

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: A home garage is a business asset; you don't need a storefront.
- *operations*: Batching jobs dramatically increases hourly earnings.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Buy in the off season, sell in peak season (bikes: buy winter, sell spring).
- *customer_acquisition*: Overflow from backlogged established shops is a ready source of customers.
- *scaling*: Stack seasonal services so the asset is used year-round.
- *validation_testing*: Size the pond: you only need a tiny slice of local demand.
- *pricing*: Price by condition and hours, not flat rates.
- *validation_testing*: Learn your local market by checking what three shops charge.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Buying furniture first and counting costs later loses money.
- Store fuel outside the bay and drain tanks; insurance and fire code care.
- Check city home-occupation rules before running a garage business.
- Mixed-up customer items cost time and trust; use tickets.
- Pricing every detailing job the same is a mistake.
- Propane safety and liability insurance are required for shrink wrapping.
