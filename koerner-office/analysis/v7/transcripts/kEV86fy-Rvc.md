## Summary

Chris Koerner flies in Shannon, an experienced liquidation reseller, to teach him to buy Costco customer-return pallets via the B-Stock auction platform and resell them on Facebook Marketplace. They bid on two lots (18 pallets of appliances at about $243/unit and a 12-unit patio/shed/gazebo lot at about $222/unit landed) and then test, photograph, list and sell the items. Chris aimed for $10K profit in 10 days but took 12.5 days; exact final totals are not stated in the transcript. Key lessons: buy at a very low per-unit cost, list the best items first for momentum, respond fast, and cut prices when listings get no hits. This is hands-on physical work, not hands-off.

### I1. Costco return pallet flipping via B-Stock auctions

- **Customer:** Local consumers on Facebook Marketplace looking for discounted appliances, patio furniture, grills and sheds
- **Problem:** Consumers want big-ticket items cheaper than retail; Costco needs to offload returns and excess stock
- **Offer:** Brand new or like-new appliances and outdoor items at roughly 50-70% of retail with local pickup or free delivery nearby and a 14-30 day goodwill support promise
- **Business model:** One-off resale margin on liquidation goods
- **Pricing:** Price at about 50-70% of retail; cut price if no hits; set an aggressive price and explain why for flawed items. LG stackable washer (retail ~1,300) USD 879 per unit; LG Styler garment steamer (retail ~1,000) USD 767 per unit; Patio furniture set (Costco ~1,500) USD 900 per set; Scratch-and-dent black Styler after negotiation USD 850 per unit Shannon suggests worst-case comps from Facebook Marketplace and sells at 50-70% of retail; first-day prices may be underpriced.
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: Facebook Marketplace listings with video first, up to 12 photos showing positives and flaws, ChatGPT-written SEO titles and descriptions, a personal credibility line, model number, colour and condition, and free delivery within 5-10 miles. Shannon says Whatnot is good for food.
- **Delivery:** Owner personally receives pallets in a garage or shop, cuts them open, tests electrics, cleans, photographs, lists, answers Messenger chats, and hands over locally or delivers; heavy items are moved by hand, with help from friends.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Lot 1 cost about $243 per unit landed against Costco price of about $1,150 per unit; Lot 2 cost about $2,600 for 12 units (about $222 per unit incl. freight) with $13,000 retail value; Shannon says tripling money is conservative and 2x is worst case; Chris took 12.5 days instead of 10 to reach the goal; totals not stated
- **Tools:** B-Stock, Facebook Marketplace, ChatGPT, Whatnot, Venmo, text expander keyboard shortcuts, Amazon (replacement parts), 10x10 storage unit
- **Startup cost:** Credit card or net-30 terms; the two lots cost roughly $5,600 plus about $130/month for a storage unit if needed
- **Time to first revenue:** Within a few days of the pallets arriving; first sale was within a day or so of listing
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — UK has equivalents (B-Stock UK, Costco UK liquidation pallets, Facebook Marketplace, eBay, Gumtree), but the Costco returns lots on B-Stock are US-centred. UK Consumer Rights Act applies to sales of used goods by traders, and sellers who resell regularly are trading, so need HMRC registration; electrical goods need PAT or safety care. Garage space and UK-sized appliances differ from the US.
- **Risks:** Condition grade uncertainty: some items arrive broken, missing parts or damaged; Heavy, bulky stock needs space, vehicles and manual labour; Capital tied up and storage costs if items do not sell; Buyers ghost or lowball; Items like sheds may not sell (the shed went unsold); Speaker-promoted course and mastermind may bias claims

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Check worst-case resale comps before bidding** (sales)
1. Look up the item on Facebook Marketplace within about 80 miles of your area
2. Use the conservative figure and aim to get your investment back fast
3. Target a landed price of about $200-250 per unit for mixed big-ticket lots
> "You want to look like, okay, what's worst case scenario." — Shannon (~4:50)

**Choose lots by manifest and condition grade** (operations)
1. Read the manifest for unit count, retail prices and low average bid per unit
2. Prefer 'used good' returns and new; avoid 'used fair' and 'mixed'
3. Look for photos showing items still in boxes with factory stickers
4. Prefer categories with demand and barriers such as appliances, outdoor gear and Blackstone grills

**List the best items first for momentum** (mindset)
1. Test and list the best items on arrival day
2. Defer fixes like broken doors and cleaning
3. Get an early sale to reinforce motivation and recover capital
> "I like to say focus is overrated. momentum is underrated." — Chris Koerner (~19:38)

**High-converting Facebook Marketplace listing** (acquisition)
1. Put a 60-second walk-around video first
2. Use all available photo slots including flaws and install kit
3. Have ChatGPT write an SEO title and description with model number and colour
4. Add a personal credibility sentence
5. State the retail price and link to it
6. Offer free delivery within 5-10 miles
7. Create separate listings per item or colour

**Messenger response and negotiation** (sales)
1. Reply fast with a call to action, such as a specific pickup time
2. Use text expander templates ending with availability
3. Say you have other inquiries and will follow up Monday
4. Offer a small discount for same-day pickup or delivery
5. If no hits after correct keywords and category, boost then cut price
> "Always end the templated thing or the message with, "Okay, I'm available Saturday from 10:00 to 1." — Shannon (~29:34)

**Frame flawed items honestly** (pricing)
1. Disclose scratches, dents and missing parts clearly
2. Say it is priced aggressively because of the flaw and link to retail price
3. Keep a complete item next to the flawed one when selling in person

## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: Buy at a very low per-unit price relative to worst-case resale so a single sale can return the investment.
- *mindset_execution*: Momentum beats perfection: list the easy winners first rather than getting stuck fixing problem items.
- *customer_acquisition*: Speed of response and willingness to cut prices matter more than perfecting listings.
- *offer_design*: Honest framing of flaws increases buyer trust and conversion.
- *operations*: Hands-off this is not; the business is physical problem-solving and logistics.
- *customer_acquisition*: Local reputation and a goodwill return window can differentiate you and create referrals.
- *validation_testing*: Test unfamiliar niches with a modest lot first; surprising items like garment steamers sold fastest.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Carrying costs such as storage mean slow stock should be discounted or cleared rather than held.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Avoid 'used fair' and 'mixed' grades; mixed can be anything and salvage can be mostly poor.
- Customer returns are not tested in the warehouse, so expect at least one defective unit in a load.
- Procrastinating on listing and slow responses delayed Chris's goal beyond 10 days.
- Underpricing at first can signal that you priced too low; new sellers often panic-discount.
- Assembled sheds are unattractive because of the many screws and the shed did not sell.
