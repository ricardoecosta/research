## Tactics and playbook steps

**Find cult local brands with no online store** (product)
1. Run a deep-research prompt to list beloved regional restaurants/attractions (Lambert's, Big Texan type)
2. Check whether they sell merch online
3. Either resell their goods (buy at retail) or pitch a licensing deal where you handle print-on-demand and fulfilment
> "Someone needs to run a deep research prompt and find all of the the throat rolls, the Lambert's cafes, the big Texan, all of those of the world" — Chris Koerner (~10:30)

**Borrowed-supply service brokerage (goats)** (operations)
1. Search Facebook Marketplace for goat farms within ~90 miles
2. Arrange to borrow goats and the farmer's animal trailer

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Look for proven single-location businesses and replicate the model in other markets; experiences and middleman services travel well.
- *offer_design*: Middleman models let you sell a service without owning the supply: borrow assets from people who already have them.
- *pricing*: Price against the customer's real alternative: goat grazing at $1,000 an acre versus $28,000 manual clearing leaves room for a middleman.
- *customer_acquisition*: Many exciting businesses are badly under-marketed; consistent unedited content alone could grow them.
- *customer_acquisition*: Paid shout-outs and sponsored donations on huge creators can be very cheap media on a CPM basis.
- *mindset_execution*: Reframe a perceived curse (fame, attention) as an asset to monetise.
- *customer_acquisition*: Small businesses abandon channels because they aren't desperate enough; good is the enemy of great.
- *customer_acquisition*: For home services, Google captures hot intent while Facebook works for awareness and retargeting, needing about seven touches.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Pre-selling bookings before you have the asset free gives a negative cash conversion cycle.
- *operations*: Have plan A/B/C for any time-boxed launch because outside factors (weather, partners) fail.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Government/municipal contracts (e.g. goat grazing for a city) need competitive bidding and have long sales cycles; target private landowners instead.
- Copycat ideas often fail on the details: reselling Wawa or Trader Joe's like Buc-ee's didn't work for Chris.
- Don't judge an ad channel from one failed agency test; owners who don't understand the channel can't hold agencies accountable.
- Experiences involving automatic weapons are heavily regulated; checking federal and state rules is required.
- Destination stores with captive demand may not offer bargains; Unclaimed Baggage was pricier than a thrift store.
- Cold text outreach (Outscraper lists) mentioned as a tactic would breach PECR in the UK when targeting individuals or sole traders without consent.
