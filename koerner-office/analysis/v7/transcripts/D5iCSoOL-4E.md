## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate demand before committing to a product** (validation_testing)
1. Read marketplace reviews to find repeated customer complaints and requests.
2. Compare competitors' product, price, customer service, and marketing.
3. Use a small test or dropshipped product to learn whether customers respond before making a large inventory bet.
4. Treat customer feedback as product input rather than relying only on industry trend reports.
> "go validate please on Amazon and come back to me with something really tangible and then I'll feel much better about investing into product." — Ryan Bartlett (~20:01)

**Narrow down dropshipping tests** (product)
1. Test a range of products.
2. Identify repeated winners, such as the fidget spinner Bartlett described.
3. Build a focused website around a winning product rather than continuing to sell an unfocused mix.
> "Test everything, find your winners, scale the winners." — Ryan Bartlett (~21:25)

**Solve one clear apparel problem** (offer_design)
1. Choose a specific customer and product problem.
2. For True Classic, focus on fit, price, and comfort.
3. Make the product itself demonstrate the benefit, such as showing how the fit changes the wearer's appearance.
> "Once I like figured out the three value props that we kind of live and die by which is like just fit, price and comfort." — Ryan Bartlett (~33:51)

**Iterate around a winning product** (product)
1. Keep improving the product customers already buy.
2. Test variations such as curved hems, different fits, and inclusive sizes.
3. Delay unrelated product categories until the core product and its variations are strong.
> "lean into what works and then do all your iterations against that winner." — Ryan Bartlett (~30:21)

**Use gift with purchase to introduce adjacent products** (pricing)
1. Choose a high-quality product from a related category, such as activewear or underwear.
2. Add it as a gift during checkout and use order thresholds for shipping or additional gifts.
3. Measure average order value and whether recipients later buy from the new category.
4. Do not use unwanted overstock as the gift if it will disappoint customers.
> "I did it specifically. Well, number one, boost AOV. That's like the obvious thing, right? Like average order value." — Ryan Bartlett (~8:47)

**Scale ads with a clear performance rule** (acquisition)
1. Start with a manageable ad budget and increase it when results support the increase.
2. Bartlett described going from $100 per day to $300 per day within about a week to a week and a half.
3. If performance collapses, pull spend back rather than continuing blindly.
4. Compare platform-attributed returns with the full profit and loss statement.
> "Now if I push and I look tomorrow and rorowaz is like 0.2 two I'll pull back, right? Like that's how I thought about it." — Ryan Bartlett (~31:41)

**Build retention before relying on acquisition cash** (operations)
1. Set up welcome, abandoned-cart, and post-purchase email flows.
2. Use post-purchase upsells to introduce related products.
3. Track whether customers return and buy across categories.
4. Reinvest cash when the business can afford to do so.
> "So that comes back to like Clavio, email flows, post-purchase upsells, and abandoned cart flows, welcome flows, optimizing the crap out of all of that." — Ryan Bartlett (~53:01)

**Diversify channels after finding product-market fit** (scaling)
1. Use the early paid channel to learn, but avoid becoming dependent on it.
2. Develop wholesale and retail relationships so the business can reach people who distrust online-only brands.
3. Use retail presence to build credibility as well as immediate sales.
> "diversifying sooner than later is incredibly important to the success of your brand." — Ryan Bartlett (~25:56)

**Use a 3PL from the start** (operations)
1. Outsource physical fulfillment when the founders cannot reliably handle it themselves.
2. Review 3PL software and order-routing behavior.
3. Monitor split shipments and shipping charges because errors can erase margins.
> "We knew like there was no way we were going to be able to tell actually ourselves." — Ryan Bartlett (~52:01)

**Negotiate cash-flow terms with suppliers** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Explain the business's potential to manufacturers and ask them to invest through credit terms.
2. Ask for a defined credit amount and 60- or 90-day terms.
3. Talk to multiple potential partners because many may decline before one agrees.
4. Review supplier and software contracts again as volume grows.
> "our ask was, Give me 20,000 in credit, right? Give me terms on that credit. Give me 60 days. Give me 90 days, whatever it is." — Ryan Bartlett (~53:51)

**Make comedy ads explain the product** (acquisition)
1. Work with people who can make genuinely funny creative.
2. Show a clear before-and-after product reveal.
3. Include the key product benefit and related categories in the script.
4. Test iterations and keep the elements that improve results.
> "if you really break down that ad, one of the biggest components that you'll notice is that there's this reveal moment" — Ryan Bartlett (~67:27)

**Check marketing attribution with post-purchase surveys** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Ask customers where they heard about the brand after purchase.
2. Compare survey responses with platform-reported performance.
3. If a channel receives no survey credit and is not showing up in sales, consider stopping spend.
> "If it's not showing up in the post-purchase survey, guess what? It's not netting out." — Ryan Bartlett (~62:11)

**Use a small free project to prove agency value** (customer_acquisition)
1. Find e-commerce owners in a relevant Facebook group.
2. Offer to review or improve their email flows for free.
3. Request read-only access and do not send or publish changes without approval.
4. Document results as case studies before pitching a retainer.
> "Let me mess with your flows. Let me see if I can get your revenue per recipient up." — Chris Koerner (~82:33)

**Sell agencies on attributable impact** (sales)
1. Tie the offer to a measurable business result.
2. Show the client work or data that supports the proposal.
3. Avoid sending generic decks that list impressions or contacts without explaining the impact.
4. Use a flat fee when starting if that is easier to explain and deliver.
> "If you can't do that, you're going to be fighting uphill the entire time." — Chris Koerner (~80:35)

**Prioritize impact over busy work** (operations)
1. Learn which outcomes matter to the business before choosing projects.
2. Put roughly 80% of effort toward high-impact work and 20% toward routine work, as Bartlett advised.
3. Use AI and agents to reduce repetitive tasks so people can focus on higher-impact work.
> "80% of your energy towards impactful things for the business, 20% to busy work and learn AI, learn a AI agents" — Ryan Bartlett (~90:49)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Start from a customer problem and validate it through customer feedback, reviews, and product tests rather than building only around a founder's preferred brand idea.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Good product-market fit and repeat purchasing can matter more than profitable first-order acquisition.
- *niche_selection*: Improve the winning offer and its variations before scattering effort across unrelated products.
- *offer_design*: Customer feedback can reveal the emotional value customers receive, which may be more compelling than the product's basic function.
- *offer_design*: Keep adding genuine value across product categories; a new category should meet the same standard as the original product.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Measure marketing with multiple sources, including post-purchase questions and full business economics, rather than relying only on ad-platform attribution.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Negotiate supplier and software contracts because small cost improvements can matter greatly in a low-margin business.
- *customer_acquisition*: A service business can be a strong way to learn sales and build recurring revenue, but the founder must be able to listen and explain value.
- *offer_design*: Specific, provable impact makes a service offer easier to buy than vague promises.
- *mindset_execution*: Treat earlier experiments and failures as skill-building when they teach transferable skills such as research, marketing, logistics, and customer service.
- *automation_ai*: Focus effort on work that changes business outcomes and use AI tools to reduce routine work.
- *mindset_execution*: A small idea from a junior person can become valuable when leaders investigate it and act on the customer need behind it.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not expand into unrelated product categories just to raise sales; Bartlett said brands often fail to give new products the same attention as the original winner.
- A business that depends too heavily on one paid channel can be vulnerable to platform changes or a loss of attribution.
- Retail stores can appear unprofitable if their influence on later online purchases is not attributed to the store.
- The guest described 3PL software failures that split shipments and doubled shipping charges.
- Do not assume platform-attributed ROAS represents full business profitability; operating expenses and other P&L items also matter.
- Comedy ads can be difficult to create, and Bartlett said ads without a product reveal performed poorly for them.
- The guest said generic partnership proposals based on impressions or logos do not persuade him; proposals should explain customer impact.
