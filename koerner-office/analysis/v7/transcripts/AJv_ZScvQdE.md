## Summary

Tim Richard lists seven beginner-friendly side hustles he says he has tried or knows about: ShareTown furniture-return resale, Amazon retail arbitrage, Craigslist flipping, finding contract gigs on Indeed, Etsy print-on-demand with Printful and Canva, building a niche content following, and home services. The home services business (starting with junk removal, later outsourced to contractors) is his own and the one he favours, with claimed earnings of $3,000+ a day and seven-figure annual profit. Most other ideas are described in general terms with clips of other people's earnings. The key takeaway is that low-barrier, local, hands-on work like flyering for home services is the easiest start, though it is the least automatable.

### I1. ShareTown furniture return pickup and resale

- **Customer:** Local buyers on Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist and OfferUp; supply comes from online furniture companies via ShareTown
- **Problem:** Online furniture/mattress returns can't be repacked and are costly for companies to handle
- **Offer:** Local rep collects returns, stores them at home, lists them using ShareTown-supplied photos and descriptions, and sells them
- **Business model:** Revenue split on resale of returned furniture with ShareTown
- **Pricing:** Split of final sale price with ShareTown. typical online mattress sale price USD 300-700 per mattress sale price Split percentage not stated.
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: Post items on Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist and OfferUp using images and descriptions provided by ShareTown.
- **Delivery:** Rep picks up items with a pickup truck or SUV, stores them at home, and hands over to buyers.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Testimonials of $3-4k/month; One day of about $800 (testimonial clip)
- **Tools:** ShareTown, Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, OfferUp
- **Startup cost:** not stated; needs a pickup truck or SUV
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 1/5 — ShareTown is a US service and the marketplaces named are mostly US; no UK equivalent is stated.
- **Risks:** US-only program; Bulky physical handling and storage; Income figures come from testimonials
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 2/5 (Listings are pre-supplied but must be posted on marketplaces and buyer messages handled.); onboarding 3/5 (Online form and proof of vehicle.); delivery 0/5 (Physical pickup, storage and handover.); billing 3/5 (Platform handles the revenue split, though payouts are not described.); support 1/5 (Buyer questions and no-shows need a human.)
> "ShareTown hires reps in each town to come pick up these basically brand new furniture returns and bring them back to their house." — Tim Richard (~1:21)

### I2. Amazon retail arbitrage

- **Customer:** Amazon shoppers
- **Problem:** Price gaps between clearance retail and Amazon
- **Offer:** Brand-name products at Amazon prices sourced from store clearance
- **Business model:** Buy low in-store, resell higher on Amazon
- **Pricing:** Resale margin. example in-store price USD 5 per unit buy price; example Amazon price USD 29-30 per unit sell price Illustrative example only.
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: Sell on Amazon; no separate marketing.
- **Delivery:** Buy stock in stores, package at home and ship into Amazon.
- **Unit economics (claims):** $2,000 profit off one shipment (clip); Sold almost $5 million of name brand products (clip); Some make six figures full-time
- **Tools:** Amazon barcode scanning app, Amazon, TJ Maxx, Walmart clearance, Ross, Marshalls
- **Startup cost:** not stated; inventory purchase
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Works with UK stores such as TK Maxx and Amazon UK; margins and fees differ.
- **Risks:** Inventory risk; Brand gating and Amazon restrictions; Fee erosion
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 4/5 (Amazon marketplace supplies demand.); onboarding 3/5 (Seller account setup.); delivery 0/5 (Store sourcing, packing and shipping are physical.); billing 5/5 (Amazon pays out.); support 4/5 (Amazon handles customer service for FBA.)
> "You could buy a hundred cell phone cases that are selling on clearance at $5. When you scan them, you see on Amazon they're selling for $29." — Tim Richard (~2:46)

- **Unit economics (claims):** $1,000-2,000 a week flipping couches; A camera flipper making well into six figures a year
- **Tools:** Craigslist
- **Startup cost:** not stated

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Personalised flyer door drop** (acquisition)
1. Print a flyer with your face, a simple logo and a few services, leaving blank lines
2. Handwrite a personalised note for each house with a service and price
3. Hand out 100-200 flyers around your complex or neighbourhood
4. Ask people to text or call
> "Go around every apartment in your apartment complex or every house in your neighborhood and write a personalized note to each house." — Tim Richard (~8:51)

**Genuine local Facebook community post** (acquisition)
1. Post in your local community page that you are starting a business
2. List the services you offer
3. Avoid spammy promotion because many groups ban business posts

**Outsource to contractors to scale** (operations)
1. Start by doing the work yourself
2. Once demand grows, contract the work out
3. Keep no employees and contract all work

**Scan clearance products to find arbitrage** (sourcing)
1. Install an Amazon barcode scanner app
2. Scan marked-down products in discount stores
3. Buy where the Amazon price is much higher
4. Package and ship to Amazon
5. Track which stores and products perform best

**Etsy niche validation by review counts** (product)
1. Find a niche on Etsy with listings that have many reviews
2. Create a differentiated design in Canva
3. Upload it to Printful products linked to your Etsy store

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Boring, traditional local service businesses are accessible to beginners and can be very profitable, as competitors are complacent about answering phones.
- *customer_acquisition*: Personalisation in outreach (a face and a handwritten note) lifts response versus generic marketing.
- *scaling*: A service business can scale without employees by subcontracting delivery once demand exists.
- *operations*: Arbitrage and flipping work through sourcing knowledge; experience shows which stores and items perform.
- *offer_design*: Print-on-demand removes inventory risk, but niche differentiation is still needed.
- *mindset_execution*: Long-game content has a delayed payoff but many monetisation paths later.
- *customer_acquisition*: Local entrepreneurs often win trust over big businesses that customers fear will overcharge.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Success is not guaranteed; these are the host's opinions and some businesses fail.
- Don't spam local Facebook groups, since many ban business posts.
- Content monetisation is slow and not for people who need money immediately.
