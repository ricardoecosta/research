## Tactics and playbook steps

**Price physical prospect gifts against customer value** (pricing)
1. Target prospects where a new customer is worth enough to justify an expensive gift.
2. Estimate the full cost per booked meeting and per acquired customer using actual conversion data.
3. Compare acquisition cost with customer value before scaling.
4. Use a personalized message to make the gift relevant to the recipient.

**Sell recurring employee recognition at company level** (sales)
1. Pitch HR teams rather than selling one cake at a time.
2. Offer recurring birthday recognition so each employee birthday becomes a scheduled order.
3. Explore a company-wide agreement covering employees, partners or shareholders.

**Use marketplaces and wholesale to lower acquisition costs** (acquisition)
1. List products where customers already search, such as Amazon.
2. Approach independent retailers for wholesale relationships.
3. Use a wholesale marketplace such as Faire as a discovery page and sell sheet.

**Keep physical product SKUs simple** (operations)
1. Limit the number of core products and variants.
2. Use multipacks to raise order size without adding flavours.
3. Check how sizes or other variants create uneven inventory and unsold stock before expanding.

**Sell matchmaking as time saved** (pricing)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: A physical, personalized approach can stand out when prospects are overwhelmed by generic digital outreach, but calculate its cost per meeting and customer.
- *scaling*: Sell to organizations when one company relationship can lead to many individual orders or customers.
- *niche_selection*: A good side hustle can support the founder's main business by creating an on-ramp, leads or customer relationships.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Recurring consumables can create repeat revenue, especially when customers keep buying the same simple product.
- *operations*: Keep physical product ranges narrow because variants and inventory can tie up cash and reduce profit.
- *niche_selection*: Look for valuable, painful problems and customers with enough budget; a narrow high-value B2B problem may have better economics than a crowded low-ticket market.
- *mindset_execution*: A physical service can be a practical side hustle when equipment is affordable and the work can be sold locally, but it still depends on a person doing the job.
- *offer_design*: For AI services, lead with the costly business problem and a measurable promise, not a list of tools.
- *automation_ai*: Implementation can be more valuable than generic advice when customers already need someone to put a working system in place.
- *customer_acquisition*: Partnerships work best when the partner has used and believes in the product being presented to their audience.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Treat event marketing as a portfolio: track the cost and outcome of each event, then repeat the strongest opportunities.
- *mindset_execution*: Choose how to live and run a business deliberately rather than assuming there is only one acceptable path.
