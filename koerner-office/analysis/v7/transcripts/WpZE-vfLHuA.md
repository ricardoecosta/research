## Tactics and playbook steps

**Give the model context before asking for a plan** (validation_testing)
1. Ask the model to ask targeted questions before it recommends a business.
2. Answer questions about skills, comfort with sales, location, experience, available resources and constraints.
3. Tell the model to produce specific steps and avoid vague recommendations.
> "Ask it to ask you the right questions that it needs to get the most context that it needs to give you the best output." — Chris Koerner (~1:39)

**Use a small automation project as an entry offer** (offer_design)
1. Choose one painful task that is easy to explain and automate.
2. Use a small project such as transferring web-form leads into a CRM or spreadsheet.
3. Demonstrate value before proposing a larger operational project.
> "The initial offer will focus on solving one specific, highly painful, and easily automated task for the client." — Chris Koerner (~17:06)

**Build credibility with early demonstrations and real proof** (sales)
1. Create working demos before broad outreach.
2. Offer a limited number of free beta setups in exchange for video testimonials.
3. Document completed work as case studies and seek one established contractor as a showcase client.
4. Do not present fictional case studies as actual customer results.
> "The beta tester play is offer three businesses free setup in exchange for video testimonials." — Chris Koerner (~11:05)

**Position services around the outcome** (offer_design)
1. Use a value-focused profile title rather than a tool-specific title.
2. Lead the first lines with the business problem and the result.
3. Describe time or money saved instead of talking only about technology.
> "Do not use the title Zapier developer. Use a valueoriented title like productivity consultant. I automate repetitive tasks for service-based businesses." — Chris Koerner (~17:55)

**Start customer outreach early and track activity** (customer_acquisition)
1. Claude's plan starts outreach on day one.
2. Track messages, engaged businesses, demos and prospects in a Google Sheet.
3. Treat the model's day-by-day targets as targets to test, not as proven conversion benchmarks.
> "Day two is complete when 30 plus outreach messages sent, 10 plus businesses engaged, five plus demos scheduled." — Chris Koerner (~11:57)

**Compare model outputs with consistent criteria** (validation_testing)
1. Give each model the same prompt and relevant context.
2. Compare usefulness, clarity, likely feasibility and answer length.
3. Treat the host's scores as subjective, and test recommendations with customers before relying on them.
> "I'm going to judge these by overall, which is just like a gut feel, and then density of quality, how many words, how likely is it to work" — Chris Koerner (~18:57)

**Verify time-sensitive information before acting** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Check current prices and market data independently before placing a trade.
2. Compare the model's suggested strike and premium with the live order ticket.
3. Do not treat a model's trading recommendation as reliable just because it gives a confident explanation.
> "The price was outdated. It was using an outdated price because the current share price is 5.56." — Chris Koerner (~28:28)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Give a model relevant context about your skills, constraints and preferences, and ask it to clarify before recommending a plan.
- *other*: A detailed prompt can produce more actionable steps, but a longer answer is not automatically better.
- *customer_acquisition*: Start customer outreach early instead of spending the whole launch window learning and preparing.
- *offer_design*: A small project that fixes one clear, painful task can be an easier first sale than a broad transformation proposal.
- *other*: Explain the value in customer terms such as time and money saved, and avoid promising results that have not been demonstrated.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use real demonstrations, testimonials and documented client results to build credibility.
- *niche_selection*: Do not assume that a new technology is a must-have for every small business; test whether a specific customer will pay for it.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Check live, primary information before acting on an AI response when prices or conditions can change quickly.
- *legal_risk*: Confident AI explanations do not remove the need to understand the downside of a decision.
- *validation_testing*: Treat a one-day investment result as a single observation, not proof that a strategy works.
