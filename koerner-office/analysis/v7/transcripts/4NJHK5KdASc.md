## Tactics and playbook steps

**Spray-and-pray Marketplace listings** (acquisition)
1. Create many listings; each costs nothing without ads
2. Use ChatGPT to generate listing ideas and vary wording (change one word)
3. Vary price across listings ($25/$32/$40 per space) to test demand
4. Double down on the listing that works
> "It doesn't cost you a thing to make a listing. So, make as many as you can. Use chat GPT to come up with ideas" — Alex Nelson (~21:53)

**Boost listings in competitive markets** (acquisition)
1. Post listing organically
2. Put ~$20 behind it as a boost when competitors crowd search
3. Higher visibility lets you hold price against undercutters
> "the more competing listings there are, the more you're boosting uh can make a difference. It can put you at the top of the search results" — Chris Koerner (~7:00)

**Before/after photo listings** (acquisition)
1. Photograph stacked messy totes vs organised shelf side by side
2. Let photos sell; video not needed early

**Messenger qualification then in-person measure** (sales)
1. Send price and photos
2. Ask customer to send pictures of their space to look expert
3. Offer to come measure; in-person visits close 100%
> "what's your space look like? Send me pictures and kind of create that professionalism there and let them know that I'm the expert" — Alex Nelson (~27:22)

**Paint the emotional picture** (sales)
1. Tell prospects how they will feel when the job is done
2. Anticipate emotional reactions (land clearing customers cry) and state it upfront

**Turn a non-recurring product into recurring/leveraged revenue** (product)
1. Sell plans and a course
2. Route excess leads to installer network on 70/30 split
3. Paid Pro tier with territory exclusivity
4. Upsell organising/cleanup services to past customers

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Listings are free, so volume testing of many listing variants is the cheapest validation.
- *customer_acquisition*: In crowded Marketplace categories, small paid boosts can restore visibility and let you keep higher prices.
- *niche_selection*: A winning product can usually be replicated with a different product using the same distribution playbook.
- *customer_acquisition*: Getting into the customer's space in person drives very high close rates for physical services.
- *scaling*: For a one-off product, add leverage: plans, courses, installer networks, and services to past customers.
- *validation_testing*: Customer feedback on early listings (too expensive, other versions) tells you what to build next.
- *pricing*: Price perception varies by customer; some say it's cheaper than expected, so test higher prices.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Time-cost your craft: if hourly earnings are below a job's wage, speed up or change product.
- *customer_acquisition*: You can monetise competitors by making them referral partners instead of fighting them.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Boosting a listing mainly amplified to his own friend network; ads reach outsiders better.
- Shelving is a one-off purchase with no repeat customers, so growth stalls without new offers.
- Competitors now undercut on price.
- Low-value crafts may pay less than minimum wage once time is counted.
- Facebook does not like service listings on Marketplace.
