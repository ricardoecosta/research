## Tactics and playbook steps

**Communicate a clear vision at scale** (customer_acquisition)
1. State what the work is trying to achieve in a way other people can understand.
2. Use a story, analogy, writing, or public speaking to make the value clear.
3. Choose the communication medium that fits your strengths and repeat the message.
> "You don't become great by working hard. You become great by getting other people to work hard." — Ben Wilson (~2:05)

**Start with a small test and iterate** (validation_testing)
1. Find the smallest practical action that puts a product or prototype in front of reality.
2. Test it and use what happens to choose the next step.
3. Repeat the cycle before spending heavily on a complete plan.
> "And their step one is just build something, get in the air and their approach wins and and no one else is even close, right?" — Ben Wilson (~25:46)

**Focus serially, then move on when the opportunity is wrong** (niche_selection)
1. Give one opportunity focused effort rather than dabbling across many.
2. Look for evidence that the timing and opportunity fit.
3. If it is not working or does not fit, leave it behind and focus on the next opportunity.
> "So a lot of times people think oh well I don't want to focus on the wrong thing so I need to dabble in a lot of things but when you dabble in a lot of things you never really figure out any of them." — Ben Wilson (~32:11)

**Use a failure goal to make rejection part of the process** (mindset)
1. Set a daily rejection target when outreach is part of the work.
2. Treat a rejection as progress toward the target instead of as a signal to stop immediately.
3. Use the target to build tolerance for repeated rejection; do not assume the stated 20-to-one ratio applies to every market.
> "I set a failure goal, which was literally I need to get rejected 20 times today." — Ben Wilson (~41:36)

**Repeat the business vision to other people** (sales)
1. Start from a goal you sincerely believe in.
2. Describe that goal clearly to other people, even when it feels embarrassing.
3. Use the response to practice communicating the ambition and building conviction.
> "I think you just have to get in the habit of repeating it to others too. I think it's important to put yourself out there" — Ben Wilson (~49:56)

**Study both inspiring and weak examples** (product)
1. Find an exceptional person or business whose work makes you want to improve.
2. Notice shortcomings in weaker examples and check whether you have the same gaps.
3. Keep peers at a similar level for comparison and competition.
> "You need to have both perspectives. >> It's kind of like in raetball or like in any like one-on-one sport, you want to you want a partner that's about your level." — Chris Koerner (~47:02)

**Choose work that gives you energy** (mindset)
1. Experiment with activities instead of assuming there is only one lifelong passion.
2. Notice which work draws you back and gives you energy.
3. Use that signal when choosing where to invest sustained effort.
> "But you should follow passion. When you find something that you love, then doing it gives you energy rather than taking energy." — Ben Wilson (~0:27)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: A founder's output is limited alone; communicating clearly can help other people understand and work toward the same goal.
- *offer_design*: A useful explanation connects a product to something the audience already understands, as Jobs did with the bicycle analogy.
- *validation_testing*: Start by taking a small action that tests reality, then use what happens to decide what to do next.
- *niche_selection*: Focus on one opportunity at a time, but be prepared to change direction when the timing or fit is wrong.
- *mindset_execution*: People can have several passions; trying new activities can reveal work that gives them energy.
- *customer_acquisition*: A rejection target can help make rejection part of a deliberate process rather than an unexpected personal setback.
- *operations*: A clear vision can help people coordinate, but it should be communicated sincerely and backed by action.
- *mindset_execution*: Confidence may help a leader maintain buy-in during setbacks, but the interview's anecdotes are not evidence that confidence alone creates success.
- *validation_testing*: The right idea at the wrong time can fail; founders should pay attention to whether the opportunity fits the market now.
