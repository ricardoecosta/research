## Tactics and playbook steps

**Match objective to customer action** (offer_design)
1. Choose Messages when the goal is an inbox conversation.
2. Choose Leads to collect names and emails.
3. Choose Sales or conversions for website purchases.
4. Choose video views only when video views are the intended outcome.
> "if you want messages in your inbox you choose messages if you want to collect names and emails you choose leads" — Chris Koerner (~6:35)

**Write a specific local-service offer** (offer_design)
1. Name the painful problem the customer has now.
2. Describe the specific service and a time frame.
3. Make the next action clear, such as messaging for a free quote.
4. Use proof or concrete expectations where available.
> "you want a clear solution to a painful problem with a timeline and a path to action" — Chris Koerner (~7:14)

**Build the campaign in Ads Manager** (operations)
1. Use Meta Ads Manager rather than boosting a post.
2. Name the campaign with service, objective and date.
3. Set the objective, audience, placements and daily budget at the campaign and ad-set levels.
4. Use a single image or video, then write primary text, a short headline and an appropriate call to action.
5. Review the offer and creative before publishing.
> "always use Meta Ads Manager it gives you full control" — Chris Koerner (~8:11)

**Start with broad local targeting** (customer_acquisition)
1. Set the service area around the current city.
2. Start broad and let the platform optimize before adding interests.
3. Use manual placements if you want to limit where ads appear.
4. Compare feed and story placements only after an ad has shown results.
> "you want to start broad let the algorithm work dial it in as you go" — Chris Koerner (~5:17)

**Use a four-part ad-copy framework** (customer_acquisition)
1. Open with a hook tied to a problem or curiosity.
2. Show the customer that you understand the problem.
3. Explain the offer with specifics and a time frame.
4. Tell the reader exactly what to do next.
> "the hook the problem the offer the call to action so let's break it all down" — Chris Koerner (~19:29)

**Wait before changing a new ad** (operations)
1. Let a newly published ad run for 72 hours unless it is rejected or clearly broken.
2. Change one variable at a time so the result is interpretable.
3. Use the observed result to decide what to change next.
> "do not touch your ad for 72 hours unless it's completely broken like the wrong photo a typo or it's rejected" — Chris Koerner (~27:31)

**Test one variable at a time** (validation_testing)
1. Start with the image or video.
2. Then test the primary text and hook.
3. Next test the headline, audience and placement.
4. Keep budget even between variants, then retain the winner and stop the loser.
> "only test one variable at a time that's it one thing don't pull more than one lever at a time" — Chris Koerner (~30:01)

**Troubleshoot based on where the funnel stalls** (operations)
1. If people do not click, improve the image or hook.
2. If they click but do not message, clarify the offer and call to action.
3. If they message but do not book, respond faster and provide clear next steps.
4. If clicks are expensive, try broader targeting or a different image.
> "if people are messaging and you're not closing these deals then you got to improve your speed to respond" — Chris Koerner (~17:35)

**Retarget engaged prospects** (customer_acquisition)
1. Create a second ad for people who interacted with the original ad or visited the site but did not convert.
2. Use a more personal reminder and a clear next action.
3. Keep the retargeting budget low and let the ad run in the background.
> "this one is for warm traffic people who saw your ad or visited your site but never converted" — Chris Koerner (~32:52)

**Scale after repeatable results** (scaling)
1. Wait for consistent leads and actual customers before scaling.
2. Increase the budget gradually, checking that performance holds.
3. Alternatively, duplicate a winning ad set and compare it with the original.
4. Refresh worn-out creative before increasing spend.
> "only scale ads that are profitable getting consistent results not showing signs of fatigue" — Chris Koerner (~34:42)

**Handle inbound leads with a simple system** (operations)
1. Set an instant reply in Messenger or Instagram to confirm receipt and set expectations.
2. Track name, contact details, date contacted and lead status in a spreadsheet or CRM.
3. Write reusable replies for common questions about price, timing and scope.
4. Review and update the lead tracker regularly.
> "at a bare minimum use a Google sheet you just want to get their name the contact info and the date contacted and the status" — Chris Koerner (~38:59)

**Research competitors’ active ads** (customer_acquisition)
1. Search the Meta Ads Library for competitors.
2. Review active ads and compare format, headline, hook and image.
3. Treat ads that appear to have run for months as candidates to study, not guaranteed winners.
> "go to the meta ads library google it search for your competitors sort by active ads scroll through" — Chris Koerner (~42:52)

**Test social proof and customer language** (validation_testing)
1. Ask customers to record authentic vertical testimonials.
2. Use real customer wording in ad copy.
3. Poll or survey an existing audience, then ask willing respondents follow-up questions.
4. Test a hook organically before paying to distribute a video ad.
> "screenshot their answers and use that exact language in your ad copy now you know you're getting to the heart of the problems" — Chris Koerner (~45:05)

**Validate demand before building** (validation_testing)
1. Create a simple landing page for the proposed product.
2. Run ads to measure clicks or collect email addresses and pre-orders.
3. If the product cannot be fulfilled, refund pre-orders.
> "sell it before you build it run ads for an idea before you even have the product" — Chris Koerner (~45:42)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Align the customer’s problem, the offer and the audience before spending on creative.
- *customer_acquisition*: Pick the campaign objective that matches the action you want; otherwise the platform may optimize for the wrong behavior.
- *offer_design*: Make the offer concrete by naming the problem, the solution, the timing and the next action.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use clear, direct language and real proof instead of polished but vague brand copy.
- *validation_testing*: Treat advertising as a measured system: change one variable at a time and use results to guide the next test.
- *operations*: A winning ad only helps if the business can respond quickly and fulfil the resulting demand.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use customer language and authentic customer stories to make ads specific and credible.
- *validation_testing*: Validate demand before investing heavily in a product; provide refunds when a pre-order cannot be fulfilled.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Revenue return on ad spend does not establish profitability unless product costs and overhead are also accounted for.
- *automation_ai*: Automate routine lead acknowledgement and tracking, while using templates to speed up human replies.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The host says boosting a post gives little control over audience, optimization goal and budget, and recommends using Ads Manager.
- Do not edit a new ad during its learning period unless it is rejected or clearly broken; changes may reset learning.
- Traffic campaigns can optimize for clickers rather than buyers or message senders.
- Changing multiple test variables at once makes it impossible to tell which change caused the result.
- Scaling too quickly can disrupt a campaign; the host recommends gradual increases or duplicating the ad set.
