## Summary

Short clip from an interview with Nick Huber (Sweaty Startup) about building a social media audience as an operator. He says audience growth requires first doing something interesting in business and then writing good copy without caring what others think. He estimates he spent about two hours a day on Twitter/X in 2021 and calls it the highest-ROI work he has done, but says the same posts that gained 3,000-4,000 followers in 2021 now gain about 50. The key takeaway is that radical transparency builds trust and a network that sells your companies and products, but timing on the platform mattered a lot and it is much harder today.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build an audience from lived operator experience** (acquisition)
1. First do something interesting in business (he built self-storage facilities and ran companies)
2. Open up deeply about real deals, numbers and stories
3. Write good copy and post consistently (about 2 hours a day at his peak)
4. Capture ideas continuously in a phone notes app or a bedside notebook
5. Accept some exposure in exchange for trust that drives sales
> "just leave an interesting life and just share the stories And then just it just comes" — Nick Huber (~2:30)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: People follow those they can learn from or want to be like, so substance comes before audience.
- *customer_acquisition*: Transparency about numbers builds trust that turns followers into customers of your companies and products.
- *mindset_execution*: Writing good copy and not caring what people think are the two skills that make content work.
- *customer_acquisition*: Channel timing matters: early movers on a platform got growth that the same effort cannot buy later.
- *scaling*: An online audience can act as a network accelerator, producing mentors and close friends, not only customers.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Audience growth on X is far harder now than in 2020-2021; timing drove much of his growth.
- Radical transparency exposes you to public takedowns of business owners.
- Without real achievements or experience, building a following is very hard.
