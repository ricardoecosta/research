## Tactics and playbook steps

**Match the franchise to the operator** (niche_selection)
1. Assess the buyer's background, risk tolerance, and preferred working style.
2. Compare those traits with the daily work: for example, relationship selling for freight brokerage or managing subcontractors for facilities work.
3. Avoid choosing only by headline revenue; the buyer must be willing and able to do the work long enough to persist.
> "The wrong person in the right franchise is still the wrong franchise." — Alex (~39:22)

**Screen a franchise system by maturity** (validation_testing)
1. If risk-averse, look for a system with more than 50 open units, according to Alex.
2. Check whether the franchisor has enough royalty income to be selective and fund operations.
3. Balance operating history against the remaining opportunity to develop locations.
> "if you're more riskaverse, try to find a system that has more than 50 units already open." — Alex (~18:37)

**Inspect FDD openings, closures, and transfers** (legal)
1. Read Item 20 for store openings, closures, and transfers.
2. Treat transfers as possible closures and ask why the owner exited.
3. Investigate sharp rises in transfers, particularly in a young system.
> "if you look at transfers and there's a high number of them almost look at a transfer as a closure like what happened here?" — Alex (~21:55)

**Verify Item 19 financial claims** (finance)
1. Read the Item 19 figures and footnotes.
2. Ask why a mature brand does not report profitability.
3. Be cautious if the disclosure shows only top-performing locations rather than a representative group.
> "if the item 19 doesn't have profitability or footnotes that are clearly made up" — Alex (~44:15)

**Contact franchisees beyond the franchisor's references** (validation_testing)
1. Ask to speak with franchisees who are performing well, struggling, and doing average.
2. Find former franchisees or those no longer in the system independently.
3. Ask about the experience behind the financial data and why owners transferred or left.
> "Let me talk to some that have failed. Let me talk to some that are performing well." — Alex (~44:35)

**Plan more working capital than the FDD minimum** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Use the FDD startup range as a starting point.
2. Add a larger working-capital cushion because the stated FDD requirement may cover only three months.
3. Alex recommends planning for six to nine months of working capital.
> "I tell people double or triple that. You probably need six to nine months of working capital." — Alex (~30:48)

**Use operating experience to attract capital** (hiring_outsourcing)
1. Build a track record operating a business or managing locations before seeking capital partners.
2. Show the specific operating capability that complements the capital partner.
3. Consider earning ownership through demonstrated performance as an operator.
> "if you don't have capital you need to come with something else." — Alex (~42:27)

**Consider buying an existing location** (acquisition)
1. Search franchise resale listings and ask multi-unit operators about locations they may want to sell.
2. Compare acquisition price with verified cash flow and the cost of building a new location.
3. Investigate why the seller is exiting and whether the buyer can improve operations.
> "I'd rather buy existing if I can." — Alex (~27:25)

**Check franchise financing options** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Check whether the franchisor is on the SBA registry before relying on SBA financing.
2. Prepare a personal financial statement and supporting account records.
3. Approach multiple banks after preparing the financial information once.
4. Compare SBA, conventional lending, and friends-and-family capital against the buyer's collateral and guarantees.
> "once you do it one time, you have all the information. Like, the annoying part is doing one of those personal financial statements" — Alex (~31:51)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Choose a business that fits your skills, risk tolerance, and preferred daily work; headline earnings do not compensate for a poor fit.
- *validation_testing*: A franchise system's age and unit count can signal operating maturity, but buyers should still examine the details.
- *legal_risk*: Look beyond openings and closures: transfers can conceal distressed locations or owner exits.
- *validation_testing*: Use franchisee interviews to test the disclosure documents and learn how the system operates in practice.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Plan more working capital than the minimum shown in a startup estimate, because reaching profitability may take longer.
- *offer_design*: A franchise can provide technology, brand, or systems, but the buyer should ask what value the franchisor continues to provide.
- *exit_acquisition*: Buying an operating location can reduce build-out risk, though the buyer may have less upside than with a new location.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Operating experience can attract capital when the prospective owner does not have much of their own.
- *finance_unit_economics*: High gross revenue does not equal high profit when fuel, subcontractors, or other pass-through costs make up much of sales.
- *legal_risk*: A franchise can have a strong brand and still be a bad deal for a particular owner if location strategy creates cannibalization.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The guest says transfers can be used to obscure franchise closures; investigate transfer counts and ask why owners left.
- Do not rely only on the franchisee references supplied by the franchisor; contact former and average-performing operators too.
- Be skeptical of financial disclosures that highlight only the top few locations or use unclear adjusted figures.
- A high-revenue facilities franchise can have substantial pass-through subcontractor costs and still require the owner to recruit and manage contractors.
- Franchising does not eliminate business failure; Alex says a minority of franchise businesses close and describes a case where an owner lost substantial personal assets.
- The guest and host have promotional interests: Alex invites viewers to work with Franzy, Chris promotes TKO Owners, and the video includes a GoHighLevel sponsor read.
- Chris criticizes Subway for opening stores close enough to compete with existing franchisees, which can damage individual operators.
