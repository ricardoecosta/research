## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate buyers before bidding** (validation_testing)
1. Find the likely resale market for a product before bidding.
2. Use eBay Product Research to check sold prices and the number of sales.
3. Call likely business buyers to ask what they need and what price they would pay.
4. Only bid once you have a credible route to recovering the purchase cost.
> "the first law of auctions is never buy anything unless you know what you can already sell it for." — Shannon (~31:01)

**Calculate a conservative landed bid** (finance)
1. Discount the stated retail price because Shannon says it can be misleading.
2. Estimate a cautious resale value and subtract freight and other costs.
3. Use a spreadsheet or product equation to set a maximum bid.
4. Bid with math rather than emotion.
> "I use math to bid, not emotion. I've studied. I've done my research." — Shannon (~20:32)

**Study a category before scaling** (validation_testing)
1. Watch auctions and research the category before making large purchases.
2. Make small mistakes while the stakes are low.
3. Learn how listings, photos, authenticity, shipping and returns work.
4. Move into larger lots only after the market and process make sense.
> "So studying that when you can make small mistakes is really powerful and that's what I was doing." — Shannon (~9:54)

**Build platform relationships before problems arise** (customer_acquisition)
1. Find platform category managers and product managers on LinkedIn.
2. Interact with their public posts and congratulate them on relevant work.
3. Send a short introduction explaining the category and sales activity.
4. Offer to speak about the seller experience, then build the relationship over time.
> "You don't want to ask him for anything, but you want to get in his conversation flow." — Shannon (~4:09)

**Differentiate listings with evidence** (offer_design)
1. Look at what other sellers include in their listings.
2. For used goods, record a short video showing the item working.
3. Use clear photography and explain condition accurately.
4. For high-value goods, package them carefully and include a thank-you card or coupon where economics allow.
> "I've got video of me walking around or if it's used I have a video of it working all that kind of stuff." — Shannon (~12:28)

**Handle complaints with empathy** (operations)
1. Acknowledge the customer's disappointment instead of arguing about fault.
2. Offer a prepaid return label promptly.
3. Consider a future-purchase coupon when it is economically sensible.
4. Take responsibility for the overall transaction and delivery experience.
> "So, you always choose the end of the world token and you say, Oh my gosh, I'm so sorry." — Shannon (~14:52)

**Sell bulk goods to the businesses that use them** (customer_acquisition)
1. Identify businesses that consume or transform the product, such as screen printers for blank apparel.
2. Use the lot manifest to explain what is available.
3. Ask potential buyers what they would pay before buying the inventory.
4. Follow up when a suitable lot becomes available.
> "The story just tell them the truth. Hey, I'm I'm in the resale business and I get all these opportunities" — Shannon (~34:44)

**Look for an unfair advantage** (niche_selection)
1. Identify what you can do better than other sellers: capital, storage, delivery, expertise or customer service.
2. Choose product categories where that advantage matters.
3. Use the advantage to make an otherwise difficult lot easier to buy or sell.
> "I call it what is your unfair advantage? What do you have that other people don't?" — Shannon (~12:41)

**Track product and category changes** (scaling)
1. Watch how product design and repairability change over time.
2. Notice when a previously profitable category loses parts or margin.
3. Move research to another category rather than assuming the old opportunity will persist.
> "Nothing stays the same. And if and if you don't continually invol evolve what you're working on, you'll be left behind." — Shannon (~19:32)

## Lessons

- *operations*: A repeatable sourcing, bidding and selling system can matter more than choosing a supposedly perfect category.
- *validation_testing*: Use research and small experiments to learn a category before risking money on large lots.
- *customer_acquisition*: Build platform relationships before you need help, so people know your business and track record.
- *operations*: Take responsibility for the customer experience, even when a delivery issue was outside your control.
- *offer_design*: Differentiate the offer with better evidence, service or presentation instead of competing only on price.
- *scaling*: Do not let a successful category stay fixed; product changes and margin shifts can make a once-good business less attractive.
- *niche_selection*: Look for an advantage you can use, such as category knowledge, storage, delivery capacity or customer service.
- *customer_acquisition*: A phone call can reveal buyer needs and pricing that online research alone may not show.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Make sure there is a realistic path to recovering your investment before treating remaining inventory as upside.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not buy an auction lot until you know what you can sell it for; estimate a conservative value and include freight.
- Do not assume stated retail or store prices equal realistic resale value; Shannon says those prices can be misleading.
- Reselling physical lots is not passive income and can require significant selling and fulfilment work.
- Large or obscure lots can be hard to sell and may require calls, storage, freight and a buyer network.
- The transcript includes a suggestion to scrape phone numbers and text screen printers. In the UK, unsolicited marketing texts to individuals and sole traders require consent under PECR.
- Shannon warns that consumer electronics resale has lost much of its former margin.
- A rushed review of a manifest can lead to buying inventory in the wrong sizes or mix.
- Marketplace dependency can leave sellers exposed to account restrictions; Shannon recommends building platform relationships before a problem occurs.
- Resale lots and customer service involve returns, shipping delays and negative feedback risk.
