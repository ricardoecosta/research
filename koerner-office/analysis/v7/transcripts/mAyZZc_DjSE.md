## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews David (Fort Wayne, Indiana), a 'multipreneur' running several small rental businesses. He details a tote (moving box) rental business sold via a realtor gifting subscription, a travel-trailer (RV) rental business that made about $7,000 net in its first season, and a side-hustle recording/video studio. The takeaway is that small rental businesses need little capital, reuse the same asset repeatedly, and can be validated by asking existing operators and using marketplaces as lead-gen before moving customers to your own site.

### I1. Moving tote rental with realtor gifting subscription

- **Customer:** People moving house, and local realtors (also moving companies, wedding planners) who gift totes to clients
- **Problem:** Cardboard boxes are wasteful, hodgepodge and need taping; realtors want unique, hands-off client gifts
- **Offer:** Delivery and pickup of 20 sturdy totes per move; realtors prepay credits and totes carry a branded pouch 'move powered by [realtor]'
- **Business model:** Per-rental fees plus monthly subscription credits paid by realtors
- **Pricing:** Per-rental price plus realtor subscription at discounted rate. consumer price USD 99 per 20-tote rental (retail); realtor discounted price USD 60 per gift rental (realtor price); 2 gifts/month tier USD 120 per month; 5 gifts/month tier USD 300 per month Credits roll over; recipient gets a $100 credit toward a bigger package as an upsell.
- **Acquisition:** networking_events: Wife attends monthly Real Producers magazine vendor networking events, uses the magazine's list of top-producing agents, hands out business cards and invites to coffee. No cold outreach yet.; partnerships: Networking with local realtors and moving companies led to traction; realtor gifting program suggested by the Real Producers manager.; marketplace_platform: Early Facebook Marketplace posts with AI-generated images before having totes got no traction.; seo_organic_search: Latching onto 'moving boxes' search term; Google Trends for 'tote rental' rising.
- **Delivery:** Owners deliver totes to customers and pick them up after the move; they do not handle packing or moving. Custom software tracks credits, with a realtor claim portal and gift order creation.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Totes cost about $12 each wholesale, about $1,500 per pallet of 120 with shipping; Started with 120 totes, bought another 120 after overbooking, now almost 400
- **Tools:** ChatGPT, Nano Banana, Facebook Marketplace, Custom-built claim portal software, Real Producers magazine
- **Startup cost:** About $1,500 for the first pallet of 120 totes plus a website
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated; traction came after switching to local networking
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Crate rental for house moves exists in the UK; sourcing and realtor/estate-agent gifting works similarly. Real Producers also operates in the UK in some form, but estate agent culture differs; check storage and van costs.
- **Risks:** Low awareness; nobody searches for tote rental; Overselling on a weekend with a 24-48 hour restock lead time; Seasonal slow real estate periods; Dependence on a few realtor relationships
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 1/5 (In-person networking with realtors is the working channel; only a content/SEO side could be automated.); onboarding 4/5 (Agent login, credit tracking and gift order creation via a claim portal already built.); delivery 0/5 (Physical delivery and pickup of totes, plus cleaning and storage.); billing 4/5 (Subscriptions and rental payments can be handled by payment software.); support 3/5 (Mostly simple scheduling questions; agents can handle most with occasional human input.)

### I2. RV / travel trailer rental

- **Customer:** Families and groups camping in state parks, festivals and concerts in the Midwest
- **Problem:** People want to camp but do not own an RV or cannot afford to
- **Offer:** Fully equipped 26-ft bunkhouse trailer with cookware, linens, towels and toilet paper, optionally delivered and set up
- **Business model:** Nightly rental fees; delivery and setup
- **Pricing:** Nightly rate, same on platforms and direct.  Nightly rate not stated. Market in Indiana is much lower than Florida or Colorado where about $200 a night is possible.
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: Listed on Outdoorsy, RVshare and RVezy using own iPhone photos; platforms treated as lead-gen even though they take about 25%.; facebook_groups_communities: Facebook Marketplace posts pointing to listings; joined an RV rental Facebook group for advice.; existing_audience: Repeat customers rebook directly via own website, avoiding platform fees.
- **Delivery:** Owner delivers the trailer to the campsite, sets it level, checks everything works, and walks through the unit with the customer. Customers sign a supplemental rental agreement, with rules on tank dumping.
- **Unit economics (claims):** About $5,000 per season per camper claimed by other owners; About $7,000 net in first season; about $10,000 gross; Payment about $400 a month, insurance $30-60 a month, trailer cost about $20,000
- **Tools:** Outdoorsy, RVshare, RVezy, Facebook Marketplace, Own booking website, Facebook group for RV renters
- **Startup cost:** About $20,000 for the trailer, financed at about $400 a month
- **Time to first revenue:** Within a couple of weeks of listing (first rental at Halloween weekend)
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — Caravan hire exists in the UK (e.g. Camplify, Hirethecaravan, Outdoorsy UK) but towing licence rules, site storage, shorter season and insurance differ; no HOA issue but storage is costly.
- **Risks:** Breakdowns (slide-outs, leaks) and 3am calls; Storage restrictions such as HOA limits; Seasonality (mid-June to October); Customer misuse such as tank dumping; Needs financing and a tow vehicle or delivery
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 4/5 (Marketplace listings, direct booking site and repeat customers are inbound and can be automated.); onboarding 3/5 (Digital rental agreement and instructional videos or quiz can be automated; the walk-through is human.); delivery 0/5 (Owner physically tows, sets up, cleans and turns the trailer between rentals.); billing 4/5 (Platforms and direct booking software process payments.); support 1/5 (Breakdown calls need a human, possibly outsourced.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate rental markets by messaging existing operators** (acquisition)
1. Search Outdoorsy / Facebook Marketplace for local listings and their prices
2. Message owners with multiple listings and ask about revenue
3. Join the niche Facebook group and ask about risks and horror stories
> "Don't be afraid to reach out to people that are already doing it because you'd be surprised how open and friendly people are" — David (~36:32)

**Use marketplaces as lead-gen, then move repeat customers direct** (acquisition)
1. List the asset on all marketplaces
2. Build own booking site with same nightly rate
3. Send repeat customers directly to own site to avoid ~25% fees

**Realtor gifting subscription** (pricing)
1. Get the list of top realtors from the local Real Producers magazine
2. Attend its monthly vendor events in person
3. Offer 2-per-month and 5-per-month discounted gift packages
4. Give recipients $100 credit toward larger packages as upsell

**Rental fleet buying rule** (operations)
1. Start with one pallet of 120 totes
2. Buy more only when oversold
3. Use a supplier within an hour with 24-48h restocking

**Customer instruction videos and quiz** (operations)
1. Record unlisted YouTube walkthrough videos of the unit
2. Require customers to watch them before booking
3. Use AI to create a quiz that must be passed before pickup

**Choose feature-light, slide-out-free units** (product)
1. Choose bunkhouse layout to widen market
2. Avoid slide-outs, TVs and Wi-Fi to limit breakdowns

## Lessons

- *operations*: Rental businesses reuse one asset repeatedly with no need to re-source goods, giving low capital and low overhead.
- *validation_testing*: Test with a small, reversible purchase where the worst case is acceptable.
- *customer_acquisition*: Local in-person networking beat AI-driven online marketing for a B2B2C offer.
- *offer_design*: Recurring revenue can be created by selling a subscription to a third party (realtors) who gifts to end customers.
- *customer_acquisition*: Treat marketplaces as lead generation and capture repeat customers on your own site.
- *niche_selection*: Know your local market's pricing; the same asset earns very different rates by region.
- *operations*: Remove friction and breakable features to reduce support calls.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Only buy inventory when oversold to keep capital efficient.
- *validation_testing*: Ask people already in the niche; they are generally open and helpful.

## Warnings and mistakes

- AI-generated product images and sell-before-you-have-it Facebook posts got no traction for totes; manual local networking worked.
- Class C motorhomes break often and slide-outs leak or fail; Chris sold his after a breakdown call.
- HOA rules and lack of driveway space limit scaling RV rental from home.
- Tank dumping misuse is a major horror-story source; put rules in the rental agreement.
