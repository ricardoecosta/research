## Lessons

- *legal_risk*: Treat AI accounts as sensitive assets: chat history can hold business plans, financials and client data, so protect logins and review account security and history settings.
- *automation_ai*: Only install AI apps and browser extensions from verified official sources, as lookalikes are the main attack vector.
- *mindset_execution*: Scammers exploit human trust rather than technical weaknesses, so scepticism toward unsolicited opportunities is the main defence.
- *legal_risk*: Do not put client confidential or sensitive data into AI tools without considering account security, especially in a UK business subject to GDPR obligations.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Fake apps and fake browser extensions impersonating ChatGPT can install malware that steals logins; OpenAI itself was not hacked, the users were.
- A stolen AI login exposes saved conversation history, including business ideas, financial numbers, client details and personal details.
- Searching for 'make money with ChatGPT' opportunities can lead to malware-laden fake offers; check the legitimacy of any AI opportunity before engaging.
- Investment scams approached via messaging apps such as WhatsApp can cause catastrophic losses.
