## Summary

Chris Koerner visits his neighbour Rayma, a stay-at-home mom of four in Dallas who runs Gifty Gal, a corporate gifting business focused on personalised prospecting gifts for tech sales teams. She started in 2022 with baby shower gifts, grossed about $75k (netting $7,800) in year one, and reached $357k revenue and $119k net in 2025 working about 20 hours a week from home and a shared warehouse. Growth came entirely from referrals and LinkedIn after one LinkedIn post reshared by her husband's network. She explains her pricing (about $100 per prospecting box, 30-50% net margin target, shipping billed separately), wholesale sourcing via Faire and reseller licences, and a four-step framework: connect, collaborate, curate, fulfil.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Connect, collaborate, curate, fulfil framework** (operations)
1. Connect: post on LinkedIn about what you do (e.g. help sales teams close more deals with personalised gifts) and respond quickly
2. Collaborate: discovery call with the client on what the gift must achieve and who it must reach
3. Curate: source products at the best wholesale price that fit the recipient's alma mater, hobbies etc.
4. Fulfil: assemble, collect shipping info, double-check names and addresses, ship
> "You need to connect with the people, you need to collaborate with them, you need to curate, and you need to fulfill" — Rayma (~35:29)

**Wholesale setup to protect margin** (finance)
1. Form an LLC and get an EIN
2. Open a separate business bank account
3. Get a reseller's licence and wholesale accounts
4. Buy through Faire or ask brands directly for wholesale programmes
5. Bring customisation in-house (laser engraver) or use local vendors
> "I knew immediately I need to get a wholesale account and a reseller's license" — Rayma (~7:42)

**Bill shipping separately** (pricing)
1. Quote the gift price without shipping
2. Use the client's corporate shipping account where possible
3. Otherwise use negotiated carrier rates (ask FedEx/UPS for a rep, or ShipStation, Pirate Ship, Stamps.com)
4. Confirm shipping cost after a sample is approved
> "don't make it into your prices or you'll sell yourself short" — Rayma (~35:13)

**Minimum order rules** (pricing)
1. No gifts under $50
2. No orders under 10 gifts
> "I have minimums now where I don't do gifts um under 50" — Rayma (~30:05)

**Launch with one LinkedIn post amplified by a network** (acquisition)
1. Post on LinkedIn announcing the business
2. Have a spouse/friends in corporate reshare to their networks
3. Say yes to the first inbound corporate request and learn the domain
4. Ask that first client to introduce you to other sales reps
> "he reshared my post which went to his whole network" — Rayma (~22:21)

**Sell on the rep's cost of time** (sales)
1. Calculate how long it takes a rep to source, pack and ship a gift
2. Compare against the rep's hourly value
3. Pitch outsourcing as cheaper and faster
> "I did the numbers of how much it takes or how much time it takes me to put a gift together and how much that costs" — Rayma (~24:31)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Generic gifts get ignored because decision makers receive many; personalised gifts researched from sales-call details get responses.
- *customer_acquisition*: A product people naturally photograph and share turns clients into the marketing channel.
- *validation_testing*: Start with low-stakes consumer work to learn, then move to B2B where one mistake can cost the reputation.
- *niche_selection*: Pick one customer type; focusing on corporate and being strategic with pricing drove the jump to six figures net.
- *pricing*: Your real competitor is often the customer doing it themselves; educate them on the cost of their time.
- *scaling*: Shared warehouse spaces with flexible leases bridge from home to a full warehouse; a one-year lease helps negotiate price.
- *offer_design*: Packaging and first impression matter as much as contents.
- *mindset_execution*: Be willing to say no to clients and jobs that do not fit, and set minimums.
- *mindset_execution*: Every business is find the customers and fulfil the customers.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Buying products at retail and marking them up for consumers was not profitable; she netted only $7,800 on $75k in year one.
- Trying to serve both B2C and B2B at once caused financial trouble.
- Unplanned expedited shipping from China (normally 20-25 days by sea) ate into profit around deadlines.
- Building shipping into your gift price undersells you; bill it separately.
- Crinkle paper and packing supplies are surprisingly expensive and sneak up on you.
- Missing state filing deadlines or EIN filings can get you in trouble.
- Be careful with assumptions like alcohol in personalised gifts.
