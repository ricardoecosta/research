### I1. Yard sign greeting rentals

- **Customer:** Local families (birthdays, new babies, graduations, homecomings), realtors, school booster clubs
- **Problem:** People want a celebratory surprise on the lawn without buying or making signs.
- **Offer:** Standard yard greeting with setup, 24 hours on the lawn, and pickup.
- **Business model:** Per-booking rental with delivery and pickup
- **Pricing:** Flat per booking incl. delivery/setup/pickup. standard yard greeting range USD 75-125 per booking; most common price USD 100 per booking Price the whole trip and set a delivery radius from day one.
- **Acquisition:** referrals_word_of_mouth: Families on your own street/side of town with birthdays.; partnerships: Realtors wanting welcome-home signs on closing day; school booster clubs wanting every senior's yard done in a week.
- **Delivery:** Owner drives out, sets up stake-mounted letters, returns to collect and repairs; physical and local.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Lean start of 3-5 greeting sets plus a stork sign and graduation set costs about $300-$800; About five lawns at ~$100 to earn back $500; Part-timers report 8-15 bookings a month

- **Startup cost:** About $300-$800 (call it $500)
- **Time to first revenue:** Not stated; payback in about five bookings, second month signs fund rental two

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Sequential reinvestment ladder** (finance)
1. Spend the first $500 on the cheapest, fastest-payback rental (yard signs)
2. Fund each next rental only from rental earnings, not savings or loans
3. Order: yard signs, lawn games, snow cones, baby gear, patio heaters, movie screen, photo booth
4. Buy liability insurance once running multiple rentals
> "Everything after [music] that gets bought with rental money, not savings, not a loan, and the customers grow as you go." — Harry (~0:48)

**Two-way customer route per rental** (acquisition)
1. Way one: sell to consumers on your own street and cross-sell existing customers
2. Way two: partner with suppliers who already have the crowd (realtors, venues, breweries, schools, HOAs, DJs, planners)

**Deposit and piece-count control** (operations)
1. Take a $50-$250 damage deposit
2. Count every piece at drop-off and pickup

**Put operating math in the booking text** (operations)
1. Include '1 lb ice per 4-6 kids' in the booking confirmation
2. Sell syrup and cups as add-ons

**Spare-tank add-on for heaters** (pricing)
1. Include a full spare tank per heater as a priced add-on since one tank lasts 4-6 hours

**Preferred vendor list for photo booth** (acquisition)
1. Start with an open-air booth
2. Get on preferred vendor lists at 2-3 local venues

**Validate local demand before buying** (mindset)
1. Search your town for who already rents this
2. Check what they charge now
3. Identify what would make it fail locally
4. Look at three price pages and text one realtor about welcome-home signs
> "I'd search my town for yard sign rentals, look at three price pages, and text one realtor to ask if her buyers ever get a welcome home sign." — Harry (~12:28)

## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: Sequence capital so the cheapest, fastest-payback asset goes first and funds the next, avoiding debt.
- *validation_testing*: Rental inventory is only valuable when a customer already exists; check competitors, prices and failure modes locally first.
- *customer_acquisition*: Cross-selling different rentals to the same customer base lowers acquisition cost per booking.
- *niche_selection*: Stack complementary seasons (summer snow cones, winter heaters) so equipment earns year-round.
- *customer_acquisition*: Selling to suppliers who already hold the crowd (venues, realtors, HOAs) is a repeatable B2B route.
- *pricing*: Price the full trip, including travel and repairs, and set a delivery radius from day one.
- *offer_design*: Add-ons (spare tanks, syrup, cups, deposits) protect margin and fix common failure points.
- *automation_ai*: Physical rental businesses need the owner for delivery, so they are poor fits for hands-off agent operation; booking, payments and support are the automatable layers.
- *mindset_execution*: Treat headline income figures (e.g. $5,000/month) as top-of-range, not typical.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Pricing yard signs like a product rather than a booking ignores delivery, setup, pickup and repair costs.
- No deposit and no piece counting leads to lost game pieces.
- Running out of ice mid-party for snow cones.
- Car seats expire and crashed ones can never be rented; avoid or track expiry dates.
- A propane tank only lasts 4-6 hours so long weddings can lose heat without spares.
- Starting movies before the sky is dark washes out the picture; 16-ft screens act like sails in wind.
- Buying trendy 360 platforms instead of open-air booths narrows event types booked.
- A rental does not book itself; you must find customers, set prices and make the phone ring.
