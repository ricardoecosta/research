## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate before building** (acquisition)
1. Pick a high-ticket niche that gets after-hours calls and is not consolidated by private equity
2. Scrape local businesses from Google Maps with Outscraper (about $1 for 500 results)
3. Clean the data and keep mobile numbers only
4. Send a short first text to confirm the business does the service
5. Only after interested replies, pitch and then build the agent
6. Skip LLC, website, logo and business plan until a customer says yes
> "We need to start by getting our first customer." — Chris Koerner (~3:54)

**Lead list cleaning in Google Sheets** (operations)
1. Export Outscraper to Google Sheets and delete unneeded columns
2. Keep name, website, address and the more accurate phone column
3. Highlight businesses with 'repair' in the name using conditional formatting
4. Standardize numbers by removing +1 and dashes
5. Remove duplicate rows by phone number
6. Filter to mobile numbers only, discarding unknown line types
7. Randomize with =RAND() and sort, putting 'repair' names first
> "remember this data cleanup process will be the same no matter what industry you're in." — Chris Koerner (~15:24)

**Short first-text message** (sales)
1. Keep under 160 characters to avoid splitting into two SMS
2. Use a first name and local city so it looks personal
3. Add a city variable to vary messages and reduce blocking
4. Ask only a qualifying question first ('Do you still fix garage doors?')
5. Pitch only after reply, mentioning local, free setup, no contract, pay-per-lead
> "shorter texts have a higher response rate." — Chris Koerner (~19:09)

**Risk-free launch pricing** (pricing)
1. Make the first offer no contract, no upfront payment, free setup
2. Do not let price decide whether the business fails
3. Raise prices, add contracts or retainers once demand is proven
> "I don't want the price to get in the way of the business being successful or not." — Chris Koerner (~23:16)

**Voice agent setup and call routing** (product)
1. Create a custom Voice AI agent in GoHighLevel with business name, voice and welcome message
2. Use ChatGPT to write the knowledge base prompt (background, rules, script)
3. Collect name and address; email the summary after calls
4. Give the owner a dedicated or ported number
5. Add workflow forwarding urgent calls to the owner, then secondary and tertiary contacts
> "It's basically one prompt. And then every time a call is made, it's going to refer to that prompt and answer accordingly." — Chris Koerner (~11:55)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Validate demand by getting a real prospect's yes before building a company, website or brand.
- *niche_selection*: Choose niches with high ticket value and urgent, after-hours need, since those customers can pay more for leads.
- *niche_selection*: Avoid niches already consolidated by private equity; look for fragmented, unsophisticated owners.
- *offer_design*: A risk-free offer (free setup, pay only on results) lowers friction so the test measures demand, not price sensitivity.
- *customer_acquisition*: Keep first outreach tiny: confirm fit with one question and save the pitch for people who reply.
- *automation_ai*: AI voice agent tooling has collapsed in cost and time; a funded startup's core experience can now be approximated in minutes with a trial.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Treat extrapolations from a tiny sample (one yes in 50) with caution; the $1.2M/yr figure is illustrative, not evidence.
- *operations*: Cheap local lead data plus a repeatable cleaning process can be reused across any industry.
- *mindset_execution*: Acting fast and publicly on an idea builds momentum and gets real market feedback within hours.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Plumbing, HVAC and electrical are already targeted by private equity, so avoid them.
- Undifferentiated bulk messages can get iMessage blocked; personalize with names or cities.
- Do not delete or ignore uncertain numbers: only mobiles were kept to avoid risk, since landlines cannot receive texts.
- A yes is not a closed deal; most deals never close.
