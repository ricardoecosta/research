## Tactics and playbook steps

**Target one service and one keyword** (niche_selection)
1. Choose a narrow home service with a clear search phrase.
2. Build a website focused on that service rather than presenting a broad list of unrelated services.
3. Expand into nearby locations after establishing the initial service focus.
> "They just focus on a single service and one single keyword." — Tim Richard (~0:03)

**Build local pages for a focused service** (acquisition)
1. Create pages for the chosen service in each target city.
2. Use the service and city together in the page targeting.
3. Expand coverage only where service delivery can be arranged.
> "They built out a whole website with pages dedicated to each city that they want to rank for." — Tim Richard (~2:31)

**Dispatch online bookings to subcontractors** (operations)
1. Accept job details through a website.
2. Send each booked job to a local subcontractor.
3. Have the subcontractor perform the physical work while the operator coordinates remotely.
> "So, I book jobs online on our website and send the jobs out to the subcontractors, and they go do the labor, pick up the junk for me, and I just manage it all remotely from the computer." — Unnamed junk removal operator (~0:39)

**Make simple bookings self-serve** (operations)
1. Let customers enter a zip code and choose the item to be removed.
2. Show a guaranteed price for the described job.
3. Allow customers to select a day, book, and pay online.
4. Send the resulting job to a subcontractor.
> "You can choose the day and actually book and pay online." — Tim Richard (~3:27)

**Use a focused service to expand into more locations** (scaling)
1. Start with a specific service in one location.
2. Build pages for other locations after identifying a service and keyword that can rank.
3. Use subcontractors or local providers where the company expands.
> "I'm sure they started out with drywall repair in a single location, started realizing they could build out specific drywall repair pages for other locations" — Tim Richard (~4:09)

**Capture interest in a niche list** (customer_acquisition)
1. Offer a list of remote home service niches.
2. Ask interested viewers to enter an email address to receive the list.

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: A narrow service and search phrase can make a small operator look like a specialist in a local market.
- *customer_acquisition*: Local service pages can extend a focused offer into additional cities, but the video does not show that rankings automatically produce profitable work.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Subcontractors can let an operator coordinate work remotely while other people perform the physical service.
- *operations*: Online booking can combine item details, a displayed price, scheduling, and payment in one customer flow.
- *finance_unit_economics*: A high-ticket job may require fewer bookings to produce substantial sales, but the host gives no costs, margins, or profit figures.
- *scaling*: A focused niche can broaden into related services after the original offer gains traction.
- *validation_testing*: Look for a service that is needed broadly but is not described as highly competitive, then test local demand before expanding.
- *mindset_execution*: Treat niche ideas as hypotheses to investigate rather than guaranteed business opportunities.
