## Tactics and playbook steps

**Start with a small set of providers** (acquisition)
1. Find a mystery shopping organization or provider with legitimate partnerships.
2. Register directly on a handful of provider websites.
3. Complete a few assignments to learn the process before expanding to more providers.
> "I would suggest starting off with a few and then going from there 'cause what happens, you can get overwhelmed." — Mercedes (~7:57)

**Build routes from separate assignments** (operations)
1. Choose a day for assignments.
2. Combine nearby shops from multiple providers into one route.
3. Mix assignment types when practical, such as an apartment visit, meal shop, and gas station shop.
4. Account for travel and report-writing time when deciding whether a route is worthwhile.
> "you can piece together different types of shops. So a lot of times like when I would go out, I would do apartment mystery shops." — Mercedes (~11:37)

**Protect shopper reputation** (operations)
1. Accept assignments only when you can complete them.
2. Follow the assignment instructions, document the requested details, and submit reports on time.
3. Write clear, chronological narratives when the report asks for them.
4. Meet provider representatives through industry organizations and conferences.
> "The key is to number one, do good work, right? Don't flake out on shops that you have to do." — Mercedes (~17:09)

**Screen out mystery shopping scams** (legal)
1. Treat a check sent before work as a warning sign, especially if you are told to cash it and send money elsewhere.
2. Do not buy gift cards and send the redemption codes.
3. Check whether the sender's email domain matches the real company domain.
4. Use an organization that can confirm its provider partnerships.
> "Common Sense tells us jobs and companies don't typically pay you before you go and do your work." — Mercedes (~22:25)

**Track mileage and expenses** (finance)
1. Use a mileage-tracking tool to record business travel.
2. Track required purchases and reimbursement status.
3. Check each provider's pay schedule and payment method before accepting assignments.
> "There's a lot. I mean, you've got mile IQ that shoppers love to use" — Mercedes (~33:26) [quote not matched verbatim]

**Use training to reduce avoidable report errors** (operations)
1. Teach shoppers how to complete an assignment from start to finish.
2. Practice writing complete, chronological narratives.
3. Explain provider instructions and report requirements before shoppers accept work.
> "sometimes there are narratives where you have to write in full complete sentences chronologically, what happened?" — Mercedes (~40:17)

**Build a directory around the provider fragmentation** (product)
1. Identify that shoppers must register with many providers and search each website.
2. Aggregate available jobs by shopper location.
3. Add useful filters and features based on shopper requests.
4. Keep listing data current as providers add and remove assignments.
> "They want a place to be able to do that." — Mercedes (~34:46)

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: Start with a manageable number of providers, learn the process, then expand based on the kinds of assignments that suit you.
- *customer_acquisition*: Reliability and clear, timely reports help build a reputation that can generate referrals between providers.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Route planning can improve the return on assignment work by combining nearby jobs from different providers.
- *finance_unit_economics*: In per-job work, judge the full time and cost of a job rather than treating its payout as an hourly wage.
- *operations*: Training can reduce time spent figuring out provider requirements, avoiding scams, and correcting preventable mistakes.
- *niche_selection*: A fragmented job-search process can create a product opportunity: shoppers want one place to find assignments across providers.
- *scaling*: Build a reputation by meeting the requirements consistently; providers and shoppers depend on each other for the work to happen.
- *mindset_execution*: Give an opportunity a fair trial before deciding it is not worthwhile; small commitments can lead to unexpected connections.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not cash a check sent before an assignment or follow instructions to send money elsewhere; the guest says this can lead to bank-account problems.
- Do not buy gift cards and send the codes, even if someone promises reimbursement.
- Verify provider legitimacy: a mismatched email domain or an offer that sounds too good to be true is a red flag.
- Some legitimate shops require upfront purchases, so confirm the provider and reimbursement terms before spending.
- A shopper can lose time and pay if a report does not follow the instructions or is not written clearly.
- Signing up with too many providers immediately can become overwhelming; start with a few and expand at a manageable pace.
- Third-party mystery shopping apps may not show all available jobs and may surface lower-paying assignments.
- Provider pay schedules differ, so there is no single payment timetable; PayPal is described as common.
- Some providers rotate shoppers or restrict repeat visits to keep employees from recognizing the shopper.
- The guest sells paid training and promotes ShopQuest, so her recommendations and success stories have a promotional interest.
