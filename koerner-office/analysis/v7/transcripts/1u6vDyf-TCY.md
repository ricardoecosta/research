## Tactics and playbook steps

**Evaluate partners on trust and value** (hiring)
1. Check for moral/trust issues: would they steal or ignore your interests?
2. Check the value equation: do they add more value than they take from the business?
3. Reassess as the business grows, because a high performer may outgrow others' equity stakes.
4. Avoid splitting equity many ways in small businesses with a capped market.
> "there's two reasons why partnerships fail. It's like a moral issue. Somebody is literally untrustworthy." — Nick Huber (~1:25)

**Run a portfolio but cut losers** (finance)
1. Expect a power law: a few businesses produce nearly all the value.
2. Shut down businesses that are unprofitable and have no potential.
3. Leave a bad business when its structure is brutal, rather than pushing on with blinders on.
> "if I would have had blinders on trying to take storage squad to 200 million dollars of revenue, I would still be banging my head against the wall" — Nick Huber (~4:15)

## Lessons

- *hiring_outsourcing*: Good partners are hard to find and finding one can be partly luck; Huber's 50/50 deal with Dan Haggberg worked but he has also had bad partners.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Partnerships fail for two reasons: moral failure (untrustworthiness) and an unbalanced value equation.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Business outcomes follow a power law: even with strong advantages, about three of 11 companies drive almost all the wealth.
- *niche_selection*: Focusing on one business raises the odds of success statistically, but only if it is a good business.
- *niche_selection*: Business choice matters: labour-heavy, seasonal, low-margin models like student moving can be worth leaving for better models like self-storage.
- *mindset_execution*: Business is about momentum: as you earn, build a network and gain skill, better opportunities arrive, so stay open-minded without scattering focus.
- *mindset_execution*: Huber no longer thinks everyone should be an entrepreneur, since business is hard even with big advantages.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Splitting equity three ways in a small local business like car detailing can leave nobody making money even at max potential.
- Partnerships fail from untrustworthiness or from an unbalanced value equation where someone takes more than they add.
- Staying in a structurally bad business (agency, labour-heavy moving and storage) can trap you; focus only pays if the business is good.
- Trying to do 10 things at once can lead to a lot of nothing.
