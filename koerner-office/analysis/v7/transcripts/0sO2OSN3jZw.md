## Tactics and playbook steps

**Dogfood on the ground in your core market** (product)
1. Travel to the app's biggest hotspot (he spent four months in Bangkok)
2. Attend meetups through your own app
3. Talk to users to find feature ideas and underserved sub-segments
> "going to meet up to my own app and talking to people. And I got so many ideas for new things to add" — Jay (~5:07)

**Work in focused blocks with breaks and location changes** (operations)
1. Check phone first for anything broken or time-sensitive
2. Two hours locked in on the top task
3. Take breaks to re-centre; move location after 3-4 hours
4. Light work in the evening
> "after three, four hours, even one place like I need to just move" — Jay (~4:11)

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: Having a safety net (a big tech job you could get again) lowers the real risk of trying; regret can be the bigger risk.
- *validation_testing*: Being physically among your users generates better feature ideas and reveals sub-segments.
- *mindset_execution*: Passion is a competitive advantage: someone having fun every day is hard to out-compete.
- *operations*: Hours have diminishing returns without breaks; breaks restore full focus.
- *validation_testing*: You can build things very fast now and test ideas alongside a job.
- *mindset_execution*: A non-entrepreneur engineer can bootstrap a consumer app to large scale as a solo founder.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Products that mature often drift away from the original problem they solved; stay grounded with users.
- Working only for money or status on something you do not enjoy will be miserable.
