## Summary

Justin Hall (The Hall ATL) rented a 3,000 sq ft former church unit in a Stone Mountain, Georgia strip mall and converted it into an event venue for birthdays, baby showers, repasses, anniversaries, weddings and fashion shows, while keeping his 9-5 job. He claims about $16K/month average revenue (about 50% net margin) with only his wife helping. Key tactics: organic build-in-public Instagram, cheap Facebook/Instagram ad specials, direct mail aimed at milestone birthdays, a QR-code photo wall that generates repeat leads, and recurring Sunday church rentals that cover rent. Main takeaways: parking, zoning/licensing and lease terms matter more than the building, and local competitors' weak marketing and booking systems are the opening. All figures are self-reported and unverified.

### I1. Rented strip-mall event venue (The Hall ATL)

- **Customer:** Locals in a 5-7 mile radius: people aged 50-70 for milestone birthdays, baby showers, repasses, anniversaries, weddings; East African, Mexican and apartment-dwelling communities; churches.
- **Problem:** People want a venue that is easy to book, looks good, and handles decor/convenience; existing venues are low-tech, poorly marketed and double-book.
- **Offer:** Furnished event hall with LED wall, screens, tables/chairs, prep kitchen with warmers, security, plus decor packages; the pitch is convenience, not just space.
- **Business model:** Per-event venue rental with tiered packages and upsells (screens, decor, linens, step-and-repeat, standees); deposits; monthly church rentals.
- **Pricing:** Tiered 6-hour packages plus weekday discounts and monthly church rates. - **Acquisition:** organic_social_content: New venue Instagram page with build-in-public videos (pulling carpet, kids painting, building DJ booth/chafing table/bar); this alone produced about 10 events before opening and could have produced about 50. Church-space posts outperform everything else.; paid_social: Facebook/Instagram ads with static images of package specials; initially targeted 22 miles out, now only 5-7 miles around the venue's zip. ### I2. Recurring church space rental

- **Customer:** Small churches (15-40 members) lacking their own building.

- **Startup cost:** Uses existing venue.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build in public on a fresh Instagram page** (acquisition)
1. Create a new venue-specific Instagram account
2. Post renovation videos (pulling carpet, kids painting, building the DJ booth, bar, chafing table)
3. Convert personal followers to the page
4. Have pricing ready since enquiries arrive before opening
5. Offer the unfinished venue at a discount to start earning

**Package-special Facebook ad blitz** (acquisition)
1. Create static ads for a discounted decor package (e.g. gold package at $1,000 instead of $2,800)
2. Geo-target 5-7 miles around the venue
3. Book tours and close about 70%

**Milestone-birthday direct mail** (acquisition)
1. Pull lists of people turning 50/55/60
2. Start mailing 3 months before the birthday
3. Send three touches at about 50 cents each
4. Use custom birthday cards from the hall

**QR-code photo wall referral loop** (acquisition)
1. Place QR codes on pillars, tables and screens
2. Guests upload selfies shown on the LED wall, entering phone and email
3. Add a tipping feature for the guest of honour
4. Send the full gallery afterwards with a Google review request
5. Market to captured leads; about 8% book

**Pre-sell with personalised mockups** (sales)
1. Have clients fill out a questionnaire
2. Create screen visuals with their colours and photos before the tour
3. Show them on screens when they arrive to sell the transformation

**Negotiate free rent for build-out** (finance)
1. Submit a detailed business plan to landlord
2. Ask for months of free rent while building out (he got 4-6 months)
3. Check there's no clause allowing competing venues

**Source furniture cheaply** (operations)
1. Buy tables on Facebook Marketplace/Craigslist for about $30 vs $120 retail
2. Spend about $5K on all tables and chairs

**Fill weekdays** (sales)
1. Offer $450 blank canvas Monday-Thursday
2. Give free networking events to Chamber of Commerce
3. Target corporate events at reduced rates

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Low-tech, poorly marketed incumbents are an opportunity: simple online booking, social presence and fast follow-up beat them.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Rent rather than buy; asset-light venue businesses need little capital.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Fixed overhead makes margins rise with revenue; fill capacity with higher-priced packages and upsells.
- *offer_design*: Sell convenience and transformation, not square footage.
- *validation_testing*: Launch imperfect and start earning; early demand arrived before the venue was finished.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Recurring tenants such as churches stabilise cash flow and cover fixed rent.
- *customer_acquisition*: Customers can be the marketing: every guest is a lead if you capture contact info and offer a reason to share.
- *mindset_execution*: Local relationships with regulators (showing up with muffins) can unblock permitting delays.
- *customer_acquisition*: Focus ads tightly on a 5-7 mile radius rather than a whole metro area.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Don't fall in love with the property; parking, county zoning and liquor licence rules matter more.
- Ensure the lease has non-compete protection; four rival venues moved into his plaza.
- Get written confirmation on licences; a wrong answer on an entertainment licence cost four months.
- Perfectionism delayed launch and burned rent; launch earlier.
- Put no-open-flame and animal rules in the contract and hold deposits; a goat was slaughtered in the venue.
- A full wedding for $3,500 was unprofitable; underpricing early.
