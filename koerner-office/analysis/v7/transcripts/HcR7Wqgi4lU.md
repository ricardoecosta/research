## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Mike from Iowa City, a tile installer who launched a garage-floor concrete coatings business (flake floors using polyurea base and polyaspartic top coat) around February-March 2026 with his brother and two experienced partners. He claims $60K revenue in April and $69K in May with over 50% profit, driven almost entirely by Meta ads, instant phone follow-up, upfront $8/sq ft pricing and a 10% deposit. Key takeaways: blue-collar service bars are low (answer the phone, show up, be transparent on price), AI (Claude) writes all ad copy and advises on ad budgets, and startup cost can be roughly $15-25K or under $1K by renting a grinder.

### I1. Garage floor flake coatings (Old Cap Coatings)

- **Pricing:** Flat $8 per sq ft for everything, published in ads; add-ons for steps and stem walls. Standard price USD 8 per sq ft; Average job (~700-750 sq ft) USD 5600 per job; Optional extra to have contents moved USD 1 per sq ft; Referral bonus USD 200 per referral Premium vs competitors; one competitor about 50% cheaper. ## Tactics and playbook steps

**Instant callback on every lead** (sales)
1. Dedicated sales phone to a salesperson
2. Call every lead within minutes; overnight leads first thing in the morning
3. Offer to visit same day or afternoon
4. Take 10% non-refundable deposit to book
> "If somebody submits a lead on our if they click on our ad, we just call them and they're always like, "Wow, you called me right away."" — Mike (~12:05)

**Publish pricing in ads (They Ask You Answer)** (pricing)
1. Put the price per sq ft in the ad and make 'how much does it cost?' the hook
2. Quote the price at the start of calls to filter tyre-kickers
3. Pre-qualify distant leads: '$8/sq ft, 500 ft is $4,000, is that within expectations?'
> "People want to know the cost of what you're selling them." — Mike (~12:58)

**Add a qualifying question to Meta lead forms** (acquisition)
1. Replace one-tap instant form with a form asking 'what are you interested in' (interior, garage, exterior, other)
2. Confirm contact info then submit
3. Accept higher cost per lead for better intent; redirect to website after submit
> "that one question has like made our quality of leads so much better." — Mike (~17:36)

**Reverse-engineer price from cost** (pricing)
1. Calculate materials per sq ft
2. Add monthly overhead divided by target monthly sq ft (7,000)
3. Add labour at about $35/hr lead plus helper
4. Double the total to get about 50% gross profit
> "So then that's where I say just double it." — Mike (~10:36)

**Use Claude to run Meta ads** (acquisition)
1. Tell Claude your business, location and audience
2. Ask for headlines and descriptions for each video
3. Screenshot Meta Ads Manager and ask Claude how to reallocate budget
4. Use real process videos, not still images
> "All of our ads are copy and pasted from Claud." — Mike (~0:55)

**Validate with early-bird discount jobs** (acquisition)
1. Do your own garage first as a test
2. Offer early-bird discount to friends/church for 3 jobs
3. Learn from failures then launch paid ads
> "We'll give you an early bird discount if you want to be our guinea pig." — Mike (~23:20)

**Rent equipment to test cheaply** (finance)
1. Rent grinder at ~$300/day
2. Buy consumables
3. Do one job to recoup costs
4. Buy equipment later
> "You could rent one, buy the equipment, prove out the model, you do one job, you make all your money back." — Chris Koerner (~39:46)

**Choose fast-cure products for one-day jobs** (operations)
1. Use polyurea base so base, flake and top coat finish in a day
2. Schedule Mon-Thu with Friday as flex for weather
3. Pay crew 40 hours a week regardless
> "if we can do a job in one day and my competitors doing in two, I'm making more money than them" — Mike (~53:07)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: The bar in blue-collar services is low: answering the phone, showing up on time and being honest about price differentiates you.
- *pricing*: Charging a premium (not matching the cheapest competitor) attracts better customers, avoids volume grind and holds up in recessions.
- *customer_acquisition*: Visually satisfying processes make cheap, high-performing social video ads; lead-gen on Meta can beat Google for discretionary visual services.
- *validation_testing*: A cheap, low-risk test (own garage, friends at discount, rented equipment) validates the business before heavy investment.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Reverse-engineer price from full cost (materials, overhead per unit, labour) and double for about 50% gross margin.
- *automation_ai*: Using AI instead of an agency for ads saves thousands per month; paste dashboards into the model for optimization.
- *operations*: Choosing materials that shorten the job (one day vs two) is a business decision that raises throughput.
- *mindset_execution*: Don't over-plan; start, learn by doing and treat early failures as tuition.
- *pricing*: Fixed capacity means you can hold your price: if a lead balks, another buyer will fill the slot.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: A skilled-trade background gives a sales and execution edge but isn't required; partners with field experience de-risked the launch.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Using a supplier-suggested product they didn't know caused an early floor failure (bubbling and peeling) and a full regrind and redo.
- Instant lead forms produced low-intent leads who did not recall submitting; add friction.
- Partnering with strangers is risky; Mike says he got lucky and wouldn't recommend it generally.
- Rushing without education makes mistakes costly (wasted materials and time to redo).
- Some US states (e.g. California) require contractor licences with prior experience.
- Visually appealing ads invite more competitors.
