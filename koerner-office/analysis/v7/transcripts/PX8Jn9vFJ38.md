## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Brandon Gdosc, a Dallas-based former corporate 'AI operations' lead who five months earlier started a solo AI consulting and services business. He claims roughly $30k of small projects in the first six weeks, then growth to an ~$800k annual run rate (about $60-80k/month). He sells audits ($10-15k), fractional AI-executive retainers ($20-35k/month) and custom builds, mainly to 400-6,000 employee companies. Key takeaways: price high, ask discovery questions instead of solutioning, use a three-level solution framework, and build solutions yourself rather than just advising. ## Tactics and playbook steps

**Ratchet pricing upward deal by deal** (pricing)
1. Research market rates using ChatGPT/Claude, noting big-consultancy premiums
2. Price the first jobs low ($4-5k) for proof
3. Raise the price on each next proposal while you're confident of delivering value
4. Stop raising when value no longer justifies it
> "I would do one at a at a certain price and the next one I would price a little bit." — Brandon Gdosc (~19:39)

**Discovery calls that ask, not solution** (sales)
1. Intro and rapport
2. Ask about their journey and feelings about AI
3. Ask about workflows and pain points ('where does that happen, how long')
4. Use SPIN-style implication questions
5. Do not offer solutions on the call
6. Return later with several engagement options
> "I try to never solution on those calls." — Brandon Gdosc (~40:19)

**Three-level solution laddering** (operations)
1. Level 1: existing features in ChatGPT etc.
2. Level 2: stitch tools together (Zapier, Relevance AI)
3. Level 3: custom orchestrated builds mixing deterministic code and AI
4. Always start at level 1 and move up only if needed; level 2 often gives 80% of the benefit

**Install internal AI operators** (operations)
1. Ask who in the client wants to work with you (5-10% are already keen)
2. Train them in discovery
3. Have them submit templated AI solutions briefs on a platform
4. Aggregate briefs for leadership to compute ROI and prioritise

**Capture baseline time during discovery to prove ROI** (sales)
1. Ask how many hours each task takes
2. Log time saved per run on a dashboard
3. Point to savings later (e.g. 92 hours, $2,455)

**Workflow-first mapping** (sales)
1. Use Miro or Lucid with the client
2. Map each box and what happens next
3. Find workflow opportunities before app ideas

## Lessons

- *automation_ai*: A broad skill set plus AI leverage lets a technical generalist deliver work much faster; being able to build matters more than only advising.
- *pricing*: People tend to underprice; hot demand lets you ratchet price up until value is questionable.
- *offer_design*: Sell outcomes tied to revenue or cost savings; executives need proof AI isn't hype.
- *offer_design*: Consultants who build solutions, not just recommend, remove client friction and win more.
- *scaling*: Start small projects, deliver, then expand; each project uncovers follow-on work.
- *customer_acquisition*: Closing larger companies is not necessarily harder than small ones, but delivery involves more bureaucracy.
- *mindset_execution*: Credibility and honesty are a differentiator in a hype-filled market.
- *automation_ai*: Hybrid deterministic code plus probabilistic AI is the most reliable agentic pattern right now.
- *niche_selection*: Timing of a hot market plus matched skills makes the work hard but not complicated; reputation reduced the need to hustle for leads.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Don't solution on discovery calls; it ends discovery and risks offering something undoable.
- Vibe coding without understanding models, databases and connections leads to apps that break and can't be fixed.
- Companies with heavy PII (fintech, healthcare) are harder due to clearances and security.
- Agentic AI promises are not yet fulfilled; be honest with clients about hype.
- Staff may see consultant as threat to jobs; address fear and concerns.
