### I1. Single-function utility site monetised with ads (Word Unscrambler)

- **Customer:** Word-game players (Scrabble, Wordle, Words with Friends) searching for help
- **Problem:** Players want to find words from a set of letters
- **Offer:** Free web tool that unscrambles letters into words, supported by supporting content on game tips
- **Business model:** Display advertising on high-traffic free tool pages
- **Pricing:** Free to users; ad revenue.  Revenue estimates third-party: up to 5-6 million USD per year
- **Acquisition:** seo_organic_search: Targets search intent keywords with extra content (Wordle tips, Sudoku tips, Boggle strategy) to build content authority.
- **Delivery:** Fully automated website; built by one person (Ramesh in India) in 2014 as a weekend project, portfolio now about 17 million monthly visitors.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Traffic estimated ~8 million visits/month; Ad income estimates up to 5-6 million USD per year, host says even 100k USD/month on low end; Portfolio about 17 million monthly visitors
- **Tools:** AI coding tools
- **Startup cost:** not stated
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 5/5 — Works identically in the UK; ad networks pay in GBP/USD. Google ranking competition and AI-search risk apply.
- **Risks:** Traffic estimates are third-party and unverified; Google algorithm updates or AI answers can remove demand; Highly competitive; early mover advantage dated 2014
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 4/5 (SEO content and programmatic pages can be agent-generated; occasional human review.); onboarding 5/5 (No onboarding; visitors use the tool.); delivery 5/5 (Tool runs unattended once built.); billing 5/5 (Ad network pays automatically.); support 5/5 (Essentially none needed.)
> "it's absolutely something you could build with AI today. 100%." — Nick Loper (~1:45)
> "some estimates range as high as five or six million dollars a year from the traffic on this site built in 2014 by this guy, Ramesh in India" — Nick Loper (~1:20)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Facebook content factory with VA and SOP** (operations)
1. Get approved for Facebook monetisation (around 10k followers; Deakin had 50-60k) by clicking the interest button
2. Write a brand SOP with filters (no debt promotion)
3. Have a VA use Canva templates to produce posts
4. Start with 1 post/day, double up on formats that work (e.g. sample budgets), scale to ~16/day
5. Use AI to generate content ideas and graphics
6. Use a reward app to track daily output (7 unique posts)

**Closed-loop ad reporting via MCP** (operations)
1. Connect Supermetrics to Claude
2. Connect Google Ads/CallRail to CRM like Jobber
3. Match ad click to revenue per job
4. Ask Claude for best and worst ads and actions

**Best, worst or only niche positioning** (acquisition)
1. Choose a niche where you can be the only provider
2. Pair with a gimmick that earns press

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Niche down to be best, worst or only in a market to stand out.
- *offer_design*: You only need to stay one step ahead of students to teach a skill.
- *scaling*: Build a system for one platform before syndicating to others.
- *operations*: Early revenue is small; systems and SOPs make content creation sustainable.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Closing the loop between ad spend and revenue improves ad decisions.
- *automation_ai*: Experience-based businesses are harder for AI to replace.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Home-food marketplaces face heavy food safety and licensing regulation.
- Relying on going viral is risky.
- Facebook now flags AI content.
- Simple sites can be killed by Google updates (episodeguidetv.com).
