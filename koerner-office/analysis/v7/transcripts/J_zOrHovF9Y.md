## Tactics and playbook steps

**Spread risk across several ventures** (mindset)
1. Avoid focusing on a single business
2. Start things with only a little money
3. Treat time and opportunity cost as the main risk
> "which is why I preach the opposite of focus. Spread out your risk. Focusing on just one thing is a risk in and of itself." — Chris Koerner (~1:39)

**Revisit your original numbers and plan** (mindset)
1. Go back to the spreadsheet from when you first got excited about the idea
2. Re-read the metrics and the plan
3. Assume it will take longer than expected (about 95% of the time)
4. Write your mission or core values somewhere you see daily
> "go back to that spreadsheet that you made when you were hyped about this. Look at it. Remember those numbers." — Chris Koerner (~6:00)

**Log small wins as traction** (mindset)
1. Notice and name small progress, such as an email being opened
2. Call out traction regularly to dampen emotional swings
> "the more traction you point out and call out, the less turbulence you'll have." — Chris Koerner (~8:11)

**Tell your spouse how you want to be supported** (mindset)
1. Say when their feedback raises your stress
2. Ask them to listen and let you vent without follow-up questions
> "I just want you to listen. Just soak it in." — Chris Koerner (~7:26)

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: The main risk of starting a business is time and opportunity cost rather than money, so run small, cheap bets in parallel.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Plan for income volatility, including whole years of zero income, and make sure the good years outweigh the bad.
- *mindset_execution*: Expect timelines to run long: most projects take longer than planned and only about 5% come in faster.
- *mindset_execution*: Emotional turbulence is the hardest part; a stoic, steady demeanour helps family and team feel safe.
- *validation_testing*: Regularly record the original thesis and metrics so you can judge whether to continue or stop, instead of reacting to mood.
- *mindset_execution*: Build interaction with other founders, since non-entrepreneurs often cannot relate and isolation worsens fear.
- *mindset_execution*: Efforts compound even when a specific initiative fails, so failed ventures are not wasted.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Entrepreneurship always takes longer, costs more and is harder than expected; without understanding the downsides you should not attempt it.
- Founders underestimate isolation, and friends and family often cannot relate.
- Deals that seem life-changing frequently fall apart; avoid pinning hopes on a single deal or partnership.
