### I1. Karaoke booth on revenue share in a host venue

- **Customer:** Groups of friends wanting private karaoke in the evening
- **Problem:** No private place to sing without an audience
- **Offer:** One private hour (later 30-minute base unit) in a soundproof booth, self check-in by code
- **Business model:** Hourly room rental; 20-30% revenue share paid to host venue instead of rent
- **Pricing:** Hourly / 30-minute slots. USD 30-150 per hour; example rate USD 50 per hour Weekday afternoons 10-20% below peak; repriced to a 30-minute base unit with hourly as a discount.
- **Acquisition:** partnerships: Host venue (bar, bowling alley, entertainment complex) funnels its regulars; revenue share 20-30%.; seo_organic_search: Local search for 'karaoke room near me'.
- **Delivery:** Booth installed in host venue; online calendar generates a code per booking that expires at slot end; 15-minute buffers; no staff.
- **Unit economics (claims):** 11 booked hours/week at $50/h = ~$1,800/month; Running cost ~$15/hour vs $50-150 rent; With drinks/snacks operators report $1,100-4,000 per week
- **Tools:** Online booking calendar, Keypad smart lock
- **Startup cost:** not stated
- **Time to first revenue:** Months 1-2: $300-500/month in the composite story
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — Works in UK venues; UK needs music licensing (PPL PRS) for public karaoke, and host venue alcohol licence. Revenue share with pubs plausible.
- **Risks:** Utilisation risk; most walk-ups want 30-45 minutes; Music licensing (PPL PRS in UK); Dependence on host venue footfall
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 4/5 (Inbound local search plus host venue footfall; listings and ads automatable.); onboarding 5/5 (Online booking calendar issues the slot and door code.); delivery 2/5 (Booth runs itself; cleaning/reset between groups and host coordination are human.); billing 5/5 (Card payment taken at booking.); support 3/5 (FAQs and rebooking automated; damage/lockouts need a human or host venue.)
> "A room like that costs about 15 dollars an hour to run. It rents for 50 to 150. That gap is the whole business" — Narrator (~2:43)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Host-venue revenue share instead of rent** (operations)
1. Place the unit (booth, pod, bay) inside a venue with existing footfall
2. Pay the host 20-30% of takings instead of fixed rent
3. Online booking calendar generates an expiring door code per booking
4. Add 15-minute buffers between bookings
> "Put 15 minutes of buffer between every booking from day one" — Narrator (~5:07)

**Price the unit customers want** (pricing)
1. Check real utilisation vs booked hours
2. Reprice around a 30-minute base unit
3. Make the hourly rate the discount for staying longer
> "Price the unit the customer actually wants to buy, not the unit that is easiest to bill" — Narrator (~10:45)

**Three filters before buying a unit** (product)
1. Does the crowd already walk past the door?
2. Can the space be reset without you?
3. Which hours does it fill; stack units whose peak hours differ
> "Does the crowd already walk past the door? If the answer is no, that is a marketing problem being bought" — Narrator (~17:57)

**10-minute demand check on maps** (acquisition)
1. Search maps for nearest karaoke room, golf bay and private dog field

**Certificate moat plus recurring contract** (sales)
1. Pick a trade with a cheap fast certificate
2. Earn it
3. Sell a recurring contract (weekly/quarterly/nightly) not one-off jobs
4. Use a one-page scope and term contract for every account
> "He wrote a simple scope and term contract for every account after that" — Narrator (~27:25)

## Lessons

- *automation_ai*: The calendar and the expiring lock replace the employee for space-rental businesses.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Utilisation, not sticker price, decides unit economics.
- *scaling*: Stack units whose demand peaks at different hours in one footprint.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Revenue share with a host turns fixed rent into a variable cost.
- *niche_selection*: A cheap certificate most competitors skip can be the moat.
- *pricing*: Never sign a recurring contract at a price you cannot service twice.
- *exit_acquisition*: A business is worth more the less the owner is required (Built to Sell).
- *operations*: Route density drives profit in service routes.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Buying two pods before the first proves its building.
- Hourly pricing hid half-empty bookings; real utilisation was half of the booking sheet.
- Handshake deals with no written scope lead to unpaid extra work.
- Chasing one-off jobs instead of recurring contracts.
- Figures are composite ranges from a faceless channel; 'Harry' is a narrative device.
- UK: unsupervised UV sunbeds are banned; door-to-door and cold calling to homes need TPS checks under PECR; cold SMS to homeowners is illegal without consent.
