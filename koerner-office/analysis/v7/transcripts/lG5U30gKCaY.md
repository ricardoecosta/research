## Tactics and playbook steps

**Search a less crowded adjacent Upwork category** (acquisition)
1. Search for the client's underlying need, such as SEO, rather than the trend label AI automation.
2. Look for a slightly different but related niche where the guest perceived less competition.
3. Use AI to learn enough about the client's problem to propose a relevant workflow.
> "I didn't type AI automation. I didn't type AI SEO. I only typed SEO." — Sandy Lee (~8:14)

**Build a portfolio from transferable experience** (sales)
1. List past work, including customer-facing jobs.
2. Ask ChatGPT to connect those experiences to automation skills and produce example numbers or use cases.
3. Create portfolio examples that demonstrate work; Sandy says they do not need to be testimonials.
> "Portfolio doesn't necessarily mean it's your testimonial. It's what you've been building." — Sandy Lee (~6:41)

**Send a short Loom pitch before building** (sales)
1. Record a Loom video explaining the proposed client workflow and where you would help.
2. Keep it about two to three minutes.
3. Practice and re-record if needed; Sandy says she did not edit the video.
4. Pitch the workflow before building a full product.
> "It was about two to three minutes. I found that that's the sweet spot because a lot of people don't really want to watch a long video." — Sandy Lee (~16:50)

**Use AI to learn immediately before performing** (operations)
1. Ask an AI tool to explain the steps for the task or topic.
2. Study the answer and use it to prepare a workflow or lesson.
3. Record or deliver the work, then check the result and remain accountable to the client.
> "if you don't know anything what you're going to film, you learn right before." — Sandy Lee (~20:35)

**Expand a successful retainer by extending the same workflow** (pricing)
1. Observe when the client's SEO work is producing results.
2. Identify adjacent markets or languages where the existing automation can be extended.
3. Explain the additional value, then propose a higher retainer.
> "So that's I said, "Hey, I'm gonna expand this to Korean and Spanish, and that way you can you're going to pay me more."" — Sandy Lee (~17:59)

**Use content as proof even with a small audience** (acquisition)
1. Publish demonstrations and practical videos about the work you are learning.
2. Reference relevant videos when approaching prospects so they can assess your knowledge and style.
3. Use the content to support inbound opportunities instead of waiting for viral reach.
> "I was still referencing my YouTube video and that was when I had 200 subscribers, very little, but still is giving that authority" — Sandy Lee (~20:20)

**Choose a creator topic with audience and monetization fit** (niche_selection)
1. Assess what you love doing and what you are good at.
2. Also assess what people will pay for and what the world needs.
3. Consider which audience and distribution paths make the offer easier to monetize.
> "I focus on all different four things of what do I love to do and what are you good at? What can you get paid for? What does the world need?" — Sandy Lee (~22:43)

**Turn audience feedback into app ideas** (product)
1. Publish a practical build or workflow video.
2. Listen for repeated audience comments about what is missing or hard to use.
3. Test the app idea with the audience before investing more in development.
4. Use an app as a lead magnet for a related community if that fits the offer.
> "And again, I didn't build it and then sell it. Do you see the difference? I sold the idea first and then saw what is needed and then build a product after." — Sandy Lee (~26:33)

**Copy proven content formats and adapt them** (product)
1. Find outlier videos and formats that already work for the intended audience.
2. Use the structure as a starting point rather than trying to invent every format from scratch.
3. Adapt the format to your own topic and audience.
> "if you're original all the time, it's hard for you to get visible." — Sandy Lee (~28:07)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Sell a proposed solution and confirm interest before spending time building a full product.
- *customer_acquisition*: A slightly different adjacent niche may have less competition than the obvious AI-labelled category.
- *offer_design*: A portfolio can show examples of what you have built, even when you do not have client testimonials yet.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI to accelerate learning and execution, but remain responsible for checking the work and following up with clients.
- *customer_acquisition*: Publishing useful content can establish credibility and generate leads before an account has a large audience.
- *niche_selection*: Choose a content niche by considering skill and interest alongside audience need and ability to monetize.
