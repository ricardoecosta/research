## Tactics and playbook steps

**Start with a real problem and a founder advantage** (validation_testing)
1. Identify a problem you understand from repeated direct contact with customers.
2. Use experience from an existing job or business to shape a solution; Catherine drew on her yoga teaching and her own ability to sleep.
3. Test the solution as a small podcast instead of waiting for a book deal or a large launch.
> "I just thought there's a problem. I know how to fix it. How can I help fix it? That was really the extent of my focus at the time." — Catherine (~2:57)

**Publish reliably and improve the product before chasing promotion** (operations)
1. Choose a release schedule you can keep.
2. Make the content quality and listener experience the main early focus.
3. Build a repeatable production calendar and publish when promised.
> "That was really focused on just having the show publish when I said it was going to publish regularly and reliably and developing the content in a way that I felt like was going to be the highest quality it could be." — Catherine (~10:09)

**Let a meaningful result create referrals** (acquisition)
1. Solve a problem in a way customers can describe to someone else.
2. Tell people in the communities you already serve and let satisfied users recommend the product.
3. Use listener comments and customer feedback to see which results prompt sharing.
> "Because if sleep is something you struggle with and you sort that out, that is a life changer. You're going to tell people about that." — Catherine (~3:55)

**Ask your network for a specific introduction** (acquisition)
1. Tell a trusted network what help you need.
2. Ask directly whether someone knows the right person.
3. Use the introduction to reach a qualified agent or partner.
> "I reached out to the network I was with at the time. I said, do you know anybody? I'm never afraid to just ask for help" — Catherine (~14:02)

**Use proof of audience to open publishing doors** (sales)
1. Build a measurable audience around the core idea.
2. Work with a literary agent to turn the idea into a proposal.
3. Use evidence of demand when approaching publishers.
> "I don't think I would have had that response if I didn't have 10 million downloads. That's what opened the doors for me." — Catherine (~14:42)

**Set ad placement rules around the product promise** (pricing)
1. Decide which ad placements preserve the core experience before agreeing to a network deal.
2. Allow ads only before the sleep story or near the end of the introduction.
3. Do not interrupt after the listener has been tucked in.
> "And it really shortened the list of advertisers for us. If we were with Stitcher and I heart at different times, and because we weren't always the lowest hanging fruit" — Catherine (~21:51)

**Choose an ad partner that understands the audience** (sales)
1. Compare ad networks based on how much effort they put into selling the show and whether their advertisers fit.
2. Explain why listeners are attentive even though they may be falling asleep.
3. Move to a smaller specialist network if it provides more focused sales support.
> "So we then moved to just in the last two years to a much smaller kind of boutique network, and it has been such a good choice for us." — Catherine (~22:35)

**Hand off specialist work when it is outside your skill set** (hiring_outsourcing)
1. Identify work that is distracting from making the product or that you do not know how to negotiate.
2. Use an agent, manager or specialist partner for that work.
3. Keep your own time focused on the product you are best at creating.
> "I just knew enough to know I would not be successful in trying to negotiate those things myself. Luckily, by the time we moved a little bit forward in time, I had a manager, I had an agent" — Catherine (~24:07)

**Make premium access easy in the listener's existing player** (offer_design)
1. Offer ad-free and bonus content through a listener's familiar podcast player where possible.
2. Support both Apple and Android listeners through suitable routes.
3. Keep the free show available so a paid offer does not block existing listeners.
> "For a lot of people, nothing changed, except for the logo was a little different and they didn't hear ads. And that's the level of experience I wanted people to have." — Catherine (~26:12)

**Present membership as optional support, not an apology** (pricing)
1. Explain what paid members receive and that the free version remains available.
2. Frame the annual price as a small daily amount if that is accurate.
3. Ask confidently for payment instead of apologizing for charging for the work.
> "Even though I understood that I deserved to be paid for the work I did, I still think I came at it from this place of I'm so sorry that I have to do this." — Catherine (~27:39)

**Keep a listener-friendly free experience** (offer_design)
1. Leave a meaningful free offering in place for listeners who do not want to subscribe.
2. Keep ads in a format and tone suited to the content.
3. Avoid changing the free experience in ways that undermine trust as paid membership grows.
> "And even now, we don't have programmatic ads on the first eight episodes. So you're only ever gonna hear my voice and I deliver ads in this tone of voice." — Catherine (~28:04)

**Build a story idea bank and revisit established storylines** (product)
1. Record small moments that could become stories in a running notes list.
2. Return to characters and settings listeners already know.
3. Batch related writing into focused production periods.
> "So I have a running list in my notes app that is probably 100 points long and I add to it constantly of ideas for stories." — Catherine (~37:28)

**Use a repeatable calming story structure** (product)
1. Open with a naturally calming activity.
2. Add sensory details to make the listener feel present in the scene.
3. Use familiar or nostalgic situations and past-tense framing to reassure anxious listeners that events have already ended safely.
4. Leave characters unnamed so listeners can imagine themselves in the story.
> "I also do stuff very deliberately, none of my characters have names. Because I want you to sort of feel like the character, you could be the innkeeper, you could be the baker" — Catherine (~40:02)

**Batch production ahead of release** (operations)
1. Write a month ahead using a strict calendar.
2. Group writing by show, then edit and record the batch.
3. Keep a schedule that matches the creator's energy and creative workflow.
> "I write a month ahead and I have like a really strict calendar that I stick to." — Catherine (~40:59)

**Meet customers in the format they already use** (product)
1. Offer the core content in audio, book and video formats when customer behavior supports them.
2. Design each format for its context, such as dimming video for bedtime.
3. Use the app concept to add playback features that standard podcast feeds cannot provide.
> "My goal is always like, I'm gonna meet you where you are. If you've got your phone in your hand, I'm gonna meet you there. You read books, I'm gonna meet you there." — Catherine (~18:31)

**Do not wait to feel ready** (mindset)
1. Make a decision to begin with a small experiment.
2. Learn from actual customers instead of waiting for confidence or perfect preparation.
> "Stop waiting to feel ready. Ready isn't a feeling, it's a choice." — Catherine (~47:45)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Start with a problem you understand well enough to solve, then test a simple version before building a larger product.
- *niche_selection*: A niche can have a broad market when the underlying need is common and the product has a clear point of view.
- *operations*: Consistent publishing and high quality can build a durable audience even when early growth is uncertain.
- *customer_acquisition*: A strong customer result can create word of mouth without a paid marketing budget.
- *offer_design*: Build a product experience that keeps its promise; monetization should not disrupt the reason customers came.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Use a paid membership to let loyal customers support work they value, while preserving a free option.
- *pricing*: Price for sustainability and perceived value instead of automatically making the offer as cheap as possible.
- *scaling*: A built-in audience can reduce a publisher's uncertainty and help open doors to partners.
- *automation_ai*: Keep creative work human when the human skill is central to the product's value.
- *mindset_execution*: Do not wait for confidence to appear before starting; make a small decision and act.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Podcasting can be hard to grow; the host says most podcasts do not reach episode 10.
- Do not place disruptive ads after the listener has been tucked in; breaking the sleep promise risks losing trust and subscribers.
- Limited ad inventory made the show harder for networks to sell and contributed to concerns about low valuation during contract renewal.
- Catherine says she has repeatedly charged too little, which can hurt perceived value and sustainability.
- A paid offer can be weakened by apologizing for charging; listeners told Catherine they were happy to pay.
- Catherine says people steal her content, so content copying is a real risk for this format.
- The creator's voice is a production bottleneck; Catherine says the only time she missed an episode was when she lost her voice.
- Catherine says she chose not to use AI to help write stories because she enjoys the work and does not want that assistance.
