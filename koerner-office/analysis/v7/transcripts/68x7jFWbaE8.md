## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Nathan, a 31-year-old with a full-time auction job, who bought 10 used trailers from a retiring local operator in Lancaster, Pennsylvania for 35,000 USD and turned them into a contactless trailer rental business. After 15 months it grosses 5,500-7,500 USD a month with about 2,000-2,500 USD of overhead, taking him 2-5 hours a week. He explains the lockbox self-pickup system, pricing at ~1.5% of purchase price per day, deposits, insurance, maintenance and lead sources (Google 55%, Facebook 14%). Key takeaway: a low-tech rental asset with systemised booking can be nearly passive, and expansion via cloned small lots is the next step.

### I1. Contactless trailer rental yard

- **Customer:** Local homeowners, movers, landscapers, people towing cars, motorcyclists taking bikes on trips
- **Problem:** People need a trailer occasionally for moves, junk, landscaping or hauling and incumbents require in-person pickup at set hours
- **Offer:** Online-bookable trailers with automated rental agreement, ID/insurance upload and 24h-before pickup instructions with lockbox code
- **Business model:** Per-day rental fees plus a security deposit hold (2x daily rate); asset retains resale value.
- **Pricing:** Daily rate, roughly 1.5% of trailer purchase price per day. trailer daily rate range USD 50-150 per day; median daily rate USD 90 per day; dump trailer USD 95 per day Deposit is held on the card at two times the daily rate (about 100-250 USD). Benchmarked against larger local competitors.
- **Acquisition:** google_business_profile_maps: New Google Business Profile plus SEO website built by a trailer-rental software vendor; Google is about 55% of leads.; marketplace_platform: Facebook Marketplace listings (about 14% of leads, ~100 USD total boost spend in 15 months) plus peer-to-peer trailer platforms (Neighbor, trailer rental sites) that take a commission.; referrals_word_of_mouth: Previous owner forwards his old customers for a 35% kickback on their first rental; repeat customers and word of mouth make up the rest.; partnerships: Suggested: offer local businesses such as landscapers a 10% discount program for 3+ day rentals and easy text booking.
- **Delivery:** Owner-operated, contactless: customer books online or by phone, signs agreement on phone, gets lockbox code by text 24h before, takes before/after photos; owner checks lot twice a week; a mobile mechanic does maintenance.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Buy a small rental book from a retiring operator** (acquisition)
1. Watch Facebook Marketplace for rental businesses for sale
2. Negotiate to pay asset value only (35,000 USD for 10 trailers)
3. Replace the 20,000 USD customer-list ask with a 35% first-rental referral fee to the seller
4. Seller forwards calls; you launch fresh brand, domain and Google Business Profile
> "I made a deal with him that I'd just give him a referral fee for any previous renter" — Nathan (~4:30)

**Three-phase move to contactless pickup** (operations)
1. Phase 1: meet renters in person
2. Phase 2: hide-a-key lockbox on each trailer with code sent after booking
3. Phase 3: numbered lockbox board on a rented half-acre lot, automated agreement and ID/insurance upload, pickup text 24h before
4. Renter takes before/after photos; deposit held at 2x daily rate

**Price trailers off purchase price** (pricing)
1. Charge ~1.5% of purchase price per day
2. Check larger local competitors
3. Payback in ~75 rental days; resale value only 20-40% below cost

**Demand-led fleet expansion** (product)
1. Log every call for a trailer type you do not have
2. Buy the most-requested type next
3. Buy trailers, rent 2-3 months to test, then sell

**Market density research for next location** (acquisition)
1. Find sister cities with similar population and income
2. Count competitors and total trailers for rent from websites/calls
3. Compute residents per trailer and go where supply is thinnest

**Zero-capital demand test** (validation)
1. Post a stock trailer image on Facebook Marketplace and count enquiries
2. Or list on Neighbor/peer platforms that charge commission only
3. Host idea: rent someone else's trailer and re-list it to test at break-even

**Local business discount program** (acquisition)
1. Approach landscapers and other businesses
2. Offer 10% off rentals of 3+ days and text booking
3. Rely on repeat usage once they rent once

## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: Value a small-business purchase on the resale value of its assets so the downside is capped if it flops.
- *exit_acquisition*: Replace an up-front payment for a customer list with a performance-based referral fee.
- *automation_ai*: Front-load the time building systems so ongoing ownership is a few hours a week.
- *niche_selection*: Pick rental assets with no engines and few moving parts to minimise maintenance.
- *offer_design*: Contactless self-service can be a differentiator when no local competitor offers it.
- *validation_testing*: Test demand with listings before buying assets.
- *niche_selection*: Use residents-per-unit supply metrics to pick the next market.
- *legal_risk*: Layered theft controls (ID upload, payment before code, cameras, GPS) make unattended rental viable.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Meeting renters in person wastes time because you are on their schedule.
- Damage is inevitable; buy used first and use deposits rather than getting attached to the trailers.
- A narrow lot where trailers stack behind each other forces extra moving with a skid loader.
- Expanding to golf carts, campers or equipment adds delivery, breakdowns and time.
- Open trailers are seasonal and slow in snowy winters.
- Previous owner's books were poor; value the deal on asset resale value instead.
