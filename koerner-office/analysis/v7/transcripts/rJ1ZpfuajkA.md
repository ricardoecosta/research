## Tactics and playbook steps

**Sell expertise at the intersection of AI and a specific industry** (niche_selection)
1. Choose an industry or function where you already understand the work.
2. Learn which processes and software cause the most friction for those businesses.
3. Pair that knowledge with the ability to build and monitor agents.
4. Explain the value in terms of the client's workflow rather than the novelty of AI.
> "Few people live in the intersection of those two. And when you do like you can you can deliver a lot of very differentiated value." — Flo (~23:38)

**Show a prospect useful work before asking for a meeting** (sales)
1. Research the prospect and its public needs or active campaigns.
2. Prepare a small, relevant sample, such as matching candidates or a competitor-ad review.
3. Email the sample with a clear next step and a specific offer to provide more.
4. Follow UK PECR and data-protection rules when contacting the prospect.
> "Because sales like this brings sales from here's what I can do for you to here's what I just did for you." — Chris Koerner (~13:18)

**Use a small sample to demonstrate recruiting fit** (sales)
1. Read job descriptions from a company's careers page.
2. Extract role requirements such as skills, experience, and location.
3. Find a few candidates that appear to match.
4. Send a short example list and offer to provide more, with consent and lawful data handling.
> "This is an example of like three leads that we found and we have their email address. We can reach out to them." — Flo (~12:42)

**Use customer-language prompts and iterate** (operations)
1. Describe the task in ordinary language, including the trigger, action, and information needed.
2. Give more context by dictating if that makes it easier to explain the workflow.
3. Review the agent's proposed plan and answer its clarification questions.
4. Run the workflow, then use follow-up prompts to correct errors or refine behavior.
> "we actually recommend dictating it because you can give a lot more details when you dictate." — Flo (~5:57)

**Break complex workflows into specialized agents** (operations)
1. Split a large process into narrower tasks.
2. Assign agents to specialized tasks and define the information they pass to each other.
3. Keep the agents in one platform where their work can be monitored.
4. Review the overall workflow for failures and unintended actions.
> "You can create agents that are specialized in certain tasks and you can get them to like talk to each other and and collaborate with each other." — Flo (~10:03)

**Build the offer around demonstrated social workload** (offer_design)
1. Identify repetitive comment or inbox tasks that the creator currently leaves undone.
2. Agree on moderation or response rules with the account owner.
3. Automate routine work and keep ambiguous or sensitive cases available for human review.
4. Measure workload and outcomes before making savings claims.
> "you can offer this as a service for people that don't want to read all these toxic comments" — Chris Koerner (~38:39)

**Turn lead-magnet comments into a repeatable response workflow** (operations)
1. Publish a post offering a useful template or resource to commenters.
2. Have the agent identify each commenter and try the permitted contact method.
3. If private messaging is unavailable, use an approved public response that invites the commenter to initiate contact.
4. Monitor replies and respect platform rules and UK marketing requirements.
> "So, for example, five days ago, we posted this tweet, this comment on this post on on LinkedIn, and it's like, "I built this AI agent, you know, this is what it does." — Flo (~32:42)

**Research a brand before negotiating sponsorship** (sales)
1. Screen inbound sponsorship messages for a credible sender and relevant offer.
2. Research the brand and its apparent audience before replying.
3. Tailor the rate response to the brand and creator's audience fit.
4. Require creator approval before accepting terms or committing to deliverables.
> "as you offer a number, you could also re research the brand first, right?" — Flo (~19:46)

**Check contingency placements after introduction** (operations)
1. Record which candidates were introduced to each client and when.
2. Agree clear placement-fee terms before making introductions.
3. Use an appropriate follow-up process to confirm hiring outcomes.
4. Resolve discrepancies through the contract and human review rather than relying solely on a profile change.
> "you can have another AI agent that checks every month the LinkedIn profiles of the candidates that you introduced to customers to see if they joined the customer." — Flo (~40:59)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Choose a niche where you understand the business problem, then add technical skill; the overlap can be more valuable than generic AI knowledge.
- *other*: Customers value a concrete demonstration of work already done more than a generic promise of what a service might do.
- *automation_ai*: Reduce the gap between the person who understands the business and the person who can change the automation; faster iteration can improve the solution.
- *operations*: Use plain-language prompts, review the proposed plan, and iterate when the agent does not behave as intended.
- *operations*: A single platform for agents may simplify monitoring and coordination, especially when agents need to work together.
- *mindset_execution*: People's knowledge of their own field may be more valuable than they assume, even if they are not technical.
- *scaling*: Technology adoption moves from early advantage to basic expectation and eventually to a disadvantage for those who do not adopt.
- *legal_risk*: Automated workflows should leave room for human judgment when handling sensitive, ambiguous, or consequential interactions.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Unsolicited marketing SMS, WhatsApp, DMs, or emails to UK individuals and sole traders generally requires consent under PECR. The transcript's automated outreach examples need to be adapted for UK law and platform terms.
- The host's claim that a tailored lead email has a 100% chance of getting a reply is hypothetical and should not be treated as a measured response rate.
- The host's proposed $30,000 monthly savings for a roofing business is an unsupported hypothetical sales claim.
- Browser-based agents can take consequential actions, such as issuing refunds or blocking social users; access, permissions, and escalation rules need to be carefully limited.
- The host repeatedly promotes Lindy and says to use his affiliate link; the guest is Lindy's founder, so product claims are promotional.
- The guest reports a customer's complex agent and agency examples but gives limited independently verifiable results, pricing, or customer evidence.
