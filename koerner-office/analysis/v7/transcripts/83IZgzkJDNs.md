## Tactics and playbook steps

**Three-tier pricing with highlighted 'most popular' tier** (pricing)
1. Offer three options rather than four
2. Mark the higher-priced middle or best option as 'most popular'
3. Place an upsell tier (e.g. a membership at about $320 or a flagship package) above it to anchor price
> "Having three options is kind of good. But if you do have the fourth option, you always want to have something as the third as your best" — George McConnell (~5:35)

**Fix underperforming short-form video with a verbal hook** (acquisition)
1. Compare against the creator's winning videos to see what differs in the first seconds
2. Add a verbal hook in the first 1-2 seconds instead of starting cold on item one
3. Open with a real sales-call objection or show the wrong way vs the right way
4. Keep fast pacing and end with a comment-to-get-free-stuff CTA, which builds an audience for retargeting
> "Don't say X, say Y. I do agree with this one. All right, that is a good way to actually introduce things." — George McConnell (~16:59)

**Use richer prompts for better AI output** (product)
1. Give detailed instructions rather than 1-2 sentences
2. Include source info, images, camera angles and structure
3. Expect large quality gains, e.g. a 13-page website prompt vs a two-sentence prompt
> "That's what a 13-page prompt does versus a two sentence prompt." — George McConnell (~30:35)

**Run the same prompt on both models with the same effort setting** (operations)
1. Set both models to the same reasoning level (medium)
2. Use identical minimal prompts
3. Time each run and compare output side by side

## Lessons

- *automation_ai*: Model leadership changes week to week, so pick tools by task fit and your own workflow rather than loyalty to one model.
- *customer_acquisition*: For short-form video, a spoken hook in the first 1-2 seconds is the main determinant of retention; cold-open list videos underperform.
- *mindset_execution*: Domain expertise lets you judge AI output; the host rejected AI advice that was wrong for the context, so use AI as a draft and apply your own judgement.
- *pricing*: Pricing page design drives revenue: three tiers, a highlighted 'most popular' option and an upsell tier anchor spend upward.
- *offer_design*: Interactive booking elements (popup with service, size, add-ons, location, confirmation, and a live price calculator) make a service website convert better than a plain contact link.
- *operations*: Speed matters in agent workflows: Opus took 10 minutes where Gemini took under a minute on analysis tasks, which affects iteration cost and throughput.
- *customer_acquisition*: Using the same top-performing content across platforms (e.g. TikTok winners reposted to Instagram) is a common creator tactic.
- *validation_testing*: Single minimal-prompt comparisons are weak evidence; results vary with settings, connectors and test selection (some tests used the model's own suggested demos).

## Warnings and mistakes

- Vague one-line prompts give generic results; AI output quality depends heavily on prompting.
- AI design defaults (overused turquoise/black palettes, broken links, buttons that do nothing, missing images) need human review before shipping.
- Claude cannot generate video or images natively; third-party connectors are required and can glitch (the Opus + Higgsfield run produced a weak, off-brief video).
- Do not blindly follow AI marketing advice; the host disagreed with several suggestions (e.g. slowing down speech, 'putting the money aside' sales line).
