## Tactics and playbook steps

**Three-level evidence ladder for demand validation** (product)
1. Level one: check for existing spend, meaning people already paying for something close to your idea; competition shows the market exists.
2. Level two: collect pain in buyers' own words from forums, reviews and comments, without prompting them.
3. Read the three-star reviews of competing products to see what buyers wanted but did not get.
4. Level three: get commitment, such as a specific sign-up, a booked call, or a pre-order (the gold standard).
5. Treat opinions, such as a friend saying they would buy, as level zero that does not count.
> "Real evidence comes in three levels. Level one, there's existing spend." — Host (~0:56)

**Pre-commit a go/no-go criterion** (mindset)
1. Before testing anything, write your go / fix / park / kill criteria on a sticky note.
2. Decide what counts as a yes before you see results so you do not grade on a curve afterwards.
3. Treat a 'kill' result as a win because you learn it in a weekend rather than after six months.
> "Decide what counts as a yes before you see results because if you decide after you see results, your brain is going to grade on a curve every single time." — Host (~1:50)

**Falsifiable hypothesis sentence** (product)
1. Write: 'I believe this buyer will pay this price for this result.'
2. Underneath write: 'The evidence that would prove me wrong is...' and finish the sentence.
3. Use it to turn hope into a test.
> "I believe this buyer will pay this price for this result." — Host (~2:17)

**Weekend validation sprint (teaser of full episode)** (product)
1. Run six steps from Friday night to Sunday evening.
2. Write the offer before building the product.
3. Use a simple mock-up rather than a finished build.
4. Ask for a real commitment without being pushy.
> "why a simple mock-up beats the finished build, and how to ask for a real commitment without being pushy or even shady" — Host (~2:36)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Evidence of demand must cost the buyer something (money, time, commitment); free opinions don't count.
- *niche_selection*: Existing competitors are good news: they prove people already spend money on the problem.
- *validation_testing*: Mine three-star reviews of competing products for unmet needs in the buyers' own words.
- *validation_testing*: Pre-orders are the strongest validation signal, followed by booked calls and specific sign-ups.
- *mindset_execution*: Set kill/go criteria before testing to avoid motivated reasoning.
- *mindset_execution*: Killing an idea after a weekend test is a win that saves months of work.
- *offer_design*: Write the offer before the product, and test with a mock-up rather than a finished build.
- *automation_ai*: AI collapses build time, so the scarce resource is demand, not production; validation matters more, not less.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Building first and hoping it sells wastes weekends and money; speed of building with AI is not proof of demand.
- Friends' verbal enthusiasm is worthless as evidence because saying yes costs nothing.
- Defining success criteria after seeing results lets your brain grade on a curve.
