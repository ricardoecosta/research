## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Jay Meldrum, who left corporate professional services to open Swing Cave Golf, a single-bay members-only golf simulator in a Texas strip mall (Allen area). Six months in he has about 28 members on three tiers (USD 175/275/325 per month), roughly USD 7,000 monthly revenue, break-even at about 15 members after three months, and claimed 50-55% net margins. The business is run with 24/7 app-based door access, scheduling software and cameras, with about an hour a week of owner time. Upfront cost is about USD 100K, and he plans a second location and eventual scaling. Takeaway: a low-opex, membership-based, physical-space business can be near-passive once built, but it needs significant capital and a physical site.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Pre-sell to friends before opening** (acquisition)
1. Talk to 10+ friends and acquaintances before opening
2. Get verbal commitments from about 5 who are all in
3. Use the first early member for product feedback
4. Use feedback to adjust amenities (e.g., club straps, storage)
> "I just need these 10 friends and my costs are at least covered and I'm breaking even." — Chris Koerner (~0:19)

**Tiered membership with caps** (pricing)
1. Offer three tiers by visit count (Par/Birdie/Eagle) at 175/275/325
2. Add punch cards (5 and 10, 6-month expiry) for occasional users
3. Target a mix of enthusiasts rather than daily users
4. Cap members at about 35-40 per bay to keep tee times available

**Take over a failing tenant's lease** (operations)
1. Find a unit whose tenant is in trouble
2. Offer the landlord to step into the lease at 15% off the current rent
3. Negotiate no rent until opening
4. Use a real estate attorney friend to get tenant improvement allowance
> "If you give me 15% off of their current rent, I will step into their lease" — Jay Meldrum (~20:28)

**Location choice near homes** (operations)
1. Pick a unit within about 5 miles of the owner's home
2. Prefer neighbourhood proximity and road frontage over shopping-centre footfall
3. Check roughly 100,000 people within 5 miles for 40 members

**Low-touch operations via automation** (operations)
1. Use scheduling software to book tee times
2. Email confirmation then phone app unlocks the door 10 minutes before
3. Use cameras to monitor and ping new members for walkthrough
4. Owner or family cleans about one hour a week

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Build for a customer of one: designing the business around your own need makes decisions and persistence easier.
- *validation_testing*: Pre-sell to a small group of friends to validate demand and reduce break-even risk before spending on fit-out.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Negotiate lease terms (no rent until opening, improvement allowances) to cut risk on physical businesses.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Membership models with capped capacity give predictable recurring revenue and a break-even benchmark (15 of 40 members).
- *mindset_execution*: Passive income is rarely passive; the effort is front-loaded, and this one still needs a physical footprint and capital.
- *mindset_execution*: Don't wait for perfect conditions; learning from early members before permits arrived was valuable.
- *pricing*: Price at market and compete on product quality rather than undercutting.
- *scaling*: Use the second location to test the opposite strategy (heavy marketing) versus the first (word of mouth only).

## Warnings and mistakes

- Franchising too early with only two or three locations risks friction if franchisees cannot hit 40 members quickly
- Customers who use it every day reduce capacity, while customers who rarely use it risk churn; aim for enthusiasts but not obsessives
- Other businesses in the same strip mall had not done well, so location risk is real
