## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build the missed-call workflow from a template** (operations)
1. In GoHighLevel, start from the missed-call text template and use busy, voicemail or no-answer calls as triggers.
2. Add a short delay before texting, assign an owner or employee, tag the contact and configure notifications.
3. Replace the static text with Conversation AI and tailor its personality, objectives, questions and examples to the client’s trade.
4. Set a reply-wait period and a response cap, then add branches for booked estimates, emergencies and unqualified contacts.
5. Publish the workflow and test the handoff and notification behavior.
> "This is the most important part. Remember this step right here where you first send the text to the customer? We're going to delete that." — Chris Koerner (~4:20)

**Tailor the agent to one trade** (product)
1. Use a specific business name, service area and appointment goal in the prompt.
2. Have the agent ask what service the caller needs and gather the details needed for an estimate.
3. Use an LLM to draft or trim the instructions and example responses to fit the platform’s stated word limits.
4. Add separate handling for urgent cases and requests outside the service area.
> "So, you don't have to like upload this huge repository of FAQs, just have AI create that knowledge base for you." — Chris Koerner (~5:23)

**Duplicate the workflow for each client and for prospecting** (operations)
1. Duplicate the configured client workflow rather than rebuilding it for every business.
2. Swap the trade-specific details and prompt for each new client.
3. For prospecting, duplicate the workflow again, change the agent to answer as the seller and qualify interested business owners, and remove irrelevant emergency handling.
4. Flag hot leads with their name and phone number so a person can follow up.
> "Now that you have this one workflow, you can copy and paste it over and over and over for multiple customers." — Chris Koerner (~8:55)

**Dogfood the text-back agent to get prospects** (acquisition)
1. Use a business directory scraper to collect local businesses in the chosen trade and area.
2. Normalize the phone numbers and export the list as a CSV.
3. Record a short, broad voicemail asking business owners to call back, then upload it and the list to a ringless-voicemail service.
4. Sync the caller ID with GoHighLevel so returned calls can trigger the prospecting workflow.
5. Let the AI handle basic questions and pricing, then personally call or meet qualified leads to close.
> "Hey, this is Chris Kerner. I'm here in Dallas, Texas, and I'm looking for a tree trimming business. So please give me a call back. Thanks." — Chris Koerner (~12:46)

**Offer a measurable payback guarantee** (pricing)
1. State a monthly price clearly when a prospect asks.
2. Tie the refund promise to whether the service pays for itself within the stated period.
3. Track client outcomes carefully before relying on the guarantee as a repeatable sales offer.
> "Great question. It is $500 a month, but if it doesn't pay for itself in the first 90 days, then I'll give you all your money back." — AI agent (on-screen text scripted by Chris Koerner) (~14:06)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Responding quickly matters in a missed-call funnel, but the video’s 57% figure is cited as data without a source in the transcript.
- *offer_design*: A single acknowledgement may not resolve a caller’s questions; a two-way conversation can gather the details needed to move toward an appointment.
- *niche_selection*: Make the agent specific to the customer’s trade, services and service area instead of relying on a generic prompt.
- *automation_ai*: Build guardrails and escalation paths into the workflow: cap responses, distinguish emergencies and notify a person when a lead is ready.
- *scaling*: Reuse a working workflow across similar clients, changing the trade-specific prompt and information for each business.
- *validation_testing*: Use the service’s own acquisition method to test it on prospects, but distinguish a callback or price question from a closed sale.
- *pricing*: A payback guarantee can make an offer easier to understand, but the seller needs a way to measure whether the service paid for itself.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The demo has not closed a customer: it shows a callback and price question, not revenue or proof of repeatable conversion.
- The outbound prospecting method uses ringless voicemail to a list of scraped business numbers. The transcript does not address UK compliance. Marketing SMS to individuals and sole traders requires prior consent, and cold-call follow-ups must be screened against TPS/CTPS.
- The host treats a response cap as a safeguard against the bot continuing to message a person excessively.
- Urgent cases, such as a fallen tree on a house, need a clear human escalation path.
