### I1. OpenClaw / agent persona exchange marketplace

- **Customer:** Business owners who want ready-made AI agents that act like employees; creators who want to sell their own versions
- **Problem:** Hard to find pre-built, trusted AI agents for specific roles such as cold email or marketing
- **Offer:** A searchable exchange with filters, tech stack and approximate token costs for each persona, like an eBay for agents
- **Business model:** Marketplace commission on persona sales (subscription or licence); creators sell free/open or closed-source versions
- **Pricing:** Per-month subscription or upfront licence per persona; marketplace takes a cut. example cold-email persona price (hypothetical) USD 30 per month Buyer also pays token costs
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: The exchange itself is the platform; creators bring their own audiences and personas
- **Delivery:** Platform hosts or links personas that run on the buyer's OpenClaw setup; creators build the personas
- **Unit economics (claims):** Marketplace takes a cut of all persona sales (no rate stated)
- **Tools:** OpenClaw, Instantly, Mailchimp
- **Startup cost:** not stated
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 5/5 — Software marketplace works the same in the UK; consumer-protection and VAT on digital goods apply.
- **Risks:** Early market; depends on OpenClaw adoption; Domain names like openclaw exchange are already taken; Quality and security of third-party agents
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Listings and SEO are agent-friendly, but a two-sided marketplace needs seeding of supply and demand); onboarding 4/5 (Self-serve creator and buyer signup); delivery 4/5 (Software distribution; vetting and quality review need occasional human checks); billing 5/5 (Stripe-style commission splits); support 3/5 (Disputes and broken agents need human escalation)
> "the future is people are going to be buying fully deployable AI agents to operate on your behalf. And they're going to feel like employees." — Michael Girdley (~0:45)
> "you basically become the eBay of these things, right? You become the place that takes a cut on all this kind of stuff." — Michael Girdley (~4:13)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Audit as gateway to better AI businesses** (acquisition)
1. Offer a paid AI audit to small businesses as a low-competition entry
2. Use the audit to learn their real problems
3. Upsell consulting, custom builds, or monthly-fee software
4. If competition rises, make a cheap vibe-coded audit tool as a freemium front end and convert about half to a retainer
> "it is gateway drug for you to get into a better business around AI" — Michael Girdley (~37:47)

**Deposit plus success-fee pricing for advisory** (pricing)
1. Charge a $2-3k refundable deposit to screen serious clients
2. Add a percentage of money saved
3. Optionally combine with a flat fee
> "I think you charge a percentage of savings. I think you charge like a $2 to $3,000 deposit that is, yeah, fundable." — Michael Girdley (~35:12)

**Billboard truck outside target high schools** (acquisition)
1. Put a billboard on a truck offering a free consult on cutting tuition
2. Park it across from the wealthiest local high schools
> "You go buy a truck and you put a billboard on the side of the truck that says, hey, would you like to say 50% off your college tuition?" — Michael Girdley (~35:54)

**Skincare funnel from Instagram to consult to subscription** (acquisition)
1. Run a skincare Instagram for top of funnel
2. Offer a 15-minute video consult or AI selfie analysis
3. Sell a recurring subscription with a check-in after a year

**Refund-if-you-stay-focused pricing** (pricing)
1. Charge $30 a month
2. Refund fully if the user avoids distraction all month

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Starting in a mediocre business (audits) can reveal the better business hidden in customers' problems
- *pricing*: Charge for work while competition is low; add freemium only when the market gets crowded and you can't differentiate
- *pricing*: Refundable deposits and success fees help screen out unserious clients
- *scaling*: A marketplace or exchange that takes a cut can beat building the individual products
- *niche_selection*: New platform permissions (e.g. Apple's) plus AI create windows for products that weren't previously possible
- *niche_selection*: Information asymmetry (parents vs. colleges) is a niche for paid advocacy
- *customer_acquisition*: Consistent niche and format on YouTube beats chasing broad topics, according to Girdley's observation of audience expectations
- *automation_ai*: AI multiplies the output of a skilled human in labour-intensive fields like journalism

## Warnings and mistakes

- Auditing alone is a one-and-done consulting business, so use it as a lead-in to recurring work
- Local reporting depends on human relationships and sources that AI alone can't replicate
- Putting generic lotion in custom bottles would be deceptive; the speaker says he is not recommending it
- Existing 'local news' sites often just copy-paste press releases, so depth is the differentiator
