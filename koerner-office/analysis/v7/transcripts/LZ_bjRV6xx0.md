## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Peter Askew (askew.org, Deep South Ventures), who has spent 20 years building small online businesses around exact-match domains, mostly bought patiently at expired-domain auctions. He started by buying neglected but well-ranked mom-and-pop websites and adding AdSense (Travel Envoy cost $10k, earned $1-2k/month and paid back in six months), then bought DudeRanch.com for about $18k and turned it into a flat-fee advertising directory (about 60 advertisers, a little over $50k a year from about an hour a month). He also runs RanchWork, a hand-curated ranch job board ($99+ per posting, 1.3M visits a year), and Vidalia onions sold direct to consumers with a partner farm (100,000 lb shipped this year, using Shopify and ShipStation). Key lessons: let a great domain's type-in traffic and authority be the marketing channel, keep your day job until side projects out-earn it, give partners no-risk offers, and be patient.

### I1. Exact-match industry directory (DudeRanch.com)

- **Customer:** Dude/guest ranch owners (advertisers); families searching for ranch vacations (users)
- **Problem:** Ranches need bookings for $10k-30k family vacations; searchers want a trusted overview with photos and video
- **Offer:** Listing on DudeRanch.com (and GuestRanches.com) with photos, video and info, driving leads directly to ranches
- **Business model:** Flat yearly listing fee paid by advertisers; no commission on bookings
- **Pricing:** Flat annual listing fee. flat listing fee USD 575 per year He describes it both as a flat yearly fee and as a one-time fee; no cut of conversions.
- **Acquisition:** networking_events: Signed first advertisers at the dude ranch industry's annual conference held at a ranch.; seo_organic_search: Exact-match domain plus SEO; type-in traffic of 30-50 visits a day from day one; AI assistants now also cite it.; partnerships: Partnered with competitor GuestRanches.com so cancelling both would lose ranches ~50% of traffic; later bought him out.
- **Delivery:** WordPress site; a contractor fixes PHP/plugin issues for a couple hundred dollars a year.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Domain cost just under $18,000; ~60 advertisers at $575; a little over $50k a year; About an hour a month of work; Traffic up 39% year over year; Costs: hosting, domain renewal, ~$200s/year developer
- **Tools:** WordPress, GoDaddy Auctions, Clicky
- **Startup cost:** ~$18,000 domain plus build time
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated (advertisers came after the first conference)
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Model works for UK niches (e.g. glamping, farm stays) if a strong exact-match domain is obtainable; dude ranches themselves are US-specific.
- **Risks:** Depends on owning a scarce premium domain; Seasonal traffic; Google/AI search changes; Small market caps revenue
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (SEO/type-in traffic is automatic; advertiser sign-ups via self-serve, though early sales were in person. UK PECR permits cold email only to limited companies; many small venues are sole traders.); onboarding 4/5 (Self-serve listing purchase and profile forms.); delivery 4/5 (Static directory serving leads; agents could maintain listings and content.); billing 5/5 (Online annual payments.); support 3/5 (Agents can handle listing edits; site breakages need a developer.)
> "I still have 575 as my flat yearly fee to be included on the site and it's just a onetime fee. I don't take any cut of the conversion" — Peter Askew (~23:40)

- **Customer:** Ranches (cattle, equestrian, outfitters, farms, dude ranches) hiring staff; job seekers

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Buy under-monetised ranking websites** (acquisition)
1. Google high-CPC terms (e.g. tourism destinations)
2. Find dated sites ranking top 3 with no ads
3. Ask owners for analytics
4. Estimate AdSense income at 1-3% CTR x CPC x 70%
5. Offer roughly a 6-9 month payback price (e.g. $10k)
6. Secure the domain first, then migrate content and add ads/affiliates
> "I got to get control of this domain because if I control the domain, I control the whole site" — Peter Askew (~4:52)

**Validate an expired domain before bidding** (acquisition)
1. Check search volume for the term and intent (e.g. 'dude ranch vacation' vs band album)
2. Check state/long-tail variants
3. Estimate customer ticket size and their margin
4. Check domain age and GoDaddy traffic stats for type-in traffic
5. Bid patiently; wait for the right name rather than building ideas first
> "I wanted search volume. I want to fish where the fish are" — Peter Askew (~22:38)

**Corner a niche by partnering with the competitor** (sales)
1. Notice advertisers cancel one directory but keep the other
2. Partner with the competing directory
3. Cancelling both would cost them ~50% of traffic, reducing churn
4. Eventually buy the partner out
> "if they cancel both of us, they're going to lose about 50% of their traffic" — Peter Askew (~22:01)

**No-risk partner offers** (sales)
1. Offer to do all the online work for free
2. Domain owner keeps the domain and points nameservers to you (RanchWork 75/25 split)
3. Farm keeps fulfilment; you only get paid if you generate sales
4. Keep talking to people until you find the one who 'just gets it'
> "I'll build you a website for free you have to pay me a penny and then let's just see if we can ship some onions" — Peter Askew (~51:18)

**Test demand with a landing page** (validation_testing)
1. Put up a simple page with email signup ('interested in having Vidalias delivered?')
2. Watch sign-ups
3. Then visit the industry body and farms to find a fulfilment partner
4. Get the trademark holder's blessing

**Run a job board that looks alive** (operations)
1. Buy an off-the-shelf WordPress job board theme (~$60) and tune it for SEO on a fast server
2. Seed listings from existing advertisers and ranch websites
3. Post jobs every day to signal activity
4. Hand-curate each listing within 24 hours; refund sketchy ones
5. Don't force account creation; cross-post to email and Facebook group
6. Tier pricing by listing duration; offer a free tier for ranches in trouble
> "I like consistency on my job board to alert folks that this thing is alive and kicking" — Peter Askew (~35:45)

**Cheap 30-second phone video ads for DTC** (acquisition)
1. Film three clips (tractor, product in hand, packing shed)
2. Write a quick script, aim for ~30 seconds
3. Edit in CapCut, track with Facebook pixel
4. Use to test whether a boutique product sells

**Proactive lost-parcel handling** (operations)
1. Track all orders in ShipStation
2. Detect lost boxes before the customer notices
3. Claim with UPS or ship a make-good immediately
> "I'd like to find out that the box has been lost before the customer realizes that the box has been lost" — Peter Askew (~70:22)

**Use a premium domain for credibility email** (operations)
1. Give partners and staff emails on a category-killer domain (e.g. @onions.com via Fastmail)
2. Use the simpler domain to redirect customers who misspell the brand

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: Keep your day job until the side project out-earns it (the wing walker's code).
- *customer_acquisition*: Let a great descriptive domain be the marketing channel: type-in traffic and instant trust.
- *niche_selection*: Build from assets that inspire you (domains, channels) rather than from ideas first.
- *exit_acquisition*: Information arbitrage: owners often don't know what their traffic is worth.
- *offer_design*: Partners make projects work; offer them no-brainer, no-risk deals.
- *customer_acquisition*: Sales is finding the person who 'just gets it', not convincing everyone.
- *niche_selection*: Suppliers who hate DTC are opportunities: you become their distribution and they capture higher margin.
- *automation_ai*: Directories still work in the AI era when they offer trusted photos, video and info; AI tools even recommend them.
- *mindset_execution*: Build a failure callous: iterate through failures until a path emerges (5 years from start to DudeRanch).
- *other*: Premium domains come up at reasonable prices once in a lifetime; buy when available.
- *operations*: Mix seasonal and year-round projects to spread attention across the year.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Don't quit your job to go all in on a side project; pressure kills creative ideas.
- Regional product names can be trademarked by law (Vidalia); get the industry's blessing.
- Customer support at volume overwhelms a solo operator; delegate.
- Handshake partnership deals carry risk, though it worked for him.
- Sites get burnt down, de-indexed or fail to earn; expect many failures over years.
- If you don't control the domain, a seller can back out of a site deal.
