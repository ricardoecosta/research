## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build credibility before sharing a Reddit link** (acquisition)
1. Lurk in relevant subreddits and learn the community's questions and norms.
2. Reply usefully to build karma before promoting anything.
3. When sharing a link, answer the question fully in the post or comment first.
4. Explain how the newsletter helps and share the link only when relevant.
> "The first thing she does is she'll lurk to kind of like understand the subreddit and figure out what's working" — Chanel Basilio (~1:40)

**Run a subscriber milestone giveaway with relevant prizes** (acquisition)
1. Choose a subscriber goal and keep the giveaway open until the goal is reached.
2. Ask relevant software companies or resource providers to contribute prizes.
3. Promote the giveaway in the newsletter and remind readers while it is active.
4. Ask readers to share it; make prizes useful to the intended audience rather than generic.
> "And so it only ended once she actually hit that 30,000 number." — Chanel Basilio (~4:46)

**Give away a useful slice of a LinkedIn resource before offering the full guide** (acquisition)
1. Publish a useful framework or part of a playbook directly in the LinkedIn post.
2. Give readers enough value to make the post useful without opting in.
3. Offer the deeper guide as an optional content upgrade in the comments.
> "He's giving you the full thing and then saying like, hey, if you want to get the whole like detailed piece of it, I guess." — Chanel Basilio (~9:46)

**Partner on a seasonal giveaway** (acquisition)
1. Choose a seasonal theme that fits the audience.
2. Partner with relevant tools or communities to assemble one useful freebie per day.
3. Email the audience with each day's offer and promote the campaign on LinkedIn.
4. Ask partners to share the promotion where appropriate.
> "And so she kind of partnered with 12 different tools, communities, that kind of thing, giving away freebies." — Chanel Basilio (~10:37)

**Test a landing-page button label** (validation_testing)
1. Test a specific alternative to the default “subscribe” or “submit” wording.
2. Compare conversion results on the actual website rather than assuming one wording works everywhere.
3. Use a short test period if a paid testing tool is only needed temporarily.
> "But yeah, the cool thing with these is like, you kind of have to test it for your own website 'cause it doesn't always work on every single one." — Chanel Basilio (~13:48)

**Create a self-written challenge lead magnet** (offer_design)
1. Choose one narrow audience and a problem the creator can address.
2. Write the complete sequence yourself if coordinating contributors is too difficult.
3. Promote repeatedly on LinkedIn and to the existing email list.
4. Encourage readers to share it with people who fit the audience.
> "And she did the smart thing of just like writing the whole thing herself." — Chanel Basilio (~16:38)

**Remove signup questions from cold-traffic onboarding** (onboarding)
1. Ask only for the essential signup fields.
2. Send new subscribers to a welcoming thank-you page rather than a questionnaire.
3. Ask for preference data later, after subscribers have experienced the publication.
4. Compare churn and retention before and after the change.
> "And so instead, what they did was they just sent them right to a, hey, welcome page" — Chanel Basilio (~22:11)

**Use a personal video to invite webinar questions** (engagement)
1. Send a standard video to each registrant thanking them and asking what they want answered.
2. Reply with a custom video to people who send a question.
3. When time runs short, use a standard response rather than leaving registrants without an answer.
4. Reuse the approach for newsletter or podcast onboarding if personal replies fit the workload.
> "But if somebody replied to that, she would then reply with a custom video." — Chanel Basilio (~26:21)

**Use AI to find subject-line patterns in your own email history** (automation)
1. Export or connect a period of past broadcast emails.
2. Ask an AI assistant to identify patterns in the subject lines with the best open rates.
3. Review the patterns rather than assuming the obvious explanation is correct.
4. Use the patterns as ideas for future subject lines, then check results.

1. Choose an inactivity trigger based on the publication's sending frequency.
2. First help subscribers who may not be receiving the emails.
3. Then remind them what the publication covers and why they signed up.

**Use an early low-ticket offer to identify buyers** (pricing)
1. Offer a relevant lower-priced product during the first week after signup.

**Turn a repeated audience question into a free affiliate tool** (offer_design)
1. Notice a question the audience asks repeatedly.
2. Build a tool that demonstrates the creator's own process.

2. Send interested sponsors a media kit and an option to book a call.
3. If they do not book, send periodic audience and inventory updates.
4. Show relevant sponsor results or upcoming availability and remind them when pricing or inventory may change.

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Give a community a complete, useful answer before asking it to visit your newsletter or resource.
- *niche_selection*: A giveaway is more likely to attract the right people when its prizes closely match the audience's interests.
- *validation_testing*: Reduce friction for new subscribers, especially when they came from cold paid traffic; collect extra preference data after they have joined.
- *validation_testing*: Small copy changes can have large effects, but test them with your own audience because results may not transfer.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use personal follow-up where the value of a customer justifies the time; it can build trust and produce useful questions and feedback.
- *validation_testing*: Analyse your own historical results instead of assuming which subject lines, offers, or tactics should work.
- *offer_design*: Match offers to subscriber needs, and use a relevant affiliate offer if you do not have a suitable product yourself.
- *pricing*: A paid introductory offer can identify buyers early, but non-buyers may still be valuable for other offers or sponsorships.
- *operations*: Give inactive subscribers a clear way to pause or leave, and protect list health by reducing unwanted sends.
- *customer_acquisition*: Reach adjacent audiences whose interests overlap with your offer instead of relying only on direct competitors' audiences.
- *offer_design*: Turn repeated audience questions into a resource that can help many people instead of answering the same question one person at a time.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Treat reported case-study metrics as directional until you know the baseline, audience, time period, and measurement method.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Reddit promotion can fail if a new account immediately posts links; the founder should learn community norms, build credibility, and answer the question fully.
- Generic giveaways can attract freebie seekers; choose prizes that fit the intended subscriber audience.
- Post-signup questionnaires can cause cold paid-ad subscribers to leave before they experience the newsletter.
- Do not describe Sam's 99% webinar show rate as applying to all registrants: Chanel clarifies that 100% referred to the people she replied to with a custom video.
- Email open rates are imperfect engagement signals. The speakers say clicks and actual replies can be more useful, while recognizing those signals also have limits.
- The host says Crazy Egg cost about $99 per month and suggests using a testing tool temporarily if the ongoing cost is not worthwhile.
- Chanel says her crowdsourced 30 Days of Growth was difficult to coordinate; Jess Campbell avoided that work by writing her challenge herself.
- A snooze link can be triggered accidentally by corporate email security systems that click links. Chanel used a form-based approach to reduce that problem.
- The transcript's “$9.29 offer” is unclear and may be a transcription error; do not treat it as a verified price.
- UK marketing automation must respect consent and PECR rules. Do not automate unsolicited marketing email, SMS, WhatsApp, or DMs to individuals or sole traders; cold email to limited companies and screened cold calls have different rules.
