## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Alex, who started Brushworks Co, a forestry mulching (brush clearing) business near Cincinnati after failing to find anyone within 100 miles to clear his own land. With no home-service experience, he validated demand via keyword research, bought a financed used Bobcat, and got jobs within two days using Meta lead ads tied to an organic YouTube channel. He shares detailed numbers: 23 USD CPL, 49% close rate, ~3,100 USD average job, 69.75% gross margin, 54,000 USD last month and ~500,000 USD projected with two machines. He also pitches a remote-controlled Green Climber slope-mowing business for someone else to start.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Lay-term keyword research for an obscure service** (acquisition)
1. Ask friends/family what they would search to fix the problem
2. List terms (clear my woods, destroy honeysuckle) in a spreadsheet
3. Check volumes in SEMrush free trial
4. Use the volume to validate demand before buying equipment

**Hyper-targeted Meta lead ads for land owners** (acquisition)
1. Pick zip codes likely to have large properties instead of a radius
2. Target age 35+ with interests in Tractor Supply or Rural King
3. Run all placements for maximum exposure
4. Video only: problem, local landmark drone shot, slow-motion shredding, time lapse
5. Link the ad to the YouTube channel to reinforce trust
6. Refresh creative as it decays; niche ads (fence lines) unlock horse communities
> "highlight the problem first. Show that you're local. Local is the number one" — Alex (~10:26)

**YouTube as local sales support** (acquisition)
1. Film from the first job, about 1 in 5 then 1 in 10 jobs
2. Put local keyword plus service in tags so Google shows the video locally
3. Do not chase view counts

**Keep machine payment under one job's revenue** (finance)
1. Finance equipment so monthly payment is below one job (~3,100 USD)
2. Buy new with manufacturer 0% offers for warranty

**Quote in person, price by day** (sales)
1. Call each lead quickly and book an on-site quote
2. Do not charge for quotes
3. Price by half day/day/multi-day because customers prefer it

**Neighbour effect sales** (sales)
1. After clearing one property line, approach neighbours whose side now looks worse

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Look for supply-demand imbalances you hit personally; if you have the problem, others do too.
- *customer_acquisition*: For a service people do not know the name of, research the words customers actually use.
- *customer_acquisition*: Interruption ads (Meta) must create desire by showing a familiar problem and local identity.
- *mindset_execution*: Competitors grow an educate-the-customer category, so welcome them.
- *pricing*: Pricing should match how customers think, not what is easiest for you.
- *niche_selection*: Sell expensive services to wealthy customers for resilience.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Business track record helps get low-down-payment equipment finance.
- *scaling*: Capacity, not demand, is the constraint once ads work; reinvest cash flow into machines and logistics (bigger truck).

## Warnings and mistakes

- Buying used heavy equipment can cost double in repairs; new machines with warranty are worth it.
- Switching to per-acre pricing halved the close rate even at equal prices.
- Referral-commission deal with a tree service was never paid.
- Rain and mud stop work; residential customers dislike ruts.
- SaaS vendors gate APIs behind expensive plans, blocking lead automation.
- Lead ad respondents sometimes forget they submitted the form.
