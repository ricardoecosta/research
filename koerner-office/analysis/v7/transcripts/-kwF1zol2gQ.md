- **Delivery:** Operator shows up on site, loads the design and paint, and runs the wall printer
- **Unit economics (claims):** Hard upfront cost is the printer; machines can be financed and come with a warranty; About 1.8 million US retail businesses
- **Tools:** Wall printing machines (three or four companies, unnamed)
- **Startup cost:** Cost of the printer (financeable); amount not stated
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated

### I2. Riding lawn mower flipping and export

- **Customer:** Local Facebook Marketplace buyers; buyers in Bolivia
- **Problem:** Used mowers are cheap in the US; John Deere is a premium brand in Bolivia and hard to find there
- **Offer:** Refurbished or exported John Deere riding mowers
- **Business model:** Buy low, repair, resell; or export arbitrage
- **Pricing:** resale margin. Bolivia price as told to host USD 6000-7000 per mower (Bolivia resale); US buy price paid to host USD 2200 per mower Shipping about $1-2 per pound, around $500 per mower
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: Source and sell on Facebook Marketplace; one seller had 30-40 listings in Dallas-Fort Worth
- **Delivery:** Buy, crate, ship by truck via Lubbock TX to Bolivia; or repair (parts $50-500) and relist locally
- **Unit economics (claims):** Buy under $1,000, parts $50-500; Export: about $3,000 all-in, sells for about $6,000; A YouTuber claimed $51,000 in one month
- **Tools:** Facebook Marketplace, ChatGPT (repair guidance), U-Haul trailer
- **Startup cost:** Under $1,000 per mower plus parts
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 2/5 — The Bolivia export route is US-specific; local flipping works on Facebook Marketplace or Gumtree, but ride-on mowers are a smaller market in the UK
- **Risks:** Time-intensive repairs; Export logistics and customs; Hearsay evidence only
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 2/5 (Listing and messaging on Marketplace is hard to automate and platform terms restrict bots); onboarding 1/5 (Buyer negotiation is manual); delivery 0/5 (Physical pickup, repair and hauling); billing 2/5 (Mostly cash or transfer at pickup); support 2/5 (Minimal after-sale support)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate imports before buying (list first, pre-sell)** (product)
1. Browse Alibaba/AliExpress for interesting products
2. Copy photos to Facebook Marketplace listings at varying price points
3. Track clicks, messages and impressions in a Google Sheet
4. Pre-sell with $50-$500 deposits
5. Only import the winners
> "You don't need to spend a dime before you know which one of these are sellers." — Chris Koerner (~0:00)

**Use 'jet ski' keyword in Marketplace listing** (acquisition)
1. Include the term 'jet ski' in the jet boat description so searchers find it
2. Lead with how much cheaper it is than a jet ski

**Bin cleaning guerrilla marketing** (acquisition)
1. Find the neighbourhood's trash day
2. Print shiny flyers with QR code and stick them to bins the day of or before
3. Drive around that evening with a magnet sign on the vehicle
4. Do not wrap the vehicle at first; test the concept cheaply

**Batch car seat cleaning** (operations)
1. Pick up seats in bulk
2. Clean all at once following manufacturer instructions
3. Drop off together at doorsteps
4. Take payment in advance to minimise interaction time

**Free-service time-lapse content** (acquisition)
1. Offer free work to homeowners in exchange for filming
2. Test about 10 service concepts (soft washing, fence restoration, roof washing etc.)
3. Favour visually satisfying or expensive-looking work for retention
4. Use editors abroad and ChatGPT for ideas

**Sell AI video production as a service** (sales)
1. Collect a client headshot
2. Generate 60-second scripted videos with HeyGen
3. Charge a monthly retainer (suggested $1,500 for 10 videos)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: New equipment technology creates new service businesses around it; being early in a local area gives an advantage.
- *niche_selection*: Unglamorous, 'unsexy' services face little competition because most people won't do them.
- *customer_acquisition*: Visually satisfying work (time-lapse transformations) markets itself on short-form video.
- *validation_testing*: Test demand before spending: list products before importing them and take deposits.
- *offer_design*: Many people will pay a premium to avoid risk; being the middleman who absorbs import risk is valuable.
- *pricing*: Presentation can raise the price of a commodity item (hot chocolate $6 to $9) for minimal extra labour.
- *pricing*: Operators often underprice; the host suggests tripling the price for car seat cleaning to $150.
- *automation_ai*: Imperfect AI content can still generate leads; do not wait for polish.
- *niche_selection*: Arbitrage can come from brand perception differences between countries.
- *mindset_execution*: Stop finding reasons it won't work and run a cheap test.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Mower flipping will not be an efficient use of time at first; repairs take many hours before you build skill.
- Bin cleaning is dirty, unglamorous and hard to scale, and the host admits the $25 price may be unrealistic.
- Importing from China carries scam risk; use reviews, samples and Alibaba trade assurance.
- AI avatar videos may look visibly fake (odd mouth movement), though host says they still worked.
