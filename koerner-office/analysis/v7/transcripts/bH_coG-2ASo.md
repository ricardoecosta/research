## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Will Cannon, who built the B2B lead-data SaaS UpLead and the e-signature SaaS Signaturely. Will describes going from a failed mortgage company to selling email lists, then an agency, then UpLead (about $1M ARR in year one, cold email plus SEO). He then explains how he cloned the e-signature category by mining G2 reviews, building a 'stupid simple' product and proving he could get customers before building. The pair also pitch a free vertical CRM monetised through payments and payroll, and both stress selling before building and that 10k a month is a life-changing target.

### I1. UpLead (B2B lead data SaaS)

- **Customer:** Small and mid-sized businesses and sales teams needing clean lists of ideal prospects
- **Problem:** Sales teams need accurate B2B prospect lists; the incumbents were ZoomInfo and Sales Genie
- **Offer:** A B2B-only lead database where users build their own prospect lists
- **Business model:** Monthly subscription to B2B contact data
- **Pricing:** subscription.  not stated
- **Acquisition:** cold_email: First customers came from cold emailing with lists built using their own tool ('eating our own dog food'), with a script and multiple follow-ups.
- **Delivery:** Software platform with data sourced from industry relationships and kept clean; emails verified cheaply, phones sampled via a Philippines call center. Built by an outsourced dev team found on Upwork.
- **Unit economics (claims):** MRR went 2K, 5K, 8K, 10K in the first months; Roughly $1M ARR in year one; About 2M ARR by year 3 (approximate)
- **Tools:** Elance, Upwork, Cold email
- **Startup cost:** not stated (a first MVP attempt on Elance was lost to a scam)
- **Time to first revenue:** Not stated; reached about 10K MRR within a few months
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — Works in the UK but B2B personal data is regulated by UK GDPR and PECR; data sourcing must be lawful and legitimate interest documented.
- **Risks:** Competing with ZoomInfo; Data accuracy decay; Team and process scaling
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 4/5 (SEO and cold email to limited companies can be run by agents; PECR requires care with sole traders); onboarding 4/5 (Self-serve signup); delivery 4/5 (Software delivers data; data quality upkeep and sourcing need occasional human work); billing 5/5 (Subscription billing); support 3/5 (Agents handle most tickets; he hired a human support person early)
> "I think it was like 2,000 a month, 5,000 a month, 8,000 a month, 10,000 a month. It just started going." — Will Cannon (~22:44)
> "I think within the first year, we were probably doing a million ARR first year." — Will Cannon (~25:41)

### I2. The Data Supplier (list-selling agency)

- **Customer:** Businesses wanting prospect lists, from SMBs to some Fortune 500s
- **Problem:** Companies need a clean list of their ideal prospects
- **Offer:** Industry lists such as realtors, dentists and chiropractors, with custom segments and appended data
- **Business model:** One-off sales of bulk lists (his side) and per-lead sales (wife's side)
- **Pricing:** bulk list price or per-lead. Every realtor in the US USD 500 one-off; California realtor list USD 200 one-off; Low end of per-lead pricing USD 0.5 per lead Wife ran a separate per-lead business; customers bought from both
- **Acquisition:** seo_organic_search: Ranked number one for terms like 'realtor email lists' using borderline gray-hat SEO.; cold_email: Cold emailed businesses.
- **Delivery:** Digital file/link; manually merged and appended CSVs; a team built helper software; emails re-verified and phone accuracy sampled by a Philippines call centre (500 of 10,000 hand-dialled).
- **Unit economics (claims):** Business reached 30-50K a month within 2-3 months; Agency profit high six to low seven figures per year; Lists acquired for next to nothing and resold unlimited times
- **Tools:** BizBen, Philippines call centre, Email verification
- **Startup cost:** Next to nothing per list; around $100 for a list
- **Time to first revenue:** Within 2-3 months at scale
- **UK applicability [E]:** 2/5 — Reselling personal data lists, especially of consumers and sole traders, collides with UK GDPR and PECR; reselling bought lists without a lawful basis is risky.
- **Risks:** Data quality decay; Legal exposure on personal data; Buyers rarely execute outbound successfully
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 4/5 (SEO plus cold email to companies); onboarding 4/5 (Order and download); delivery 3/5 (Manual CSV manipulation could be agent-driven; sourcing is the hard part); billing 5/5 (Online checkout); support 3/5 (Agent handled with checks)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Mine G2 reviews to pick and position a software clone** (product)
1. Browse G2 categories for fragmented markets with at least one or two billion-dollar players
2. Read one- and two-star reviews of leaders to find recurring complaints (e.g. too complex)
3. Build around one angle such as 'stupid simple' rather than price or features
4. Check you can acquire customers profitably before building
> "I want to make sure and I want it to the best of my ability that I think I have a the ability to acquire customers profitably before I build the business." — Will Cannon (~28:30)

**Free tools and templates SEO funnel** (acquisition)
1. Build free online tools (e.g. signature creator) and downloadable contract templates
2. Rank them via SEO
3. Funnel visitors to a freemium signup
4. Use a keyword-rich domain

**Window-shop business marketplaces for ideas and benchmarks** (acquisition)
1. Browse BizBuySell or BizBen listings
2. Read due diligence materials without breaching NDAs
3. Learn margins, acquisition channels and what to copy
> "you can learn so much digging into their due diligence docs." — Chris Koerner (~8:35)

**Pre-sell before building** (sales)
1. Run cold email campaigns for the unbuilt product
2. Throw up a landing page and test Facebook ads
3. Build only once you get yeses
> "Figure out how to acquire customers first before you go build the thing." — Will Cannon (~48:03)

**Phone list quality sampling** (operations)
1. Randomly sample 500 from a 10,000-record list
2. Have a call center hand-dial them
3. Use the disposition report to estimate disconnected and wrong numbers against a threshold
> "Phones, we had a call center in the Philippines, where we take a subset of the list." — Will Cannon (~15:56)

**Dog-food cold email** (acquisition)
1. Use your own data product to build prospect lists
2. Cold email with multi-step follow-ups

**Retail plus wholesale dual-channel list sales** (pricing)
1. Sell bulk lists at flat prices
2. Run a separate per-lead brand
3. Capture the same buyer on both ends

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Prove you can acquire customers profitably before building the product.
- *offer_design*: Cloning works best with one clear angle (simplicity) found from competitor reviews.
- *customer_acquisition*: A strong owned distribution skill, such as SEO or cold email, is the real moat when anything can be copied.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Digital goods with one-time sourcing cost can be resold unlimited times at high margin.
- *pricing*: A free product monetised through higher-margin ancillary services is an untapped model as software costs fall.
- *mindset_execution*: A target of 10K a month is life-changing and does not require massive scale.
- *scaling*: Switch to recurring SaaS from agency when the recurring revenue starts to match, and keep the agency running during transition.
- *customer_acquisition*: Building a mini brand so you are on buyers' shortlists matters for differentiation.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Bootstrapped growth to scale needs good people and processes; trial and error in hiring is normal.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Paying an Elance team to build an MVP resulted in lost money and nothing delivered, delaying the SaaS by about five years.
- Timing risk: the mortgage boom collapsed in 2008, leaving foreclosures and a repossessed car.
- Buying remote houses without local knowledge lost money; local knowledge cannot be replaced by data or AI.
- Building software is easier now but scaling is hard; reaching 10M ARR differs from 100K.
- Price and features are weak differentiators because they are copied; need angle and brand.
