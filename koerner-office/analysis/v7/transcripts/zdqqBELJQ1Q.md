### I1. Door-to-door lawn mowing / yard work

- **Customer:** Rural/suburban homeowners
- **Problem:** Homeowners don't want to mow or do yard work
- **Offer:** Mow lawn, weed eat, gutters, pressure washing, windows for cash
- **Business model:** per-job service fee
- **Pricing:** per job. Lawn mow, tipped to $30 USD 20-30 per job; Initial offer to a woman mowing her own lawn USD 10 per job Customer tipped 50%
- **Acquisition:** door_to_door_in_person: Knocked many doors asking about gutters, weed eating, pressure washing; most said no; one yes after offering $20 to someone mid-mow. Leads with a story ('I threw a dart at a map').; marketplace_platform: Posted pressure-washing photo on Facebook Marketplace disguised so it wouldn't be rejected as a service; no results reported.
- **Delivery:** Chris physically mows the lawn himself.
- **Unit economics (claims):** $30 for about an hour of work; Extrapolated to $60,000 a year at 40 hours a week
- **Tools:** Facebook Marketplace, Zillow
- **Startup cost:** Mower and tools supplied by the customer in this case; otherwise not stated
- **Time to first revenue:** About a day of door knocking, around mid-afternoon
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Works in the UK, but door-to-door soliciting is allowed with less friction than cold messaging; insurance and local norms apply. Rural scale is more limited.
- **Risks:** High rejection rate; Physical labour; not automatable; Rural areas have few customers; Safety and trespassing signs
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 0/5 (Door-to-door in-person sales; no software route to this.); onboarding 1/5 (Agreed verbally on the doorstep.); delivery 0/5 (Physical mowing.); billing 3/5 (Cash/Venmo; invoicing could be automated.); support 2/5 (Minimal; mostly handled by a human.)
> "30 bucks. 30 bucks for what? An hour's worth of work? You do that 40 hours a week, that's $60,000 a year working for yourself." — Chris Koerner (~21:25)

### I2. Sell-on-behalf listing service for offline sellers

- **Customer:** Older rural owners who don't use computers and have items to sell
- **Problem:** They have items worth money but can't use the internet to sell them
- **Offer:** Photograph and list items on Facebook Marketplace, find buyers, split proceeds
- **Business model:** commission (10% or amount above seller's ask)
- **Pricing:** commission or spread. Proposed commission USD 10 percent of sale Alternatively buy at seller's price and keep the spread, e.g. smoker for $400, resale value claimed $2,000-4,000 after restoration
- **Acquisition:** door_to_door_in_person: Found Danny by knocking doors and asking about tasks; he mentioned a tractor ($500 ask) and smoker ($400 ask).; marketplace_platform: Resale via Facebook Marketplace listings.
- **Delivery:** Photograph items, write listings, handle buyers, and possibly collect and restore items; part two was teased but not shown.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Smoker buy price $400, resale potentially $2,000-4,000; Tractor asked $500
- **Tools:** Facebook Marketplace
- **Startup cost:** Low; $400 if buying the smoker outright
- **Time to first revenue:** Not stated; outcome deferred to part two
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Works with Facebook Marketplace, Gumtree and eBay. Trading standards and goods-ownership rules apply, and agent commission arrangements should be documented.
- **Risks:** Item value claims are speculative; Disputes with seller over proceeds; Transport and storage of large items; Restoration costs

### I3. Reselling bottled water at an event

- **Customer:** Fairgoers walking in
- **Problem:** Fair water costs $3
- **Offer:** $1 bottled water, bundles like 3 for $3
- **Business model:** one-off product resale
- **Pricing:** per unit. Water price USD 1 per bottle Price anchored against the $3 in-fair price
- **Acquisition:** door_to_door_in_person: Stood near the fair entrance with a hand-drawn sign ('the fair is a rip-off, $1 waters') and pitched passers-by, using anchoring against the $3 price.
- **Delivery:** Hand over lukewarm bottles in person.
- **Unit economics (claims):** $5 for a 24-pack plus sign supplies; selling all 32 would give $32; Ended with $8 cash plus $30 Venmo = $38
- **Tools:** Dollar General, Poster board, Sharpie
- **Startup cost:** About $10 on water, $2 on signs, $10 on gas
- **Time to first revenue:** Same evening, after a slow start
- **UK applicability [E]:** 2/5 — Selling at events in the UK usually needs street-trading permission or landowner consent, and the fair operator could object.
- **Risks:** Event may stop sellers; Low margins and volume; Cannot sell cold water
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 0/5 (In-person selling.); onboarding 5/5 (None needed.); delivery 0/5 (Physical handover.); billing 1/5 (Cash/Venmo by hand.); support 5/5 (None needed.)
> "When in doubt, sell water." — Chris Koerner (~0:05)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Lead with a story** (sales)
1. Explain your 'why' (e.g. dart on a map, YouTube challenge) when approaching people
2. Ask for ideas or small jobs
3. Use it as an icebreaker
> "people buy the why, they're not necessarily buying the what." — Chris Koerner (~3:33)

**Target homes for sale or high-value areas via Zillow** (acquisition)
1. Open Zillow
2. Find the neighbourhood with the most expensive homes or listings for sale
3. Knock on those doors for exterior jobs

**Price anchoring against venue price** (pricing)
1. Find the in-venue price ($3 water)
2. Stand outside selling for $1
3. Offer bundles (3 for $3)
4. Mention the savings to passers-by
> "This could be a lesson in price anchoring." — Chris Koerner (~27:56)

**Sell before you have it** (sales)
1. Say yes to the job
2. Buy or rent the needed equipment after being hired
3. Ask for a deposit if cash is tight

**Brokerage for offline sellers** (operations)
1. Find owners who don't use computers
2. Photograph the item
3. List it on Facebook Marketplace
4. Take 10% or anything over their ask

**Reward and reinvest in the first customer** (mindset)
1. Return to the customer who tipped you
2. Give a thank-you gift

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Go where the people are; transactions need a critical mass of buyers, so rural markets are tough.
- *mindset_execution*: Rejection is part of the numbers game; keep knocking.
- *niche_selection*: People who already invest in their property are better customers than those who don't.
- *offer_design*: Offline people with assets they can't sell online are an underserved market for brokers.
- *customer_acquisition*: A story or personal why makes cold approaches more effective.
- *pricing*: Anchoring against a visible higher price makes a cheap offer attractive.
- *validation_testing*: Starting with tiny capital forces creativity and service-based businesses.
- *mindset_execution*: Being introverted is not a barrier to trying sales.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Facebook prohibits selling or rehoming pets, and services on Marketplace may be rejected.
- Rural areas have few customers; door-to-door is among the hardest sales methods and rejection is constant.
- Some properties have signs warning of trespassers being shot.
