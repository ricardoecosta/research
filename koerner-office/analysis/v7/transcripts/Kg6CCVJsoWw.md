## Tactics and playbook steps

**Turn down wholesale to protect DTC margin** (sales)
1. Sell by the cut as well as in shares so customers return weekly
2. Decline restaurant contracts that would take the premium cuts
3. Keep the margin by staying retail
> "but the meat that they want is the same meat that the customers are asking for." — Vicki (~10:34)

**Free experience as a funnel for meat sales** (acquisition)
1. Put pet animals out front on weekends
2. Offer lunch on the farm from the food truck using farm meat
3. Feature less common cuts at events so people try and then buy them
> "Honestly, I feel like the experience is what brings people here. And then when they're here, we try and talk to them about the meat" — Vicki (~18:35)

**Concentrate opening hours** (operations)
1. Close on weekdays; open Friday-Sunday only
2. Meet customers by appointment via message if needed
> "We used to be open every day. And then we just kind of decided it would be better to fund a lot of people in two certain hours." — Vicki (~18:52)

**Pricing: cover costs, then check local market** (pricing)
1. Calculate costs, including fluctuating feed
2. Compare local meat prices
3. Set prices that are reasonable for families
> "So we, first and foremost, you know, we want to make sure our costs are covered." — Vicki (~15:06)

**Partner with similar farms to fill gaps** (operations)
1. Build relationships with farms that use similar practices
2. Offer their product with disclosure when you sell out (e.g. ground beef)
3. Stock other local family-business products
> "And if somebody comes in, we just say, hey, we build out of ours. Would you mind using some ground beef from this farm?" — Vicki (~16:07)

## Lessons

- *pricing*: Selling direct to consumers beats wholesale on return for a small farm.
- *customer_acquisition*: The experience brings people in; it is the main acquisition engine.
- *mindset_execution*: Start messy and where you are; don't wait for a Pinterest-perfect setup.
- *scaling*: Grow with demand; start small and build infrastructure (like the store) when it's ready.
- *offer_design*: Events can start as awareness builders and later become profit centres.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Premium inputs (feed) are a deliberate cost that drives quality, but they halve margin.
- *mindset_execution*: Find good mentors and make connections.
- *operations*: Fewer, busier opening days didn't lose customers.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Farming margins are thin: half of revenue goes on feed and only ~5-10% is left.
- Land is the main constraint on scaling; you can't overstock acres.
- Food trucks are a big investment; only buy if you'll use them.
