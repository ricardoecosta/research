## Summary

Chris Koerner and a co-host brainstorm business ideas built around the theme that people will pay for almost anything, and that ego stops people from doing weird, attention-getting things. Ideas include novelty competition events (horse haircut show, Excel esports, vibe-coding competitions, survival challenges), a plaque-selling service triggered by magazine features, a YouTube-to-print-magazine service, and quirky marketing stunts such as a realtor 'ghost free guarantee'. Evidence is mostly brainstorming and second-hand anecdotes, with few verified numbers. Key takeaway: memorable, talk-worthy content or events can create demand and customers in otherwise boring niches.

### I1. Novelty competition events (horse hair show)

- **Customer:** Horse owners, spectators, sponsors, and the barbers who organise it
- **Problem:** Niche communities lack a fun gathering and organisers lack customer attention
- **Offer:** Live competition event where horses with intricate shaved patterns are judged
- **Business model:** Ticket sales, competitor entry fees, sponsor booths
- **Pricing:** Tiered event fees. general admission USD 30 per ticket; horse entry judged and graded USD 110 per entry; sponsored booth USD 320 per booth Reported from a viral Instagram video, second-hand.
- **Acquisition:** organic_social_content: Viral Instagram video of the shaved horse promoted the event
- **Delivery:** Physical in-person event run by organiser (reportedly a barber) in Clovis, California.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Thousands of signups reportedly; $30 GA, $110 horse entry, $320 booth
- **Tools:** Instagram
- **Startup cost:** not stated
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Works in the UK with event licensing, insurance and animal welfare considerations.
- **Risks:** Event liability and insurance; Hard to replicate virality; Animal welfare rules
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Short-form video content can be produced and posted largely by agents.); onboarding 3/5 (Ticketing and registration platforms handle sign-ups.); delivery 0/5 (Physical event needing venue, staff and judging.); billing 5/5 (Online ticketing handles payments.); support 2/5 (Day-of logistics need humans; pre-event queries can be automated.)
> "They're selling general admission tickets for $30. They're selling, you know, you could admit your horse to be judged and graded for $110." — Chris Koerner (~1:04)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Pay-to-play competition pot structure** (pricing)
1. Charge competitors an entry fee ($20-40) so they take it seriously
2. Charge spectators about $5
3. Give winner half of the door fees to incentivise inviting friends and family
4. Limit to nine competitors in a 3x3 screen grid
5. Use a 60-minute clock graded on predetermined categories
> "the winner gets like half of the pot, like half of the door fees." — Chris Koerner (~8:08)

**Creator magazine revenue share** (acquisition)
1. Approach a creator with a large audience
2. Offer to produce and fulfil the magazine at about $2 per issue
3. Creator sells at $12 per quarter and promotes via weekly email and midroll
4. Take 20% of topline
> "I'll do a mag. It'll be a magazine." — Brandon (~17:49)

**Bottom-up demand creation** (acquisition)
1. Get friends and family to repeatedly ask retailers or realtors for the product
2. Pitch the retailer once enquiries build
3. Or target home shoppers on Facebook within one city with heavy ad spend
> "They just asked all their friends and family to call up all the J C Penney every day" — Brandon (~22:00)

**Trigger-based personalised outreach** (sales)
1. Monitor publications for features of individuals
2. Find their email
3. Send a personalised-looking offer shortly after publication

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: Ego and fear of looking stupid stop people from doing attention-getting marketing that could make money.
- *customer_acquisition*: There are no boring businesses, only boring content; creative events and stories get attention in dull niches.
- *customer_acquisition*: Marketing stunts do not need to be good ideas, just talk-worthy.
- *niche_selection*: If a strange niche has paying customers, it is evidence that almost any passion niche can monetise; find analogues in your own area.
- *scaling*: Plug into existing audiences with a revenue-share product instead of building your own audience.
- *niche_selection*: As the world gets more digital, physical products like print magazines gain value.
- *customer_acquisition*: Personalised, trigger-based outreach tied to a moment of pride converts well.
- *pricing*: Entry fees, even nominal, increase participant commitment.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Survival competitions need waivers to avoid lawsuits if someone is hurt.
- Finding sponsors adds friction that stops people launching events.
- The plaque company's outreach can look like an official gift, which is misleading, and it relies on a disclaimer in fine print.
