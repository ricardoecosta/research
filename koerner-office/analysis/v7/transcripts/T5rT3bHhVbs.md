## Tactics and playbook steps

**Use a franchise decision checklist** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Ask whether the brand itself will bring customers.
2. Assess whether the operating systems are genuinely difficult to recreate.
3. Check whether franchise operators have a track record of succeeding.
4. Calculate whether the economics still work after fees and royalties.
> "The economics still work after fees and royalties." — Unnamed host (Johnny & Sergio channel) (~11:22)

**Evaluate the customer experience in local home services** (customer_acquisition)
1. Check how local customers actually find providers: the host says they compare Google results and reviews.
2. Make it easy to book and answer the phone quickly.
3. Deliver the service reliably and do a good job.
> "At the end of the day in the home service space, customers want somebody who answers their phone, shows up, does a great job, makes booking very easy." — Unnamed host (Johnny & Sergio channel) (~3:31)

**Build the operating system before adding fixed assets** (operations)
1. Start with customer acquisition and the operating system.
2. Use cleaning contractors who already know the work and bring their own equipment, as described by the host.
3. Avoid buying vans or leasing a building at the outset of this model.

**Build recurring cleaning schedules** (offer_design)

2. Set recurring weekly, bi-weekly, or monthly visits.

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: A brand premium is easier to justify when customers already seek the brand and its reputation creates demand.
- *customer_acquisition*: In a local service, customers may choose based on reviews, responsiveness, and ease of booking rather than the company logo.
- *mindset_execution*: A first business can be valuable for the skills it teaches, even if it is not the business an entrepreneur keeps forever.
- *validation_testing*: Starting with a simple, existing service category can help an operator learn before committing a large amount of capital.
- *offer_design*: Repeat demand can make routine services more valuable than they first appear.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Before buying a franchise, calculate the economics after royalties and other fees and check whether the systems and support are worth the investment.
- *operations*: Support and a playbook can be useful, but they do not remove the owner's need to make decisions, serve customers, and solve problems.
- *scaling*: Building independently can preserve control over the brand, customer relationships, systems, and the choice of marketing channels.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The host says starting a cleaning business does not guarantee earning anything, and business success depends on effort, market, execution, and skills.
- A business is not absentee ownership; the owner still has to find customers, recruit people, execute, and solve problems.
- Franchises can limit territory, vendors, and promotions while charging ongoing royalties and fees.
- The Home Service Academy pitch is promotional, and the host says it cannot guarantee success or build the business for the buyer.
- The host says a franchise owner may need franchisor approval to sell and may have rules around how the business is eventually sold.
