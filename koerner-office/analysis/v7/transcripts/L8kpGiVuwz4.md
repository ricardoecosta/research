## Summary

UpFlip visits Ty at Smoking Chimneys (as transcribed), a revitalised 1930s federal trout hatchery on four acres in Newcastle, southwest Virginia, fed by a 3,000-gallon-a-minute limestone spring. With zero fish experience, Ty spent a year cleaning the site, a year learning to raise trout and a year getting state processing approval, burned through $60k of savings and worked at a grocery store before reaching $120k in sales; the farm now does $1.1M revenue with about $218k (19.8%) net margin, selling 85% fresh to Michelin-star and high-end restaurants and 15% frozen/smoked to co-ops, grocers and direct-to-consumer boxes. Growth came with no debt and no ads, using organic reels, chef referrals and in-person cooler drops. The key lesson was raising price from $11.50/lb (below a $15/lb cost) to $18/lb, which saved the business.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Know your cost per unit and price above it** (pricing)
1. Run the numbers on full cost per pound (~$15)
2. Raise price from $11.50 to $18 so you earn a few dollars per pound
3. Accept losing a few customers; restaurants can absorb it better than retail co-ops
> "if it's cost in 15, we need to make a couple bucks per pound to run our business" — Ty (~30:14)

**Product-first pitch to chefs** (sales)
1. Show up in person with a cooler of fish
2. Explain briefly what you do
3. Let the chef taste it
4. Build exactly what top chefs ask for (raised brook trout over two years for Blue Hill)
> "I would show up with the product, tell them a little bit about what we were doing, and then let them travel the trout" — Ty (~27:01)

**Fixed weekly operating rhythm** (operations)
1. Monday: harvest, process, chill, pack, UPS
2. Tuesday: local deliveries and retail orders
3. Wednesday: smoking
4. Thursday/Friday: feed runs and pond work
5. Weekly one-hour weed trimming; check dissolved oxygen (target ~7.5-7.8 ppm)

**Exit low-volume channels** (sales)
1. Calculate how much volume each channel can move
2. Quit farmers markets selling one or two fish at a time
3. Focus on bulk restaurant orders
> "That was the single biggest contributor to me like quitting all farmers' markets" — Ty (~23:08)

**Cheaper cold-chain packing** (operations)
1. Use ice packs instead of dry ice
2. Fill dead air space with paper
3. Replace any box that arrives warm
> "Paper is really important, 'cause it's killing a lot of that dead air space" — Ty (~33:11)

**Content to beat product stigma** (acquisition)
1. Post 2-3 reels a week showing natural raising conditions
2. Use content as the only marketing; no ads

## Lessons

- *pricing*: Charging properly protects customers too, since they rely on you staying in business.
- *mindset_execution*: Take messy action and iterate; every wall is a turn in a maze.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Avoiding debt preserves creative freedom, at the cost of slower growth.
- *niche_selection*: A unique physical asset (the spring) is the moat; water quality drives taste.
- *customer_acquisition*: Pick channels that move volume; tiny retail sales can't cover fixed costs.
- *offer_design*: Deliver exactly what demanding flagship customers ask for to earn a reference account.
- *mindset_execution*: Things take much longer than expected; persistence through the low point matters.
- *mindset_execution*: Identify what you're really afraid of; fear keeps you in your current paradigm.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Underpricing below cost slowly bleeds a business out; he agonised six months before raising price.
- Inexperience killed fish early on; there were few resources for raising trout in an old system.
- Overgrown pond weeds cut oxygen and nearly suffocated a pond of fish.
- It takes years before trout can be sold; cash burn on feed is heavy.
- Boutique farming has thin margins: you spend a lot to make a fraction.
