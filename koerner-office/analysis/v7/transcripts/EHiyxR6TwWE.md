## Summary

UpFlip host walks through Chris Conrad's 'Long Yards' business, which rents fenced, gated dirt lots (storage yards) to contractors and other tenants on month-to-month terms. Chris explains how he found tenants early on (approaching vehicles, flyers at Home Depot, online marketplaces), how land is evaluated (net rentable square footage, NOI, cap rate) and how deals can be done via purchase, lease or profit share with landowners using investor money. The key takeaway is that the model is simple operationally but is really a land and real estate business that needs capital, partners and underwriting skill.

### I1. Contractor storage yards (Long Yards)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Cold approach for first tenants** (acquisition)
1. Go where people with trucks and trailers are (Home Depot car parks, red lights, tow truck drivers)
2. Ask 'Do you need a storage yard?' and target people who look like they can pull equipment
3. Leave flyers in vehicle door handles if they are not there
4. Also post on online marketplaces and be everywhere
> "Excuse me, sure. Yeah, you mean, do you need a storage yard for any equipment?" — UpFlip host (~9:21)

**Pre-sell yards to fund build-out** (finance)
1. Pre-sell yards by the year to tenants
2. Use the cash to afford front gates and fencing for the first five yards
> "I had to pre-sell them by the year to afford the front gates to get it going." — Chris Conrad (~12:20)

**Price by conversion rate** (pricing)
1. Price as high as you think the market will bear
2. Track leads, show-ups and closes as a sales pipeline
3. Lower price until conversion rate hits target
> "we priced it as high as we thought we could. And then we let the market tell us." — Chris Conrad (~18:57)

**Underwrite land with net rentable sq ft** (finance)
1. Measure usable acres and convert to sq ft (3 acres = 130,680 sq ft)
2. Subtract about 30% for roads, wetlands and setbacks to get net rentable sq ft
3. Multiply by rate per sq ft (e.g. $0.25) and subtract about 10% vacancy
4. Deduct expenses (about $4k/month in example) to get NOI
5. Multiply NOI by 12 and divide by cap rate (8%) to find what the dirt is worth and what you can offer
> "So now you got 92,000 net rentable square feet. So we need to figure out how much money you can make per square foot from this." — UpFlip host (~7:02)

**Lock up a deal with a refundable deposit** (legal)
1. Send a contract to the landowner with a 90-day due diligence period
2. Put down a refundable deposit of about one month's rent
3. Confirm zoning/permits with the city
4. Then assemble investors and funding
> "Here's one month's deposit. I have 90 days of due diligence, which I need to confirm with the city" — Chris Conrad (~35:45)

**Qualify tenants with a few questions** (sales)
1. Ask what problem they are solving, what size yard they need and when
2. Get them to visit the site and see security
3. Close on site when urgency is high
> "Three, four pillar questions is all you need." — Chris Conrad (~32:01)

**Let demand set yard size mix** (product)
1. Watch which sizes rent fastest and have the longest waitlist
2. Build more of those sizes
3. Use movable fencing so yard sizes can be reconfigured
> "let the demand tell us and let's build this supply" — Chris Conrad (~15:49)

## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: Learn the numbers (cost, value, income) before approaching landowners or investors, otherwise you are flying blind.
- *finance_unit_economics*: You can find the deal without owning the money: lock up a deal with a deposit and bring in partners.
- *customer_acquisition*: Sticky, high-lifetime-value customers justify a higher CAC.
- *niche_selection*: A boring, unsexy niche with a physical moat (scarce land) can protect against competition.
- *mindset_execution*: Act early and scrappily; learn faster by doing than by reading.
- *automation_ai*: Use automation for enforcement (gate lockout on non-payment) to reduce chasing payments.
- *validation_testing*: Let demand data (waitlists, fastest rentals) drive product mix and supply decisions.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Hire hungry, persistent people; Chris hired Eddie because of his relentless persistence.
- *scaling*: Maximise income per square foot by adding options like boxes and offices on the same land.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Chris lost everything after a big box store did not pay his construction company, going from 14 employees to filing a consumer proposal.
- Land is the real constraint: expensive, scarce and hard to get, so deals are hard to find.
- If rent per sq ft assumptions are wrong (e.g. 15 cents instead of 25 cents) the deal does not work, so validate market rates before approaching landowners or investors.
- Spending tens of thousands per month on marketing did not work at some locations.
- Don't trust people on words alone; inspect what you expect and use cameras and presence.
- Permitting can take three months to three years, so plan interim use of existing buildings.
