## Summary

Chris Koerner explains 'the great unbundling': as Craigslist was split into billion-dollar companies, platforms like Apify, Clay, Zapier and OpenAI can be split into single-purpose tools. He profiles Nico, a former pasta maker turned SDR who vibe-coded a Google Maps lead scraper on an Apify actor. It charges $35/month convertible into credits and reached $3,500 MRR in about 7 months with near-zero costs and word-of-mouth growth. Chris then lists 12+ ideas built the same way, including niche scrapers, Amazon review reports, a Zapier support bot, a micro-influencer finder, an Upwork search tool, a single-feature Clay, a niche 'Zillow' for RV parks or laundromats, and a Shopify-only BuiltWith. His advice is to move fast before the window closes.

### I1. Google Maps lead scraper SaaS (Nico's API arbitrage)

- **Customer:** B2B sales teams, SDRs, cold callers, marketing agencies
- **Problem:** Building lists of local businesses manually is slow; Apify is too technical for many
- **Offer:** Simple search-bar website returning local business lists
- **Business model:** Monthly subscription convertible into credits
- **Pricing:** Subscription with rollover credits. USD 35 per month Cancelled customers keep unused credits and can return months later.
- **Acquisition:** referrals_word_of_mouth: No paid ads; told friends in sales who spread it.
- **Delivery:** Apify actor does the scraping via API key; Stripe for billing; technical friend fixes what AI breaks.
- **Unit economics (claims):** $3,500 MRR after ~7 months; Costs almost zero, support almost zero; Growing double digits monthly
- **Tools:** Apify, Stripe, Claude, Codex
- **Startup cost:** Almost zero
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated (about 7 months to $3.5k MRR)
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Works from the UK; UK GDPR applies to scraping personal data (names, emails, followers); buyers using the lists for marketing must follow PECR (no cold email/SMS to individuals or sole traders; cold email to limited companies is fine). Platform ToS risk.
- **Risks:** Many competing scrapers; Dependence on Apify actor and Google ToS; UK GDPR/PECR exposure for customers' use of data
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 4/5 (Inbound content, SEO, LinkedIn posts and paid ads automate well; self-serve funnel.); onboarding 5/5 (Self-serve sign-up and credits.); delivery 5/5 (API call to an Apify actor or other API returns results unattended.); billing 5/5 (Stripe subscriptions/credits.); support 4/5 (Self-serve product; an AI agent can answer most tickets.)
> "today he's running a software business doing 3,500 bucks a month in recurring revenue. No technical knowledge whatsoever" — Chris Koerner (~1:13)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**API arbitrage build** (product)
1. Pick an Apify actor (or Clay/Zapier feature)
2. Copy its API key
3. Vibe-code a simple site with a search bar in Replit/Lovable/Codex/Claude
4. Add Stripe
5. Buy a ~$10 domain
6. Charge a markup per result or subscription
> "You're just putting a simple storefront on top of someone else's software" — Chris Koerner (~7:15)

**Subscription-to-credits pricing** (pricing)
1. Charge monthly
2. Convert unused spend into non-expiring credits
3. Let cancelled users return and use credits
> "if they canceled their account, they could still come back 9 months later and use the rest of their credits" — Chris Koerner (~11:16)

**Shopify list cross-sell** (acquisition)
1. Get a list of all Shopify stores (BuiltWith or an Upwork VA)
2. Sell 10-20 different tools to the same list
3. See which resonates

**Find hot business categories on BizBuySell** (product)
1. Scrape all Texas listings
2. Scrape again a week later and subtract what sold
3. Repeat weekly for a month
4. Build niche marketplaces for fastest-selling categories
> "scrape everything in Texas on Bisby by sell again and then subtract what sold" — Chris Koerner (~21:24)

**Ad-test multiple micro-SaaS ideas** (validation)
1. Build five ideas
2. Spend $100 ads on each
3. Keep the one with lowest CPA

**Weekend build challenge** (mindset)
1. Pick something to unbundle
2. Build a site with a search bar calling an API
3. See how far you get in an afternoon/weekend

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Big platforms bundle thousands of features; unbundling one into a focused product for a niche is the opportunity.
- *offer_design*: Customers assume a single-purpose tool is better and will pay more for an easier UI.
- *pricing*: Pricing that respects usage patterns (rollover credits) beats subscription fatigue.
- *automation_ai*: Non-technical founders can ship with AI coding tools plus a technical friend for fixes.
- *mindset_execution*: 'A feature can be a company' is now true; build lifestyle businesses.
- *customer_acquisition*: One customer list (e.g. Shopify stores) can be sold many different tools.
- *offer_design*: Design quality matters; ugly niche sites are vulnerable to well-designed competitors.
- *automation_ai*: Self-serve tools can run with near-zero support and grow via word of mouth.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The efficacy of a growth hack is inversely correlated with its lifespan; windows close fast.
- Marketplaces are hard to launch and most fail; scrape supply instead of relying on listings.
- Many competitors already offer Google Maps scraping.
- Scrapers were unreliable in 2019 (Chris gave up an influencer idea).
