## Tactics and playbook steps

**Old-school Etsy reverse engineering via reviews** (product)
1. Search 'printable' on Etsy and filter to instant download
2. Sort by top reviews and open the top few listings in separate tabs
3. Prefer sellers with thousands of reviews but few products (e.g. under about 2,000 SKUs)
4. Check the category on Google Trends over 5 years to confirm growth
5. Discard items from sellers with huge catalogues or declining trends
> "finding a seller that has a rare combination of thousands of reviews but not a lot of products those are the diamonds in the rough" — Chris Koerner (~4:02)

**Etsy best-seller URL hack** (product)
1. Search a term such as 'printable' on Etsy
2. Open All Filters and tick Star Sellers
3. In the results URL replace the word 'star' with 'best'
4. Browse the resulting best-sellers and open the store to see total sales
5. Optionally use ChatGPT Operator or Manus to scrape best-selling products and stores
> "go to the URL and replace the word star with the word best and look all the products change now now you have best sellers" — Chris Koerner (~5:56)

**Create a variation with Picsart AI** (product)
1. Save or screenshot the best-selling listing image
2. Upload to Picsart and use AI enhance to improve quality and remove watermarks where possible
3. Use the AI image generator with a prompt (e.g. rabbit in grass) and the uploaded image as style reference
4. Pick the best generated options and list them
> "use PixArt and their AI generation tools to come up with some other designs for yourself" — Chris Koerner (~8:58)

**Cheap demand testing before Etsy** (acquisition)
1. List about five design variants on Facebook Marketplace with identical title, description and price
2. Compare views, clicks and messages per listing
3. Alternatively run Facebook ads at $20 per design with identical copy
4. Move the winning design to Etsy
> "you could list all five of them in the exact same market with the exact same description a pure AB test" — Chris Koerner (~10:12)

**Copy first, change later** (mindset)
1. Copy the existing winning business as closely as possible at the start
2. Change nothing until your own data shows what to improve
3. Optimise gradually and differentiate later
> "I'm going to start by 100% copying the existing business because everything I change increases the likelihood that I'll have suboptimal success" — Chris Koerner (~11:16)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Use marketplace social proof (reviews and sales counts) as free demand data before building anything.
- *niche_selection*: The best targets combine high sales with a small catalogue, which suggests a few winning products rather than a broad shotgun approach.
- *validation_testing*: Validate demand with cheap A/B tests on Facebook Marketplace or about $20 of ads before investing in a full catalogue.
- *niche_selection*: Cross-check marketplace popularity with Google Trends to avoid declining niches.
- *mindset_execution*: In the early stage, copy proven formats exactly and only change things once your own data justifies it.
- *automation_ai*: AI tools can cut design production for digital goods to minutes, so product creation is no longer the bottleneck; finding demand is.
- *offer_design*: Seasonal products let you reuse the same research and generation workflow for each upcoming holiday.
- *other*: Marketplace-specific quirks such as hidden filters can give a research edge but may disappear, so do not build a business that depends on them.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Digital products have low friction so far more competitors; avoid saturated products like generic personal finance spreadsheets.
- Check Google Trends before committing; some popular niches (e.g. steampunk) are shrinking.
- AI image tools struggle with words, so text-heavy designs need extra checking.
- Copying existing designs and buying established Etsy accounts raise IP and Etsy terms-of-service risks; the video does not address them.
- The space is full of scammers and gurus selling courses.
