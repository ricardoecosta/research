## Tactics and playbook steps

**Price-anchored ad against premium incumbents** (acquisition)
1. Find a service where big brands sell via in-home sales at high prices
2. Pick price that still nets ~$1,000/job
3. Run a static image ad: product photo + '$X installed'
4. Target broad radius, age 30-60
5. Raise price as reviews accumulate
> "we opened up a uh a Chevy dealership next to a Ferrari dealership" — Parker Smith (~12:31)

**Speed-to-lead text then relentless follow-up** (sales)
1. GHL auto-texts instantly: 'Hi X, this is Jack with Raleigh Water Pros, when's a good time for 5 minutes to quote?'
2. Wait ~1 hour, then call and text
3. Follow up until they buy, opt out or say leave me alone
4. Expect 4-8 touches before first conversation; closes up to 49 days later
> "Hey, Kevin, this is Jack with Raleigh Waterpros. I got your form. When's a good time for us to chat for 5 minutes" — Parker Smith (~26:01)

**Hire independent plumbers via sponsored Indeed post** (hiring)
1. Post: licensed, own supplies, $75-125/hour, 2+ jobs/week, only softener/filtration/RO installs
2. $90/day for 4 days
3. Pick plumbers busy ~30 hrs/week who want more, not struggling or fully booked
4. Take them to coffee/lunch to onboard

**Piggyback on competitors' water tests** (sales)
1. Ask every lead if they have a water test
2. Use competitor's test to remove knowledge gap
3. For city water, use EWG public database instead of in-home test
4. Send wells to third-party testing

**Review maniac for high-ticket service** (acquisition)
1. Ask every customer for a review after the job
2. Offer to drop free salt later and ask spouse/brother to review too

**Remote-access inventory storage** (operations)
1. 10x20 storage unit with smart lock
2. Per-plumber access codes and delivery driver code
3. LTL freight with same driver
4. Owner visits twice a month

**Pay ops manager per closed job, not %** (hiring)
1. $200 flat per closed job plus base
2. Owns lead-to-install end-to-end

**Take deposits if no inventory** (finance)
1. Collect deposit at signing if ordering after sale
2. Prevents customers walking during delays

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Your margin is my opportunity: many trade services have made-up market rates.
- *customer_acquisition*: Putting the price in the ad replaces a skilled salesperson by filtering for educated buyers.
- *pricing*: Pick the cheapest price that still hits your target net per job, then raise with reputation.
- *offer_design*: The model (sub out installs, own marketing) matters more than the product.
- *customer_acquisition*: Most high-ticket sales close after multiple touches; quitting early wastes leads.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Hire one owner for the whole lead-to-job pipeline rather than a pure salesperson.
- *customer_acquisition*: The higher the ticket, the harder you must push for reviews.
- *niche_selection*: Blue-collar niches are overlooked because people think margins are only in tech.
- *scaling*: Prior businesses teach delegation: stop being the means of production.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Running out of inventory halted installs for a week; hold stock.
- A one-person operation can't follow up with all leads; CAC rises when leads go cold.
- About 10% of Meta lead-form leads deny filling the form.
- Don't hire struggling or fully booked subcontractors.
- Don't judge sales/marketing data until at least a month in.
- Don't sell as an outsider without enough technical knowledge to talk to tradespeople.
- UK: follow-up texts are fine to people who requested a quote, but buying or cold-texting lists of homeowners breaks PECR.
