## Tactics and playbook steps

**Launch through Product Hunt with a small existing network** (acquisition)
1. Build a simple service site and make the offer clear enough to buy without a call.
2. Submit the product to Product Hunt on launch day.
3. Ask friends and family to support the launch; Brett used his school network on Facebook to request upvotes.
4. Share the founder story on forums, even without an established audience.
5. Treat marketplace exposure as a launch spike, then build a repeatable acquisition channel.
> "My personal network was my friends and family I went to school with on Facebook. That was all I had at the time." — Brett (~5:22)

**Make a service purchasable without a sales process** (offer_design)
1. Turn a broad custom service into a clearly described package.
2. Let customers select and pay on the website instead of requesting a quote and attending several meetings.
3. Set an expected turnaround so the customer knows when value will arrive.
4. Offer pausing and resuming when demand is intermittent.
> "So instead of filling in a form on a website, getting a quote, jumping on a call, having three or four meetings discussed pricing and proposals and estimates and all sorts of stuff" — Brett (~2:32)

**Raise prices when demand exceeds capacity** (pricing)
1. When demand becomes hard to manage, test a higher price rather than immediately shutting down the offer.
2. Watch what happens to demand and customer fit after each increase.
3. Do not assume a price increase will reduce demand; Brett says each increase sometimes moved the offer into a new category and increased demand.
> "Every time I felt like I was hitting a wall and wanted to shut things down, I just, my gut reaction was just like, just gonna increase prices and see what happens." — Brett (~8:31)

**Use AI to prepare design requirements and flows** (operations)
1. Start from the client's supplied description and materials.
2. Use Claude to help generate requirements, features, and user flows.
3. Bring the result into Figma and begin designing.

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Make it possible to buy a clearly defined service without a quote request, sales call, or several meetings.
- *offer_design*: Customers may value speed and a short time to value, even when a slower process offers more customization.
- *customer_acquisition*: A small personal network can help seed a marketplace launch, but the launch itself does not remove the need for ongoing distribution.
- *niche_selection*: When a digital product is easy to build and copy, distribution and brand distinction become more important.
- *pricing*: Test price increases against demand and customer fit; a higher price may change the market category rather than simply reduce demand.
- *offer_design*: Recurring services are more attractive when customers have a repeated need, rather than a one-time project.
- *automation_ai*: Do not confuse using AI with removing the need for human expertise: the guest still relies on taste, developers, and review.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI to help prepare prompts for the specific tool that will receive them, and save reusable rules for repeated work.
- *operations*: Set workload and client limits before growth turns a low-cost solo service into an unsustainable job.
- *offer_design*: Flexible pause-and-resume service terms can fit customers whose workload changes over time.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Brett says the business grew to a workload that nearly killed him and led to a hospital stay. He later improved his systems, became more selective with clients, and reduced his hours.
- Raising prices did not reliably curb demand; Brett says price increases sometimes increased demand by moving the service into a different category.
- Monthly recurring revenue is volatile because clients churn or pause subscriptions.
- The Tea dating app suffered a data leak, according to the guest.
- The guest says AI output can fall short when the user lacks design taste, and the tools are not yet equivalent to an experienced designer.
- AI-first development can lead non-technical users into repeated troubleshooting loops and high token bills; security, payments, hosting, and support still require capable people.
- The host promotes his own community and podcast and offers a referral payment for people whose story is selected for an interview. This is a promotional conflict for the host, not evidence about the guest's business results.
