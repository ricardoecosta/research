**Look for community and nonprofit buyers** (customer_acquisition)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Design around how students actually study. Hector says many learners wanted to take courses on their own time rather than attend fixed weekday, night, or weekend classes.
- *customer_acquisition*: Treat a school as a sales and operations business: write scripts, track prospects, and follow up instead of relying on a prospect to show up after one call.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Avoid committing to buildings and staff before testing whether students will use them; the guest says his early in-person setup carried costs that online delivery did not need.
- *scaling*: A digital course can scale across a wider territory because it does not depend on filling a classroom.
- *niche_selection*: Choose a focused course and use a knowledgeable operating partner rather than trying to teach every trade yourself.
- *niche_selection*: Look for niches where employers need workers and have limited time to train them.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: A business can add value to its hiring pipeline by training potential workers itself, especially when the employer struggles to recruit.
- *automation_ai*: The guest's model separates trade expertise from systems expertise: operators teach the skill while the founder handles automation and CRM work.
- *validation_testing*: Use job listings and employer conversations to check whether a course leads to plausible work, but do not treat openings as guaranteed placements.
- *offer_design*: Build the course around the capabilities employers need. The guest says some employers want a credential quickly and intend to teach practical work themselves.
