## Tactics and playbook steps

**Verify laundromat revenue with water bills** (finance)
1. Ask the seller for 24 months of water bills
2. Divide gallons used by 15-25 gallons per front-load cycle to get the number of loads
3. Multiply loads by the price per load to estimate wash revenue
4. If the result is 15-20% below the seller's claim, treat the claim as inflated and renegotiate or walk away
> "If that number comes in 15 or 20% under the seller's claim, somebody's rounding up." — Narrator (~6:08)

**Make a long lease a condition of sale** (legal)
1. Check how many years remain on the lease
2. Make a new lease a condition of the deal ('no new lease, no sale')
3. Ask the seller to obtain a 10-year lease with two 5-year options
4. Make sure the lease outlasts the loan term
5. Use the lease, with its rent increases written in, if a new landlord later pushes for more rent
> "No new lease, no sale." — Narrator (~6:39)

**Structure seller financing to reduce cash in** (finance)
1. The bank wants 10% down
2. The seller carries half of the equity as a standby seller note until the loan is paid
3. The buyer's cash in is then about $12,500 plus closing costs
> "But Ry agrees to carry half of it as a seller note that sits on standby until the loan is paid." — Narrator (~7:07)

**Defend a vending location against a rival** (sales)
1. Add card/phone readers ($200-$600 each plus a small monthly fee)
2. Offer the location manager a commission in the normal 10-15% range
3. Sign a 2-year location agreement to lock the site in
> "You offer the manager a commission right in the normal range" — Narrator (~4:42)

**Write checklists so a manager can run stores without you** (operations)
1. Write an opening checklist and a closing checklist
2. Write a 'who to call when a dryer won't heat' list
3. Keep it in a binder so an assistant can open the store
4. Promote the assistant when the manager leaves, with two weeks of handover training
> "Opening checklist. Closing checklist. Who to call when a dryer won't heat?" — Narrator (~8:08)

**Diagnose a competitor's impact from your own water bills** (operations)
1. Track each store's water bills month by month
2. Compare stores in different neighbourhoods and entities to see the effect of a new competitor
3. Respond with card payments, cleanliness and a wash-and-fold service
4. Replace washers one row at a time from the reserve
> "you do to yourself what you did to Rey. You watch your own water bills month by month." — Narrator (~10:13)

**Fund the next acquisition from the previous business's cash** (finance)
1. Use the first store's cash for the second store's roughly $25,000 down payment
2. Use laundromat cash for a 15% car wash down payment
> "it came out of the first store's cash, not your paycheck." — Narrator (~11:30)

## Lessons

- *legal_risk*: Decide who owns the business before buying anything; separate entities with separate bank accounts and books contain the damage if one asset has a bad year.
- *validation_testing*: Verify seller claims with third-party data (utility bills) rather than trusting cash-heavy numbers.
- *operations*: Beat competitors with boring paperwork (long leases, location agreements, card readers) rather than fighting them.
- *scaling*: Start with a small cheap asset (a vending route) to learn how machines and collections work before taking on bank debt.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Written checklists and promotable staff make a business survive the loss of a manager; the structure should hold when the owner is absent.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Use cash from one acquisition to fund the next rather than personal income, and keep a reserve for equipment replacement.
- *niche_selection*: Boring, overlooked niches (independent car washes, laundromats) can be less contested than areas that big capital targets, but larger competitors still affect them.
- *mindset_execution*: Doubt and setbacks (landlord, competitor, staff loss in the same week) are normal; the response is to return to the documented rules rather than sell.
- *mindset_execution*: Wealth built this way is slow (about 12 years in the story) and modest per asset; the story's own framing is 'not 18 months'.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not buy the first store on the seller's word, uncheckable cash numbers and a lease with only 3 years left; get 24 months of water bills and make sure the lease outlasts the loan.
- A lease with only 3 years left is a trap because the landlord can raise rent or refuse to renew, and the bank will not lend against it.
- Early laundromats can lose money for a long time; Jordan Barry reportedly had a six-figure hole before getting it right.
- Private equity chains carry heavy debt (Zips filed for bankruptcy protection in 2025), but they are also well-funded competitors in car washes.
