## Tactics and playbook steps

**Start in a familiar local market** (validation_testing)
1. Choose a city, preferably the one where you live.
2. Use local familiarity and existing connections to understand the area.
> "We do recommend it being the city you live in because you're most familiar with it and you probably have some connections there." — Sergio (~11:25)

**Set up the local business basics** (operations)
1. Create a simple website.
2. Set up a Google Business Profile.
3. Get a phone number separate from your personal one.
> "So, you need a simple website, a Google business profile, and a phone number to keep it separate from your personal one." — Sergio (~11:35)

**Recruit and screen cleaners** (hiring_outsourcing)
1. Post in local Facebook and Nextdoor groups asking for reliable cleaner recommendations.
2. Look for at least two years of professional cleaning experience, the cleaner's own transport, and their own equipment.
3. Check recommendations and trust before sending a cleaner to a paying customer.
> "Now, what you're looking for is at least 2 years of cleaning experience, professional experience. Two, they have their own transportation, and they have their own equipment." — Sergio (~12:12)

**Run a test clean before assigning customer work** (operations)
1. Choose a smaller, reasonable area for the cleaner to clean.
2. Use the test to assess reliability, cleaning quality, and whether the cleaner may try to take the customer.
3. Limit the number of tests by vetting candidates first.
> "This is how we test if someone is reliable, are they good at cleaning, and are they going to try to steal our customers?" — Sergio (~12:43)

**Use a personal grand-opening post** (customer_acquisition)
1. Post in a local Facebook group or Nextdoor as a person rather than as a business.
2. Introduce yourself and mention that you have a cleaning company.
3. Offer neighbours a discount.
4. Make the post relatable; Sergio suggests including family or a dog if available.
> "Do you posting in a local Facebook group or Nextdoor, but as a person, not a business. You basically introduce yourself, mention who you are, and just kind of like throw it out there that you have a cleaning company." — Sergio (~13:12)

**Add paid lead sources** (customer_acquisition)
1. Test Google LSA, Thumbtack, and Meta ads as additional sources of leads.
2. Increase advertising only while monitoring whether the resulting jobs cover acquisition and delivery costs.
> "So, there's things like Google LSA, Thumbtack, Meta, which is like Facebook and Instagram ads." — Sergio (~13:53)

**Convert a first-time clean into recurring service** (sales)
1. After each job, make a quality-control call.
2. First ask whether the cleaner did a good job and whether the customer is happy.
3. Once the customer is happy, ask whether they want weekly, bi-weekly, or monthly cleans.
> "Now, once you've established that they're happy, you're going to ask them if they're looking to do weekly, bi-weekly, or monthly cleans." — Sergio (~14:20)

**Protect the customer relationship after a poor clean** (operations)
1. Have cleaners do a walk-through before leaving the home.
2. If a customer reports poor work, send someone back to make it right.
> "We do have systems in place to make sure that cleaners will do a walk-through before they leave the house. So, we really do try to prevent this, but like I said, it'll happen from time to time." — Sergio (~9:29)

## Lessons

- *operations*: A service can gain leverage when the operator owns the customer relationship and coordinates people who perform the work.
- *offer_design*: Recurring demand can make each customer more valuable than a one-time booking; the operator should ask about a repeat schedule after confirming satisfaction.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Screen service providers before putting them in front of paying customers, and test the actual work.
- *operations*: Treat service quality as an operating system: check the result, ask the customer, and correct failures quickly.
- *niche_selection*: Choose a local market the founder understands and can reach through existing connections.
- *niche_selection*: A business that looks ordinary may still have room for local operators when the market is fragmented and existing competitors have weak marketing or systems.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use paid advertising as a channel to test, but the speaker's return claim is conditional and should not be treated as a guaranteed result.
- *mindset_execution*: Do not treat revenue examples as guaranteed outcomes; the speaker says results depend on effort and market conditions.
