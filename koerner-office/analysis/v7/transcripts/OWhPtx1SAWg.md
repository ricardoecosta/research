## Tactics and playbook steps

**Free pitch in exchange for goodwill** (acquisition)
1. Approach a high-traffic local business (hardware store) with a parking lot
2. Offer free lunch occasionally instead of rent
3. Pitch the mutual benefit: pizza customers may shop at the store
> "Symbiotic promoting each other" — Chris Koerner (~2:27)

**Start cheap and upgrade later** (operations)
1. Start local, then expand across the state
2. Buy used equipment to launch faster
3. Replace equipment as the business grows
4. Move from tent to food truck, later a bakery/commissary producing dough for several trucks
> "If we would have gotten you know just used stuff and we probably would have gotten out a little quicker" — Clayton Bowman (~4:36)

**Neapolitan-style home dough recipe** (product)
1. 65% hydration dough: 1 kg flour, water (stated as 65 g, meaning about 650 g), 25 g salt, 2 g yeast, splash of olive oil
2. Rest an hour on the counter, then 2 days covered in the fridge
3. Split into eight 200 g balls, proof about 2 hours
4. Dust with 50/50 00 flour and cornmeal; stretch with fists without pinching the edges; never use a rolling pin
5. Bake above 800F (turn down near 925F) for 60-90 seconds, rotating
> "let it sit on the counter for an hour, cover it, put it in the fridge for 2 days" — Chris Koerner (~8:38)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Barter for a pitch: high-traffic host businesses may give free space in exchange for goodwill and extra foot traffic.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Food-service margins can be high (20% food cost) when you control labour yourself, but the work is fully physical.
- *validation_testing*: Launch with used gear and upgrade gradually instead of waiting for a perfect setup.
- *scaling*: Start local and expand outward once the product is proven.
- *scaling*: A central bakery/commissary that supplies dough can enable multiple food trucks later.
- *mindset_execution*: Skill can be self-taught through repetition; expect early versions to be bad.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Waiting to buy everything new delayed the launch; used equipment would have got them selling sooner.
- Their first pizzas were bad; quality took about two years of practice.
