## Summary

Side Hustle Show host Nick Loper interviews Kyle of 10 Mile Mushrooms (Pennsylvania), who started growing gourmet mushrooms with a $300 investment after searching for a niche, small-space business. He sold at farmers markets, then added tinctures, dried mushrooms, grow blocks sold to other farms, restaurant wholesale, and courses (Pro Grow Club, Farm Launch). The main takeaways are the high margin of specialty mushrooms, the USDA beginning farmer loan used to buy a warehouse and 70 acres, grant funding, and agritourism Airbnbs. Much of the land-hack and grant material is US-specific, and the guest sells programs.

### I1. Gourmet mushroom farm (fresh mushrooms at farmers markets, CSAs, restaurants)

- **Customer:** Farmers market shoppers, local restaurants and chefs, CSA customers
- **Problem:** Specialty mushrooms are unavailable locally, even in large supermarkets, and growing needs little space.
- **Offer:** Fresh gourmet and medicinal mushrooms (oyster, lion's mane, reishi) grown on hardwood blocks
- **Business model:** Per-pound product sales at markets, wholesale to restaurants, CSA participation
- **Pricing:** Per pound, retail at market and lower wholesale. market retail USD 24-30 per pound fresh at market; wholesale USD 10-12 per pound wholesale Sold in half and quarter pound portions to hit the price; wholesale margin is about half of retail.
- **Acquisition:** retail_placement: Farmers market table; the unusual product stops passers-by. Market fees range from $25 to about $500 a year in his area.; referrals_word_of_mouth: Restaurant wholesale grew from relationships built at the market and chef introductions.; partnerships: Joined other farmers' CSAs rather than running their own.
- **Delivery:** Grow on 10 lb fruiting blocks (about 1 sq ft each, 2-4 lb yield) in a closet, tent or grow room; harvest, pack and sell personally at markets; start fresh blocks weekly.
- **Unit economics (claims):** A $10 block yields about 3 lb worth $60-80 at retail; Better than 75% gross margin; Wholesale roughly half the margin; A 4-block tent yields 16 lb every two weeks
- **Tools:** Grow tent, Hardwood fruiting blocks, Small business insurance, Market tent, table and cash box
- **Startup cost:** $300 initial (straw, poly tubing); $2-3k for a full setup; $200-300 for market supplies
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — UK farmers markets and specialty mushroom demand exist (guest found a UK page); prices, food hygiene registration, insurance and scale rules differ.
- **Risks:** Sales and marketing trips up beginners; Physical labour and market time; Mold or contamination; Fad-driven demand; Strain degeneration if re-propagating
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 0/5 (In-person farmers market sales and personal relationship building.); onboarding 1/5 (Mostly informal relationships; restaurant accounts need human setup.); delivery 0/5 (Physical growing, harvesting, packing and attending markets.); billing 2/5 (Cash and card at market; invoices for wholesale.); support 1/5 (Face-to-face and ad hoc.)
> "you're able to get anywhere between 24 to $30 a pound out of these mushrooms" — Kyle (~0:14)
> "for one block that we sell wholesale for $10, you're yielding three pounds of mushrooms, which is yielding you some sort of revenue between 60 and 80 bucks" — Kyle (~11:49)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Map outlets then test at farmers market** (acquisition)
1. Grow a few batches first
2. List potential outlets: farmers markets, CSAs, direct to customer, wholesale
3. Test interest and put mushrooms in people's hands
4. Double down on the best-performing channel (markets)
> "You make a list of all your potential outlets as to where you can sell these things" — Kyle (~8:55)

**Layer secondary products from market stand** (product)
1. Start with fresh mushrooms
2. Add tinctures, then dried mushrooms
3. Sell grow blocks to people who tried the mushrooms
4. Respond to inbound bulk demand from other farms and advertise to them

**Break fresh mushrooms into small portions** (pricing)
1. Sell in half- and quarter-pound units
2. Achieve $24-30 per pound even in a lower-income area

**Use cycles of fresh blocks weekly** (operations)
1. Run only first flush commercially
2. Introduce fresh blocks each week
3. Predict about 50 lb a week for steady supply

**Grant pipeline with AI** (finance)
1. Monitor Grants.gov, SAM.gov, SARE, FSA and local USDA office, and local economic development bodies
2. Use AI to research and draft applications
3. Keep cash cushion for reimbursement

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Choose a business by constraints (small space, niche, little local competition, draws a crowd) rather than passion first.
- *validation_testing*: Start small and learn by trial and error with minimal capital ($300) before investing.
- *customer_acquisition*: Be where buyers already are and primed to spend, even if there is an entry fee.
- *offer_design*: Unique products at a market stand stand out and sell themselves.
- *scaling*: Expand from the core product into adjacent high-margin products and supplying other businesses.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Government programs (loans, grants) can fund growth for beginners but depend on local jurisdiction.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: As the business grows, move from operator to owner with a manager and team.
- *mindset_execution*: Community and mentors matter; avoid being a lone wolf.
- *automation_ai*: AI can double output on grant research and applications.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Sales and marketing, not growing, is what trips most beginning farmers up.
- Get some small-scale growing experience before building out a full grow room.
- Re-propagating from store-bought mushrooms degrades genetics over time; commercial growers start fresh blocks.
- Second and third flushes can cause a traffic jam in a space-limited grow room.
- Grant funding is reimbursement-based, so you need cash cushion.
