## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Freychu, a solo operator who builds SEO-driven niche directory websites. He owns six directories (three fully built) that draw roughly 70-90k organic monthly visitors and earn about $2,300-$3,000/month passively. The episode walks through finding niches via Ahrefs 'near me' keyword research (low keyword difficulty, decent volume), analysing example directories (dry ice, movie locations, towing capacity), tooling, monetisation and a cheap backlink-swap tactic. Key takeaway: directories are slow-burn SEO assets; pick low-competition niches, build ugly-but-useful mobile-friendly sites, enrich the data, and buy a few high-quality swapped backlinks.

### I1. Niche SEO directory portfolio (thrifting directory and others)

- **Customer:** Consumers searching for specific local or niche information (thrift stores, dog parks, flea markets, plasma donation, etc.); advertisers as the paying side
- **Problem:** Google Maps and generic results handle very specific location or niche queries poorly, leaving more search demand than good supply
- **Offer:** A specialised directory website with enriched, filterable data answering a narrow query set
- **Business model:** Display ads (Mediavine Journey), digital product sales, optionally sponsored listings/affiliate
- **Pricing:** Free to users; monetised via ads and a digital product. PDF built in Canva sold on one directory USD 19 one-off RPM about $16-17 on the thrifting niche; Mediavine main network denied for the first directory, accepted into Mediavine Journey
- **Acquisition:** seo_organic_search: 100% organic; keyword research in Ahrefs, exact-match-style domains, programmatic pages, and backlinks built by paying sites to swap a competitor's link for his (e.g. $50 swap).
- **Delivery:** One person builds the site in WordPress with Elementor Pro, GeoDirectory, WP Import and JetEngine; data is collected and enriched with AI; then the site is left alone. He says he hasn't touched his sites for about six months.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Three profitable directories earn about $2,300-$3,000/month combined passively; Traffic of about 70-90k monthly visitors across the sites; One $50 backlink led to rank 1 and about $2,000-$2,500/month in the thrift niche over two years; A $19 PDF sells almost daily
- **Tools:** Ahrefs, WordPress, Elementor Pro, GeoDirectory, WP Import, JetEngine, Mediavine Journey, Canva, Lovable, enrich.directory, Google Maps reviews, Reddit, TikTok
- **Startup cost:** About $100 for a domain and basic hosting per the host; Ahrefs is about $150/month
- **Time to first revenue:** Slow: months for SEO to rank; he says it took months to find out his first directory worked
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — SEO and display ads work the same in the UK. Examples are US-centric and UK volumes are smaller, so niches need UK keyword validation. Mediavine and Ezoic are available. GDPR and cookie consent are needed for ads and for any business data scraped about sole traders.
- **Risks:** Google core updates and AI overviews can cut informational traffic; Slow to rank, with uncertain payoff; Copycats can quickly clone a successful niche; Ahrefs traffic data is third-party and can be inaccurate; Low-ceiling income unless sponsored listings are sold
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 4/5 (SEO is inbound; agents can do keyword research, content and data pages. Backlink outreach to sites (mostly companies) can be semi-automated but needs human approval and payment.); onboarding 5/5 (No customers to onboard on the consumer side; ad networks are a one-off setup.); delivery 4/5 (Data collection, enrichment, page generation and updates can be agent-driven with occasional human QA.); billing 5/5 (Ad network and digital product payouts are automatic.); support 4/5 (Minimal support; occasional listing corrections or emails.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Find directory niches with the 'near me' keyword method** (acquisition)
1. Open Ahrefs Keywords Explorer and search 'near me'
2. Skip branded keywords (Target, McDonald's)
3. Scroll deep, sorting by keyword difficulty; look for low KD (green) with decent volume
4. Aim for KD at or below about 20 generally, 0-5 for beginners; volume of 15k-60k worked best
5. Reject product-led or fad queries where e-commerce dominates (e.g. Dubai chocolate)
6. Check search intent and narrow broad terms (e.g. 'emergency towing')
> "I find a lot of promise in keywords that hover around like 15,000 monthly search volume to like 60,000" — Freychu (~23:18)

**Use a 'bounty' competitor as validation** (acquisition)
1. Find a mom-and-pop-looking site already getting traffic in the niche
2. Check its traffic in Ahrefs Site Explorer
3. Judge whether you can beat it; being 2nd-5th can still pay
4. Snipe its keywords and backlinks
> "finding a website that already succeeded that looks like kind of a mom and pop publisher" — Freychu (~4:41)

**Backlink swap outreach** (acquisition)
1. Pull the competitor's backlinks in Ahrefs
2. Contact the sites linking to them
3. Offer about $50 to swap their link to yours (not just add)
4. Prefer pages with high domain rating, real traffic, and few outbound links
5. Avoid bulk Fiverr or low-quality link services; if buying, route through an intermediate article
> "Can I pay you 50 bucks to swap out the link for ours" — Freychu (~45:10)

**Validate demand with Reddit, TikTok and Google Maps reviews** (acquisition)
1. Search Reddit for the question; many threads signal unmet need
2. Use TikTok comments for unfiltered social listening
3. Read Google Maps review tags
4. Spend a couple of hours reading threads to find nuance for the directory's value

**Programmatic build with data enrichment** (product)
1. Collect location data in a database
2. Bulk import with WP Import and publish with GeoDirectory
3. Add filters with JetEngine (e.g. fenced area for small dogs)
4. Use AI to enrich listings with answers visitors need
5. Make it mobile-friendly; design matters less

**Stack low-effort sites and wait** (mindset)
1. Launch a directory and build another while you wait
2. Expect months for SEO results
3. Monetise only after traffic: ads, digital products, affiliate
> "while you wait go build another one and then another one" — Chris Koerner (~51:40)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Directories are slow passive SEO assets; expect months before traffic, so run several in parallel rather than judging one after two weeks.
- *scaling*: Choose passive over maximum revenue: display ads on many sites give lower ceilings but avoid becoming a sales or agency business.
- *niche_selection*: Niche selection beats design: balance low keyword difficulty with search volume, and pick evergreen subjects unlikely to change.
- *offer_design*: Utility beats polish; mobile-friendliness matters more than modern design since about 80% of visitors are on mobile.
- *mindset_execution*: Constraints can drive creativity; treat the first project as a skill-learning playground (SEO) and monetise later.
- *customer_acquisition*: Cheap targeted link swaps can beat expensive link-building services; one link can transform a site's rankings.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Check monetisation potential before choosing a niche: RPMs vary hugely (gaming low, finance high), so traffic alone doesn't indicate revenue.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI mainly for data enrichment that saves visitors time, not just for ideas; user-generated content gives better ideation signal.
- *niche_selection*: Mine your own life moments (moving, graduation, hobbies) for directory ideas, since your experience gives unique angles.
- *other*: Sharing the domain publicly invites copycats; keep winning niches quiet.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not buy cheap bulk backlinks (e.g. Fiverr); they are spammy, may not help, and can get you de-indexed.
- Posting your exact domain publicly invited copycats within 24 hours.
- Broad service niches like towing near me need many high-quality backlinks and are intermediate to advanced for beginners.
- Informational niches are vulnerable to Google core updates and AI overviews; avoid fad or product-led keywords.
- Ahrefs traffic is third-party data and can be off by tens of thousands of visits.
- Backlink purchasing is technically against Google's rules; vet vendors carefully.
- B2B sponsorship model failed for plasma because few buyers exist.
