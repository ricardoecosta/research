### I1. Strategic partnership playbook (DVLA) for customer acquisition

- **Customer:** Side hustlers and small businesses with no audience
- **Problem:** No followers or network; ads are expensive and organic content is slow.
- **Offer:** A repeatable partner outreach system: build a list, lead with value, prove with testimonials, run a small test, scale what works.
- **Business model:** Method for acquiring customers via referral/affiliate/swap partnerships; guest's own business is a partnership platform.
- **Pricing:** Not stated for the playbook; free playbook download tied to Spark platform.  Partner commission or affiliate share is back-ended after trust is built.
- **Acquisition:** partnerships: List 10-100 partners who already serve the same buyer; send a four-part message (what we bring, what you bring, what we build together, why now) with a 15-minute call CTA; claimed 5-10 responses per 100 sent.; outbound_linkedin: Find a marketing or growth person on LinkedIn and ask about a referral program (Nick's example).
- **Delivery:** Run outreach, hold short calls, agree a small time-bound test with swipe copy, measure attribution after about 30 days, then repeat or escalate.
- **Unit economics (claims):** 5-10 responses per 100 outreach messages; 15 minutes a day
- **Tools:** ChatGPT, Claude, Spark platform
- **Startup cost:** Near zero (free AI tools)
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Works in the UK; cold outreach to named individuals or sole traders must respect PECR, and affiliate disclosures follow ASA/CMA rules.
- **Risks:** Misaligned partner audience wastes legal and time cost; Unequal value exchange breeds resentment; Deals stall without deadlines; Guest's platform is promotional
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (AI can build partner lists and draft outreach; cold email to companies is legal under PECR but individuals and sole traders need care; relationship follow-up needs a human.); onboarding 2/5 (Calls and agreeing terms are mostly human.); delivery 3/5 (Swipe copy, scheduling and tracking can be automated; partner deliverables depend on the partner.); billing 4/5 (Affiliate tracking and payouts can be automated.); support 3/5 (Partner check-ins can be templated, with human judgement for disputes.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Four-bullet partner outreach message** (sales)
1. State what we bring in one sentence
2. State what you bring, showing you researched their audience
3. Propose what we can build together, e.g. a co-branded campaign
4. Give a timely why-now hook
5. End with a 15-minute call CTA with 'no harm, no foul'
> "what we bring, what you bring, what can we build together and why now" — Kyle (~17:41)

**Build partner list with AI** (acquisition)
1. Give an AI tool your ideal customer profile
2. Ask where they get information, who they trust and where they spend
3. Branch with 'alternatives to' and 'people like this person'
4. Start with 10-20 partners, not 100

**Minimum viable partnership with a deadline** (operations)
1. Pick a small commitment such as a newsletter swap, script or creative brief
2. Provide swipe copy to reduce partner work
3. Set a launch date
4. Measure after 2-4 weeks and rebalance exposure
5. Escalate to paid placement once attribution exists
> "the launch is on this date. Are you in? Are you out?" — Nick Loper (~27:49)

**Fast proof for verification** (sales)
1. Do 5 free or at-cost jobs for friends and family
2. Film 30-second testimonials
3. Show them to prospective partners

**Audit partners by analytics, not followers** (acquisition)
1. Ask for engagement analytics and past purchases driven
2. Prefer micro-influencers with trust over big follower counts
3. Request a script or creative brief before contracting

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: You do not need an audience first; borrow trust from partners who already serve your buyer without competing.
- *niche_selection*: Choose partners by customer overlap and influence over your ICP, not by name or follower count.
- *offer_design*: Lead with a free, specific offer of value before asking for anything.
- *validation_testing*: Start with a small time-boxed test with attribution so you have data to justify scaling.
- *customer_acquisition*: Translate your pitch into the partner's outcomes, not your features, and make the ask easy and dated.
- *scaling*: Seek one-to-many channels (realtors, newsletters, communities) over one-to-one selling.
- *operations*: Treat relationships as a system with owners, milestones and check-ins rather than a favour bank.
- *mindset_execution*: Consistent small daily outreach (15 minutes) beats sporadic effort; comfort-zone discomfort is where growth happens.
- *automation_ai*: AI can generate unlimited partner lists, so the bottleneck is consistent outreach.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Partnering with brands whose customers are not your customers wastes contract, legal and time spend.
- Common failure red flags: no clear goals or attribution, clout chasing, and unequal value exchange.
- Leading with an affiliate offer feels too transactional; build trust first.
- Follower counts mislead; big audiences can deliver zero results.
