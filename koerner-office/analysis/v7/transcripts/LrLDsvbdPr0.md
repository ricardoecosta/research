## Summary

Starter Story host Gus interviews Kurosh, a London-based product manager who built Boise (transcribed as 'Voice C'), a cross-platform dictation app using OpenAI's Whisper API, as a side project. It reportedly makes 16-20K USD per month including lifetime deals, with 11K MRR. He competes with well-funded tools like Whisper Flow by targeting seniors and people with disabilities, who see dictation as a painkiller rather than a vitamin. Growth comes mainly from SEO (about 200 blog posts, 30 landing pages, localisation), plus the Chrome Web Store, Reddit and a new YouTube series. Key takeaways: talk to users to discover your real customer, a crowded market can be won by serving a neglected niche, and take action in volume rather than strategising.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Seed a Chrome extension launch with friend reviews** (acquisition)
1. Publish a simple extension to the Chrome Web Store
2. Ask about five friends to leave reviews on day one
3. Rely on store search for free traffic to reach the first ~100 users
> "I asked like five friends to leave reviews, we instantly got like 10 or 20 page views" — Kurosh (~8:32)

**Programmatic-scale SEO content** (acquisition)
1. Write many blog posts (about 200)
2. Build many landing pages (about 30) for different use cases
3. Localise the site into 4-5 languages
4. Aim to be the first result people see in Google and AI search
> "We have like 200 blog posts. We have like 30 different landing pages." — Kurosh (~9:00)

**Find the painkiller segment through user calls** (sales)
1. Talk to many users on calls
2. Segment them: 60-70% professionals wanting a 10% boost vs 20-30% seniors/disabled who need it
3. Notice who loves the MVP and does not demand improvements
4. Pivot positioning and content to that segment
> "Those guys love the product. They didn't care about any improvements." — Kurosh (~10:18)

**Disability discount and lifetime deal pricing** (pricing)
1. Offer a 20% discount to disabled users who email the founder
2. Sell a lifetime plan (~$260) alongside an $8.49 monthly plan to pull cash forward

**Platform expansion with AI coding tools** (product)
1. Start with a Chrome extension MVP in two weeks
2. Add Windows (Electron, ~3 months), then Mac (~1 month), Linux (~2 weeks), iOS and Android
3. Use Cursor, Codex and Claude to speed up builds

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: A crowded market can be entered by serving a segment that competitors ignore, here seniors and disabled users who are not talking about the well-funded tools.
- *offer_design*: Sell painkillers, not vitamins: users who truly need the product tolerate an MVP and pay without demanding features.
- *validation_testing*: User calls reveal the real customer; the ideal customer profile often picks you rather than the reverse.
- *customer_acquisition*: In crowded markets the product the customer sees first often wins, so search visibility matters more than being best.
- *niche_selection*: Go for the easiest, lowest-barrier market early rather than the biggest.
- *mindset_execution*: Do high volume of content and experiments rather than strategising; quantity produces learning faster and quality follows.
- *automation_ai*: AI coding tools let a non-expert solo founder ship across many platforms quickly (Android app one-shotted, Linux in two weeks).
- *finance_unit_economics*: Lifetime deals boost cash revenue well above MRR, but MRR is the truer recurring base to compare.
- *niche_selection*: Don't dismiss simple apps for non-tech audiences; many people outside the AI/Twitter bubble just want a problem solved.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Posting the same viral angle repeatedly on Reddit got him blocked.
- His earlier app Meatwaves stalled at 5K MRR because he did no marketing.
- Don't assume only Mac users pay for apps; the business world is on Windows.
