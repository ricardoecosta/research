## Tactics and playbook steps

**Three number test before any payment plan** (finance)
1. Write down the sticker price first.
2. Calculate total interest: monthly payment x number of months minus sticker price.
3. Calculate opportunity cost: future value of the sticker price invested at about 7%.
4. If the full number embarrasses you, the monthly number is hiding something.
> "Monthly payment times number of months minus sticker price. That equals your total interest." — Codie Sanchez (~2:16)

**50/30/20 split of every raise** (finance)
1. Measure income and expenses with a personal P&L first.
2. Commit 50% of a raise to future you (investments that make money back).
3. Put 30% to financial clean-up (high-interest debt, taxes, insurance gaps).
4. Spend 20% on an upgraded life.
5. Assign the split before the cash arrives.
> "50% to future you, 30% to financial clean up, 20% to your upgraded life." — Codie Sanchez (~7:54)

**Friction audit of recurring charges** (operations)
1. Search card statements for recurring charges, trials and PayPal.
2. Cancel anything that only survives because you forgot it.
3. Remove saved cards and auto-pay opt-ins.
4. Set calendar reminders with cancellation dates when signing up.
5. Review on the first Friday of every month (financial Fridays).
> "you're going to find every recurring charge. Open your credit statement and search for recurring trial paypal." — Codie Sanchez (~13:34)

**Emotional receipt before purchases** (mindset)
1. Ask what am I feeling and what am I trying to change.
2. Ask what actually fixes the feeling and what this will cost tomorrow.
3. Use replacement rules: tired = sleep, lonely = call someone, anxious = walk.
> "write the emotional receipt first." — Codie Sanchez (~10:58)

**Pre-design cheap defaults** (operations)
1. Buy groceries early or arrange cheaper meal help.
2. Make the cheap choice the easy default so weak moments don't set the budget.
> "Don't let your weakest moment design your budget." — Codie Sanchez (~6:16)

## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: Ask the total price, not the monthly payment; payment framing exists to reduce felt pain.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Business margins erode through lifestyle creep (hires, offices) as quickly as personal budgets do; watch cost growth versus revenue.
- *operations*: Recurring-subscription audits can save a business large sums because most tools go unused; review them on a fixed cadence.
- *mindset_execution*: Pre-commit future raises to savings before the cash arrives; automate discipline for when you are not rational.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Measure before you manage: you can't split a raise or plug a leak you haven't tracked (personal or business P&L).
- *operations*: Add friction to spending (remove saved cards, no auto-pay) so cheap choices are the easy ones.
- *scaling*: Shift from defense (plugging leaks) to offense (buying cash-flowing assets); spend only on things that make money.
- *mindset_execution*: Name the emotion behind a purchase impulse; affect labeling reduces the urge for a temporary fix.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Monthly payment framing hides the true cost of a purchase; sellers sell you the whole pie in slices.
- Business lifestyle creep (assistants, nicer offices) can cut healthy margins to about 15%.
- Taking quick high-interest loans or vendor payment plans without thinking about payback is a trap for business owners.
- Buy-now-pay-later borrowers are more likely to have higher debt, delinquencies and lower credit scores.
