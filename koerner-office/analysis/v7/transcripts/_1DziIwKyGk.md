## Tactics and playbook steps

**Start with the builder's buy box** (acquisition)
1. Identify builders that sell newly built homes, using listings as a lead source.
2. Ask what locations, lot sizes, and prices the builder will accept.
3. Record the criteria and buying contact in a simple spreadsheet.
4. Only source parcels that match the stated requirements.
> "So the first thing, the way we do it with the builder first method is you get the builder's buy box." — Jackson (~9:48)

**Find owners and make offers below the buyer price** (pricing)
1. Build a list of owners whose parcels match the builder's buy box.
2. Prioritise owners who have held the property for a long time and paid less, as the guests describe.
3. Use parcel records and owner lookup tools to find contact details.
4. Calculate an offer that leaves a spread while still making sense against current alternatives.
> "And then we would reverse engineer it, go find sellers, go pull a list of owners that are offmarket properties" — Jackson (~10:06)

**Use consistent lot criteria for batch offers** (operations)
1. Choose a market with multiple builders and repeated, similar parcels.
2. Group parcels by location and lot characteristics.
3. Avoid mixing parcels with materially different sizes or conditions in one offer campaign.
4. The guests say similar parcels let them make many consistent offers.
> "So I want to send a thousand offers at once. I can't do that if every lot's different." — Jackson (~19:32)

**Use direct mail with an offer and contract** (acquisition)
1. Send a one-page introduction to the owner.
2. Include the offer and purchase contract in the mailing.
3. Give the owner a phone number and email to respond.
4. Track signed contracts and follow up on responses.
> "we'll send them a one-page introduction letter and then we'll send them a contract. And on that contract, I'll put our offer on it." — Jackson (~20:15)

**Explain the seller's net proceeds** (sales)
1. Compare the offer with the seller's likely net after agent fees and closing costs.
2. Explain the time difference between a direct close and a listing, using specific local comparisons only when known.
3. State the amount offered, the expected closing time, and who pays closing costs.
4. Do not claim a buyer is ready unless one has actually been lined up.
> "And when you explain it like that, like they actually are benefiting from it. You already have somebody lined up and you're not really messing with them" — Jackson (~14:54)

**Keep seller outreach honest** (legal)
1. Tell the seller that you work with builders and intend to assign or pass the property to a builder.
2. Explain the intended margin where relevant, as the guests say they do.
3. Do not pretend to be the builder or misrepresent who will buy the property.
4. Make offers that can be completed and disclose material uncertainty.
> "We have always taken an honest approach. anybody we've taught to do this, we tell them to be honest" — Jackson (~13:33)

**Use a contract feasibility period and buyer deposit** (operations)
1. The guests say their seller contract includes a feasibility study through the closing date.
2. They say earnest money is not due until closing under their contract.
3. They make sure the builder's assignment contract has a due date for its earnest money.
4. Confirm contract terms with qualified local counsel before using them.
> "And we have a feasibility study. That means we can back out to the day of the closing date with no risk." — Jackson (~17:29)

**Check land constraints before closing** (operations)
1. Check aerial imagery and local records for wetlands, slope, utilities, access, wildlife, and other constraints mentioned by builders.
2. Ask the city or county what reports are available when a local term is unfamiliar.
3. Confirm parcel-specific concerns with the builder before relying on a contract.
4. Use a termination right if the parcel fails the stated feasibility checks.
> "you just basically just call the city and say, "What does this mean? How do I get a report for it?"" — Jackson (~39:07)

**Call neighbouring owners before a sale** (sales)
1. Look up the owners of neighbouring parcels after securing a deal.
2. Contact them as a courtesy and explain that a builder may buy the land.
3. Ask whether they would like to extend their yard or buy the lot.
4. Do not assume a neighbour will pay more; check whether they have interest and funds.
> "the first thing I do is I call the neighboring houses, right? Because the most annoying thing is a homeowner." — Jackson (~40:19)

**Ask for a meaningful referral** (acquisition)
1. After closing, send a handwritten thank-you letter.
2. Ask the seller to refer a friend or family member who may sell land.
3. Offer a stated cash payment only if the referral results in a completed deal.
4. Track referral source and closing so the reward is paid consistently.
> "if you refer a family member or a friend to sell their property with us and we get a deal done, we'll send you $1,000 in cash" — Jackson (~42:22)

**Keep leads warm after an initial no** (sales)
1. If the owner rejects the offer, stay polite and invite them to save the caller's details.
2. Explain that the price may change and that the business continues to buy land.
3. Record the conversation and follow up only through a lawful channel.
4. Revisit the lead if the owner gets in touch later.
> "save my name Jackson land guy and when if you need to sell for whatever reason if you need cash I will be able to close in two weeks" — Jackson (~46:08)

**Make a specific counteroffer rather than accepting immediately** (sales)
1. If a seller counters, pause briefly and confirm the counter with a partner.
2. Return with a clear answer and restate the agreement.
3. The guests say they sometimes counter slightly below the seller's requested amount to improve the chance the contract is signed.
4. Do not use a counteroffer to mislead the seller about the buyer or terms.
> "If they text back and say, "I want 20 plus closing costs." We're not going to say yes again" — Jackson (~48:10)

**Screen and train callers for tone** (hiring)
1. Interview several candidates together on Zoom.
2. Ask about experience and have each candidate tell a short story about themselves.
3. Prioritise friendliness and attitude, which the guests say affected lead volume.
4. Treat overseas assistants respectfully and train them on the actual process before delegating calls.
> "the biggest thing is it hasn't been accident. It hasn't been how smart they are, it's how friendly they are and their attitude." — Jackson (~57:30)

**Monitor new land listings** (acquisition)
1. Set alerts for new listings in a market with active builders.
2. Review new parcels as they appear.
3. Make an offer early only after checking buyer demand and the parcel.
4. The guests say older listings may already have attracted many offers or have an undisclosed issue.
> "if I just go and I have notifications on and first thing in the morning and as the new ones come in" — Jackson (~51:59)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Start with a real buyer's criteria, then source land to match it; the guests say this avoids trying to sell an unqualified deal afterward.
- *pricing*: Price an offer against what a buyer can currently obtain, rather than relying only on historic sold prices.
- *legal_risk*: Explain the transaction honestly so the seller understands who is buying and how the operator earns a margin.
- *mindset_execution*: Build call skill through repetition before handing the process to assistants; the guests say direct experience helped them hear objections and improve.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use more thoughtful outreach when it is likely to earn a stronger response, and compare the cost with the response it produces.
- *mindset_execution*: Consistency compounds: the guests say repeated calls and offers build market knowledge and bring old leads back over time.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Do not hire people to run a process you have not learned; the guests say inexperienced delegation led some beginners to make mistakes before they understood the work.
- *niche_selection*: A reliable buyer and a parcel with clear, repeatable characteristics can reduce the number of variables compared with house deals.
- *validation_testing*: Pay attention to local details that broad valuation models may miss; the host cites soil, schools, nearby employers, and neighbouring uses.
- *operations*: Do not keep spending time on a deal that will not close; the guests say there are many possible leads and relationships matter more than forcing one transaction.
- *finance_unit_economics*: An attractive cash-flow business may still have limited resale value; the guests distinguish their assignment operation from a business with recurring transferable assets.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The guests say not to send mass offers above a parcel's plausible value, because an owner may anchor on an offer that the sender cannot complete.
- The guests say some wholesalers misrepresent themselves to sellers; they recommend explaining that they work with builders and expect to assign the property.
- The guests say land can have hidden constraints including wetlands, slope, utilities, access, protected wildlife, and roads that builders do not want.
- The guests say they are not attorneys, that texting laws change, and that operators should research the rules before sending mass texts.
- The guests say mass texting is not required and they stopped using it to limit their own liability.
- The guests say the business may be difficult to sell as an exit and that high profit margins gave them a large tax bill.
- The host says his own automated home-buying venture lost money and argues that local details can defeat remote valuations.
- In the UK, unsolicited marketing texts, WhatsApp messages, DMs, and emails to individuals and sole traders require consent; cold calls must be screened against TPS/CTPS.
