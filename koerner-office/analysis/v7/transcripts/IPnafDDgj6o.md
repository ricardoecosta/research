### I1. Facebook creator monetization with value-added commentary on viral content

- **Customer:** Facebook's 2.1B daily users (advertisers pay Meta)
- **Problem:** Facebook lacks enough creators posting content for its daily users
- **Offer:** Green-screen commentary videos, screenshot posts, photos, carousels or text posts adding insight to viral content
- **Business model:** Platform ad-revenue share (Facebook creator monetization) plus newsletter funnel
- **Pricing:** Platform payout per view. Facebook reels RPM USD 0.25 per 1,000 short-form views; YouTube long-form, for comparison USD 17 per 1,000 long-form YouTube views Business/finance niches pay more than gaming/unboxing
- **Acquisition:** organic_social_content: Post natively (never cross-post) on Facebook, Instagram, YouTube Shorts, Snapchat Spotlight, X, Threads, LinkedIn; source ideas from a fresh Instagram research account and X bookmarks
- **Delivery:** Chris sources viral content (~3 hours/week), records a phone video with a DJI mic or uses a Gemini-written script on a teleprompter, edits green-screen overlay in CapCut, or pays an editor ~$10 per video; tags original creator
- **Unit economics (claims):** $10k-$35k/month profit; Individual posts $70-$2,000; $0.25 RPM on short-form; Videos taking 5-10 min + $10 editing earned $500-$2,000 each
- **Tools:** Meta Business Suite, Instagram, CapCut, Edits, Gemini, DJI Mic 2, Teleprompter app, X/Twitter
- **Startup cost:** Near zero (phone, mic; ~$10/video editor optional)
- **Time to first revenue:** Took a long time for first dollar; need Facebook invite to monetization (no published thresholds)
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Facebook monetization is available in the UK (eligibility varies by country); UK copyright has narrower 'fair dealing' (criticism/review, quotation) than US fair use, so commentary must be substantial and attributed.
- **Risks:** Copyright strikes if commentary is not transformative; UK fair dealing narrower than US fair use; Platform dependency: Facebook can change payouts or ban accounts; Monetization is invite-only with no published criteria; Needs a niche and consistent posting; new accounts get a few hundred views
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 4/5 (Sourcing viral posts and scheduling across platforms can be agent-run; virality is inbound.); onboarding 4/5 (No customer onboarding; monetization requires Facebook invite.); delivery 3/5 (AI (Gemini) can script and edit; face-to-camera recording and genuine commentary needed for fair use and reach; fully AI reposting risks strikes.); billing 5/5 (Platform pays out automatically.); support 4/5 (Minimal; comment moderation.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Viral-content commentary workflow** (operations)
1. Create a fresh Instagram research account (up to 4 sub-accounts) and train it on your niche
2. Check reel views by tapping the like count; prefer already-viral (500k+) videos
3. Bookmark ideas to bank for creative moods
4. Record commentary with a hook and a retention hack; or have Gemini watch the video and write a 60-second script for a teleprompter
5. Edit in CapCut: cut dead space, overlay, remove background, mute original, add captions and images
6. Tag the original creator
7. Upload natively to every short-form platform
> "Every video needs a retention hack. There needs to be a reason for the person to stay until the end." — Chris Koerner (~11:22)

**Never cross-post** (acquisition)
1. Turn off the 'also post to Facebook/Instagram' toggle
2. Upload natively on each platform for more reach
> "Never, ever, ever cross-ost." — Chris Koerner (~4:56)

**Screenshot viral tweets in your niche for Facebook photo posts** (acquisition)
1. Search X/bookmarks in your niche for already-viral posts
2. Pick ones fitting one screenshot
3. Stay in your niche so the algorithm matches
4. Add a hard-stance take in the caption
> "I'm looking for content that would be easily screenshottable and preferably something that is already viral" — Chris Koerner (~18:01)

**Turn Facebook traffic into owned emails** (acquisition)
1. Build a Carrd or Beehiiv landing page
2. Put the link in the top comment of every post
3. Monetize signups via SparkLoop at $0.50-$5 each

**Bootstrap junk removal without a truck** (operations)
1. Get first paying customer via personal Facebook
2. Charge ~$50/hour per person to start and raise
3. Rent a 26ft U-Haul (~$40/day + $0.75/mile)
4. Buy a truck only at ~$20k/month revenue

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Go where platform supply/demand is imbalanced: Facebook pays creators because it lacks content.
- *customer_acquisition*: Building on already-viral source material improves odds of virality.
- *customer_acquisition*: Take a hard stance to go viral; nuanced takes underperform.
- *customer_acquisition*: Stay in your niche so the algorithm serves your content to the right audience.
- *operations*: Re-recording is faster than editing out mistakes; reps improve quality.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use platform traffic to build an owned email list; it can be monetized twice.
- *validation_testing*: Find a paying customer before buying equipment.
- *niche_selection*: Unsexy, sweaty businesses have less competition because most people want to build AI apps.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Plain reaction videos or downloaded reposts are not fair use and risk bans.
- Cross-posting via the toggle significantly cuts reach.
- Social platforms can kill your reach anytime; own your audience by email.
- Viral claims often present revenue as profit.
- New accounts get shown to only a couple hundred people; it is active, not passive income.
- CapCut keeps a lot of user data.
- UK: copyright 'fair dealing' is narrower than US fair use; commentary must be genuine criticism/review with attribution.
- UK junk removal needs a waste carrier licence; buying a dump truck first wastes tens of thousands.
