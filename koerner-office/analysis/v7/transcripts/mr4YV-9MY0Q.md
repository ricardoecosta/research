## Tactics and playbook steps

**Use in-person demos to find and explain the product** (acquisition)
1. Visit open local businesses, initially without limiting outreach to one industry.
2. Show the product in person; the team began with an iPhone, then switched to iPads to make the website embed clearer.
3. Print brochures that explain what the product does.
4. Explain the customer benefit in concrete terms: the widget can help capture sales outside business hours.
> "So we switched to iPads and then printed like brochures explaining what the product was." — Tom (~6:56)

**Use prospect responses to identify a better niche** (validation_testing)
1. Show the offer to a range of businesses and observe which groups respond.
2. Ask for direct feedback, including from prospects who do not need the product.
3. Follow referrals to businesses the prospect believes are a better fit.
4. Adapt the product to suit the needs of the group that shows sustained interest.
> "So trades kind of found us. We weren't looking, but they kind of kept being interested or kept showing kind of interest in the product." — Tom (~8:21)

**Sell before building** (validation_testing)
1. Explain the customer problem and the solution before investing in a full product.
2. State the price and ask whether the prospect is willing to commit or hand over the money.
3. Treat a real commitment as evidence that the idea may be worth building.
> "If you can get someone to commit before building a product, you've probably found something worth this showing." — Tom (~10:58)

**Build an MVP, then add specialist roles** (operations)
1. Ask a developer friend to help create an MVP that tests whether the embed can work on a client's website.
2. After the layout begins to work, bring in design and development specialists to improve the interface and build the backend and frontend.

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Get a prospective customer to commit at a stated price before building the full product.
- *offer_design*: A simple product can work when it solves a real problem for a specific customer.
- *niche_selection*: Direct conversations can reveal which customer group has the clearest need, even if the founder did not initially target that group.
- *validation_testing*: Treat rejection and direct criticism as useful product feedback, and use referrals to find a better-fit customer.
- *customer_acquisition*: In-person selling can help explain a product that is difficult to understand from a description alone and can build familiarity with larger prospects.
- *offer_design*: Make the product's value concrete by explaining what it helps the customer do, such as capturing sales outside opening hours.
- *finance_unit_economics*: An existing business can provide cash to fund a new venture, but this can make the new venture dependent on the old business's income.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: A founder does not need coding experience to start a software venture if they can bring together the people needed to build and test it.
- *mindset_execution*: Software can appeal because it may keep earning after the initial work, but finding a real customer problem still takes iteration.
