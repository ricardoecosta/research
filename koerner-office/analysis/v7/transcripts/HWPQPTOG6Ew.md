## Tactics and playbook steps

**Mine the HighLevel awards page for proven white-label businesses** (product)
1. Open HighLevel's awards page (diamond/gold/platinum = 1,000/500/100 sub-accounts)
2. Give the URL to Claude and ask for companies that hit seven figures within a year
3. Have the AI search articles, LinkedIn and Reddit for each
4. Google each company's site, pricing and niche
> "look at some of these company names and find me some that reach seven figures in a year or less" — Chris Koerner (~1:44)

**Find winning ads in the Meta Ads Library** (acquisition)
1. Search all ads by keyword, company or geography
2. Filter end date to ads running for 4+ months (long-running = profitable)
3. Prefer advertisers with many long-running ads (sign of scale)
4. Screenshot and give to an AI agent (Manus, ChatGPT Operator) to find which run on HighLevel
5. Copy the ad, changing as little as possible
6. Use UK listing to see spend disclosures
> "if you see an ad that has been up for months then it is a banger ad and you should copy everything about that ad and change as little as possible" — Chris Koerner (~6:35)

**Reverse-engineer a competitor with Wayback Machine, Similarweb and BuiltWith** (product)
1. Wayback Machine: see launch pricing, headline and offer (e.g. Agent CRM $47 -> $97, 14-day trial kept)
2. Similarweb (free account): weekly visits and traffic sources to infer outbound vs ads vs SEO
3. BuiltWith Chrome extension: see payments, pixels, ClickFunnels/HighLevel
4. LinkedIn Sales Navigator 30-day trial (cancel immediately) to see which industries and company sizes they serve
> "what did their website look like when they first launched because that's going to save us x number of years of heartache and trial and error" — Chris Koerner (~7:58)

**Launch your white-label in a 30-day trial window** (operations)
1. Start HighLevel white-label trial (30 days via affiliate link vs 14 standard)
2. AI-generated logo and colours
3. Pick a niche by passion/experience or ask ChatGPT which awards-page industry has least competition
4. Copy proven ad angles, pricing, offer and site structure
5. Copy onboarding: welcome emails, setup calls, training materials
6. Get prospects on the phone before the trial ends
7. Upgrade to $500 unlimited plan only once revenue covers it
> "step three copy their proven ad angles their pricing their offer the structure of their website everything do not try to reinvent the wheel" — Chris Koerner (~14:29)

**Rent someone else's BuiltWith account via Upwork** (acquisition)
1. Post a $20-$50 Upwork job for someone with a BuiltWith subscription
2. Ask them to export a list (e.g. Shopify or HighLevel sites)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Positioning a horizontal tool for one profession beats competing as a generic product.
- *offer_design*: The key to scaling is an offer people feel stupid saying no to; copy it from proven competitors.
- *customer_acquisition*: Long-running ads in the Meta Ads Library reveal what is profitable.
- *validation_testing*: Traffic-source mix (Similarweb) tells you whether a competitor grows by outbound, ads or SEO.
- *customer_acquisition*: Outbound is faster than waiting for SEO and cheaper than heavy paid ads.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Scale platform costs only when revenue covers them.
- *operations*: Onboarding (welcome emails, setup calls, training) is a core part of what to copy.
- *scaling*: Partnering with a franchisor can deliver hundreds of customers at once.

## Warnings and mistakes

- This is active work, not passive income: you must learn sales and marketing and handle difficult clients.
- Most Facebook ads are killed within days; only copy ads that have run for months.
- Avoid analysis paralysis: research one company for a week then talk to customers.
- Revenue estimates come from AI searches and back-of-napkin maths, not verified data.
- UK: the outbound text/ringless voicemail tactics inferred for US resellers breach PECR if aimed at individuals or sole traders without consent; screen calls against TPS/CTPS.
