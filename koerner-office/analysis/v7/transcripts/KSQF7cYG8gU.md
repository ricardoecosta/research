## Tactics and playbook steps

**Use a footbridge chalk-ad routine for student storage** (acquisition)
1. Choose a bridge or path that nearly every target student uses.
2. Write five short chalk advertisements there each morning.
3. Run the ads during finals week and time them before classes.
4. Use a simple URL and state the free boxes, pickup, delivery, and packing supplies.
5. Send people to a signup page that captures their details.
> "I got up at 7:30 and I went to the bridge where North Campus went to campus where every kid walked across and I wrote five chalk advertisements on that bridge" — Nick Huber (~37:43)

**Use bandit signs around local service jobs** (acquisition)
1. Order simple double-sided signs; Koerner says they cost about $2 each.
2. Leave a sign at each tree-trimming job and use signs as a leading channel for lawn care.
3. Check landowner permission and local sign rules before placing them.
> "My brother runs a lawn care company and bandit signs are his number one customer source." — Chris Koerner (~38:30)

**Start with an obvious service and get a paying customer** (validation_testing)
1. Pick a simple service with visible demand, such as cleaning something.
2. Find one person who does not know you and get them to pay.
3. Work out how to fulfil the service after confirming demand.
> "just get someone that doesn't know you to pay you for a service, then figure out how to fulfill it." — Chris Koerner (~31:18)

**Run a practical market study before choosing a business** (validation_testing)
1. Call the market and ask questions before committing to the idea.
2. Use what you learn to judge whether customers have a real need.
3. Huber says his book explains this process but does not give the call script in this conversation.
> "literally how to call and do a market study. It's pretty tactical." — Nick Huber (~39:09)

**Keep frontline process knowledge before delegating** (operations)
1. Learn the work well enough to explain it to the team.
2. Write or review the templates and process used by staff.
3. Coach the team through real calls and tasks.
4. Set direction, then check progress and hold people accountable.
> "People delegate too fast. A lot of CEOs delegate too fast and COOs, meaning a problem comes in, they know the solution. They're just going to send a quick message." — Nick Huber (~19:29)

**Use negative customer feedback as a quality-control alert** (operations)
1. Send client feedback into a shared Slack channel.
2. Notify the owner when a client gives negative feedback.
3. Review the feedback and decide what to change in the process.

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: A simple service with an obvious customer problem can be a more practical starting point than an idea chosen for novelty.
- *customer_acquisition*: A specific, repeated promotion placed where target customers already pass can generate demand without paid marketing.
- *validation_testing*: Get a customer to pay for a service, then work out how to fulfil it.
- *customer_acquisition*: Build a network by becoming useful and skilled at something people value.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Know how frontline work happens before delegating it; teach, follow up, and hold people accountable.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI where it improves a specific workflow, such as surfacing quality problems, and judge it by delivered value rather than demos.
- *operations*: Business owners should examine customer feedback and operating signals instead of disconnecting from them.
- *mindset_execution*: A business can take a long time to show progress and then change quickly; keep evaluating whether work is moving the business forward.
- *mindset_execution*: Business ownership is a skill set involving stress, decisions, and delegation, built through practice rather than reading alone.
- *other*: Let children make mistakes while the stakes are low instead of protecting them from every difficulty.
- *mindset_execution*: Stay open to changing your mind and listening to people who identify risks, even when you are the owner.
