## Tactics and playbook steps

**Choose a niche and check demand** (niche_selection)
1. Start with a service niche that has a clear missed-call problem; Chris considered pool-service and tree-trimming businesses.
2. Use Google Trends to compare search interest for related services over time.
3. Compare related terms, such as gutter cleaning and gutter repair, and use the Google Keyword Tool to check local search demand and keyword bidding competition.
4. Treat higher search interest as a clue about demand, not proof of profit or low competition.
> "No, it's like there could be even more um supply to meet this demand. And this could be the most saturated category." — Chris Koerner (~9:49)

**Scrape and prepare a focused lead list** (acquisition)
1. Search for relevant business categories and locations in Outscraper; Chris selected three pool-related categories for one example, then used already-cleaned tree-trimming leads for the live campaign.
2. Filter for businesses with phone numbers and set a maximum results count based on the number of campaign credits available.
3. Use existing free lead lists when available; Chris says his group lets members request them through Typeform.
4. If scraping directly, compare the estimate with the stated cost and turnaround time; Chris reports paying about USD 4 for the displayed list.
5. Keep only the fields needed for the campaign, then remove blanks and invalid numbers.
6. Randomize lead order in Google Sheets before taking a sample, to reduce location or alphabetical-order bias.
> "And so whatever I'm doing, even if it's probably already randomized, I like taking 10 seconds and randomizing them again just in case." — Chris Koerner (~11:59)

**Keep lead cleaning proportional to the value of the data** (operations)
1. Prioritize the first phone number in the scrape rather than spending extensive time checking every extra phone-number column.
2. Upload lists to Slide Broadcast's list manager to add a cell-versus-landline column, even without running a campaign.
3. Treat the added classification as a second check; Chris says the scrape data and validation are not perfectly accurate.
> "because like if you can scrape unlimited leads for next to nothing, like why spend a bunch of time cleaning and scrubbing and validating a bunch of stuff." — Chris Koerner (~8:24)

**Write a short voicemail around one customer pain** (sales)
1. Keep the message brief because Chris expects many recipients to listen for only a few seconds.
2. Lead with a problem that fits the niche, such as missing calls while working on a tree job.
3. Personalize only with information actually known about the prospect; in this example Chris knew they owned a tree-trimming business in Texas.
4. Give a clear callback instruction and use a callback voicemail greeting that matches the offer.
> "We want to speak to a painoint. We want to solve a problem. We want to personalize as much as we can." — Chris Koerner (~13:27)

**Prepare to answer callbacks** (sales)
1. Set the callback number and voicemail greeting before sending the campaign.
2. Turn off call screening and check that the number will ring as intended.
3. Avoid sending all messages at once if the operator cannot answer the callbacks; Chris says he would normally throttle the campaign.
4. Treat callbacks as an opening for a conversation, not proof that a prospect heard the pitch or wants to buy.
> "Now, if I were being like responsive and dutiful, I would not let these go to voicemail. I would answer all of them and I would throttle them to not send 500 all at once because I want to be able to answer these calls because that's how you're going to close it." — Chris Koerner (~14:50)

**Test a multi-touch sequence** (acquisition)
1. Use a voicemail drop as one touch rather than assuming a single message will produce strong results.
2. Test other appropriate channels, such as email, a call, or LinkedIn, and track which combination produces meaningful conversations.
3. The audience participant reports a sequence of voice note, email, then text; Chris says he would test it, but the transcript does not establish UK legal compliance for those touches.
> "But like like any marketing, you want multiple touches and preferably you want multiple touches in different mediums." — Chris Koerner (~2:13)

**Build an initial voice-agent knowledge base quickly** (product)
1. Create an agent with a clear name, language, voice, and initial greeting.
2. Use ChatGPT to draft a simple knowledge base for common questions and the intended next step, such as arranging an on-site quote.
3. Add the client's website to the HighLevel web crawler to pull in existing FAQs and business information.
4. Review the generated material, connect the knowledge base, choose the information to collect, and test the agent with a realistic call.
5. Use an email notification to pass completed call details to the business team.
> "I'm building a simple AI voice agent to receive inbound calls at a tree trimming business called Chris's tree trimming. It needs to answer typical questions about scheduling an on-site quote for a trimming or removal job." — Chris Koerner (~32:12)

**Use a template or marketplace to reduce setup friction** (product)
1. Start from a tested agent template when one fits the client rather than building every configuration from scratch.
2. For clients already using HighLevel, consider selling or installing the agent through the HighLevel app marketplace.
3. Use a new HighLevel number or port the business's existing number, depending on the client's setup.

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Choose a service with a clear, costly customer problem. Chris frames missed calls as potential lost jobs for tradespeople who are out working.
- *niche_selection*: Use search trends and keyword competition to form a niche hypothesis, then validate it; search interest alone does not prove that a category is profitable or underserved.
- *customer_acquisition*: Keep outreach messages short and focused on a relevant problem, because many recipients will give a voicemail only a few seconds.
- *validation_testing*: A callback is a signal that the number is reachable, not proof that the person heard the message or is interested in the offer.
- *operations*: Plan campaign volume around the ability to handle responses; Chris says he would normally throttle sends so he could answer calls.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use multiple marketing touches where appropriate, and test which mix produces useful responses rather than assuming one channel will work by itself.
- *automation_ai*: Start the AI agent with a simple, narrow job and a practical knowledge base; test it with a realistic caller before relying on it.
- *validation_testing*: Randomize a lead sample before outreach when the original list is ordered by area or alphabetically, so early results are less skewed.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Do not over-invest time in cleaning low-cost leads; focus on the fields and validation that affect the campaign.
- *other*: Show the customer how the automation handles a real task, but maintain trust by being transparent that the caller is speaking with AI.
- *legal_risk*: Check legal requirements for the chosen outreach method in the target country; a US phone-number filter is not a substitute for UK marketing compliance.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Chris says one ringless voicemail drop used as a standalone marketing effort may produce poor results; he recommends testing multiple touches and channels.
- Callbacks can come from people who did not hear the voicemail, so they may have little buying intent.
- Chris reports some recipients were angry, and some callback conversations ended quickly or with a refusal.
- The campaign and its results are not proof of a sale: Chris demonstrates callbacks and conversations but does not report closing a paid AI-agent customer.
- UK legal review is needed before using ringless voicemail for unsolicited marketing. Automated marketing calls are restricted under UK PECR rules; the transcript's US do-not-call screening does not establish UK compliance.
- A prospect who calls back may not know why the number called; Chris notes that this can make the follow-up conversation awkward.
- The live callback number initially had a voicemail greeting for a different business, so verify the greeting and call-routing settings before launch.
