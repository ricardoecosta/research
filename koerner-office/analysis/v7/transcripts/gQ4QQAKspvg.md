## Tactics and playbook steps

**Find an experienced guide and follow their introductions** (customer_acquisition)
1. List people who have spent decades working on the problem you need to solve.
2. Offer to pay for an hour and state exactly what you need to learn.
3. At the end, ask who else you should speak with, then follow each introduction.
> "Then, at the end of that hour, ask them who else you should be talking to and follow every introduction that they give you." — David Heacock (~1:55)

**Learn on site from operators** (validation_testing)
1. Make a list of people with long experience in the field.
2. Arrange to visit facilities or work sites and ask operators why they make specific operational choices.
3. Compare how different operators lay out production, stock parts and ship products.
> "Why is your line laid out like this? Why do you stock that part instead of this one? Why do you ship out of here instead of over there?" — David Heacock (~2:27)

**Work backward from a business destination** (product)
1. Choose a concrete outcome, such as starting a specific business.
2. Use AI or a mentor to map the first steps and the skills needed.
3. Use real-world action to test the plan and decide what to learn next.
> "Once you've got the destination, use AI or a mentor to reverse engineer the curriculum back from it." — David Heacock (~4:38)

**Study how the whole business system connects** (operations)
1. After learning the basics of one task, map how it affects customers, suppliers, the team and cash flow.
2. Trace operational changes such as delivery speed through conversion, repeat purchases, support volume and advertising efficiency.
3. Use those connections to decide which problem to investigate next.
> "How does what you're studying affect the customer, the supplier, the team, the cash flow?" — David Heacock (~6:31)

**Prune distracting opportunities** (mindset)
1. Write down the opportunities currently competing for your attention.
2. Identify which ones pull time away from the business that matters most.
3. Remove most of those distractions and put the time back into customers, products, manufacturing and logistics.
> "Whatever the answer is, kill about 80% of that list today." — David Heacock (~8:31)

**Learn through a study, act and review loop** (validation_testing)
1. Study enough to choose a small, low-cost first action.
2. Take the action before waiting to feel fully ready.
3. Write down the assumptions and unknowns that become visible, then study those specific gaps and act again.
> "The real sequence is study, then act, then discover the whole list of things you had no idea you were missing, and then go study again with that list in hand." — David Heacock (~12:40)

## Lessons

- *hiring_outsourcing*: Paying an experienced operator can buy context, trusted introductions and help identifying the questions worth asking, not just advice.
- *validation_testing*: Start with a concrete destination, then use it to choose what to learn instead of studying a broad topic without a goal.
- *operations*: Understanding how operations affect marketing, delivery, repeat purchases and support can reveal advantages that are invisible when each task is considered alone.
- *mindset_execution*: Focus compounds: repeatedly switching to new opportunities spends time that could build depth in the current business.
- *validation_testing*: Being physically close to the work exposes questions and operating details that are easy to miss through books or online research.
- *finance_unit_economics*: A spreadsheet and research plan cannot reveal every operational issue; acting exposes unknowns that can then guide further learning.
- *mindset_execution*: Treat learning as a repeating cycle of study, action, discovery and more focused study rather than waiting to feel ready.
- *offer_design*: For a physical product business, availability and delivery can matter as much as the product itself and shape the economics of the whole company.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The founder's trucking plan looked sound in spreadsheets but missed the separate culture, economics, operational demands and competitive dynamics of trucking; the operation was shut down at a loss.
- Do not let attractive new opportunities repeatedly pull attention away from the business where you are trying to build expertise.
- A plan generated by AI or research cannot supply the judgment that comes from carrying out the work and seeing the real conditions.
