## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Caleb Panza, co-founder of Post for Me, a $10/month social-media posting API for developers launched after he and his co-founder Matt lost their jobs. With ~500 X followers, Caleb posted Stripe MRR milestones publicly, and built a support-driven content engine (human live chat, Linear, NotebookLM, AI articles) that got them recommended by Google and LLMs. They went from $500 MRR at five months to $5k at eight months and past $10k, but churn rose from 10% to over 17%, so they paused features to fix retention. He shares his tech stack and a playbook: solve your own problem, ship small, iterate daily.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build in public with MRR milestone screenshots** (acquisition)
1. Screenshot Stripe at every $100 (later $500) MRR gained
2. Post on X with the days taken to reach it
3. Reply to every reply for the first week
4. Shift tone from apologetic to confident as numbers grow
> "Like if, if they're journey helped me, why don't I just for fun, grab these screenshots for every $100 that we make in monthly recurring?" — Caleb Panza (~5:07)

**Support-driven AI SEO content engine** (acquisition)
1. Run human-only live chat and respond fast
2. Log any problem raised by 2+ customers in Linear
3. Consolidate notes and support/Discord threads in NotebookLM
4. Generate a resource article with AI, lightly edit
5. Publish 1-3 per week with strong metadata so Google and LLMs pick it up
> "We do things by hand, note how we do them by hand, and then apply the by hand notes to AI in order to automate it in the future." — Caleb Panza (~15:01)

**Pause roadmap to fix churn** (product)
1. When churn rose from 10% to 17%, pause net-new features
2. Invest in telemetry/analytics to see where users get value or drop off
3. Use light Google Ads as an experiment to learn

**SaaS launch playbook** (product)
1. Pick a problem you personally felt
2. Brainstorm, chisel to smallest shippable step
3. Ship fast and iterate one small improvement daily based on usage
4. Use GitHub for code even if non-technical

**Say yes to everything until you can afford not to** (sales)
1. Accept design, marketing, WordPress, Webflow jobs
2. Outsource what you can't do
3. Use agency income to fund product
> "I like to say yes to everything until you can afford not to." — Chris Koerner (~0:38)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Transparency is rare and builds trust without a big audience.
- *validation_testing*: One customer reporting a problem represents many silent ones; treat n=1 as signal.
- *automation_ai*: Do it manually first, document, then automate with AI.
- *customer_acquisition*: Structure content to be cited by LLMs (AI SEO) to earn inbound customers.
- *niche_selection*: Target an underserved segment (developers) of a crowded category (social posting).
- *pricing*: Low entry pricing with unlimited usage-based upgrade paths grows revenue from scaling customers.
- *mindset_execution*: Rock bottom moments often push people to start.
- *operations*: Fast human support early builds trust and surfaces content ideas.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Agency work can bridge years until a product launches.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Churn can quietly rise even while MRR grows; MRR posts often ignore it.
- Big spikes lead to big dips; prefer consistent growth.
- Churn equilibrium: recurring revenue flattens when churn offsets new revenue; usually users fail to onboard in the first 10 minutes.
- Don't keep code only locally or in Google Drive; use GitHub.
- Employment can be fragile: others' poor financial decisions can expose your family.
- Transcript is Whisper tiny; product and tool names (Post for Me, Unkey, Claude) are reconstructed.
