## Tactics and playbook steps

**Find a less crowded local-business segment** (customer_acquisition)
1. Use Outscraper to inspect Google Business Profile categories, including less common categories that contractors may have selected by mistake.
2. Target multiple nearby cities and zip codes so the outreach list is geographically relevant.
3. Check how much marketing competition exists in the chosen niche before committing to it.
> "These are categories that other dirty marketers like myself are not targeting." — Chris Koerner (~1:24)

**Prepare and clean a local prospect list** (operations)
1. Require a phone number when scraping the listings.
2. Remove duplicate phone numbers, then filter to mobile numbers.
3. Remove business names that look unprofessional for the planned personalization.
4. Fill missing city fields with a local area label.
5. Randomize the list before choosing a sample so the contacts do not all come from one city.
> "I want to perfectly randomize it by doing the formula equals rand open and then close parentheses" — Chris Koerner (~13:57)

**Build a reusable voice-agent template, then personalize it after interest** (product)
1. Create a sample agent with a temporary business name and a concise roofing FAQ knowledge base.
2. Use ChatGPT to draft the FAQ structure, then add it as rich text in GoHighLevel.
3. Keep business name and location as easy-to-replace variables.
4. Set call limits and response behavior to manage usage and reduce the risk of unusually long calls.
5. After a roofer agrees to test it, add that firm's actual details and a local phone number.
6. Test the call, inspect the transcript, and rate the result.
> "I'm not going to send the roofer the link or the phone number to this AI voice agent until I have a roofer say yes." — Chris Koerner (~4:32)

**Use a low-friction demonstration to start the sales conversation** (sales)
1. Tell prospects the agent is custom-built for their business.
2. Offer a free demonstration or limited number of calls to get a prospect to try it.
3. Use the demo as a path to discuss more customized paid work.
4. Respond to requests for company information with the business name and website.
> "I just want to get them to respond and to try it." — Chris Koerner (~19:51)

**Track the whole outreach funnel and compare niches** (validation_testing)
1. Record the number of contacts, delivered texts, responses, and warm leads.
2. Track whether prospects are receptive, angry, or unable to pay.
3. Test the same offer in three to five industries, then focus on the one showing the most promise.
4. Treat a small sample's response rate as an early signal, not proof of revenue.
> "Keep track of every single response, what your response rates are, what the sentiment is like." — Chris Koerner (~18:05)

**Adjust the first message to sound like a customer inquiry** (sales)
1. Review whether a highly personalized message makes the sender sound like a marketer.
2. Test a shorter question about whether the roofer inspects roofs.
3. Where appropriate, use the recipient's actual first name rather than only the business name.
> "I should have said something like, "Hey, do you inspect roofs? - Chris."" — Chris Koerner (~18:37)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Choose a niche using observable signals such as willingness to pay for marketing, then test whether local competition makes it worthwhile.
- *offer_design*: Make a demo feel specific to the prospect by using the business name, location, and relevant questions, while checking that the generated information is accurate.
- *operations*: A reusable template can speed up delivery, but customer-specific details and knowledge still need to be verified.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Keep the knowledge base sufficient for the job without adding unnecessary material that increases model usage costs.
- *validation_testing*: Test an offer across several industries and track response counts and prospect sentiment before choosing where to focus.
- *validation_testing*: Use early outreach to learn about the market, but do not confuse replies or warm leads with paying customers.
- *offer_design*: Reduce friction for a prospect to experience the product, then offer more customized paid work if the demonstration is useful.
- *customer_acquisition*: Personalization can improve relevance, but wording that appears overly tailored may reveal a marketing pitch and reduce replies.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The demonstrated unsolicited text campaign is not suitable for UK outreach to individuals and sole traders without consent under PECR. The prospect list may include business types for which the approach is unlawful.
- Koerner considers having the AI call prospects proactively, whether or not they replied. That tactic can create consent and privacy issues and should not be copied into a UK campaign without a lawful basis and appropriate checks.
- The sample agent makes claims that were not grounded in verified business information, such as saying the contractor has served the area for years.
- The first contact message may sound like marketing rather than a customer inquiry; Koerner says he would change the wording and notes that one recipient was angry.
- The campaign's small early sample does not prove that projected response, lead, or revenue rates will hold at larger volumes.
- The transcript reports inconsistent scraped-list counts: 947 at one checkpoint, then 978 after duplicate removal. Treat the figures as the speaker's on-screen claims rather than reconciled totals.
- Koerner notes that competition among marketers targeting roofers may be high and says results depend on the local market.
