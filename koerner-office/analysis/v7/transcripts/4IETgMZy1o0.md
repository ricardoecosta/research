## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Rob Walling (founder of Drip, MicroConf, TinySeed) about his 'stair-step' method: start with simple step-one businesses such as freelancing, productized services or single-feature add-ons sold in app marketplaces (Shopify, Zoom, Chrome, WordPress, HubSpot; a list of ~75), get to about $10k/month to buy out your day job, then build bigger SaaS. Examples include Castos's founder building a $30k/month podcast-editing productized service, then a seven-figure SaaS, and a developer who bought and revamped three WordPress plugins to $20k/month. Rob explains what is vibe-codable, his 2/20/200-hour validation framework, why pricing at $750-1,000+/month unlocks more marketing channels, vertical vs horizontal vs 'orthogonal' (role-based) SaaS, zoom-in pivots, and choosing customer pain or competitor pain. Chris recounts scraping every US iPhone repair store to build a wholesale parts business doing $150k by month three.

### I1. Single-feature add-on apps in app marketplaces

- **Customer:** Users of the host platform (merchants, Zoom users, etc.)
- **Problem:** Platform users need specific features the platform lacks
- **Offer:** One- or two-feature add-on with hundreds to a few thousand lines of code
- **Business model:** Subscription or one-time app fees
- **Pricing:** Subscription. lifestyle SaaS price lane USD 20-100 per month Step-one businesses may never exceed $5-10k/month.
- **Acquisition:** app_store: Almost all customers come from the marketplace; marketing is app store listing optimisation.
- **Delivery:** Vibe-coded or developer-built software; small scale so maintainability and throughput matter less.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Target ~$10k/month to quit a day job
- **Tools:** Shopify App Store, Zoom App Marketplace, HubSpot marketplace, Heroku add-ons, Chrome Web Store, WordPress plugin directory, Replit
- **Startup cost:** Low
- **Time to first revenue:** Could be live in about 3 days with AI (claimed possible)
- **UK applicability [E]:** 5/5 — Global marketplaces; works the same from the UK.
- **Risks:** Marketplaces get crowded (Shopify did); Platform policy/fee changes; Low ceiling
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 5/5 (Inbound from marketplace search; ASO is the main lever.); onboarding 5/5 (One-click install.); delivery 5/5 (Software.); billing 5/5 (Marketplace billing.); support 3/5 (AI support with occasional developer fixes.)

> "all you're trying to do is get to I usually say 10k a month because that's when most people can quit their job" — Rob Walling (~6:10)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Stair-step method** (mindset)
1. Step 1: simple business (freelancing, ebook, productized service, single-feature marketplace add-on)
2. Aim for ~$10k/month to buy out your day job
3. Use freed time and cash to build or buy a bigger SaaS (step 3)
4. Don't model yourself on famous founders' current state
> "don't go build a SAS. That's like step three. That is really hard." — Rob Walling (~4:59)

**2/20/200 validation framework** (product)
1. 2 hours per idea: keyword tools, Reddit, deep research to check demand (5-10 ideas)
2. 20 hours on the top 1-3: landing page (e.g. Carrd) with email capture, drive traffic; then 1-hour conversations (offer to pay consulting rate, or email sign-ups)
3. 200 hours: build the MVP (now far less with AI; consider skipping straight to a 3-day MVP for marketplace apps)
> "these are these are hours 2 hours 20 hours 200 hours for three stages validation" — Rob Walling (~28:20)

**Price high enough to afford marketing** (pricing)
1. Either accept a $20-100/month lifestyle lane with higher churn
2. Or build something worth $750-1,000+/month
3. At $20 ARPA only ~4 of 20 B2B SaaS channels are affordable; at $1,000 ~14; at $2,500 all 20 (including events, ads)
> "The magic part of charging that much is it allows you to spend money to acquire customers." — Rob Walling (~34:45)

**Turn custom work into an assembly line** (product)
1. List what people hire freelancers/agencies for today
2. Strip it to fixed tiers with defined deliverables
3. Staff with contractors and project managers; founder sells
> "what what are people hiring freelancers and agencies to do today and how can I possibly turn that into an assembly line?" — Rob Walling (~12:29)

**Build a proprietary market map by scraping** (acquisition)
1. Scrape every potential customer from Google Maps
2. De-duplicate manually if needed
3. Estimate spend per customer and total market
4. Back into required pickup and close rates
5. Cold call through the list
> "This database only exists on my computer. That is every potential customer." — Chris Koerner (~19:14)

**Zoom-in / zoom-out pivots** (product)
1. Launch horizontal, watch which vertical churns least or buys most
2. Rebrand website messaging for that vertical (zoom in)
3. Or expand from one vertical to adjacent ones (realtors to mortgage brokers)
4. Drop verticals that are poor fits

**Use AI to test more concepts, not to sleep** (product)
1. Use vibe coding to cut MVP time from ~200 to ~5 hours
2. Test 20 concepts instead of 1
3. Hire fewer, AI-enabled people
> "that 200 hours of like building a minimum viable product just went to five hours. So why don't I test 20 different concepts instead of one" — Chris Koerner (~32:01)

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: Start with a small, simple business to buy back time before attempting SaaS.
- *offer_design*: Productized services grow faster than SaaS and can fund the next step.
- *customer_acquisition*: App marketplaces handle distribution; your job is listing optimisation.
- *niche_selection*: Competition is a signal of demand; execution and skill win in crowded markets.
- *mindset_execution*: Edges come from doing tedious work others won't (40 hours of trial and error, 3 days of data aggregation).
- *niche_selection*: Vertical and role-based ('orthogonal') SaaS tend to have lower churn and clearer buyers than horizontal tools.
- *mindset_execution*: Entrepreneurship means making hard decisions with incomplete information; perfectionism is anti-entrepreneurial.
- *automation_ai*: AI lets small teams hire slower and do more; use it to multiply experiments.
- *validation_testing*: Validate with a landing page and real conversations before building.
- *mindset_execution*: Expect it to take longer and be harder than you think, and get paid to learn skills in step one.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Vibe coding works for small, simple apps but not for complex, high-throughput or highly maintained software.
- No-code internal apps are too brittle and poorly scaling for real customers.
- Pricing too low (e.g. $79/month) prevents spending on acquisition.
- Avoid businesses with both customer pain (non-technical, high-support buyers) and competitor pain (crowded market).
- Playing business (tweeting, coding in the basement for months) isn't business until someone pays.
- Survivorship bias: Cinderella founder stories aren't repeatable; luck is not controllable.
- Horizontal SaaS is hard to bootstrap; it plateaus once big players enter.
