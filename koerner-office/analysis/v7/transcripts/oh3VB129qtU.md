## Tactics and playbook steps

**Estimate service-business earnings per worker-hour** (validation_testing)
1. Call the top five businesses in a target service category and request a quote.
2. Write down the quoted price and how long the job will take.
3. Ask how many people will be on the crew.
4. Estimate labour cost using the full loaded cost, not just the worker's wage.
5. Subtract materials, equipment and other hard costs before judging the opportunity.
> "Those two questions allow you to do back of the map, back of the napkin math on what the hourly, you know, earnings for that company is" — Nick Huber (~1:02)

**Account for fence materials before comparing a project quote** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Estimate lumber costs.
2. Work out the fence square footage.
3. Ask how many days the job takes and how many people are on the crew.
4. Use those inputs to estimate job earnings per worker-hour.
> "Like a fence building company, you have to price out lumber and do the square footage and like figure out what their hard costs are" — Nick Huber (~0:47)

**Observe businesses during everyday errands** (validation_testing)
1. Notice how busy a business is and how much capacity it has.
2. For a restaurant, estimate occupied tables, customers per table and table turns.
3. Treat the result as a rough opportunity estimate, not as verified profit.
> "when you start to think about business that way, everywhere you go in life, you're just you're prowling for opportunities, you're prowling for opportunities." — Nick Huber (~2:16)

**Use door-to-door sales as a pest-control acquisition channel** (customer_acquisition)
1. Recruit or contract door-to-door sales crews.
2. Pay for contracts that remain active for at least six months.
3. Track which contracts meet the retention threshold before paying the full commission.
4. Compare sales-team cost with the long-term value of the customer.
> "We pay it because the long-term value of our business is there. And it's worth it." — Nick Huber (~4:30)

**Keep the founder involved in important sales** (sales)
1. Do not make sales the first function you outsource.
2. Stay involved in high-value sales calls even when you are not the CEO or owner handling daily operations.
3. Build sales skill through repeated practice.
> "I am not even the CEO on the owner of several companies and I still get involved in really high value sales calls" — Nick Huber (~5:57)

## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: Compare local service opportunities by estimating revenue per worker-hour, then account for loaded labour and hard costs.
- *niche_selection*: Choose an opportunity by how directly it can get you to your goal, rather than by novelty alone.
- *mindset_execution*: Develop business curiosity by observing customer volume, capacity and likely spending in everyday settings.
- *customer_acquisition*: Sales is a core part of entrepreneurship: founders sell customers, employees, partners and vendors on working with them.
- *mindset_execution*: Sales skill can be learned; discomfort with sales is not a fixed limit.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Keep the founder involved in high-value sales rather than outsourcing sales as the first delegation.
- *customer_acquisition*: A business can stand out through a specific acquisition advantage, such as a sales channel competitors do not use as effectively.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Do not judge a service business from its headline job price; estimate labour, equipment and other costs.
- *mindset_execution*: Business-building experience can help a founder notice opportunities in ordinary surroundings.
