### I1. Local city newsletter (Roswell Dispatch)

- **Customer:** Readers: local residents (e.g. ages 25-45) of a town of about 50k+ people. Paying customers: local businesses that sponsor the newsletter.
- **Problem:** Local residents don't know what is happening in their town. Local businesses lack a cheap, targeted way to reach residents.
- **Offer:** A free weekly (or more frequent) email with local news, events, deals and places to visit, with sponsor ads included.
- **Business model:** Sponsorship ads sold on a CPM basis, plus Beehive ad network and boosts (pay per new subscriber), affiliate links, paid subscriptions, print-on-demand merch and events.
- **Pricing:** Sponsor ads priced per CPM (per thousand opens); the subscriber side is free, with optional paid tiers.. ## Tactics and playbook steps

**Sell-before-build subscriber acquisition with Meta lead ads** (acquisition)
1. Create a Meta campaign with the Leads objective
2. Spend $100 on day one so the algorithm learns, then drop to about $20/day
3. Target the specific town and tick 'reach more people likely to respond'
4. Use an instant form asking for email only
5. Make a short 16-second talking-head video with captions and a hook text, and keep the call to action 'find the link below'
6. Use ChatGPT to write three headlines, texts and descriptions
7. Create a Facebook Page for the newsletter so the ad doesn't come from a personal brand
> "So, I'm going to put 100, but this is only going to be for a day. Then, I'm going to drop it down to about 20 bucks." — Chris Koerner (~14:25)

**Mine the Meta Ads Library for proven ad formats** (acquisition)
1. Search the Meta Ads Library for 'newsletter'
2. Filter to active ads in the target country that started before a date a few months back
3. Filter to the video media type
4. Treat long-running ads as likely winners and copy their format

**Connect Facebook lead ads to Beehive with Zapier** (operations)
1. Create a Zap with the Facebook Lead Ads 'New Lead' trigger
2. Add the Beehive 'Add a subscriber' action using an API key
3. Set UTM source to 'Facebook ad' and turn on the welcome email
4. Backfill existing leads
5. Create a dynamic segment on UTM source to track ad subscribers

**Cheap subscriber growth inside Beehive** (acquisition)
1. Use recommendations to swap promotion with other newsletters
2. Use boosts to pay other newsletters per new subscriber
3. Add a referral widget with the lowest threshold at one referral
4. Show a survey right after sign-up to capture data while interest is high

**Find sponsors among your own subscribers** (sales)
1. Email subscribers asking who wants to advertise
2. Get responders on the phone
3. Sell the first ads at $50, $100, $200 and raise the price until people start saying no
> "You get them on the phone and you sell the first out of 50 bucks, 100 bucks, 200 bucks until people start saying no." — Chris Koerner (~43:44)

**Choose a frequency you can sustain** (operations)
1. Start with a weekly send
2. Add more sends only once the routine is stable
3. Avoid dropping frequency later, as it is demoralising for the publisher

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Validate demand by getting subscribers or leads before polishing the product; pre-selling reduces fear and wasted effort.
- *mindset_execution*: Prioritise momentum over perfect setup: skip logos, design and settings until traffic exists.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Work out per-subscriber value and lifetime to set a maximum allowable acquisition cost, then compare ad cost per lead against it.
- *niche_selection*: Hyperlocal niches have tighter targeting, so ad costs per subscriber can be low.
- *customer_acquisition*: Ask for feedback early and often through polls and surveys at sign-up, when engagement is highest.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI for copy and research, but treat generated output (cities, logos) as drafts that need checking.
- *customer_acquisition*: Your audience contains your buyers: sponsors can come from your own subscriber list.
- *operations*: Start with a sustainable cadence and add volume later; consistency matters more than ambition.
- *scaling*: Compounding growth channels (referrals, recommendations, boosts) reduce dependence on paid ads over time.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Early subscribers may cost $5-$15 each before the algorithm learns, which can be demoralising. Be patient.
- Don't launch at a high publishing frequency you can't maintain; dropping frequency later feels demoralising.
- Lowering form friction raises lead volume but lowers lead quality; test the trade-off.
- Spending effort on logos and settings before having subscribers is 'advanced procrastination'.
- Revenue is not shown; sponsor selling, closing and collecting remain real work.
