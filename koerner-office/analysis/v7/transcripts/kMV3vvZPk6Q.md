## Tactics and playbook steps

**Borrow authority from a credible third party** (sales)
1. Have someone other than the seller introduce relevant expertise.
2. State the person's role and specific experience before transferring or introducing them.
3. Use authority where the buyer is alone or unfamiliar with the seller; do not simply claim expertise about yourself.
> "Let me put you through to Peter, our head of sales. He was over 20 years selling properties in this area." — Codie Sanchez (~2:01)

**Use specific, truthful social proof** (sales)
1. Explain what most customers choose or do.
2. Use a number when you have evidence for it.
3. Use the information to help customers compare options, rather than presenting an unsupported figure as fact.
> "Not just most people, but 75% of people need this offer." — Codie Sanchez (~3:12)

**Make scarcity concrete** (sales)
1. If capacity is genuinely limited, state the actual number of places and how many remain.
2. Avoid the generic phrase “Limited spots available.”
3. Do not imply a capacity limit that is not real.
> "Remember, we want to use a number, so I have four slots in this cohort. Two are gone." — Codie Sanchez (~3:37)

**Frame price against a larger comparison** (pricing)
1. Present a relevant comparison before stating the offer price.
2. Quantify what the customer may gain, lose, or save if the transcript's example can be supported.
3. State the price and then pause instead of adding more arguments.
> "Your offer should sound like, and the price is $20K. Are you in?" — Codie Sanchez (~11:37)

**Make sales language concrete and listen closely** (sales)
1. Replace vague promises such as fast delivery with a specific date or measurable outcome.
2. Remove softeners, filler words, and unnecessary apologies; pause when you need time to think.
3. Reflect the customer's own words accurately before explaining why the offer fits.
> "Make everything you say concrete. This is a good rule, because if you can't visualize it, they won't value it." — Codie Sanchez (~14:42)

**Build trust before asking for a sale** (sales)
1. Tell a prospect when the offer is not a fit and do not force it.
2. Get to the stated purpose of the call without wasting time on small talk.
3. Look for genuine shared interests before a meeting; do not manufacture similarities.
4. Give something useful and tailored before asking for a commitment.
> "The more you can give, the more trust you build and the higher the chance the person will buy from you." — Codie Sanchez (~22:53)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Sell the customer's outcome rather than listing product features; explain what good means for that customer.
- *customer_acquisition*: People use authority and others' behavior as shortcuts when judging whether an offer is good.
- *offer_design*: The visual setting and presentation can prime what a customer notices, including comfort or price.
- *pricing*: A price is judged in relation to an anchor; make comparisons relevant to the customer's likely gain, loss, or savings.
- *other*: After stating a price, silence can prevent the seller from piling on arguments that invite counterarguments.
- *offer_design*: Specific claims are easier to picture and assess than broad promises.
- *other*: Reflect a customer's actual words accurately to show understanding and connect the offer to the problem they described.
- *other*: Trust grows when sellers are willing to say an offer is not suitable and do not force a close.
- *mindset_execution*: Practice delivery, including pauses and intonation, because the same words can carry different meanings when spoken differently.
- *customer_acquisition*: Give something useful and tailored before asking for a commitment.
