## Tactics and playbook steps

**Use marketplace research before choosing a book topic** (validation_testing)
1. Start with a topic you know well enough to edit and judge.
2. Use BookBolt keyword and product research to compare search interest, competition and estimated sales.
3. Try specific long-tail phrases rather than broad topics alone.
4. Check whether searchers appear to want books; the host notes that Texas barbecue searches may be restaurant searches.
5. Look at movers-and-shakers and evergreen product lists for ideas, then validate the niche further.

**Choose a topic the publisher can review** (niche_selection)
1. List topics the publisher knows and cares about.
2. Use that familiarity to review AI-written material and spot content that does not make sense.
3. Do not assume AI removes the need for subject knowledge or editing.

**Differentiate and test journal titles** (product)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Choose a niche where the publisher has enough familiarity to edit AI output and notice when the book does not make sense.
- *validation_testing*: Research demand and competition before spending substantial time making a product.
- *customer_acquisition*: Specific long-tail searches can reveal opportunities that broad keywords hide, but search intent still needs checking.
- *offer_design*: Use templates to get started, then customize the product so it is distinct and useful.
- *mindset_execution*: Treat the first product as learning and test more than one concept before deciding what works.
- *operations*: Publishing through a marketplace can make fulfilment simpler because the platform handles printing and shipping.
- *validation_testing*: An idea is not validated just because its creator likes it; use buyer and sales evidence.
- *finance_unit_economics*: The host presents book publishing as an asymmetric experiment: limited software costs can lead either to a win or to reusable learning.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The host says many people using Amazon KDP do not find the success they want; having tools does not guarantee results.
- Avoid oversaturated topics, skipping keyword research and using generic designs; the host says these are reasons people fail.
- Do not publish default templates without customizing them.
- This is not a get-rich-quick scheme, and results are not guaranteed.
- The host corrects his interpretation of product data, explaining that the displayed sales figure is monthly rather than all-time.
- Search volume does not necessarily mean book-buying intent; the Texas barbecue search may be for restaurants.
- A first book is unlikely to be polished, and a publisher who dislikes research may not persist.
