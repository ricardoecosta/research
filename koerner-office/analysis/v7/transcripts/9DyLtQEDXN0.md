## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Phil, a semi-retiree with no coding background who started an AI agency after listening to the podcast. Phil landed a $50,000 six-month retainer with a $1.4B investment firm, a success-based grant-writing app (Grant Craft) for a nonprofit, and a $3,500 + $1,500/month product-launch automation deal for a chiropractor, all from friends and friends of friends. Key takeaways: sell to your warm network, listen more than talk, ask for referrals, and productise custom builds.

### I1. Part-time AI automation consultant selling to warm network

- **Customer:** Small and mid-sized business owners in his personal network (investment firm managing $1.4B, chiropractor, nonprofit)
- **Problem:** Owners know they need AI but don't know where to start or how
- **Offer:** Custom AI automations, voice agents, and knowledge-base content creation built with Claude/Claude Code and Go High Level
- **Business model:** Retainers and setup fees
- **Pricing:** retainer. investment firm retainer (~$8,333/month), 20-25 hrs/week USD 50000 per 6 months Client proposed the $50k figure after Phil asked how many hours a week he wanted.
- **Acquisition:** referrals_word_of_mouth: Texted 1,000+ phone contacts asking to sit down; 7-10 meetings with business owners. In the UK, texting personal contacts you know is lower risk but unsolicited marketing SMS to individuals needs consent under PECR.; cold_call: Cold-called roofing companies early with voice agents; little success.
- **Delivery:** Phil records discovery conversations, feeds transcripts to Claude to design solutions, price and draft contracts, then builds in Go High Level/Claude Code.
- **Unit economics (claims):** $50k/6 months retainer; ~$833/month per check (as stated, likely ~$8,333)
- **Tools:** Go High Level, Claude, Claude Code, Limitless pendant, Plaud
- **Startup cost:** not stated
- **Time to first revenue:** About 5 months from starting (Oct) to first $50k contract (March)
- **UK applicability [E]:** 5/5 — AI consulting works the same in the UK; warm outreach fine, cold SMS to individuals restricted by PECR.
- **Risks:** Hard to scale beyond friends and family; No niche/productised offer yet; Dependent on few large clients
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 1/5 (Relationship-based warm sales and meetings.); onboarding 2/5 (Discovery meetings are human; AI helps prepare.); delivery 3/5 (AI builds much of it but custom work needs a human.); billing 5/5 (Invoicing.); support 2/5 (Client relationships are human.)

### I2. Grant Craft: AI grant finder and drafter for nonprofits

- **Customer:** Nonprofits/ministries
- **Problem:** Finding and writing grant applications is time-consuming
- **Offer:** Automated grant discovery and drafting, human review before submission
- **Business model:** Per-grant fee plus retainer, billed only when a grant is won, capped at 8% of the grant
- **Pricing:** success-based. per grant filled out USD 1500 per grant; retainer USD 2000 per month No billing until a grant comes through; total billing capped at 8% of the grant.
- **Acquisition:** referrals_word_of_mouth: Built free for a friend's nonprofit as proof of concept.
- **Delivery:** Claude Code app plus an OpenClaw agent fills out grant applications; human reads before sending.
- **Unit economics (claims):** $1,500 per grant + $2,000/month, capped at 8% of grant
- **Tools:** Claude Code, OpenClaw
- **Startup cost:** Built for free (time only)
- **Time to first revenue:** Not until a grant is awarded
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — UK has many grant funders and charities; success fees for bid writing are debated ethically by some funders.
- **Risks:** Some funders disallow commission-based grant writing; Quality of AI drafts; Long payment lag
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Grant-seeking nonprofits can be reached by content/SEO; UK charities are often companies (CIOs/charitable companies) so B2B email may be possible.); onboarding 4/5 (Feed knowledge base about the organisation.); delivery 4/5 (Agent finds and drafts grants; human review before submission.); billing 4/5 (Success-based invoicing.); support 3/5 (Mostly automated status updates.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Warm-network AI agency launch** (acquisition)
1. Go through every phone contact and list business owners
2. Text: I've started an AI automation business, I'd love to sit down with you
3. Run 7-10 sit-downs
4. Let them talk about pain points; don't pitch features
5. Record the conversation
6. Feed transcript into Claude for solutions, pricing, contract and objection prep
7. Ask for referrals after delivering
> "Hey, I've started this AI automation business, You know, I'd love to be able to sit down with you" — Phil (~4:17)

**Record discovery calls and analyse with AI** (sales)
1. Use a wearable (Limitless/Plaud) or phone voice notes
2. Upload transcript to Claude
3. Generate solutions, pricing and contract drafts

**Success-based pricing for proof of concept** (pricing)
1. Build free for a friend
2. Set price: per-unit fee + retainer
3. Bill only when outcome (grant) lands
4. Cap total at a % of outcome

**Setup + retainer + performance bonus structure** (pricing)
1. $3,500 setup
2. $1,500/month for 6 months then re-evaluate
3. $75 per client above 10/month
4. Client pays CRM and $500/month ad budget

**Let the client name the retainer** (pricing)
1. Ask how many hours a week they want
2. Let them propose a number
3. Throw in automations free inside the retainer

**Floodgate principle** (acquisition)
1. Solve one problem for a friend
2. They bring more businesses/projects
3. Always ask for referrals

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Start with people you know; warm leads close far better than cold calls.
- *customer_acquisition*: The best selling is listening; let owners describe pain points.
- *mindset_execution*: You only need to know more than 99% of the owners you sit with.
- *niche_selection*: Revenue matters more than having a niche early.
- *offer_design*: Content for local businesses is now like a sign: a basic presence, not virality.
- *scaling*: Custom builds for one business can become vertical products for the whole industry.
- *customer_acquisition*: Always ask for referrals after delivering.
- *mindset_execution*: Close the big deal then figure out delivery: get a bigger frying pan.
- *mindset_execution*: Talk to yourself as you would to your kids; don't voice negative thoughts.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Talking too much and listing AI features lost most early prospects.
- Can't scale only on friends and family; need to branch out.
- MLM-style list-everyone-you-know selling burns relationships if the product is bad.
- Being busy is not progress.
- Hiding what you're working on from friends creates an artificial sales barrier.
- Texting your whole contact list is marketing: in the UK PECR requires consent for marketing SMS to individuals.
