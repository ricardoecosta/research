## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Spencer (Lonear Trash, Texas), a software founder who started a residential trash subscription business by pre-selling 3-month service to ~150-200 neighbours on Facebook after the incumbent hauler degraded. He bought a used under-CDL side-loader truck 100% financed, sourced used bins, and grew via referrals (free month per referral), Facebook ads (CAC $35-100), and branded bins. Claimed ~$50-60k/month revenue, ~$300k first-year revenue and ~40% margin on rough maths, with very low churn. They also discuss municipal truck auctions as a flip or list-selling side hustle, city contracts, and starting cheaper with bulk junk pickup.

### I1. Neighbourhood trash subscription (Lonear Trash)

- **Customer:** Households in neighbourhoods (HOA/utility districts) in Texas unhappy with their existing trash hauler
- **Problem:** Incumbent hauler sold to private equity, service and billing got worse, and missed pickups
- **Offer:** Weekly trash pickup with provided branded bins; pre-sale with full refund if the 200-customer target is not hit
- **Business model:** Subscription billed in 3-month increments per household
- **Pricing:** Recurring subscription, billed quarterly. Initial pre-sale payment USD 105 per 3 months (pre-sale); Typical monthly range per house USD 35-60 per month; Used in revenue-per-pickup maths USD 9 per house per week Referrers get a free month per referred neighbour; refund guarantee on pre-sale.
- **Acquisition:** facebook_groups_communities: Posted a no-risk pre-sale offer to the neighbourhood Facebook group; got ~150 sign-ups in 10-15 days and ~200 over about 45 days.; referrals_word_of_mouth: Free month of trash for each referral; one customer referred 16 houses and got a year free. Branded bins on the street act as billboards.; paid_social: Facebook ads sending to Stripe checkout with a success page so the pixel tracks conversions; needed ~$300/day for about a week to train the algorithm; CAC ~$100 early, down to ~$35 some months.; paid_search: Google pay-per-click; Spencer says he should have used Google Local Services Ads at ~$50/day (pay per lead) and collected Google reviews earlier.; partnerships: Presence in HOA and utility district recommendations as new residents ask neighbours who to use.
- **Delivery:** Driver(s) collect bins weekly with an automated side-loader arm; ~400 houses in ~4 hours per truck with one dump trip. Two employees (a salaried main driver and a part-time driver). Trucks stored at an RV storage facility; bins in a storage unit.
- **Unit economics (claims):** ~$50-60k/month revenue vs ~$30k costs, ~40% margin (Koerner's rough calc); CAC $35-100 vs customer lifetime value ~$3,500 (Koerner estimate); ~1% monthly churn estimated, no cancellations due to service; First-year revenue ~$300k, little profit due to capex
- **Tools:** Stripe, Facebook Ads, Facebook Marketplace, Facebook groups, Google Ads, Google Local Services Ads, Samsara GPS/OBD tracker, Dash cams, CRM
- **Startup cost:** Effectively no cash out of pocket: $15k pre-sales covered ~$10k used bins plus dump fees and fuel for 3 months; truck $35k + $5k shipping financed via a $40k Amex personal loan at ~7% over 10 years ($700/month).
- **Time to first revenue:** Pre-sale cash within days of posting (about 10-15 days for 150 sign-ups)
- **UK applicability [E]:** 2/5 — UK household waste is collected by councils or licensed contractors under council contracts, so private residential subscription collection is not a standard market. Commercial waste needs a waste carrier licence and waste transfer notes. The pre-sale and referral mechanics transfer, but the specific business largely does not.
- **Risks:** Incumbent is not always bad; customers rarely switch without a trigger; Truck breakdowns, tyre wear, insurance cost; HOA complaints about parking trucks; Sole reliance on drivers showing up; Personal-guarantee loan tied to founder; Requires an opportunistic trigger (incumbent failure)
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Facebook/Google ads and referral credits can be run by agents; community posting needs light human presence and Facebook group rules.); onboarding 4/5 (Stripe checkout and CRM automation; address-based setup and bin delivery needs a human.); delivery 0/5 (Physical trash collection with trucks and drivers; a human must do it each time.); billing 5/5 (Stripe recurring quarterly billing is fully automatic.); support 3/5 (Agents can handle missed-pickup and billing queries; physical fixes need humans.)
> "if I can get 200 customers to sign up, there's 400 people who live in here... And if you don't if we don't hit that number, 100% refund. There's no risk." — Spencer (~3:18)
> "I bought the truck, started making payments on it, never took a dollar out of my pocket." — Spencer (~0:09)
> "I can't win business just to win business... You have to mess up for me to win the business." — Spencer (~16:06)

### I2. Municipal garbage truck auction sourcing and flipping

- **Customer:** Trash companies and operators wanting used trucks
- **Problem:** Used trucks are expensive; municipalities replace fleets on a schedule and sell them at auction
- **Offer:** Trucks purchased far below market and resold or refurbished
- **Business model:** Buy low at auction, keep or resell for profit
- **Pricing:** One-off resale. Spencer's winning bid for a 2020 truck USD 60000 one-off; Comparable Facebook Marketplace price USD 190000 one-off Auction pay-in-cash requirement caught Spencer out; he used an equipment financing preapproval.
- **Acquisition:** facebook_groups_communities: Municipal truck auctions found in trash truck Facebook groups and Marketplace.
- **Delivery:** Attend in-person auctions, transport, optionally refurbish via dealer, and list for sale.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Bought a ~$190k truck for $60k at auction; Financed at ~$1,700/month, ~8%
- **Tools:** Facebook Marketplace, Facebook groups, Equipment financing
- **Startup cost:** About $10k cash suggested to attend an auction
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — UK councils and fleet operators sell vehicles via auction and online auction houses; discounts are likely smaller and the market is thinner.
- **Risks:** Need cash on the day; Predatory equipment financiers; Truck condition and repair costs; Competition from pro buyers
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 2/5 (Auction discovery could be scraped by agents but buyers must attend in person.); onboarding 2/5 (Buyer negotiation is mostly manual.); delivery 0/5 (Physical attendance, transport and refurb.); billing 2/5 (One-off payments handled manually.); support 2/5 (Occasional buyer queries.)
> "We got it for $60,000." — Spencer (~34:55)
> "if I would have just taken it right there and put it on Facebook Marketplace, it would have been worth 190." — Spencer (~34:59)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Risk-free neighbourhood pre-sale** (acquisition)
1. Identify a neighbourhood with a failing incumbent
2. Post a transparent offer on the local Facebook group with a target (e.g. 200 customers)
3. Offer 100% refund if the target is missed
4. Charge 3 months upfront ($105)
5. Extend Stripe payout hold to cover refunds
6. Use the cash to buy equipment
> "We put in Stripe, you can extend the escrow before the money gets transferred to you." — Spencer (~3:50)

**Facebook ads conversion tracking setup** (acquisition)
1. Hire a Fiverr specialist to install the pixel
2. Send buyers to Stripe checkout, then to a success page
3. Optimise for conversions not clicks
4. Budget about $300/day for a week to train the algorithm
5. Scale spend as CAC falls

**Referral credits** (acquisition)
1. Give a free month for each neighbour referred
2. Credit the account when the name is submitted

**Leave branded bins at addresses** (operations)
1. Keep the address open when customers cancel
2. New residents call the number on the bin
3. Use branded bins as street billboards

**Seed local Facebook groups** (acquisition)
1. Join 10-20 neighbourhood Facebook groups
2. Search for complaints about trash
3. Plant seeds, then launch when a contract fails

**Find trucks in operator Facebook groups** (operations)
1. Join the Mom and Pop Trash group and truck Marketplace groups
2. Look for under-CDL side-loaders
3. Buy from a brand with local dealer support

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Pre-sales with a refund guarantee de-risk a physical business and fund equipment.
- *mindset_execution*: Ignorance of an industry can be a superpower; industry insiders often discourage.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Route density drives profitability in route-based services.
- *exit_acquisition*: Subscription businesses are more sellable and have lower churn than per-job service businesses.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Let customers' recurring payments finance bulk purchases to preserve cash for marketing.
- *customer_acquisition*: Switching depends on timing; be present when the incumbent fails.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Stop operating in the business and hire a driver to work on growth.
- *customer_acquisition*: Branded physical assets at customers' homes work as ongoing marketing.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Equipment financiers can be sharky: no early payoff, truck repossessed if you miss payments.
- Auction requires cash on the day.
- Parking a garbage truck at home triggers HOA letters.
- Spencer did not collect Google reviews for the first 1.5 years, which he regards as a mistake.
- Customers do not switch just because of the story or reviews; they switch when the incumbent errs.
- Spent the pre-sale cash on bins rather than financing them.
