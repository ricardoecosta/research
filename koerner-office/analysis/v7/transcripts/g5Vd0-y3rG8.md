## Tactics and playbook steps

**Find long-tail directory opportunities in search** (customer_acquisition)
1. Search Google for 'near me' and inspect results beyond the first few pages for unusual service terms.
2. Search for directory pages with a niche term and inspect whether current results are outdated or poorly organized.
3. Use Ahrefs and Keywords Everywhere to check search volume and competition, then inspect the actual search results yourself.
4. Treat weak competitors as a signal to investigate, not proof that a directory will succeed.
> "Sometimes you just got to go there yourself to get a feel for it." — Chris Koerner (~4:27)

**Use maps and provider questions to find filter gaps** (validation_testing)
1. Search Google Maps for a specific local service and inspect business names and websites for keyword stuffing, weak design, missing photos, few reviews, or missing lead forms.
2. Check provider questions and answers for repeated questions about hours, languages, insurance, or service availability.
3. Use filters such as open now, mobile, kid-friendly, and languages spoken only when there is evidence that users want them.
4. Verify listing details before publishing them.
> "Each question is a filter that people care about. And you can add all of these filters to your directory, or you can make a directory out of one filter entirely." — Chris Koerner (~10:46)

**Mine Facebook groups for recurring requests** (customer_acquisition)
1. Find active local or interest groups where members ask for recommendations.
2. Search group posts for phrases such as 'looking for', 'recommend', or 'anyone know a good'.
3. Read comments as well as the original post to see whether requests are answered and which filters matter.
4. Save recurring requests, comments, and engagement in a simple Google Doc or Sheet.
5. Ask group members what filters and budget would matter to them, then use the answers to shape a prototype.
> "The gold is really in the comments there. You don't even really need to guess what filters to add to these searches." — Chris Koerner (~16:01)

**Mine Reddit for unmet local needs** (validation_testing)
1. Choose subreddits where people ask for recommendations or plan events.
2. Search for phrases such as 'recommend a', 'anyone know a good', and 'where to get a'.
3. Read comment threads for repeat unanswered questions, frustration, and links to poor-quality alternatives.
4. Sort posts by time period to spot recurring questions.
5. Use Google site search to find Reddit threads when Reddit's own search is inconvenient.
> "The comments are where people are having difficulty finding answers to their questions." — Chris Koerner (~18:44)

**Validate and launch a simple directory** (validation_testing)
1. Choose a niche with repeated search or recommendation evidence and identify the filters people ask for.
2. Find ten real providers using sources such as Google Maps, Reddit, Yelp, and Facebook; collect name, city, link, and one or two useful details.

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Start with a narrow customer question or filter; a smaller, useful answer can be easier to build and discover than a broad directory.
- *validation_testing*: Look for repeated recommendations and unanswered comments across several sources before committing to a niche.
- *offer_design*: Treat filters as part of the product: users may care more about insurance, language, availability, or policy than a basic category listing.
- *validation_testing*: Inspect real search results and local competitors; keyword metrics alone do not show what the user actually sees.
- *operations*: Use a simple research log to compare repeated requests, filters, providers, and possible monetization routes.
- *validation_testing*: Test the problem with a small prototype and real providers before building a large site.
- *offer_design*: Make it faster for a user to solve the problem than searching Google.
- *mindset_execution*: Keep tools simple and focus on execution rather than collecting software.

## Warnings and mistakes

- A directory idea needs more than one supporting data point; some repeated requests may already be well served.
- Comments may show that demand is already being met, so inspect replies rather than counting posts alone.
- The host says permission is not needed before making a prototype list. In the UK, listing information and contacting providers still require appropriate privacy, platform, and marketing checks.
- The video promotes the host's paid TK Owners community and his newsletter and podcast, which creates a promotional conflict. The promotion is not treated as a business idea here.
