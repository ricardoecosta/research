## Tactics and playbook steps

**Launch in communities where your target users gather** (acquisition)
1. Tell coworkers and personal contacts first
2. Post the tool in the relevant Reddit community
3. Add Google ads and SEO afterwards
4. Keep appearing on podcasts, YouTube and social media
> "you need to go where your community congregates and tell as many people as possible" — Host (~11:56)

**Iterative prompting with an AI app builder** (product)
1. Write a first prompt describing headline, input box, outputs and button
2. Review output and chat to fix generic results
3. Ask for expandable details: description, steps, realistic revenue
4. Optionally connect the OpenAI API for better output

**Build a tool to solve a problem you get repeatedly asked about** (product)
1. Notice questions colleagues keep asking you
2. Build a chatbot-style tool for it in evenings alongside a job
3. Test willingness to pay with a freemium plan

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Distribution matters more than building; even a great app needs deliberate marketing.
- *automation_ai*: Domain expertise plus no-code AI tools can produce an MVP without programming skills.
- *validation_testing*: Solving a problem you are repeatedly asked about is a good validation signal.
- *customer_acquisition*: Start by sharing with your personal network, then scale to communities like Reddit, then paid ads and SEO.
- *mindset_execution*: Build while keeping a full-time job; the founder worked evenings.
- *other*: Free tools can still make money through other routes, per the host's teaser of a free-tool site.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Building an app does not produce revenue without active marketing.
- AI-generated outputs can be generic or silly and need prompt refinement.
