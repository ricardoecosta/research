## Tactics and playbook steps

**Laundromat grand opening playbook** (acquisition)
1. 3 months out: coming-soon EDDM mailers, join chambers, publications
2. 1 month out: VIP red-carpet event for local business owners (few thousand dollars)
3. Announce free laundry weekends at 1 month and 2 weeks
4. Run two consecutive free weekends, all staff present, ask every customer for a Google review
> "how many fivestar reviews can we get as quickly as possible?" — Tyler (~26:41)

**Site selection** (operations)
1. Look for areas with 3-5 existing laundromats (proven market)
2. Hang out at competitors, take water meter readings to estimate revenue, note pricing
3. Match demographics; target 1990s apartments with poor shared laundry, not new builds
4. Build next to or across from the best competitor
> "We don't want new. We want the apartment. We want the 1990s apartment" — Tyler (~25:21)

**Price at parity with better experience** (pricing)
1. Survey competitor prices before entering
2. Use efficient equipment to keep utilities <10% so you can match prices
3. Offer a much better experience at the same price

**Use machine data to pick equipment** (operations)
1. Track every cycle per machine in the cloud
2. Compare turns per day by machine size
3. Add more large machines where the data shows demand

**Accept every payment method** (operations)
1. Quarters, loyalty card, debit/credit, Apple Pay, tap
2. Reward loyalty card with free dry credits

## Lessons

- *exit_acquisition*: Build new rather than buy and refurbish; you can outcompete old stores from across the street.
- *operations*: Remove all friction from receiving money; serve what the customer wants, not what is easy for the owner.
- *niche_selection*: Mid-size towns with low competition quality can be dominated with a few great locations.
- *customer_acquisition*: Get people in the door free; habit-driven customers don't go back once they've switched.
- *validation_testing*: A/B test business model choices across locations (card-only vs coin-only).
- *finance_unit_economics*: Efficient equipment lowers variable cost, enabling price parity with a better product.
- *scaling*: Self-service matures in years 2-3 as the 1-3 mile radius saturates; growth then comes from commercial and price rises.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Bonus depreciation on equipment makes laundromats attractive for offsetting other income (US).
- *customer_acquisition*: Ask for reviews when customers are getting something free.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Buying an old laundromat: books are unverifiable, people overpay, equipment breaks constantly, and you buy yourself a low-paying job.
- Laundromats are not passive; even with a GM and AGM it's work.
- Car washes go fully down when a part fails; laundromats keep earning with machines down.
- Short leases risk the landlord taking over your build-out; get long leases with options.
- Influencer hype around laundromats glosses over these problems.
