### I1. Washer and dryer rental via Facebook Marketplace

- **Customer:** Renters in apartments and houses without a washer and dryer, who would otherwise use a laundromat
- **Problem:** Tenants lack in-unit laundry and laundromats are inconvenient and costly
- **Offer:** Month-to-month washer and dryer rental, free delivery, install and maintenance, minimum 6 months
- **Business model:** Monthly recurring rental subscription with free delivery, install and maintenance
- **Pricing:** Monthly subscription per set; used $60, new $85. used set USD 60 per month; new set USD 85 per month; initial test listing price USD 50 per month Lists at $60 on Marketplace; first payment due on install day. Customers described as not price sensitive.
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: Posts 'washer and dryer for rent' on Facebook Marketplace with real-looking photos, multiple accounts with different zip codes, and shares into local groups. The rental title breaks the pattern among 'for sale' listings. Fast replies (same day) are critical, using saved message templates. Pays $12/month for the verified badge to get support when banned.; door_to_door_in_person: Hangs door hangers on apartment complexes known not to provide laundry; 30 minutes per 200 doors, 3-4 leads per complex. - **Delivery:** Kyler (with his younger brother) hauls sets in a small trailer behind a Toyota Highlander, installs in 30-45 minutes, tests, has the customer sign a contract and shows them how to use it. Service calls run 5-8 per month, some solved by phone. Removes sets on move-out, pressure washes, and re-rents in 2-3 days. Repairs use parts harvested from scrap machines.
- **Unit economics (claims):** About 120 sets, roughly $10K/month; Used sets cost $0-$200, payback in 4-5 months; About 50 churned of about 170 delivered over about 24 months (about 1.5%/month); Insurance $700/year; 5-10 hours per week

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Post, Rent, Buy validation** (product)
1. Post a rental listing with a realistic photo before owning inventory
2. Reply to every enquirer quickly and confirm interest
3. Once a customer commits, buy the machines and install
4. Scale only when demand is proven
> "I call it the PRB process. Step one, post it. Okay. Step two, rent it. Okay. Step three, buy it." — Kyler (~28:12)

**Marketplace listing recipe** (acquisition)
1. Title 'Washer and dryer for rent'
2. Price $60 and use many photos of real machines
3. Description: 'Never go to the laundromat again. Rent a washer and dryer month-to-month, free delivery, install and maintenance'
4. Post to multiple accounts with different zip codes and into local groups
5. Use saved reply templates stating $60 used, $85 new, 6-month minimum
6. Pay the $12/month badge for support

**Sourcing free or cheap machines** (operations)
1. Search Marketplace for 'free appliances' and 'broken appliances' and message first
2. Do deliveries for used appliance stores and take trade-ins
3. Befriend used-appliance sellers who list often and negotiate a bulk deal with a 2-year guarantee
4. Get scrappers to send daily pictures and drop off units
5. Give delivery drivers your number so they offer removed units for about $15-20 each
6. Part out a dead unit to repair others

**Door-hanger campaign on apartment complexes** (acquisition)
1. Pick complexes where a rented set already proves no laundry is provided
2. Hang flyers on all doors (about 30 minutes per 200 doors)
3. Expect 3-4 leads per complex
4. Print in bulk at about 5 cents each

**Install and onboarding script** (operations)
1. Check the layout and test water and dryer
2. Have the customer sign the contract and state it is rental not rent-to-own
3. Explain free maintenance and card auto-charge on a fixed day
4. Give a 2-minute usage demo, load limits and lint trap guidance
5. Take card details on the spot

**Automate recurring billing with Stripe** (finance)
1. Move from Cash App to Stripe subscriptions
2. Charge cards automatically each month to avoid chasing payments

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Validate demand with a listing before spending on inventory; the downside is only wasted time.
- *niche_selection*: A boring, year-round niche rental (in-home appliance) beat seasonal rentals like boats and paddle boards.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Recurring rental revenue with low churn (about 1.5%/month) is driven by leases and convenience.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Cheaper used inventory was just as attractive as new, so cost cuts barely hurt demand.
- *operations*: Build supplier relationships (shops, drivers, scrappers) for steady cheap inventory.
- *automation_ai*: Automate payments early (Stripe) to remove admin as you scale.
- *customer_acquisition*: Fast response and a pattern-breaking listing title beat polish in marketplace selling.
- *mindset_execution*: Resourcefulness over capital: rent a truck or hitch rather than buying one.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Lint-blocked dryer vents cause failure and fire risk; tell tenants to call the landlord rather than cleaning vents yourself.
- Slow response on Marketplace loses leads; reply within the same day.
- He ran without insurance for 3 months and used a non-binding Google Docs contract.
- Buying new machines was a costly mistake; renters just want a working unit.
