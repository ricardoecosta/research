## Summary

Chris Koerner ranks eight ChatGPT models/products from worst to best (GPT-4.1, GPT-4o, Sora, GPT-4.5, Deep Research, o3/o4-mini, ChatGPT Agent, gpt-oss), explains each and suggests ways to monetise them, then adds GPT-5 as a 'number 0.5'. Cheap models suit commoditised wrapper services (bulk descriptions, captions, chatbots, cold email), while more specialised capabilities (deep research consulting, technical services, agent implementations) command higher prices. His top pick is implementing the open-weight gpt-oss privately on company servers for big setup fees plus monthly maintenance, because data privacy is what stops companies adopting AI. Key takeaway: specialised, measurable-ROI AI implementation beats low-skill wrapper services.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Task log to find automation opportunities** (operations)
1. Keep a notepad at your computer for a day
2. Write down every non-complex task with a timestamp
3. At the end of the day photograph the list and upload it to ChatGPT
4. Ask it to help automate or outsource every task
> "every time they perform a task that's not very complex, which are most of our tasks, they need to just write it down with a timestamp" — Chris Koerner (~15:31)

**Cross-reference deep research across three AIs** (operations)
1. Run the identical deep research prompt in ChatGPT, Perplexity and Claude
2. Compare answers to catch errors
3. Upload proprietary client data (e.g. sales data) for insight the internet lacks
> "I always run the same prompt across at least three different AI companies so I can cross reference between the three" — Chris Koerner (~10:22)

**Learn private GPT implementation on yourself first** (product)
1. Ask ChatGPT how to run gpt-oss locally
2. Do it on your own data/emails as practice
3. Then sell to companies, starting with healthcare or finance
4. Partner with someone with networking/hardware experience
> "Do it for your own data. Do it for your emails. Practice on yourself" — Chris Koerner (~20:03)

**Charge premium while a capability is novel** (pricing)
1. Launch services on new AI capabilities (e.g. Sora video) early
2. Price as high as possible before commoditisation
3. Expect prices to fall and plan to move on
> "I highly encourage you to charge as much as you can while you can because I do think prices will continue going down in this industry" — Chris Koerner (~8:28)

**Supervise agent automations as 'air traffic control'** (operations)
1. Connect inbound requests to an AI agent
2. Watch runs and fix breakages
3. Tweak automations after each fix so they break less over time
> "you're just playing air traffic control and watching all these things happen and fixing things as they inevitably break" — Chris Koerner (~16:06)

## Lessons

- *pricing*: Specialisation earns more: more specialised models (and services) command higher prices, like a heart surgeon vs a pediatrician.
- *pricing*: Services whose ROI is clear and measurable (coding, labour replacement) are easier to price than subjective ones like strategy.
- *offer_design*: The 80/20 pitch: 80% cheaper and 80% as good is an easy bet for companies.
- *automation_ai*: Proprietary client data is what makes AI research uniquely valuable.
- *niche_selection*: Data privacy is the blocker for enterprise AI adoption; local models remove it and create an implementation market.
- *scaling*: Being embedded with a client creates high switching costs and repeat AI work.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Use the cheapest adequate model for wrappers; extra cost of premium models is not proportional to quality.
- *automation_ai*: Agents will make existing staff superhuman rather than cause mass layoffs, cutting future hiring.
- *mindset_execution*: Thinking on your feet becomes more valuable as everyone relies on AI answers.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Low-skill wrapper businesses (descriptions, captions, emails) face heavy competition.
- Medical consultation support with AI needs care with regulations.
- AI video commoditises fast and audiences spot AI clips.
- ChatGPT Agent still breaks often and implementations vary by platform.
- Voice mode in GPT-4o gives shallower answers than typed prompts.
- UK note (extractor): cold email/SMS outreach for client campaigns is restricted by PECR; only corporate subscribers may be cold emailed.
