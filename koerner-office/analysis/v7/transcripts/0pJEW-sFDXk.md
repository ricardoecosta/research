## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Dan Porter, former teacher and CEO of OMGPOP, who designed Draw Something, a friends-only drawing game that hit #1 in 60 countries with 25M daily players and sold to Zynga for $200M. He explains the key ingredients (simplicity, streaks, friends-only play, live drawing playback) and how a bug fix made it take off. He later led digital at WME and founded Overtime (110M followers, own leagues) and launched 67 Water from a meme. Key takeaway: building is research, small tweaks matter, and hits need all ingredients together.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Fix toxicity by restricting the audience** (product)
1. Identify why an open version fails (strangers being rude)
2. Limit play to friends instead of moderating everyone
3. Gain built-in virality from contact invites
> "you couldn't solve changing people's bad behavior on the internet" — Chris Koerner (~13:13)

**Check analytics for your real customer** (product)
1. Dig into Google Analytics
2. Discover real audience (14-year-old girls in California, not the team)
3. Rename company if users misspell it
> "I realized that the customers were actually almost entirely 14-year-old girls in California" — Dan Porter (~8:17)

**Hiring question for designers** (hiring)
1. Ask 'what is your favourite game?' then 'why is it great?'
2. Hire the 1 in 5 who describe a specific mechanic in depth

**Paid filmer network instead of UGC** (operations)
1. Build own iPhone camera/tagging software
2. Pay people $20 per game to film
3. Promote standout filmers to full-time

**Street-level customer development** (product)
1. Send interns to bodegas/gas stations to talk to owners and kids
2. Feed findings into product (e.g. add a flavour)
3. Launch in an overlooked market (Kentucky) not LA/NY

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Hits need all key ingredients together (simplicity, streaks, friends-only, live playback); remove one and it fails.
- *validation_testing*: Building is the research; a premise is just an excuse to start.
- *validation_testing*: Small tweaks can be the difference between failure and $200M; keep testing.
- *mindset_execution*: Outsiders can win because they don't know the 'right' way (forgot to add XP).
- *offer_design*: Great products must feel amazing in use.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Hire people who think deeply about why things work.
- *mindset_execution*: Passion often follows being good at something.
- *customer_acquisition*: Launch where competition for attention is lower (Kentucky, not LA/NY).
- *niche_selection*: Cultural signals (wired headphones, recurring story themes) inform product decisions.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Platform risk cuts both ways: a giant platform game (FarmVille) made his company small overnight.
- A technical bug nearly killed the hit; it bombed until developers noticed people trying to play.
- Building for 'people like us' rather than real users.
- Young people don't go to websites.
- Owning many businesses at once makes it hard to make any great.
