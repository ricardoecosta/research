## Tactics and playbook steps

**Reciprocity-first lead magnet** (acquisition)
1. Give something with a nameable, concrete value (e.g. the exact spreadsheet used to rank top 100 ads) rather than 'free training'
2. Give before asking for anything; no buy-first-then-gift
3. Do not hold the gift hostage behind a sales call
> "Give it before they have done something for you." — Codie Sanchez (~3:33)

**Self-referencing ad hook** (acquisition)
1. Use the template: if you are X or have X problem, this is for you
2. Name the buyer's exact situation, e.g. answering employee texts at 10pm
3. Get a first mental yes
> "if you own a business and you're still answering employee texts at 10pm, this is for you" — Codie Sanchez (~2:04)

**Micro-commitment ladder** (sales)
1. Offer a free registration first
2. After registration, offer the $47 VIP upgrade
3. Then offer direct work with the team
4. Track how fast you get the first yes; a buyer is a stronger signal than an email subscriber
> "only after someone had already clicked yes and registered. Did we offer them the next step of $47 VIP upgrade?" — Codie Sanchez (~6:40)

**Seven CTA rules** (acquisition)
1. One CTA repeated, as people need 12+ (maybe 24-30) touches
2. Add surprise and delight
3. Use strong verbs (claim, secure, grab) not learn/discover
4. Make it personal with your/my
5. Be direct
6. Match the size of the ask to the relationship
7. Frame it as a trade showing what they get
> "clarity beats brevity" — Codie Sanchez (~8:44)

**90/60/30/10 launch calendar** (acquisition)
1. 90 days out: subtle teasers and brand colour
2. 60 days out: announce, post 1-3 times a week
3. 45 days out: most ads are direct CTAs
4. 30 days out: more frequent posts with logo placement
5. Last two weeks: all content about the launch
6. After the event, convert the replay to evergreen video sales page
> "we do a 90, 60, 30, 10 model" — Codie Sanchez (~14:41)

**Spy on Amazon book ads** (acquisition)
1. Go to your favourite business book on Amazon
2. See who is running ads on its page
3. Treat that as evidence they are paying for sales; apply the same to Amazon products
> "go to your favorite business book and then see who's running ads on top of them" — Codie Sanchez (~14:10)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Create exclusivity subtly (e.g. only open to 100 people) rather than slapping 'secret' on everything.
- *offer_design*: Make the customer the hero and lead with the ideal state before the mechanism; sell the benefit, not the feature.
- *pricing*: Reduce friction on the first decision and build to bigger asks; most people ask for the biggest thing first and then discount.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Prefer lower revenue with higher margin; target margin per customer at 2-3x CAC.
- *scaling*: Own the customer relationship and data (emails, addresses) rather than renting audience via a publisher or retailer.
- *scaling*: Turn launch spikes into continuous sales: capture leads, pitch live, then repurpose into evergreen funnels.
- *scaling*: Multiply yourself with characters, other faces and consistent brand colour so the business does not depend only on the founder.
- *customer_acquisition*: Piggyback on cultural trends with your own twist; cheap low-effort trend videos can outperform expensive content.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Giving away value without a nameable concrete thing or after asking for a purchase turns reciprocity into a transaction and you become a donation bank.
- Don't chase revenue while ignoring profit margin.
- Two-word CTAs like 'comment X' did nothing; short is not better.
- Traditional publishing: the publisher owns customer relationship, distribution and data, and a bestseller push is followed by a big drop-off.
