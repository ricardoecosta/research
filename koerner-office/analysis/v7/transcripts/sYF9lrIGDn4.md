## Summary

Chris Koerner speaks to a homeschool class of about 20 high schoolers, telling his story from selling washed golf balls at age 10 to iPhone repair stores, an LCD screen recycling business and a failed mobile home park venture. He answers Q&A on starting with no money, validating demand by selling before building (Facebook Marketplace, a tree trimming ad test), business partnerships, and his marketing-heavy, operations-light approach. The key takeaway is to sell something before doing 'procrastination' tasks like LLCs and websites, and to use constraints as a source of creativity.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Sell it before you have it** (sales)
1. Pick an idea and offer it to someone before building anything
2. Aim for even $1 of revenue before LLC, website, business plan
3. Try to get a non-friend or non-family customer to pay
> "Sell it before you have it." — Chris Koerner (~14:04)

**Test demand with a Facebook Marketplace ad** (acquisition)
1. Post an ad for the service before buying equipment
2. Count inbound quote requests
3. Only invest if demand shows up
4. Subcontract delivery rather than buy gear
> "there's so many things we can do to test the market, test the demand before ever spending a dollar." — Chris Koerner (~25:45)

**Collect payment up front to self-fund a service business** (finance)
1. Offer a free quote
2. Take payment before or on the job
3. Use part of the money to buy supplies
4. Keep the remainder as profit
> "You can collect money up front. You could do a service business that requires no hardware or materials at all." — Chris Koerner (~22:26)

**Use door-to-door sales as a testing lab** (sales)
1. Knock on hundreds of doors
2. Vary one thing at a time such as smile, greeting, introduction
3. Refine script until it converts
> "If you knock on a 100 doors, that's a 100 different tests." — Chris Koerner (~14:51)

**Facebook Marketplace selling checklist** (operations)
1. Take photos and put a video first
2. Use AI to generate the description
3. Check sold listings for pricing
4. Respond quickly
5. Meet buyer and accept payment
> "put a video first, it will get more traction, do the market research on pricing, looked at the sold items, be very responsive" — Chris Koerner (~26:25)

**Partnership equity structure** (finance)
1. Be 50/50 only if both are full-time, all-in and side by side in person
2. Otherwise use 90/10 or 80/20 weighted splits
3. Pick a partner whose strengths cover your weaknesses
> "In any other circumstance, 50/50 is a disaster waiting to happen." — Chris Koerner (~28:20)

**Marketing-heavy, operations-light model** (operations)
1. Focus on finding customers
2. Subcontract operations
3. Exit day-to-day once launched and showing traction
> "my businesses I try to get out of the business as soon as I can once it's launched and it shows traction." — Chris Koerner (~29:25)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Opportunities close to you are the ones you notice; the golf ball business came only from living across from a course.
- *mindset_execution*: Earning your first money without a job changes your mindset and builds independence, like food storage for your bank account.
- *mindset_execution*: Starting before you know the skill works, but you must then learn it; he says not learning more was his mistake.
- *customer_acquisition*: Mass rejection (40-50k doors in Hungary) builds confidence and sales skill.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Constraints drive creativity; heavily funded companies are more likely to fail because they never need to be creative.
- *mindset_execution*: Don't take things at face value; small tweaks (paying a stranger in line to buy a shirt) solve problems.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Complement your weaknesses with partners rather than spending years fixing them; he is strong at zero-to-one and weak at operations.
- *niche_selection*: As you gain experience, filter ideas for best use of time instead of building every idea.
- *pricing*: Higher-ticket home services beat low-ticket ones for the same effort.
- *mindset_execution*: Persistence and willingness to pivot are the core success traits.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Using student loans for a house down payment is risky.
- Business partners not vetted well led to losing everything in the mobile home park venture.
- Do not wait for LLC, website or business plan before selling; it is disguised procrastination.
- Entrepreneurship is volatile, with years of zero income.
- Take advice only from relevant experienced people or people with nothing to lose; filter advice from others.
