### I1. Washer and dryer rentals via Facebook Marketplace

- **Customer:** Renters in apartments or homes with laundry hookups but no machines; people who avoid laundromats or buying
- **Problem:** Buying, maintaining and moving a washer and dryer is a headache, and laundromats are inconvenient
- **Offer:** Washer and dryer rental at about $60-85/month for a set, delivery, install and free repairs, 6 or 12 month minimum
- **Business model:** Monthly recurring rental, with an option to sell units
- **Pricing:** Monthly rental, set or single unit. washer and dryer set USD 60-85 per month; single washer or dryer USD 40 per month; single dryer USD 35-40 per month Competitor listings were $50-85. Boosted posts can justify a higher price (e.g. $70 to $85). Single units are priced above half the set price.
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: Facebook Marketplace listing with clear outdoor photo, category 'washer and dryer sets', 'free delivery, install and maintenance' description, priced $60. Boost for $1-2/day or $20 for a 1-day boost to reach the top of results.; facebook_groups_communities: Suggested joining local Dallas buy/sell groups as a differentiator; not yet done.; referrals_word_of_mouth: Customers whose dryer breaks call to rent one; install visits yield contacts and free used machines.; door_to_door_in_person: Idea to talk to property managers and use door hangers at complexes with hookups but no machines.
- **Delivery:** Operator sources used sets from Facebook Marketplace or garage sales, rents a truck, delivers and installs with new hoses and gaskets (channel locks needed), tests the units, and handles repairs. Installs take about 20 minutes. Owner does everything physically.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Set bought for $125, rents at $60-70/month, profitable after month 2; $20 boost acquired a customer worth about $1,300 over 2 years; Theft/loss rate about 1%; Two rentals ($70/month) from about 4 hours of work in Dallas
- **Tools:** Facebook Marketplace, Facebook Messenger, Hertz/Avis/National truck rental, Lowe's, maronee.com
- **Startup cost:** Roughly $125-300 per set, plus truck rental $29.99/day (Hertz, no mileage), about $15 channel locks, new hoses, optional $20 boost. Name, LLC, logo, website and insurance skipped.
- **Time to first revenue:** Same day in Dallas demonstration; two sets rented in about 3-4 hours
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — Appliance rental works in the UK (Facebook Marketplace and Gumtree exist), but many UK rentals include appliances, sets are often combined washer-dryers or freestanding with different hookups, vans are needed, and electrical safety (PAT testing) and consumer rental terms apply. Fewer unfurnished renters lack washers. Hertz-style no-mileage van hire is common.
- **Risks:** Competition from copycats after a viral video; Theft or non-payment (1-2% claimed loss); Leaks causing water damage liability, insurance skipped in demo; Customer ghosting and unit-number issues; Physical work limits scale to owner's time; Facebook policy or account risk

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Post first, buy after demand** (validation)
1. Publish the Facebook Marketplace rental listing first
2. Wait for a client
3. Then source the washer/dryer set
4. Check local price range of sets beforehand
> "we first publish the listing, wait for a client, and then we will buy the washer and dryer set" — Kyler (~21:24)

**Boost listings for visibility and price up** (acquisition)
1. Boost the approved listing for $1-2/day or a $20 1-day boost
2. Raise price when boosting (e.g. $70 to $85)
3. Respond first, since pricing is similar across competitors

**Source cheap sets** (operations)
1. Look for garage-sale posts, which skip individual listings
2. Target $100-200, older direct-drive sets with agitator
3. Avoid Samsung
4. Skip units with noise problems (bearing)
5. Message 5-6 sellers and confirm you can pick up today
6. Prefer clean units needing no work

**Rent a truck to prove demand** (operations)
1. Rent from Hertz, Avis or National at about $30/day with no mileage charge
2. Avoid Home Depot/U-Haul per-mile fees
3. Once demand proven, buy or lease a cheap truck

**Install checklist** (operations)
1. Always use new hoses with new gaskets
2. Test heating and hot water and balance the unit
3. Show customer the lint trap
4. State clearly that building plumbing is not your responsibility
5. Get a phone number before delivering

**Relentless follow-up on leads** (sales)
1. Follow up about seven times
2. Drive to the complex even if ghosted
3. Knock doors if no unit number, since each customer is worth $700/year
> "if he doesn't follow up like seven times like an obsessive girlfriend, he's not going to respond." — Chris Koerner (~10:02)

**Recycle trade-in machines** (operations)
1. Take customers' old machines at install
2. Fix for about $30 and rerent, or sell
3. Put cash into more boosted posts

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Competition can enlarge the market: awareness from many listings creates customers who weren't searching.
- *customer_acquisition*: When prices are similar, the first responsive, honest seller who delivers wins.
- *validation_testing*: Test demand before spending on branding or incorporation: skip name, LLC and website at first.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Frame ad spend by lifetime value: $20 to acquire a roughly $1,300 customer is a strong return.
- *mindset_execution*: Persistent follow-up is required to close; most leads need several touches.
- *scaling*: Physical delivery-based businesses are limited by owner time, not demand.
- *operations*: Trade-ins from customers give free inventory, compounding recurring revenue.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Hourly-rate extrapolations from a single day of sign-ups overstate profit because they ignore churn, repairs, insurance and sourcing time.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Don't deliver with only Messenger contact; always get a phone number first because customers ghost.
- Reusing old hoses risks leaks and flooding calls; use new hoses and gaskets.
- Avoid Samsung machines; avoid non-direct-drive units with control boards if you want reliability.
- Clarify that building plumbing leaks are the property's responsibility.
- The truck-rental approach is only for proving demand, not long term.
