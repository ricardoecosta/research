## Summary

Spencer Haws of Niche Pursuits tells how he built and sold Link Whisper, a WordPress internal-linking plugin, for $2.7 million in January 2025. ## Tactics and playbook steps

**Three ways to find software ideas** (product)
1. Scratch your own itch: build a tool for a problem you personally have
2. Find a gap: look at big software companies and spot features they do poorly or as afterthoughts, then build a dedicated, better version
3. Interview potential customers (he interviewed many bloggers) to confirm the pain and shape features
> "if you can find, for example, a billion dollar software company that is building all these great features, you might be able to find one of those features that they aren't doing a very good job at." — Spencer Haws (~2:55)

**Seven-channel software growth playbook** (acquisition)
1. Build the best product and keep improving it using customer feedback and competitor monitoring
2. Content marketing: blog posts, YouTube demos, showing other bloggers using it
3. Build an email list and run drip campaigns plus regular promotions
4. Affiliate programme: email buyers an invitation to become affiliates
5. Outreach to bloggers, YouTubers and SEO influencers with free copies for reviews
6. Offer a free stripped-down version on WordPress.org as a funnel to paid
7. List on as many marketplaces as possible

**Exit via broker with multiple buyers** (finance)
1. Receive inbound acquisition interest and learn the process, even if the deals fall through
2. List with a broker (Quiet Light Brokerage) in summer 2024
3. Hold calls and due diligence with many potential buyers
4. Collect multiple offers and accept the one at full asking price
5. Expect delays: SBA-financed buyers may take 90+ days, plus bank switches and slow attorneys
> "We listed Link Whisper for sale, and I started getting contacted by a lot of potential buyers." — Spencer Haws (~13:18)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Solving your own problem gives built-in product insight and conviction for a niche software tool.
- *niche_selection*: Look for features that large incumbents treat as an afterthought; a dedicated best-in-class tool can win the niche.
- *validation_testing*: Interview potential users before building to validate pain and shape features.
- *customer_acquisition*: An existing audience and email list is a massive launch advantage ($30k in month one), but it needs ongoing growth channels afterwards.
- *offer_design*: Product quality is the best marketing: great products get talked about and top review lists.
- *customer_acquisition*: A free version on a marketplace is a strong top-of-funnel for paid upgrades.
- *customer_acquisition*: Affiliates and influencers with free copies can become a major sales channel, especially in an SEO-review-driven market.
- *exit_acquisition*: Decline early offers if terms are uncertain; inbound interest teaches you the process and can lead to a better sale with a broker and multiple buyers.
- *exit_acquisition*: Closing a sale takes far longer than expected; plan for stress and delays from financing and legal counterparties.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Business sales can drag on far beyond plan: a deal expected to close in September closed on the last day of the year due to a bank switch and a slow, inexperienced buyer attorney.
- Launch revenue does not persist; expect a big drop in months two and three.
- Website free trial did not convert well; free versions on marketplaces worked better.
- A potential buyer deal fell through because the buyer wanted integrations taking up to six months, creating uncertainty.
