## Tactics and playbook steps

**Calibrate skill-game odds before charging** (pricing)
1. Run the challenge for free first
2. Record each attempt's distance, player skill level and outcome
3. Set distance and price so expected payouts stay below revenue
4. Recommended: $5 per throw or $10 for three at a long enough distance
> "I recommend doing it for free to get those numbers down" — Chris Koerner (~18:02)

**Go where the niche already gathers** (acquisition)
1. Skip general public parks where few know the sport
2. Set up at specialty shops and tournaments as a side show
3. Partner with the venue (e.g. donate proceeds to the owner's cause)

**Get venue permission before charging** (legal)
1. Confirm park rules on charging and cash prizes
2. Use private land or partner venues
> "They don't want us charging. They don't want us giving away free money" — Chris Koerner (~5:42)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Skill-game viability is a probability problem: distance, skill level and price are the variables.
- *finance_unit_economics*: The house always wins over the long run once odds are known.
- *validation_testing*: Collect data even from a failed test; it informs pricing.
- *niche_selection*: Pick a venue where the audience understands the product.
- *customer_acquisition*: Partnering with a local cause can unlock venue access and goodwill.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Public parks may forbid charging money and paying out prizes.
- Too short a distance invites skilled 'pool sharks'.
- Targeting skilled players raises both participation and win probability.
- UK note (extractor): prize-for-skill games must be genuine skill to avoid being classed as gambling under the Gambling Act 2005.
