### I1. Pallet and truckload liquidation reselling via Facebook Marketplace

- **Customer:** Local consumers on Facebook Marketplace, plus flippers, remodelers and contractors
- **Problem:** Retailers must move huge volumes of returns and overstock quickly, so goods sell far below retail
- **Offer:** Brand-new or lightly used branded goods at roughly half retail or less
- **Business model:** One-off product sales; margin between auction price plus freight and resale price
- **Pricing:** Resale at about 50% of retail or below, based on researched sold prices. Hearth & Hand wooden train set near Christmas USD 60-70 per train set; infant formula on Facebook Marketplace USD 9-20 per can Rule: 'retail price is a lie', cut MSRP in half and verify with sold listings
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: List on Facebook Marketplace (even before bidding to test demand), sell on eBay, live-sell; post in Facebook groups; garage/driveway sales; existing_audience: Capture customer emails and ask what else they buy; email them when new shipments land; share manifests with network contacts to price lots
- **Delivery:** Owner personally sources via auction, arranges freight (freight forwarder breaks truckloads into smaller pallets for residential delivery), stores in garage, tests and fixes items, and hands over locally; physical handling needs helpers
- **Unit economics (claims):** Train set lot of 240 units sold for $2,600 (about $11 each) vs Target retail $130; Costco furniture truckload retail $11,440 sold for $585; Walker Edison outdoor truckload of 250 units sold for $7,240, about $10k with freight; Sacramento member sold 600 pressure washers in 10 months; Member recouped truckload cost in 36 hours and hit $17,000 record month
- **Tools:** Costco wholesale auction site, Target liquidation auctions, Facebook Marketplace, eBay, Google Trends, Freight forwarder
- **Startup cost:** Small: a pallet can be ~$750; truckload about $7k-10k including freight
- **Time to first revenue:** Weeks (about 2-3 weeks to receive goods); one member profitable within 72 hours of receiving a truckload
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — UK has equivalents (B-Stock, Amazon/Argos/John Lewis liquidation pallets, Gumtree, eBay, Facebook Marketplace) but Costco/Target US auction access and prices differ. Distribution is local and heavy goods need storage. Consumer-law obligations on sold goods apply (Consumer Rights Act, product safety, WEEE for electricals).
- **Risks:** Resale value uncertain if you skip price research; Heavy goods need storage, helpers and freight; Seasonal inventory may sit for months; Damaged or broken items such as pressure washers need repair; Freight can erase margin; Expiry/product-safety issues on consumables
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 2/5 (Marketplace listings can be drafted and posted with AI tooling, but local buyer chat and pickup coordination need a human.); onboarding 2/5 (Auction bidding and price research can be tool-assisted; freight booking and checking lots need human judgment.); delivery 0/5 (Moving, storing, testing, repairing and handing over heavy physical goods is manual.); billing 2/5 (Mostly cash or app payments on pickup; online sales are payment-platform handled but still need a person.); support 2/5 (Buyer questions and disputes can be partly automated; returns and defects need a human.)
> "You always start at the sales side and work these deals backwards. You should know exactly what you're going to sell this product for before you place any bids" — Shannon (~23:10)
> "It's starting on Facebook Marketplace and then capturing that data, right? And capturing those customers when they come over." — Shannon (~17:33)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Work backwards from sale price** (sales)
1. Research sold listings on eBay and Marketplace
2. Halve MSRP as a conservative estimate
3. Share manifests with trade contacts for real values
4. Only bid after you know your exit price
> "You should know exactly what you're going to sell this product for before you place any bids" — Shannon (~23:14)

**Test demand before bidding** (validation)
1. List the item on Facebook Marketplace before winning
2. Include the expiry date or condition and watch reactions

**Buy boring and seasonal** (acquisition)
1. Target boring listings with poor titles
2. Buy seasonal goods off-season or just before peak
3. Scale to truckloads to eliminate beginner competition

**Build a customer list** (sales)
1. Start on Facebook Marketplace
2. Collect emails
3. Notify buyers of new shipments

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Boring products face less competition than shiny ones.
- *niche_selection*: A weak or lazy listing title is a signal that few bidders will compete.
- *scaling*: Buying truckloads removes most beginner competition and creates a moat.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Treat freight as part of cost of goods and judge on total margin.
- *validation_testing*: Seasonal stock reduces the risk of hidden defects because overstock is a forecasting error.
- *customer_acquisition*: Run a hobby flip like a business by capturing customer data.
- *mindset_execution*: Show up daily to browse auctions to develop pattern recognition.
- *mindset_execution*: Ask the auction seller questions; there are many reasons to skip, but opportunity is missed.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Never trust MSRP; research real market price
- Avoid expensive shipping from far away; buy closer to home
- Beginners should not buy lots they don't know how to sell
- Winning bidders sometimes fail to pay, which re-lists lots
