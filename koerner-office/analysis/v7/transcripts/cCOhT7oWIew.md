## Tactics and playbook steps

**Separate manager time from maker time** (operations)
1. Block the calendar according to the type of work being done.
2. Use roughly one-hour blocks for manager tasks such as meetings and reviews.
3. Protect longer, roughly four-hour blocks for deep creative or strategic work.
> "So the solution is to give yourself defined time blocks by what mode you are in. Are you in manager mode or are you in maker mode?" — Codie Sanchez (~1:54)

**Recognize staff with the three Cs** (hiring)
1. Give specific, individual recognition and name the work that was done well.
2. Connect day-to-day work to a meaningful mission.
3. Treat well-reasoned failures as calculated bets and recognize the effort when execution was sound.
> "I call this the three C's of culture. Let me break this down." — Codie Sanchez (~3:13)

**Borrow proven systems before innovating** (operations)
1. Start with systems, benchmarks, templates and examples that have already worked.
2. Use roughly 80% of the approach from proven systems and reserve about 20% for experiments.
3. Study people who have already solved the problem before creating a new process.
> "80% of business should build from proven systems benchmarks, templates, and people who have already solved the problem." — Codie Sanchez (~5:38)

**Score ideas and set a kill date** (product)
1. Score problem frequency, urgency, current cost and whether customers already pay for a solution from one to five.
2. Build ideas scoring 16 to 20; pre-sell ideas scoring 11 to 15; treat scores of six to 10 cautiously.
3. Set a 30-, 60- or 90-day review period and define benchmarks before proceeding.
4. Double down on growth, continue monitoring stable ideas, and kill declining ideas by the stated deadline.
> "So basically define a period, 30, 60, 90 days. You're gonna clear benchmarks to figure out if you should keep going." — Codie Sanchez (~9:34)

**Use the 4D task filter** (operations)
1. Ask whether the task moves the business toward money or a goal; delete it if not.
2. Ask whether it must happen now; delay it if urgency is not real.
3. Delegate it if someone else can do it.
4. Do it immediately only when it matters, is urgent and requires you personally.
> "So here's how you fix this with the 4D decision tree. Simple yes or no question." — Codie Sanchez (~11:02)

**Negotiate price and terms** (pricing)
1. Treat the first offer as a starting point and ask, “Is that the best you can do?”
2. Negotiate terms as well as headline price, including deposits, contract length and exit penalties.
3. Review the five to 10 largest contracts and expenses each year.
4. Ask what better terms are available for an existing customer, a longer commitment or a credible alternative.
> "Amateurs negotiate price. Pros negotiate terms." — Codie Sanchez (~13:53)

**Review customer profitability and fit** (finance)
1. Review customer profitability, including service effort and other costs rather than revenue alone.
2. Once a quarter, assess economic, behavioral, results and energy fit for each customer.
3. Prioritize customers who pay reliably, are easy to serve, want the work the business does best and follow its process.
4. Consider referring customers who are a poor fit to a competitor.
> "So once a quarter, I want you to log these four things about all of your customers." — Codie Sanchez (~16:39)

## Lessons

- *operations*: Protect uninterrupted blocks for complex work; meetings and quick requests carry a refocusing cost.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Staff motivation depends on recognition, meaningful work and responsibility, not only better perks.
- *operations*: Use proven processes as a starting point and reserve a smaller portion of work for experiments.
- *validation_testing*: Validate demand before investing heavily in an idea, and decide in advance what results would justify continuing.
- *mindset_execution*: Judge tasks by their contribution to goals, whether they are truly urgent and whether you must do them yourself.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Negotiating can include contract duration, deposits and exit terms, not just the headline price.
- *niche_selection*: Assess customers by delivery cost and working fit as well as revenue.
- *customer_acquisition*: A free self-assessment or other useful resource can introduce viewers to a broader advisory or education offer.
- *mindset_execution*: Do not try to change every habit at once; start with one or two and then move to the next.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Small requests and interruptions can consume more time than their meeting length because they break focus.
- Generic praise may not make staff feel recognized; feedback should identify the person and their contribution.
- Building a completely original playbook or fragile custom software can waste time when established systems already exist.
- Founders can become attached to products that have no demand; use validation and a pre-set decision date.
- Urgent-looking work can displace tasks with better long-term returns.
- Negotiation should be respectful; the presenter says revocations in the cited study involved people judged inappropriate or rude.
- Some customers may consume more service time and energy than their revenue justifies.
- The video's statistics are presented without full citations or supporting data, and several of Codie's frameworks and services are promotional.
