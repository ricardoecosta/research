## Tactics and playbook steps

**Use fixed packages to reduce buyer friction** (offer_design)
1. Offer a short menu of package sizes instead of asking every customer to invent a display.
2. Include a self-arrange option for customers who want delivery without setup.
3. Offer design as a $75 add-on for packages three and four.
4. Set a high minimum for custom packages so extra estimating time is worth taking on.
> "But if you're saying choose this, this this or this, then they kind of they have a good direction, right?" — Chris Koerner (~1:57)

**Build demand with current Instagram formats** (acquisition)
1. Watch which Instagram formats are currently performing and adapt the content mix.
2. Use porch photos and short videos to show the finished displays.
3. Boost posts to specific zip codes when paid promotion is useful.
4. Be ready to reply to comments soon after posting.
> "And then also reals like so you have to follow what Instagram algorithm is putting out there." — Heather (~17:32)

**Follow up personally with inbound leads** (sales)
1. Treat an enquiry as a lead, not a confirmed order.
2. Reply to people who comment or message and ask for the information needed to place an order.
3. Remind interested customers if a promised photo or order detail has not arrived.
4. After an email blast, follow up one to one with people who have not ordered.
> "You have to follow up with them. I mean, especially as a brand new entrepreneur, you need to follow up and make sure like, "Oh, coming back to make sure that I can help you place your order."" — Heather (~17:58)

**Start with a small live test and keep selling** (validation_testing)
1. Start before the peak season and collect orders early; Heather says she now starts collecting in July.
2. Use a few early homes to learn the delivery and design process.
3. Say yes to viable orders outside the immediate neighborhood, while putting a dollar amount on the added distance.
> "Get three homes under your belt. You know, I started September 27th." — Chris Koerner (~6:06)

**Scale delivery with separate driver and designer roles** (hiring)
1. Bring in a driver with a truck and trailer so the owner can serve more than a few homes at once.
2. Pay drivers hourly and designers by the design.
3. Ask customers who arrange their own displays to share a photo; Heather says she hired a designer after seeing one customer's work.
> "And I even found like two designers that could help me in that first year." — Heather (~7:45)

**Match sourcing method to volume** (operations)
1. Use accessible suppliers such as nurseries or produce distributors when starting small.
2. As volume grows, buy directly from farms to get the quantity and variety needed.
3. Plan inventory to avoid running out, while keeping storage and surplus disposal in mind.
> "The real secret is buying as many pumpkins as you need for your season. And so, the more pumpkins you need, then you can get them direct from farmers." — Heather (~10:33)

**Use a waitlist to fill late-season routes** (operations)
1. Track demand after the published delivery schedule fills.
2. Check the waitlist and add route days when inventory and crew capacity allow.
3. Heather says she planned a full route day on October 16 after the schedule was already through October 15.
> "We'll look at that weight list and we'll just pull additional trailers at the end of the season." — Heather (~14:02)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: A short package menu can reduce customer confusion and avoid repeatedly costing custom requests.
- *pricing*: Price for the value of the owner's time and responsibilities, rather than automatically matching an hourly wage.
- *validation_testing*: Use personal experience as a starting point for customer research, then adjust through trial and error.
- *customer_acquisition*: Inbound leads need deliberate follow-up; the number of enquiries can greatly exceed the number of orders.
- *customer_acquisition*: Choose social formats based on current platform behavior and make time to respond to comments.
- *operations*: Choose suppliers that fit the business's current volume, and move toward direct farm sourcing as demand grows.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: A growing operation can separate delivery and design work, with different pay structures for each role.
- *mindset_execution*: A seasonal business can produce substantial revenue but still require concentrated, hands-on work.
- *operations*: A waitlist can help an operator decide whether extra inventory and route capacity can support additional orders.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not assume that an enquiry or a social media comment will turn into an order; Heather and Chris stress follow-up.
- Buying from grocery stores is described as non-scalable and unlikely to support good profit or variety at volume.
- The work is not passive; the owner needs to work the business to achieve the stated margin.
- Waiting until the week before Halloween can leave too little time to build the business and get orders.
