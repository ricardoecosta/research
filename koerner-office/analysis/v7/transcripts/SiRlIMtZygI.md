## Summary

John Rush describes building a portfolio of small software products and AI agents, with SEO Bot as a standout product and the portfolio generating around 3 million a year in combined revenue. He explains how he develops tools for his own use, validates demand before building, and grows products through SEO, social content, partnerships, directories, and cross-selling. ## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build from a problem you experience yourself** (validation_testing)
1. Notice a repetitive or frustrating task in your own work.
2. Build the smallest tool that solves it for you.
3. Use it yourself long enough to learn its weaknesses before offering it to others.
> "The best ideas are the ideas that scratch your own itch. So, basically, you're doing a job and then you have a problem at the job" — John Rush (~15:39)

**Validate search demand before building** (validation_testing)
1. Use Google autocomplete to find how people complete a query such as “website builder for”.
2. List roughly 50 to 60 candidate ideas in a spreadsheet.
3. Check keyword volume and competition, then compare related phrases for the same audience.
4. Use the results to decide whether a niche has enough demand and a reachable audience.
> "I usually play with it for an hour. I make a list in the Excel for like 50 or 60 ideas and then I bring them back into the keyword research tool" — John Rush (~18:41)

**Try to disprove the idea** (validation_testing)
1. Look for evidence that the idea should not be built.
2. Check whether search demand comes from the intended buyers.
3. Test one idea at a time and stop spending build time when the evidence is weak.
> "So basically when I build something I'm trying to convince myself that I shouldn't build it" — John Rush (~21:49)

**Use a waitlist as an early demand test** (validation_testing)
1. Build a simple landing page with a clear description and a name-and-email sign-up.
2. Show it to people in the target audience and track sign-ups.
3. Use the waitlist to learn whether people will take a small step toward the product, not as proof that they will buy.
> "So now I have my landing page. Show it to potential customers and see normally don't want to join weight lists because it's silly, right?" — John Rush (~23:46)

**Ask for pre-orders to check willingness to pay** (pricing)
1. Offer a pre-booking at a discount before building the full product.
2. Track how many waitlist users pay, not just how many sign up.
3. Ask users who decline whether the issue is price, recurring billing, or the audience being wrong.
> "I give people 70% or 90% discount if they buy now. And surprisingly I had cases where a lot of people signed up for a tool" — John Rush (~29:52)

**Keep a conversation with prospective customers** (customer_acquisition)
1. Collect sign-ups through a waitlist and follow up by email.
2. Ask for a pre-booking and invite a reply if the price or billing model does not fit.
3. Use the replies to distinguish a pricing issue from a customer-selection problem.
> "if you get them into your weight And then after a while you send them the payment then you're sending it through the email. So you have the the thread on the email with them" — John Rush (~33:24)

**Start SEO early on products with a long runway** (customer_acquisition)
1. Publish SEO content while developing the product if time allows.
2. Return after a few months to compare which pages receive traffic.
3. Use page-level traffic as another signal of whether a niche can be reached.
> "In my case, since I have so many products and they're all not pressing me on on time like I have all all the time in the world, I usually start SEO work on a day one." — John Rush (~35:00)

**Use social media to earn indirect distribution** (customer_acquisition)
1. Publish useful content that can reach people beyond existing followers.
2. Look for mentions, blog posts, newsletters, directories, and backlinks that result from the attention.
3. Continue social posting if the founder has a repeatable way to create content that the niche responds to.
> "Social media is the best way to grow for small products. Right now the best part about social media is that it's not just the people you bring directly from social media" — John Rush (~41:49)

**Offer generous affiliate commissions** (customer_acquisition)
1. Set up an affiliate portal where partners can get unique links and codes.
2. Use a high commission when product costs allow it.
3. Give partners a simple way to promote the product and earn from referred customers.
> "You should be really really generous on the commission. Like a lot of people have this like 10% or 15%. You should be crazy generous." — John Rush (~43:52)

**Seed products where their audience already looks** (customer_acquisition)
1. List relevant directories, launchpads, forums, Reddit threads, and Quora discussions.
2. Share the product where it directly answers a relevant conversation.
3. Treat this as both customer discovery and a way to improve the chance that search and AI systems encounter the product.
> "There has to be everywhere on directories, launchpads, forums, Reddits and social media." — John Rush (~44:30)

**Use a free tool to introduce a paid product** (customer_acquisition)
1. Build a small free tool for a search query that the target customer cares about.
2. Offer the output through a clear sign-up flow.
3. Use the delivery message to mention a related paid product.
> "And they would go there, they would generate their album, they would enter their email to receive the PNG in a good quality." — John Rush (~46:05)

**Build an agent for yourself before selling it** (product)
1. Run the agent internally and use it on real work.
2. Let it ask questions when it is uncertain and provide feedback.
3. Release it to friends and then the public once it is stable and the operator understands its limitations.
> "they all start as my internal agent and I run them for a long while so that I'm happy with them myself" — John Rush (~3:11)

**Design billing around the value customers perceive** (pricing)
1. Ask whether customers see the work as a one-time service or continuing value.
2. If they reject recurring billing, test one-off pricing.
3. If they reject a large lifetime payment, test a lower monthly option.
> "people said that why should I pay every month? You just kind of do the work once for me and that's it." — John Rush (~32:25)

**Cross-sell products across a portfolio** (customer_acquisition)
1. Make related products easy to find from each other.
2. Offer a relevant next tool after a customer has tried one product.
3. Track whether existing users move into other products and remain in the product ecosystem.
> "when I land one user in any of my tool, like I have some tools that are making like 1K a month, like very small tools" — John Rush (~11:01)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Start with a problem you experience yourself, then use the product enough to understand its real strengths and weaknesses.
- *validation_testing*: Test demand with search data, but check whether the people searching are the same people who would buy.
- *validation_testing*: Try to disprove an idea before spending substantial time building it.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Waitlist sign-ups indicate interest, but asking for payment is a stronger test of demand and audience fit.
- *pricing*: Ask prospective customers what feels wrong about price or billing rather than guessing from a failed purchase.
- *customer_acquisition*: Treat SEO as a compounding channel and start early when the product can wait for results.
- *automation_ai*: Make the agent useful to busy customers by having it do the work and ask for judgment only when needed.
- *other*: Build and test an agent internally before exposing it to a large customer base.
- *pricing*: Choose a billing model that matches how often customers believe the product provides value.
- *operations*: For low-cost software, show customers visible progress while they wait for longer-term outcomes such as SEO traffic.
- *niche_selection*: A small niche can be attractive when larger funded companies ignore it, even if it cannot support a venture-scale market.
- *customer_acquisition*: A focused creator or partner can make a tool more distinctive when similar software is easy to build.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The guest says customers often expect SEO results quickly, even though search traffic can take weeks or longer to appear.
- A large waitlist may consist of people who are not the right audience or do not intend to pay.
- Building a subscription around one-time work can conflict with customers' expectations.
- Too many customers can create support work and a legacy burden for a small product portfolio.
