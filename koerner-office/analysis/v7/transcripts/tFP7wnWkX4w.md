## Summary

Billy Howell, a self-taught vibe coder and former defense contractor, explains how he runs an AI app agency (Stupid Simple Apps) with a team of four, building small custom internal apps for business owners using Replit, v0, Cursor and similar tools. He describes landing his first app job on Upwork by building a demo and undercutting a no-code stack, and gives detailed Upwork tips (Looms, connects, client vetting). He advises against starting with consumer apps, shares side-project ideas (recipe generator, plant time-lapse), and discusses shiny-object syndrome and building in public for inbound. Key takeaway: sell niche B2B custom apps to paying clients first, using fast AI-built demos and relationship-based sales.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Demo-first selling with AI prototyping** (sales)
1. Read the client's Upwork post
2. Build a clickable prototype in v0 or Replit within minutes
3. Record a Loom comparing the custom app to the no-code alternative with costs
4. Offer a low monthly run fee below the alternative's cost
> "get good at making like a something that's like a clickable interface that brings their idea to life and if you can do that in five minutes I think it's definitely worth doing it to make the sale" — Billy Howell (~4:12)

**Upwork profile and proposal playbook** (acquisition)
1. Use a professional shoulders-up headshot
2. List a major metro as location instead of an obscure suburb
3. Check new job posts regularly because timing matters
4. Record a Loom showing your face with a tight proposal
5. Show past work and reassure on location/trust
6. Spend connects to rank at top every time

**Vet Upwork clients** (sales)
1. Check total spend (at least a couple thousand dollars)
2. Check star rating and hiring history
3. Check past hourly rates paid
4. Read the post tone for defensive or tire-kicker signals

**Small troubleshooting jobs to enter** (acquisition)
1. Search for broken Make.com or GoHighLevel automations
2. Take small-scope jobs big agencies skip
3. Convert one-off audits into recurring consulting calls

**Research demand using Upwork from both sides** (acquisition)
1. List industries and contacts you know
2. Search workflow keywords on Upwork client and freelancer sides
3. See what packages freelancers sell
4. Pitch the validated service directly to your contacts

**Build in public and capture inbound** (acquisition)
1. Record yourself building client or personal projects
2. Post to Twitter and YouTube consistently
3. Set up an agency landing page and paid Calendly in advance
4. Share value openly

**Finish the last 10% with AI** (operations)
1. Pull code into Cursor or Windsurf
2. Ask the agent to review each page for security
3. Refactor step by step
4. Keep scope small so there are few edge cases

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Start with paying B2B clients rather than consumer apps; client money and feedback tells you what to build.
- *niche_selection*: Niche B2B tools used by one or two people avoid scale and edge-case complexity, so finishing is easier.
- *pricing*: Undercut a recurring-cost alternative with a cheaper custom build and a small monthly fee to win the sale.
- *offer_design*: Pick one AI stack and become known for it; reuse components and sell customised variants.
- *mindset_execution*: AI prototyping lets you scratch shiny-object itches in about 45 minutes instead of weeks, so use time-boxed constraints.
- *mindset_execution*: Focus filter: are people paying or trying to pay me for this? If not, limit time.
- *customer_acquisition*: Prepare distribution assets (landing page, paid calendar) before a viral moment so you can capitalise; years of posting before anything stuck.
- *scaling*: Consulting sells decisions made for time-poor people and scales better than building projects.
- *automation_ai*: Big SaaS platforms with open APIs may be displaced by cheap custom headless apps built on top.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Don't start with direct-to-consumer apps as your first app; it is a money pit without paying customers to guide features.
- The last 10% of an AI-built app takes longer than the first 90%, and even good devs prefer rebuilding from scratch.
- Upwork applications are flooded with bots; clients may filter AI-looking proposals.
- Standing up an agency quickly can exhaust bandwidth; hire early or face a deliverable treadmill.
- Offshore hires can be poor; hiring is a muscle to train.
- Don't say yes to implementation projects you lack capacity for; stick to consulting if that scales better.
