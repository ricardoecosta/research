## Tactics and playbook steps

**Research for timeless content formats** (customer_acquisition)
1. Spend substantial time reviewing niche content across Instagram, Reddit, and TikTok.
2. Review both video and text rather than limiting research to one format.
3. Save examples that catch your attention and investigate what makes them work.
4. Look for recurring audience interests and questions that can support repeatable content.
> "And I call it content thrifting, basically as you scroll and go through all of this content, you're just accumulating a bunch of stuff that grabs your attention" — Roman (~6:35)

**Repeat formats that convert** (customer_acquisition)
1. Find a format that matches a durable audience desire, such as remote and worldwide job listings.
2. Use a clear hook, list the companies that fit the promise, and end with a call to visit the website.
3. Repeat the format while it continues to work, instead of chasing every short-lived trend.
> "In my niche, people will always want to find companies hiring remotely and worldwide. So I can keep running this list over and over and over again." — Roman (~8:20)

**Give creators simple, specific briefs** (operations)
1. Put repeatable content formats and instructions in a sheet.
2. Review each brief for points that could confuse a creator.
3. Reduce research and production work so a first-time creator can follow the format.
> "It's zero research. All they have to do is open up their laptop and there's a sheet of formats perfectly instructed." — Roman (~9:51)

**Source creators through marketplaces and existing content** (hiring)
1. Try creator marketplaces as a source.
2. Scroll through platforms and look for people already making content for fun.
3. Offer paid content work and keep video costs low; Roman recommends a ceiling of $20 in most situations.
4. Choose creators whose apparent credibility fits the audience and topic.
> "But most of my creators, I find just by DMing people. They scroll, scroll, and scroll, and I find people who are already making content just for fun." — Roman (~9:25)

**Use existing spend and app demand to find product directions** (validation_testing)
1. Use Side Shift to see what companies are hiring creators.
2. Treat companies paying for creator work as a signal that they have a budget and are making money.
3. Use Sensor Tower to find apps getting downloads.
4. Choose a product direction you personally enjoy, then use existing content as marketing inspiration.
> "One creative way to source product ideas is to sign up on side shift, which is a creator marketplace." — Roman (~10:52)

**Run frequent marketing experiments and learn from failures** (validation_testing)
1. Run marketing experiments frequently.
2. Record what happened in each experiment, including failed attempts.
3. Extract a lesson from each result and use it to choose the next experiment.
> "So you wanna strive for failed results because this way you can eliminate what doesn't work and get closer to the thing that's gonna work." — Roman (~11:32)

**Start with a hard paywall** (pricing)
1. Offer paid weekly, monthly, and annual subscriptions.
2. Do not offer a free plan, according to Roman's current approach.
> "So I'm a strong believer in a hard paywall, so no free plans. Our users pay $7 a week, 20 a month, or 50 a year." — Roman (~1:55)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Start by understanding the audience's recurring questions and problems, then make content that addresses them.
- *customer_acquisition*: Build distribution around enduring audience interests; trend-only content creates a constant production chase.
- *customer_acquisition*: Research across platforms and formats to notice patterns other marketers may miss.
- *customer_acquisition*: Once a content format converts, repeat it while the audience continues to want the outcome it promises.
- *operations*: Match creator credibility to the audience and make the content brief clear enough for inexperienced creators to execute.
- *validation_testing*: Use real market signals, such as companies paying for creator work or apps gaining downloads, to choose product directions.
- *validation_testing*: Treat failed marketing experiments as useful information and change the next attempt based on what you learn.
- *offer_design*: A simple product can compete when its distribution and marketing are strong.
- *mindset_execution*: Keep faith during the early stage and build a consistent habit of testing and learning.
