## Tactics and playbook steps

**Find and assess school liquidation inventory** (acquisition)
1. Search GovDeals and Public Surplus for school book, classroom material, learning material, and curriculum lots.
2. Set a practical local pickup radius; Mindy says she often uses 250 miles.
3. Study listing photos for visible, complete curriculum sets and familiar publishers.
4. Use ChatGPT to identify titles in listing photos, then check eBay current and sold listings before bidding.
5. Ask the auction seller about grade level, contents, and whether materials are teaching resources or reading books.
> "I think that you really can do your due diligence in getting a decent idea of your profit margins on a book purchase." — Mindy (~44:58)

**Start with donated or inexpensive books** (validation_testing)
1. Post in a local community page asking whether anyone has curriculum or books to give away.
2. Alternatively, buy a small amount from a thrift store or a low-cost local auction.
3. Test the sorting, listing, and shipping workflow before investing in a larger lot.
> "And start by spending nothing and just see how it goes." — Mindy (~46:14)

**Use ChatGPT to prepare book bundles and listings** (operations)
1. Arrange books so the spines are visible and photograph boxes in batches.
2. Ask ChatGPT for a master manifest with box number, title, author, and reading level.
3. Ask it to create bundles by reading level or genre and produce a pick list.
4. Use the pick list to retrieve the books for each listing, then rubber-band the bundle.
5. Use AI-generated listing text as a draft and verify prices against eBay listings and sold items.
> "I uploaded all of those pictures into ChatGPT and I gave it all of these metrics. I want you to create a master manifest." — Mindy (~12:21)

**List consistently in small batches** (operations)
1. Photograph and process a batch of books during available work time.
2. Create a few eBay listings at a time instead of waiting to prepare the entire inventory.
3. Post listings consistently across successive days or evenings.
4. Use a simple clean photo setup with a white poster board and a folding table.
> "I would do those batches, and then, you know, I'd throw three listings up, and then the next night I would do the next three" — Mindy (~14:30)

**Prioritise curriculum and bundle formats** (product)
1. Prioritise curriculum sets because the guest says they were the most valuable part of her pallets.
2. Avoid spending time on low-value single books if the expected margin is poor.
3. Bundle books by reading level, genre, or classroom use; consider classroom quantities and complete sets.
4. Donate unwanted inventory to a library rather than spending time listing every item.
> "the gold in there for me was the curriculum." — Mindy (~6:56)

**Use free shipping on eBay listings** (pricing)
1. Mindy says she used free shipping on every listing.
2. Include the expected postage cost in the total pricing decision because the seller still pays to ship.
> "And that's something else that I did is free shipping on every single one of my listings." — Mindy (~15:14)

**Avoid pricing from AI alone** (pricing)
1. Use ChatGPT to identify items and find possible value signals.
2. Check current eBay listings and sold listings before setting a price.
3. When sold comps are sparse, compare current listings and use experience with the category.
> "the pricing that Chat GPT gives you is sometimes pretty off. And so I always ask it" — Mindy (~29:23)

**Configure Whatnot shipping before live selling** (operations)
1. Watch shipping tutorials before starting live shows.
2. Understand the Whatnot shipping settings and confirm them before listing or selling.
3. Account for the risk of absorbing shipping costs if the configuration is wrong.
> "So, I spent more hours than I would like to admit watching YouTube tutorials on the shipping." — Mindy (~45:36)

**Learn the live-selling format before scaling** (validation_testing)
1. Watch a range of Whatnot shows to learn the platform and see different seller styles.
2. Run a small number of shows and observe which items move and who the buyers are.
3. Mindy says she is still learning after six shows and has not yet decided whether to continue with high-end designer clothing.
> "I've done six shows. Like, I'm by no means like established on there." — Mindy (~36:58)

## Lessons

- *automation_ai*: Use AI to reduce repetitive identification and listing work, while keeping human review for valuation and final decisions.
- *validation_testing*: Check what a lot may contain and what comparable items sell for before bidding, even when the entry cost is low.
- *validation_testing*: A low-cost starting point can make it practical to test a resale workflow before committing substantial capital.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Choose inventory based on the time it takes to list and fulfil it, not only on whether it can be sold.
- *customer_acquisition*: Consistent small batches of listings can build a store over time and, according to the guest, align with eBay's preference for regular posting.
- *pricing*: Use marketplace data and experience to set prices; AI can help identify value but should not be the sole pricing source.
- *operations*: Before starting live sales, learn the shipping configuration because shipping mistakes can erase the economics of a sale.
- *mindset_execution*: A seller does not need one fixed on-camera personality; the guest saw a wide range of formats and comfort levels on Whatnot.
- *finance_unit_economics*: A first business can fund experiments in another category, which can limit how much new capital is at risk.
- *operations*: Work can fit around limited available time when the operator uses repeatable batches and keeps the process simple.
- *offer_design*: A resale business can create value for more than one party when surplus inventory is redirected from disposal to buyers who need it.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Separate revenue from profit and account for platform fees, postage, labour, and transport when evaluating a deal.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Unknown auction contents can include items with little resale value or incomplete sets; inspect photos and ask questions before bidding.
- ChatGPT's price suggestions may be inaccurate; verify prices against marketplace listings and sold data.
- Incorrect Whatnot shipping settings can quickly make a seller lose money.
- Headline revenue is not net profit; platform fees and shipping reduce the proceeds.
- Book sorting and resale involve physical work, including lifting boxes and transporting pallets.
- Low-value single books may not be worth the listing and fulfilment time; Mindy donated most chapter books.
- Live shopping can encourage buyers to bid beyond what they intended to spend.
- Mindy's $30,000 clothing figure is a scenario assuming every item sells for $29, not an achieved result.
