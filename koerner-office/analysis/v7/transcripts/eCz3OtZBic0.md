## Tactics and playbook steps

**Learn the work by doing it** (operations)
1. Start by understanding the actual customer work and the decisions staff make.
2. At Phone Restore, Chris accepted repair jobs and learned from YouTube when the customer left.
3. For tree trimming, learn how to quote jobs, talk to subcontractors, and set target gross margins.
4. Use that knowledge to delegate later rather than staying in every task forever.
> "You don't need to stay in the weeds, but you got to start in the weeds. For instance, in my we have we own a tree trimming business." — Chris Koerner (~25:24)

**Use a specific low-cost local promotion** (acquisition)
1. Identify where the target customers gather.
2. Put the repair-shop advertisement on custom wristbands costing two cents each.
3. Distribute them to bars so students see the offer when they wake up with a broken phone.
> "I Googled custom wristbands. They were 2 cents each. I ordered a few boxes of them. I distributed them to bars." — Chris Koerner (~3:58)

**Test a new location with a short commitment** (validation_testing)
1. Notice foot traffic that could support a new format.
2. Test a mall kiosk rather than assuming it will work.
3. Use a month-to-month lease so the location can be cancelled if demand is insufficient.
> "You know it's monthto month so if it doesn't work out we can cancel it. So crazy story." — Chris Koerner (~5:26)

**Turn stranded inventory into cash during a disruption** (finance_unit_economics)
1. When the store lost revenue after the tornado, look for inventory that could be sold through another channel.
2. List the customer buy-back iPhones that had not yet been posted on eBay.
> "And we had a bunch of iPhones that we had bought back from customers that we had never listed on eBay. So, we thought, "Okay, let's list those on eBay."" — Chris Koerner (~3:19)

**Build an online sales channel on a low-risk revenue share** (offer_design)
1. Find a good product that is not available online.
2. Offer to create and fund the online sales channel.
3. Agree to receive a cut of online sales if the channel works, with no charge to the supplier if it fails.
> "I'll do everything. I'll pay for it. If it works, give me a cut of everything I sell online. If it doesn't, no harm, no foul." — Chris Koerner (~13:39)

**Check whether growth is profitable** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Separate top-line revenue from profit when evaluating a business.
2. Include warehousing, labour, and customer concentration in the operating picture.
3. Do not let a large monthly revenue figure substitute for margin analysis.
> "We were making 300 grand a month. Asterisk topline, not profitable. Didn't know it at the time." — Chris Koerner (~15:40)

**Reduce customer concentration by winning more accounts** (customer_acquisition)
1. Keep serving a large customer well rather than firing it solely because it is a large share of revenue.
2. Continue acquiring other customers to reduce the concentration over time.
3. Check whether the account is profitable and whether its behaviour is damaging the team.
> "You just take great care of the customer and you try to get more to drop the 85%." — Chris Koerner (~18:04)

**Do partner diligence before committing** (hiring_outsourcing)
1. Check a prospective partner's character and history as well as their industry knowledge.
2. Investigate whether they have a pattern of pushing partners out.
3. Use calculated pressure and deliberate diligence before moving quickly into a deal.
> "I knew that they knew the industry. I didn't know their character. Looking back, I'm grateful for that experience." — Chris Koerner (~23:07)

**Protect strong employees and company culture** (hiring_outsourcing)
1. Address a toxic employee when they are making strong contributors leave.
2. Check in with high-performing managers and employees instead of assuming they will remain engaged.
3. Proactively recognize and reward strong employees before they ask for a raise.
> "If your rockstar employee has asked you for a raise, you're probably too late. Your rockstar employee should not have to come to you." — Chris Koerner (~20:22)

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: Develop the habit of finding a workable next step when a problem appears, rather than stopping at 'I can't do it.'
- *operations*: Learn the operational details before delegating, then step out of the work so the business can scale.
- *validation_testing*: A small, specific experiment can reveal a customer acquisition channel that desk research would miss.
- *operations*: A CEO should absorb difficult business problems and protect employees from avoidable stress.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Capital helps a business grow, but the terms and the source of advice matter; seek advice from people who have your interests in mind.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Distinguish revenue from profit and inspect margins before treating growth as success.
