## Tactics and playbook steps

**Use an AI planner to pick and scope the idea before building** (product)
1. Ask the AI to research the current app market and propose simple web app ideas with clear problem and real pain demand
2. Require ideas to be realistic as a proof of concept without a complicated back end
3. Ask it to explain why each has potential and which is strongest
4. Choose a small-scope idea with a clear target user and a reason to pay
> "find a few simple web app ideas that solve a clear problem, have real pain demand, and are realistic enough to build as a proof of concept" — Mikey (~2:40)

**Phased prompt plan for an AI app builder** (product)
1. Have the planner write three detailed plain-language prompts, one per phase
2. Each prompt states what to build, what stays unchanged, and what not to add yet
3. Phase 1: core editor with local storage; Phase 2: brand kit, presets, project library; Phase 3: export and backup/restore
4. Paste one prompt at a time into the builder and test each phase before moving on
> "Each prompt needs to build on what already exists, of course, rather than say repeating all of the same work over again" — Mikey (~4:43)

**Test each phase with a concrete checklist** (operations)
1. Create, edit, duplicate, delete slides and reload to confirm autosave
2. Apply presets, set a brand kit, rename and duplicate projects
3. Export PDF and PNG, download backup, delete project, re-import to confirm restore

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: With AI builders the bottleneck is decision quality and scope before prompting, not generation speed.
- *offer_design*: Ship a narrow proof of concept with a clearly defined user and no backend to cut complexity.
- *operations*: Break builds into phases with explicit exclusions so features do not creep in early and each step can be tested.
- *automation_ai*: Use one AI to plan and another to execute; separating research/planning from building reduces guesswork.
- *validation_testing*: AI-suggested demand is not validation; real willingness to pay still needs testing with actual consultants before investing more.
- *niche_selection*: Client-side-only apps (local storage, in-browser export) avoid hosting and data-protection overhead but limit monetisation (no accounts) until a backend is added.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Vague or overly broad prompts force the AI to make many guesses that stack up; the failure happens before the builder opens.
- A good builder cannot fix an idea that is too broad or that nobody would pay for.
- Many people build basic apps that make no money or sites that do not convert (said in a course promotion).
