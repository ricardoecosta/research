## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build a targeted owner list from Google Maps** (acquisition)
1. Choose an exact Google Maps business category, such as RV parks, and a target state, city or ZIP code.
2. Set a results cap before scraping to control spend.
3. Export the park name, phone, address, category, rating, review count, photos and owner-verification status.
4. Avoid unnecessary columns and filter out mismatched categories such as RV dealerships.
> "And then it's probably a good idea to cap your results just so you don't accidentally spend a bunch of money." — Chris Koerner (~2:45)

**Clean and validate phone data before outreach** (operations)
1. Standardize phone numbers in the spreadsheet, including removing the country prefix and dashes where needed.
2. Remove duplicate phone numbers before matching validation results back to the list.
3. Upload only phone numbers to Sly Broadcast's validation list because the host says extra columns and blanks can cause issues.
4. Compare the returned list order and counts before using it; remove landlines and retain the mobile numbers.
> "Remove the duplicates by phone. But this this is tricky though because it it's going to delete all of these ones that don't have phone numbers as well." — Chris Koerner (~9:22)

**Keep first contact short and conversational** (sales)
1. Use a city or street name as a personalization field, after checking the data for missing or odd characters.
2. Use a broad term such as 'park' when the listing category may be wrong or could mean an RV or mobile home park.
3. Keep the text under 160 characters where possible; the host says longer messages use two credits.
4. Ask to start a conversation instead of asking immediately whether the owner wants to sell.
5. Preview personalized messages before sending and avoid automated replies unless someone can pause campaigns and monitor them.
> "The best results come when you're willing to play the long game and just seek to start a conversation on the first message as opposed to ask them right up front if they're interested in selling." — Chris Koerner (~15:09)

**Research and qualify interested owners by phone** (sales)
1. Move promising replies into a CRM or spreadsheet so conversations do not get lost in a clunky texting platform.
2. Look up the property website, reviews, social media, site layout and utilities before calling.
3. Introduce who you are and why you are interested, then ask how long they have owned or operated the park.
4. Ask about occupancy, site count, included utilities, expansion potential and land ownership.
5. Build credibility before asking for detailed financial information.
> "I like to do a little more research before I call them so I don't sound like an idiot." — Chris Koerner (~17:37)

**Use a cap-rate screen and identify deal levers** (pricing)
1. Estimate annual net operating income and divide it by the potential purchase price to calculate the cap rate.
2. Use the host's stated 10% minimum as his own screen, not as a universal market rule.
3. Adjust the price if rents are below market and can fairly be raised, or if improvements such as paved roads could add value.
4. Ask about utilities, site count, occupancy, seasonality and land before deciding whether the target price is realistic.
> "So, I try to buy at at a bare minimum 10% cap rate. So, you take your net operating income for the year, divide it by your potential purchase price." — Chris Koerner (~21:52)

**Treat a seller's no as a possible future lead** (sales)
1. If an owner says no, leave the conversation open and invite them to get back in touch if their situation changes.
2. Keep the response polite rather than pressing the seller.
> "So, I could say, "Let me know if that changes cuz even like they said no, they didn't ghost me."" — Chris Koerner (~17:24)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Use lead volume to increase the number of opportunities, but qualify the list before spending time on calls.
- *customer_acquisition*: A first message can aim to start a conversation rather than ask for a sale immediately.
- *offer_design*: Reduce friction in outreach by keeping messages short and checking personalization before sending.
- *operations*: Treat data cleaning as part of the acquisition process: duplicates, blanks and mismatched rows can corrupt outreach.
- *sales*: Research the property before calling so that the conversation can be specific and the buyer sounds prepared.
- *validation_testing*: Assess the owner and the property's operating facts before discussing a purchase price.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Use income and purchase price to calculate a cap rate, then consider property-specific levers such as below-market rents and road improvements.
- *customer_acquisition*: A seller who declines now may still be worth keeping in contact with if their circumstances change.
- *automation_ai*: Use automation for repetitive data work, but keep human oversight for nuanced owner conversations and property decisions.
- *legal_risk*: Check whether the contact method is lawful in the market where the business will operate before copying an outreach playbook.

## Warnings and mistakes

- For UK use, unsolicited marketing SMS or WhatsApp messages to individuals and sole traders generally require consent under PECR. The demonstrated bulk-text approach cannot simply be copied; cold calls must be screened against TPS/CTPS.
- The host cautions that automated replies can keep running after a campaign and send inappropriate messages if they are not paused.
- Scraped owner names may be missing or inaccurate, and some phone numbers may reach a reservation desk or property manager rather than the owner.
- Duplicate phone records can break the alignment between validated numbers and the original spreadsheet; the host says duplicates can really screw up the process.
- Some map listings may not match the category searched, and unverified listings may have less complete data.
- The owner says the campground is seasonal, uses septic and is in a low-water area; new expansion sites also lack the mature tree cover of the existing sites.
- The example shows outreach and possible leads, not a verified acquisition or realized return.
- The Beehiiv segment is a promotional read with a referral discount, so its platform claims come from an endorsed promotion.
