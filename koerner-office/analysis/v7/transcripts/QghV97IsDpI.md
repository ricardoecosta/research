## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build a bounded Google Maps lead list** (acquisition)
1. Choose a Google Maps business category such as RV park and select a target state.
2. Set a result cap before scraping to limit accidental spend.
3. Select useful fields such as phone, address, category, rating, review count, photos, owner verification, and phone carrier type.
4. Review categories after export because the scraper can include adjacent business types.
> "And then it's probably a good idea to cap your results just so you don't accidentally spend a bunch of money. I capped it at 500." — Chris Koerner (~2:45)

**Clean and validate phone data before outreach** (operations)
1. Standardize phone formatting in the spreadsheet.
2. Remove duplicate phone numbers before matching validation results back to the list.
3. Upload only rows with phone numbers to Sly Broadcast for carrier-type validation.
4. Check that the validated numbers remain aligned with the correct listings.
> "And so, I'm just going to kind of do a cursory glance. Oh, and they're not. Oh, okay. Yep. There's duplicates. That's why." — Chris Koerner (~9:11)

**Personalize the first text and keep it short** (sales)
1. Use a reliable field such as city for personalization when street names may be missing or malformed.
2. Preview the text to catch bad substitutions.
3. Use a broad term such as “park” if the exact listing might be an RV park or mobile home park.
4. Start a conversation instead of asking immediately whether the owner wants to sell.
> "The best results come when you're willing to play the long game and just seek to start a conversation on the first message as opposed to ask them right up front if they're interested in selling." — Chris Koerner (~15:09)

**Use automation sparingly in seller conversations** (operations)
1. Prefer direct, high-touch replies for a small list of likely sellers.
2. If automated replies are used for a larger campaign, pause them before sending another campaign.
3. Move promising conversations to a phone call after researching the property.
> "But like I don't recommend that because I don't think anyone's trying to buy 10 RV parks a month here." — Chris Koerner (~13:00)

**Screen a park before discussing an offer** (sales)
1. Ask how long the owner has had the property and whether they built or bought it.
2. Ask about occupancy, seasonal pricing, included utilities, number of pads, and operating season.
3. Ask about land area, expansion plans, utilities, and physical condition.
4. Estimate net operating income and compare it with a target cap rate before proposing a price.
> "Well, how long have you owned it? Did you build it or did you buy it?" — Chris Koerner (~19:09)

**Set a purchase threshold before negotiating** (pricing)
1. Calculate annual net operating income.
2. Divide net operating income by the prospective purchase price to calculate the cap rate.
3. Use a stated minimum target of 10% as an initial screen, then adjust for rents below market or other value levers.
> "So, I try to buy at at a bare minimum 10% cap rate. So, you take your net operating income for the year, divide it by your potential purchase price." — Chris Koerner (~21:52)

## Lessons

- *pricing*: Set a target purchase price before a seller call so the conversation can focus on facts that change the valuation.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use a controlled, relevant lead list and check the data before contacting owners; bad categories and duplicate records waste outreach.
- *offer_design*: Begin outreach by starting a conversation rather than asking for a sale immediately.
- *customer_acquisition*: Keep messages brief and check personalized fields before sending so each recipient sees a credible message.
- *operations*: Use human follow-up when a seller shows interest; the host prefers higher-touch handling for a small list.
- *validation_testing*: Assess the physical property and its operating facts, not just the listing: occupancy, rents, utilities, season length, and expansion needs all affect value.
- *finance_unit_economics*: More leads can create more chances to find unusual financing, but the transcript does not establish that no-money-down financing is typical or assured.
- *customer_acquisition*: Treat a refusal as a possible future relationship rather than ending the conversation abruptly.
- *automation_ai*: Automate repetitive data work, but keep checks around data alignment and conversation quality.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Duplicate phone rows can cause validation results to be matched to the wrong listings. The host removes duplicates and checks that the records align.
- Automated replies can keep running into a later campaign and send inappropriate or confusing messages if they are not paused.
- The host says listing data can include the wrong business category and owner names may be missing or inaccurate.
- The scrape estimate is described as inaccurate, so the host recommends setting a result cap to avoid unexpected spend.
- The demonstrated cold SMS process needs UK legal adaptation: unsolicited marketing texts to individuals and sole traders require consent under PECR.
