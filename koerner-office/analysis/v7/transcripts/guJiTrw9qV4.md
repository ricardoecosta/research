## Tactics and playbook steps

**Randomize a small initial audience** (validation_testing)
1. Put the candidate email contacts into Google Sheets.
2. Use the RAND formula to assign a random number to each row.
3. Sort by the random number and select a limited group for the initial campaign.
4. Export the selected contacts as a CSV.
> "So, I'm going to use this formula in Google Sheets, equals rand open parentheses close parentheses, that assigns a perfectly randomized number to that row." — Chris Koerner (~1:30)

**Connect an AI agent to the email platform** (operations)
1. Add Omnisend as a custom connector in Claude using the MCP URL shown in the video.
2. Authorize the connection and import the selected CSV into an Omnisend segment.
3. Ask Claude to draft the email and create or send the campaign through the connector.
4. Review permission requests and campaign content before allowing actions.
> "This removes the need for me to log in to Omnisend at all, and I can still use it and enjoy all the features of it." — Chris Koerner (~5:09)

**Create a paid short-consult booking link** (offer_design)
1. Set up a five-minute TidyCal booking page.
2. Connect Stripe and charge $5 per booking.
3. Sync the link to the calendar to avoid double-booking.
4. Limit the available times to selected weekdays and hours.
> "Duration of the meeting is 5 minutes. We're going to go to pricing. Charge $5 per booking. I integrated Stripe just with a couple clicks." — Chris Koerner (~4:00)

**Keep the initial campaign plain and direct** (customer_acquisition)
1. Send a plain-text email with one clear booking link.
2. Use a short subject line; Koerner chose "$5".
3. Check that the booking link works before sending.
4. Make the payment requirement and the paid route explicit so email replies are not mistaken for free advice.
> "I just want this to be plain text, one link for them to click on where they can buy, and I'm going to tell it what subject line to use." — Chris Koerner (~6:33)

**Review campaign results with connected data** (operations)
1. Ask Claude to summarize the Omnisend campaign.
2. Connect Stripe data so the agent can compare clicks, buyers, and revenue.
3. Review sends, delivery, opens, clicks, replies, bookings, and revenue before deciding on a follow-up.
> "So, I also have Stripe connected through my Claude MCP, so it was able to cross-reference the clicks to the buyers and Stripe and put everything all in one chart." — Chris Koerner (~7:35)

**Follow up with interested prospects** (customer_acquisition)
1. Do not stop after the first email; send relevant follow-ups to people who may still be deciding.
2. Use the children's market example as a reminder that another vendor email led to four additional sign-ups.
3. Respect consent, unsubscribe requests, and UK PECR requirements when sending marketing follow-ups.
> "And what do you know? She sent another email, and she got four more sign-ups for the kids market." — Chris Koerner (~11:02)

**Improve the next email using observed weaknesses** (offer_design)
1. Add a reason to act now if one genuinely exists.
2. Use one clear paid path rather than mixing a paid booking link with a reply path that appears free.
3. Add relevant proof and make the offer more specific.
4. Use a clear, descriptive booking link and follow up.
5. Ask Claude to create drafts in the email platform, then review them before sending.
> "There was no reason to act now, no scarcity play. The free path ate the paid path. I guess it was confusing." — Chris Koerner (~10:06)

**Test higher pricing against the same audience** (pricing)
1. Compare the low-priced five-minute offer with a proposed $1,000 one-hour consultation.
2. Use the same email audience as a test, then measure whether any customer buys at the higher price.
3. Treat the outcome as unknown until the test is run.

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: A person can sell practical knowledge without a degree or impressive credentials if they find customers who want that knowledge.
- *pricing*: Price affects perceived quality as well as revenue; a very low price can make an offer seem less credible.
- *offer_design*: Make the paid route unambiguous. A free-looking reply option can divert customers from a paid booking.
- *customer_acquisition*: Follow-up can produce additional sign-ups, but marketing follow-ups must respect recipients' consent and preferences.
- *automation_ai*: An MCP connection lets an AI agent carry out work inside another service, including importing contacts and drafting campaign emails.
- *automation_ai*: Automating campaign operations does not automate the human expertise being sold; Koerner still has to deliver each consultation.
- *validation_testing*: Review campaign outcomes and improve the offer, proof, call to action, timing, and follow-up rather than assuming a first send is optimized.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Treat an attractive annualized calculation as a hypothesis when it depends on assumed hours, response rates, and prices.
- *operations*: Use AI to produce drafts inside the operating platform, then inspect those drafts before they are sent.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Koerner says Omnisend would not send until he had a verified sender. Email marketing requires proper sender verification and applicable legal compliance.
- Koerner granted the connector broad read and write permissions. That creates a risk if the AI account, connector, or data access is compromised or misused.
- The email's reply-by-email option confused recipients about whether they could get an answer for free, weakening the paid booking path.
- Koerner says the $5 price could make the offer seem like a joke or low quality.
- Koerner says the campaign lacked proof, testimonials, specificity, and follow-up, so the reported one-campaign results should not be treated as validated repeatable performance.
- In the UK, marketing emails to individuals and sole traders generally need consent unless an applicable exception applies; cold calls must be screened against TPS/CTPS.
- The campaign's reported response rates come from one email send; extrapolating them to a full list or a different price is not evidence that the projection will hold.
