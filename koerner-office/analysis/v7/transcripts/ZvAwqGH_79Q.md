## Tactics and playbook steps

**Choose one platform before expanding** (customer_acquisition)
1. Select one platform where the founder can produce content consistently.
2. Study what performs there and adapt to that platform's style and audience.
3. Expand to other platforms after establishing a repeatable approach.
> "Pick one platform, win on it, then expand it." — Codie Sanchez (~0:40)

**Build a swipe file and problem bank** (product)
1. Search the niche and filter to videos.
2. Save the top 100 posts with over a million views.
3. Record each thumbnail, transcribe its first 30 seconds, note its top comments, and identify sponsors.
4. Reuse proven hooks while adding original expertise; do not copy the content.
5. Write scripts before filming, and save audience questions in the audience's own words.
> "Then you screenshot the thumbnail, transcribe the first 30 seconds and log the top comments. And who's sponsoring them? That's your future revenue list." — Codie Sanchez (~18:18)

**Use unanswered audience questions for new content** (product)
1. Read comments under popular videos and look for unanswered questions.
2. Check relevant comment sections, subreddits, and Discords.
3. Turn a specific unanswered question into the next piece of content.
4. Keep a problem bank using the audience's own wording.
> "The lazy person copies the video on top, but I think the smart one actually reads the unanswered questions underneath, comment sections, sub-readets, discords." — Codie Sanchez (~18:45)

**Review content performance and iterate** (operations)
1. For each post, record why people started watching, where they left, and which line got a reaction.
2. Review winners and flops to identify topic, specificity, and hook patterns.
3. Use the relevant platform metrics: click-through and watch time on YouTube, shares and watch time on Instagram, replies on X, engagement on LinkedIn, and opens for newsletters.
4. Use the findings to revise future scripts and formats.
> "After posts, just three notes. Why did they start watching? Where did they leave? What line got a reaction?" — Codie Sanchez (~23:04)

**Build credibility without a studio** (offer_design)
1. Make content native to the platform's style, pacing, and editing conventions.
2. Explain things the creator has actually done and can discuss without preparation.
3. Show the work in context when possible, such as filming a real project on site.
> "So don't ask, what should I post? Ask, what have I earned the right to explain?" — Codie Sanchez (~14:14)

**Start with a small product ladder** (pricing)
1. Start with free content and a newsletter that captures the relationship.
2. Add one paid product, such as a guide or template.
3. Add higher-priced offers only when the offer below is full.
> "I like starting with free content and adding one paid step, a free newsletter that captures the relationship, plus a single low ticket guide as your first wrung" — Codie Sanchez (~25:26)

**Make the hook and value arrive early** (product)
1. Open with a counterintuitive hook that promises clear value.
2. Deliver the promised value quickly and avoid repetition.
3. Review retention to find where viewers leave and remove slow sections.
> "The hook should be counterintuitive, promise value. You've got to deliver that value right away and don't repeat yourself." — Codie Sanchez (~22:28)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Start with one platform and learn its audience and content style before expanding.
- *automation_ai*: Make content native to the platform instead of imposing one production style everywhere.
- *offer_design*: Build credibility from experience the creator can explain or show, rather than relying on a polished set.
- *niche_selection*: Use audience questions and wording to generate relevant content instead of inventing topics in isolation.
- *mindset_execution*: Treat performance data as feedback about audience interest, not as a judgment of personal worth.
- *validation_testing*: Review the hook, retention, and audience response so successful content can be repeated deliberately.
- *finance_unit_economics*: A large audience is not enough; platform reach and purchase behavior differ, so track the business result that matters.
- *offer_design*: Build a route from free value to offers that solve progressively deeper customer problems.
- *customer_acquisition*: A smaller, engaged newsletter audience may be more valuable to sponsors and sales than a larger inactive list.
- *mindset_execution*: Ship a useful version before perfection; adding the final polish can take time without being noticed by the audience.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not spread effort across many platforms by publishing a large volume of average content; the speaker recommends focusing on one platform first.
- Overproduced studio content may perform worse than simpler, platform-native content.
- Do not copy another creator's full content; borrow proven hooks and add original expertise.
- Do not rely only on ad revenue and brand deals; the speaker says her larger business revenue comes from other products and services.
- A newsletter with filler can train readers to stop opening.
- A newsletter promotion that only shows off a product without giving the reader something useful may feel selfish and fail to bridge customer and brand.
- For UK execution, do not automate unsolicited marketing emails, texts, WhatsApp messages, or direct messages to individuals and sole traders without consent; screen cold calls against TPS or CTPS.
