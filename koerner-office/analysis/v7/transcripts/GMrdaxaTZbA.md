UpFlip profiles Ashley and Thaddeus, a New Orleans couple who started selling dessert and soul food plates out of their car trunk in Dallas with their last $40 in 2018 and now run the Bell Sweet Factory food truck in Miami. ## Tactics and playbook steps

**Daily raw social posts to fill the truck** (acquisition)
1. Post a short unedited video every service day on TikTok and Instagram saying where you are and what is on the menu
2. Use TikTok for the younger crowd and Instagram for behind-the-scenes
3. Show mistakes and personality; be authentic
4. Don't stop posting for days: the algorithm forgets you and turnout drops
> "Keep it simple, write a couple of sentences" — Ashley (~15:02)

**Fight a bad review with proof** (acquisition)
1. When an influencer posts a hostile review, respond by posting good reviews and sold-out days
2. Ride the attention: followers and revenue grew despite negative coverage
> "For every bad one that she was trying to make out of it, I posted good ones" — Ashley (~14:13)

**Upsell the best seller** (sales)
1. Recommend the best seller (lamb chops) when customers ask
2. Suggest add-ons like crab meat and shrimp
3. Offer build-your-own loaded plates up to $100+
> "to upsell it, I'll tell them, add some crab meat, add some shrimp" — Ashley (~32:10)

**Bootstrap by flipping** (finance)
1. Spend the last $40 on dessert ingredients
2. Sell, then use the $100 to make 10 plates
3. Reinvest to 12, then 15 plates, add desserts and drinks
4. Save to $7,000 and buy a truck in cash
> "We kept flipping and flipping and flipping and flipping" — Thaddeus (~1:52)

**QR virtual queue** (operations)
1. Customers scan a QR at the window and enter name and phone
2. Staff dashboard shows the line
3. Automated texts when it is time to order and when food is ready

**Buy groceries daily, no storage** (operations)
1. Shop each service day for fresh ingredients
2. Prep at a commissary kitchen
3. Keep a backup kitchen in case the commissary falls through
> "Just always have two options because in business, you never know when a curve ball" — Ashley (~6:49)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Marketing comes before food prep: no customers, no money.
- *customer_acquisition*: Authenticity, including showing mistakes, drives social engagement more than polished content.
- *customer_acquisition*: A fixed location built a reliable brand; popularity eventually made property owners want the truck there.
- *mindset_execution*: Know your strengths: they were good at cooking but not at managing staff, so they returned to the truck.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Making money and keeping money are different; reinvest rather than splurge.
- *operations*: Always have a backup plan for critical resources like kitchens.
- *pricing*: Customers in good locations will pay premium prices for outstanding food.
- *automation_ai*: Ask customers who are technical to build tools for you; their QR system came from a customer.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Opening a brick-and-mortar restaurant failed: cooking is different from running a restaurant and managing staff.
- They didn't read the lease; a broken AC the landlord wouldn't fix forced a lawyer to get them out.
- Taking days off from posting reduced turnout.
- A hostile review from an influencer with many followers can damage reputation quickly.
- Their P&L excluded taxes and insurance details; margins quoted are incomplete.
- Lamb rack sizes vary, so the best seller's margin swings.
