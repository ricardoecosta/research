## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Alex Kirby, a former pastor and landscaping founder who sold his marketing agency and launched Bridgeway Global Staffing, which places vetted Latin American remote workers with US blue-collar small businesses. He claims about $100k in sales in the first 100 days from roughly 27-30 placements, charging a one-time fee of about 28% of first-year salary (roughly $4,500-5,200) plus an optional $200/month membership. The playbook covers getting first clients via texts to friends and network, sourcing and vetting talent through job boards and video applications, and a lean stack of QuickBooks plus a Lovable-built portal. Key takeaway: AI makes building easy, but distribution and proactive outreach remain the hard part.

### I1. Bridgeway Global Staffing (overseas talent placement for blue-collar businesses)

- **Customer:** US blue-collar small business owners (landscaping, fertilization, plumbers, electricians, land clearing), mostly first-time global hirers who are overworked and need admin, marketing or customer support help.
- **Problem:** Owners work 70-hour weeks, miss calls and do invoicing at night; local admin and support hires are expensive, unreliable or hard to find; owners are unfamiliar with hiring overseas.
- **Offer:** Find, vet and introduce about four strong candidates with video intros, help interview, run a roughly 1.5 hour onboarding with a recommended tech stack, and offer a replacement guarantee (a few months without membership, six months with). Optional membership gives support, two trainings a month, a resource library and 14% fees on later hires.
- **Business model:** One-time placement fee (about 28% of the worker's first-year salary) plus recurring $200/month membership; employer pays the worker directly via a payroll platform.
- **Pricing:** One-time placement fee plus monthly membership. Placement fee % 28 of first-year salary, one-time; Average ticket USD 4500-5200 per placement; Membership USD 200 per month; Member repeat-hire fee % 14 of first-year salary for later hires (members) Half paid up front and half at signing; early clients got discounts (one hire at half price) as proof of concept. Worker salaries cited around $1,200-1,600/month.
- **Acquisition:** existing_audience: Texted business-owner friends and contacts met via Instagram, X and Facebook, and posted videos announcing the service and showing his own overseas team.; referrals_word_of_mouth: Uses church (about 1,200 people) and local community as the first network; advises using any nucleus such as a church, Little League or club.; networking_events: Advises walking in and asking for meetings with local owners (e.g. HVAC) to pitch a beta offer.
- **Delivery:** Talent sourcing via LinkedIn ads, Facebook/Meta ads, job boards and sites like Upwork, focusing on Latin America. Candidates submit a two-minute video; Alex interviews and vets (English, degree, AI/marketing curiosity). Four candidates are shared via a Lovable-built portal; the client picks two to interview. Onboarding call about 1.5 hours, payment through Thera (or Deel/Gusto) monthly, and two membership training calls a month.
- **Unit economics (claims):** About $100k sales in the first 100 days from roughly 26-27 clients and about 30 placements; Average ticket about $4,500-5,200; 75% of clients take the $200/month membership, roughly $4-5k/month recurring; Tech stack cost about $100/month; Started with about $200
- **Tools:** Lovable, QuickBooks, Thera, Deel, Gusto, Upwork, LinkedIn ads, Meta ads, Slack, Canva, CapCut
- **Startup cost:** About $200 / about $100 per month tech stack (QuickBooks, Wi-Fi, computer)
- **Time to first revenue:** Built system in about 3 days; launched March 1 with a 90-day plan; $100k in first 100 days
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — Model works in principle with UK SMEs and overseas VAs, but needs changes: UK trades are the target, agency recruitment and worker-classification rules (IR35, employment status, right to work not an issue for overseas contractors but contract and tax compliance needed), GBP pricing, and US-specific '28% of salary' benchmarks. Cold outreach to sole traders by text or DM is restricted under PECR.
- **Risks:** Bad hires require replacement under the guarantee (one replaced after four days); Concentration on niche and referral-led growth may not scale; AI may reduce demand for admin and support roles; Misclassification and payroll or tax compliance if the agency drifts into employer-of-record territory; Reputational pushback on outsourcing jobs overseas; Claims are self-reported and early (about 100 days)
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 2/5 (Paid ads and content can be automated, but the actual first-client route is personal texts and network outreach, which is relationship-driven and PECR-restricted for sole traders in the UK.); onboarding 3/5 (A Lovable-built portal for applications and client intros can be mostly automated; onboarding calls and tech-stack advice need some human time or a recorded/agent-led version.); delivery 1/5 (Vetting overseas candidates by video, interviewing, judging confidence and fit and matching to clients is human judgement; AI can screen and shortlist but placement quality depends on a human.); billing 4/5 (QuickBooks or Stripe invoices with half-down deposits are easily automated.); support 3/5 (Membership support, training webinars and resource library can be mostly recorded and agent-handled, with human-led calls twice a month and replacement handling.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Three-sentence text to network for first customers** (acquisition)
1. List business-owner friends, family, church or club contacts
2. Send a short three-sentence text explaining the new service
3. Ask for a meeting or call, or to be a first customer
4. Swap the industry as needed and learn each owner's needs
5. Take a deposit (even $100) as proof of concept
> "You have to be willing to put ourselves out there either on Instagram or through text, through call" — Alex Kirby (~19:50)

**Place before you scale: take a deposit, then source talent** (sales)
1. Get a client willing to pay a deposit (half down, half at signing)
2. Use the commitment to justify sourcing
3. Do the first calls yourself to learn what a good employee looks like
4. Present four vetted candidates, let the client choose two to interview
> "we try to find four that we think are great." — Alex Kirby (~36:53)

**Candidate screening via video and key interview questions** (hiring)
1. Require a two-minute video or audio intro before applying
2. Favour strong English, a degree, and AI or marketing curiosity/certifications
3. Ask 'Why should I hire you over somebody else?' to test confidence
4. Ask candidates to explain what they are great at and how they'd help the company
> "we don't even let people apply if they don't send the two minute video." — Alex Kirby (~29:25)

**Membership upsell and discounted repeat hires** (pricing)
1. Offer a $200/month membership with support, two trainings a month and resources
2. Reduce later hire fee to 14% for members
3. Offer longer replacement guarantee (six months) to members
> "every hire after your first one is 14%." — Alex Kirby (~12:07)

**Pay overseas staff monthly to cut fees** (operations)
1. Use Thera, Deel or Gusto for compliant payments
2. Encourage clients to pay once a month so transfer fees are incurred once
> "we like to do it once a month. That way the fees are only one time." — Alex Kirby (~37:55)

**Show up in person when emails go unanswered** (sales)
1. Pick a warm prospect who has replied at least once
2. Dress professionally and arrive as if expected
3. Ask for the decision-maker and remove friction by solving their problem in person
> "I faked a meeting, I dressed up, I put my laptop in briefcase" — Alex Kirby (~20:09)

**Niche down to a vertical you know** (acquisition)
1. Choose a segment where you speak the language (blue collar)
2. Lean in publicly and accept losing off-niche leads
> "I think it's smart to be focused first and then if it demand grows, then you can figure that out later." — Alex Kirby (~42:32)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Building software with AI tools now takes days; the scarce skill is distribution and willingness to reach out personally.
- *customer_acquisition*: Start with your existing network and unfair advantage (church, club, community) to get first customers and a deposit.
- *niche_selection*: A favour you already do for free (connecting people with jobs) can be a monetisable business; check what others pay for the same service.
- *offer_design*: Place-and-leave recruiting is lower-headache than a staffing agency that carries payroll and compliance, at the cost of lower lifetime revenue; a membership adds recurring income.
- *niche_selection*: Specialise in a vertical you understand deeply so you can speak the language with confidence.
- *validation_testing*: Early discounts and deposits are a cheap way to get proof of concept and testimonials.
- *offer_design*: Most clients (about 90%) hire to add capacity as the business grows, not to replace local staff, which helps position the offer.
- *automation_ai*: Humans plus AI is positioned as more durable than pure AI replacement for support roles; remote talent plus AI tools is the value proposition.
- *mindset_execution*: Add or remove friction deliberately in habits and sales; removing the buyer's friction (showing up in person) closed a big contract.
- *mindset_execution*: Use business to buy back time for family rather than as an end in itself.

## Warnings and mistakes

- AI has made building easier but not business easier; distribution and getting customers is harder because everyone can build the same thing.
- Waiting for customers does not work; most people skip the hard outreach step.
- Customers don't think about you; expect to follow up many times.
- Timing risk: launching a content agency just as ChatGPT arrived gutted revenue.
- Impressive CVs don't guarantee fit; one Microsoft-experienced hire was replaced after four days for poor personality fit for customer support.
- A broad staffing offer could dilute positioning; an advisor warned him to lean into blue collar rather than also serving insurance.
