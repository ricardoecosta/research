## Tactics and playbook steps

**Burn the boats on commitment, not the paycheck** (mindset)
1. Day 1: set revenue goals and a deadline; quit only if they are hit
2. Months 1-3: learn the business on nights and weekends
3. Months 4-5: buy small used equipment (e.g. eBay cart), no big financing
4. Month 6: learn operations and repairs in your spare time
5. Month 7: work every weekend at events
6. Month 9: if numbers work, add a second unit and first employee, still employed
> "john is burning boats on the commitment level while keeping his financial safety nets in place" — Chris Koerner (~10:56)

**Make temptations a non-option** (mindset)
1. Decide in advance that an option (e.g. carbs) is as unthinkable as a firm rule
2. Treat it as not available rather than resisting it

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: Commit fully to a plan with clear revenue milestones while keeping your income until the milestones are met.
- *mindset_execution*: Wing walker's rule: do not let go of one income until you hold the next.
- *mindset_execution*: Going all in can create an inflection point when the idea already shows traction (Dude Perfect).
- *validation_testing*: Test a business cheaply with used equipment before committing big capital.
- *mindset_execution*: Quitting should feel like the obvious next step once the concept is proven.
- *mindset_execution*: Lack of necessity reduces drive; know your own temperament.
- *mindset_execution*: Adversity often precedes the biggest gains.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Quitting a big job to chase an unproven idea can destroy families.
- Having a profitable main business can make you quit side projects at the first friction.
- Burning the boats is not the same as taking a poorly calculated risk.
- A sale of a business can go wrong and drop income to zero overnight.
