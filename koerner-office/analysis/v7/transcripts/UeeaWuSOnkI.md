## Tactics and playbook steps

**Document the project publicly** (acquisition)
1. Start a social account before launch, even with no followers.
2. Use a phone and informal videos to show planning, building, and fundraising.
3. Study other creators in the niche: hooks, music, posting frequency, asset type, and comments.
4. Paste comment text into ChatGPT to identify themes that may resonate with viewers.
5. Optimize for shares, comments, and average view duration, not likes alone.
6. Repeat the formats that work and try to improve each video.
> "What are their hooks? What background music are they using? How often are they posting? What days are they posting?" — Chris Koerner (~8:53)

**Broaden the asset search and build seller trust** (acquisition)
1. Choose an asset class, then stay flexible on geography, price point, size, and type within it.
2. Look for an owner who is willing to get to know you and whom you also like.
3. Learn about the owner's asset class and acknowledge what they know.
4. Offer value, compliment the property sincerely, visit more than once, and follow up.
5. Share relevant personal context and common ground to make the relationship more familiar.
> "You've got to be willing to build a relationship of trust." — Chris Koerner (~12:46)

**Ask for alternative bank loan options** (finance)
1. If a bank presents a costly loan condition such as PMI, ask what other options exist.
2. Explain that the proposed cost makes the deal unaffordable.
3. Keep speaking with banks until one offers terms that work.
> "I read that I don't have to pay that. and they were like, "Uh, all right. I don't think it's going to work, but let me see if there are other options."" — Chris Koerner (~3:11)

**Frame seller financing around the seller's benefits** (sales)
1. Present the tangible benefits of the proposed terms to the seller.
2. Explain the buyer's story and relevant industry experience.
3. Use a specific plan for improving the asset and show how the seller can be protected.
4. Consider an ongoing profit share if the seller believes the asset has untapped potential.
> "You've got to frame it in a way that really highlights the positives for them. Both the tangibles and the intangibles." — Chris Koerner (~17:08)

**Use a balloon payment to defer principal** (finance)
1. Negotiate a small initial payment and manageable monthly payments.
2. Set a balloon due date in the contract.
3. Plan to refinance or sell the asset before the balloon is due.
4. Check the default and lien terms before signing.
> "The idea with this structure is to either refinance or sell the asset before then." — Chris Koerner (~17:58)

**Offer graduated payments to build seller confidence** (finance)
1. Start with payments the new business can afford.
2. Increase payments on a schedule as the buyer establishes a track record.
3. Use the schedule to show the seller that payments begin immediately and the buyer is acting in good faith.
> "suggest graduated payments because they can almost have the same purpose, which is to save you money in your payments upfront" — Chris Koerner (~21:22)

**Combine bank debt with a seller carryback** (finance)
1. Calculate the down payment required by the bank and the buyer's available cash.
2. Ask the seller to carry the funding gap as a note.
3. Discuss the seller's junior lien position and the risk of being behind the bank.
4. Consider bringing in friends or family for part of the buyer contribution only after weighing relationship risk.
> "A partial seller carryback would be for the seller to put 150 grand on the line" — Chris Koerner (~22:02)

**Trade future appreciation for better terms** (finance)
1. If the seller believes the asset can be worth more after improvements, ask them to improve the current financing terms.
2. Offer a defined share of future rents or profits tied to specific milestones.
3. Write the valuation triggers and payment share into the agreement.
> "How about you give me better terms on the seller financing and then I cut you into those future profits." — Chris Koerner (~24:26)

**Use a master lease with an option to buy** (finance)
1. Lease the property while the seller keeps title.
2. Set a monthly payment and a predetermined purchase price and date.
3. Define the right to walk away and what happens to the property if the buyer does not exercise the option.
4. Use time under the lease to test whether the improvement plan works.
> "It's a master lease agreement with the option to purchase at a predetermined price at a certain predetermined time in the future." — Chris Koerner (~26:25)

**Swap price, rate, down payment, services, or collateral** (pricing)
1. Offer a higher interest rate or purchase price in exchange for less cash down.
2. Consider consulting or marketing services in place of a cash down payment.
3. Consider offering collateral only after reviewing what default would mean.
4. Use ChatGPT to generate alternative term combinations, then review them with appropriate advisers.
> "How about 8% interest and 0% down?" — Chris Koerner (~26:45)

**Consider subject-to or delayed down payment terms** (finance)
1. For a subject-to structure, discuss taking over payments while the existing loan remains in the seller's name.
2. For a delayed down payment, propose a future payment date and define what happens if it is not paid.
3. Review the existing loan and contract terms carefully before proceeding.
> "The debt stays in the seller's name and you come out of pocket little to nothing." — Chris Koerner (~27:23)

**Stay willing to walk away** (mindset)
1. Evaluate the deal on its numbers and risks.
2. Do not treat one asset or seller as the only opportunity.
3. Walk away if the financing, operations, or downside protection do not work.
> "You've got to be willing to walk from any deal at any time. It should only be math to you." — Chris Koerner (~28:05)

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: A lack of cash can force a buyer to negotiate around timing, risk, payment structure, or seller priorities instead of relying on a large down payment.
- *customer_acquisition*: Build trust with an owner before asking for unusual financing; familiarity can make a seller more willing to consider a proposal.
- *niche_selection*: Be flexible about geography, price, size, and asset type within an asset class because a seller cannot be forced to sell.
- *customer_acquisition*: Publicly sharing a project's real progress can build an audience before launch and may lead to early customers or funding.
- *customer_acquisition*: For short-form content, use comments, shares, and watch duration as signals; likes alone are not the stated goal.
- *offer_design*: Creative financing should make the seller's upside and downside protection clear rather than apologizing for the request.
- *finance_unit_economics*: A small first payment followed by graduated payments can demonstrate good faith while preserving cash early in the deal.
- *legal_risk*: Understand balloon, lien, and default terms before agreeing to seller financing; a missed payment can put the asset at risk.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Do not raise outside capital automatically; funding can remove pressure to find creative, efficient ways to operate.
- *mindset_execution*: Treat each deal as a set of numbers and risks rather than becoming attached to the asset or the idea of owning it.
- *offer_design*: A seller's belief that an asset has untapped value can be turned into a proposal to share future upside in exchange for better current terms.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Borrowing from friends and family can put valuable relationships and reputation at risk.
- Do not assume that 100% seller financing for real estate is common or easy; the host rejects claims that many sellers accept it.
- A balloon payment can become due even if the buyer cannot refinance or sell, and the seller may take the asset back after default.
- A seller in second lien position may be left holding the bag if the buyer defaults and the bank is paid first.
- Interest-only payments do not reduce the principal and interest keeps compounding.
- Public project documentation may feel embarrassing if the project fails, and the algorithm may never pick up the videos.
- Do not become emotionally committed to one asset or deal; be ready to walk away.
- All the structures carry risk and the host says the episode is not financial advice.
