## Tactics and playbook steps

**Test the app through two paid traffic destinations** (validation_testing)
1. Finish payment integrations and submit the app to the App Store.
2. Run one paid campaign to the landing page and another directly to the App Store.
3. Compare the conversion and cost results before choosing which route to scale.
> "we're going to test running ads directly to that Lander to see what happens and then we're going to test direct to App Store also" — Chris Koerner (~3:27)

**Batch a month of niche video posts** (operations)
1. Search Instagram on desktop for a niche hashtag such as vegan recipes.
2. Use the Zapier browser extension to add selected video links to a spreadsheet.
3. Export the links as a CSV and use Apify to download the posts.
4. Route videos through Airtable and Zapier into a Bannerbear template that adds a recurring caption.
5. Send finished assets to Dropbox and bulk schedule them through Hopper HQ.
6. Review rights, attribution and output quality before publishing.
> "hit the zap button send a sheet send a sheet send a sheet get 60 of them upload the CSV let it ride" — Chris Koerner (~5:48)

**Use a recurring caption to convert viewers into followers** (customer_acquisition)
1. Put a clear description of the page's niche at the top of each video.
2. Add a day-count or series hook to create a reason to visit the profile.
3. Place the caption before a key moment so viewers see it before they swipe away.
4. Track view-to-follow conversion by caption format.
> "you just got to put it before like a key moment or else people will swipe away" — Chris Koerner (~8:54)

**Test guide offers and pricing from one store** (validation_testing)
1. Use marketplace data, including Etsy searches, to identify guide topics people are already buying.
2. Create a small set of niche guides and list them in one Shopify store.
3. Run separate ad campaigns for different products, titles and positions.
4. Test price points across the discussed $17 to $99 range and keep the offer that performs best.
5. Track repeat purchases before assuming customers will buy across unrelated niches.
> "you can test 15 different niches different offers different positionings overnight out of a single Shopify store" — Chris Koerner (~20:37)

**Build custom business plans from structured context** (operations)
1. Collect a customer's business idea and the inputs needed to research it.
2. Use a tested prompt to create the plan.
3. Use a research tool to support the analysis.
4. Review the generated plan for unsupported assumptions before sending it to the customer.
> "you just have to figure out how and spend the money and spend the time or we can solve this for you today" — Guest (name not stated) (~36:06)

**Sell convenience instead of proprietary process** (offer_design)
1. Acknowledge that a prospect could do the work themselves.
2. List the tools, time and recurring tasks they would need to handle.
3. Offer to take those tasks off their plate for a clear monthly price.
4. Describe the outcome in simple terms so prospects can assess the value quickly.
> "I just want to be in the reduction of headache game like and it makes my sales calls easier" — Guest (name not stated) (~35:56)

**Use a free preview to test paid access** (pricing)
1. Publish a detailed business plan with a readable free portion.
2. Place a clearly explained paywall partway through.
3. Test $5 and $10 access prices.
4. Measure paid continuation rather than assuming newsletter signups will convert.
> "what would happen if I put a kind of like a pay wall or gated access like halfway through the business plan" — Chris Koerner (~31:40)

**Keep credit and permission in the content workflow** (legal)
1. Record the original creator and source URL for each third-party video.
2. Ask permission or use properly licensed material where required.
3. Give accurate attribution when the creator agrees or the platform terms require it.
4. Respond promptly to takedown or rights complaints and do not assume an edit makes reuse lawful.
> "the nice piece that I've tried to do is like at least on the send prayers on is like I try to give credit" — Chris Koerner (~10:21)

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: Choose a small business test with a limited downside and a plausible return instead of waiting for an idea that could become a billion-dollar company.
- *validation_testing*: Use the market's response to decide whether an offer is worth further investment; the speakers emphasize that offers can be tested quickly.
- *offer_design*: Make a clear offer and explain the work you remove for the customer. A buyer may be able to do it themselves, but may value the saved time and reduced hassle.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI to speed up repeatable work, while keeping human review for quality and client-specific details.
- *customer_acquisition*: Measure the behavior that matters for the content goal, such as views converting to follows, rather than relying only on views.
- *customer_acquisition*: Reuse a working creative structure across posts, but keep track of where it appears so it supports an intentional audience journey.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Digital products can simplify fulfillment, but customer acquisition costs still determine whether the economics work.
- *validation_testing*: Do not assume a general store will create repeat purchases across unrelated interests; test whether buyers actually want the next product.
- *legal_risk*: Credit creators where appropriate and treat copyright complaints as a real operational risk, especially on platforms that enforce strikes.
- *offer_design*: For services, sell a simple reduction in work and hassle rather than relying on proprietary process claims that customers may not value.
- *mindset_execution*: A distinctive perspective can come from trying many ideas, including failed ones, but the speaker acknowledges that those experiments have a cost.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not assume that adding a face, caption or commentary makes reused video lawful. The speaker asserts fair use but reports receiving YouTube strikes; UK copyright exceptions are not the same as US fair use.
- The speaker says three YouTube strikes within 60 days can lead to account deletion, and reports receiving two strikes in two weeks.
- The proposed tactic of attaching an influencer's name without permission and sending them money unless they object carries legal, reputational and platform risk.
- A speaker's public video or technique does not automatically authorize an ebook, use of their image, or an implication that they endorse the product.
- Paid-ad testing can lose money; the cooking app's stated worst-case loss is a rough claim, not a measured cap on actual costs.
- App Store approval and payment integrations were not complete, so launch timing and the product's ability to collect payments were uncertain.
- Digital guides about fitness, food businesses, finance or other regulated topics need accurate content and appropriate review; AI output alone is not evidence of quality.
- The speakers' proposed advertising margins, conversion improvements and paywall uptake are hypotheses and should be tested before scaling spend.
