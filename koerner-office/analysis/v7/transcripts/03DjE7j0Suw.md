## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Corey, who sells a $1,000 'AI assessment' (audit) to small businesses. A voice agent (Annie, built on a voice-AI platform) interviews the owner for 10-20 minutes, another AI agent turns the transcript into a report (Docx to Gamma) recommending off-the-shelf tools, and a follow-up call upsells process optimisation, Zapier/Make automations, custom GPT knowledge systems, CRM setups and speed-to-lead agents at $1.5k-$5k+. Corey then lists tactics for finding customers with no following: local AI meetups, door knocking, free audits for a peer group, and office hours. Key takeaway: you only need to be one step ahead of the client, and a higher-priced audit makes the upsell easier.

### I1. AI assessment (audit) for small businesses

- **Customer:** Small businesses with teams, ideally 5-50 employees (realtors, business brokers, wedding venues, barber school, mortgage broker)
- **Problem:** Owners don't know where AI and software tools could save them time; most have not adopted even simple tools.
- **Offer:** A 20-30 minute voice-agent conversation, then a report within 48 hours (executive summary, effort vs impact matrix, recommended tools, 4-day quick-win plan, upsell opportunities, financial impact) plus a 30-minute follow-up call.
- **Business model:** One-off paid assessment that feeds upsell project work
- **Pricing:** Fixed fee per assessment, raised over time. first 2-3 free for testimonials USD 0 per assessment; early price USD 200 per assessment; second price USD 500 per assessment; current price USD 1000 per assessment Higher price increases client seriousness and makes a $3.5k upsell feel closer. Called 'assessment' rather than 'audit' as it sounds less aggressive.
- **Acquisition:** networking_events: Host a free local AI-for-small-business meetup (Luma, meetup.com); the organiser is seen as the authority.; door_to_door_in_person: Door-knock local businesses for a conversation about how they use AI, leading with value.; existing_audience: Offered free audits to a local CEO peer group of 11-12 people; got testimonials and warm upsell contacts.; partnerships: Free AI meetup / weekly or monthly office hours inside a realtor's office; brokers in charge may want workshops. Captured emails at the door.
- **Delivery:** A voice agent calls the owner and asks questions from a master question bank by industry. Transcript goes to a second AI agent (multiple skills) that cleans it, searches AI tools, and outputs a docx. The docx is uploaded to a Gamma template. Corey sends it within 48 hours and presents it on a follow-up call. A beginner version is a Zoom call plus Claude.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Sold at $1,000 with almost no cost, nearly pure margin; Example report: 8 hours a week saved, about $3,200 a month value vs $59 a month tool cost; Upsells commonly $1.5k-$5k
- **Tools:** Retell AI (voice agent, heard as 'retail.ai'), Claude, Gamma, Fathom, Dashthis, Go High Level, Zapier, Make.com, Loom, Futurepedia, There's An AI For That
- **Startup cost:** Near zero (Claude subscription, Gamma, voice agent platform); exact costs not stated
- **Time to first revenue:** Not stated; the first 2-3 assessments were free, then $200
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Works in the UK. Recording calls needs consent and UK GDPR handling of transcripts. Door knocking and meetups are legal. Prices would be in GBP.
- **Risks:** Recorded interviews contain business data, so UK GDPR and consent apply; Only works with owners who value their time; solo operators are a worse fit; Jack-of-all-trades upselling spreads you thin; Quick-win tools are off-the-shelf, so competition and commoditisation are likely
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 1/5 (Meetups, door knocking and peer-group outreach are in person; only the follow-up and event promotion can be automated.); onboarding 4/5 (Owner calls a number any time; the voice agent runs the interview.); delivery 4/5 (Agents build the report with Gamma; the human checks the tools and walks through the report on a call.); billing 4/5 (Fixed-fee invoice or Stripe link; not described in the transcript.); support 2/5 (A human follow-up call is needed to explain and upsell.)
> "selling AI audits to small business owners for $1,000 with basically no cost. It's almost pure profit margin." — Chris Koerner (~0:55)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Pilot free, then step the price up** (pricing)
1. Do the first 2-3 assessments free for testimonials and to fix process kinks
2. Charge $200, then $500, then $1,000
3. Use the $1,000 anchor so a $3.5k upsell feels like a small jump
> "you're almost shooting yourself in the foot later on by being cheap early on" — Chris Koerner (~18:06)

**Assessment to upsell funnel** (sales)
1. Run the voice or Zoom interview
2. Send the report within 48 hours with a booking link for a 30-minute follow-up
3. Screen-share and walk through quick wins
4. Introduce upsell opportunities and the financial-impact slide
5. Put the financial impact slide at the top as a hook (host suggestion)

**Prompt Claude to build the report** (operations)
1. Attach the interview transcript to Claude
2. Ask it to find off-the-shelf AI or software tools that fix the identified pain points
3. Tell it to search directories like Futurepedia
4. Ask Claude how each tool is implemented before the follow-up call

**Free local AI meetup or office hours** (acquisition)
1. Ask a well-connected realtor to host a free AI event at their office
2. Give a 20-25 minute pure-value talk, no pitch
3. Capture emails at the door
4. Offer weekly or monthly AI office hours at realtor offices or co-working spaces
5. Follow up with assessments

**Specialise in one upsell** (product)
1. Pick one upsell, such as a custom GPT, as the bread and butter
2. Productise it
3. Prefer an upsell tied to revenue or savings, such as a speed-to-lead agent

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: You only need to be one step ahead of your client, not an expert; about 7 days of study can be enough to sell.
- *offer_design*: Productise a low-effort diagnostic as an entry offer, then monetise the follow-on projects.
- *pricing*: Price signals value: higher entry prices make clients take recommendations seriously and ease upsells.
- *validation_testing*: Remove friction from customer input: a phone number to call beats asking people to record screens.
- *validation_testing*: Pilot free with a warm network to get testimonials and refine the process.
- *offer_design*: Sell upsells tied directly to revenue or savings, such as speed-to-lead with an $11k deal size.
- *automation_ai*: Start with manual or simple tooling (Zoom plus Claude), then automate the bottleneck step with a voice agent.
- *operations*: Fix the process before automating it.
- *customer_acquisition*: Being the organiser of a local event makes you the authority.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Asking clients to screen-record work with Loom was too much friction, so the pilot failed.
- Don't be a jack of all trades on upsells; specialise to avoid spreading thin.
- Cheap early pricing makes later bigger upsells harder, and low-priced assessments are taken less seriously.
- Custom GPT system prompts must block sensitive data such as phone numbers and financials.
