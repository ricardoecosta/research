## Tactics and playbook steps

**Place cards near target vehicles** (acquisition)
1. Choose areas with high-end cars and place business cards on vehicles, prioritizing nicer, well-maintained cars.
2. Joel says he distributed about 250 to 300 cards over a few days to a week and initially got about two responses.
3. He later estimated that about ten cars yielded a job, after he had built a reputation.

**Turn visible work into referrals** (acquisition)
1. Do good work where neighbors and passersby can see it.
2. Joel's first customer's home was at the end of a street; a passerby noticed the work and later hired him for a Porsche.
3. That customer then referred friends who became clients.

**Build reputation and social proof** (acquisition)
1. Improve business cards as the business develops.
2. Use the work and customer experience to build a reputation; the host mentions case studies, testimonials, and before-and-after pictures.
3. The host and Joel say response improved as reputation and proof increased.

**Test a paid marketplace** (acquisition)
1. List the service on Thumbtack and track the type and cost of leads.
2. Joel says lead costs vary by service and estimates closing about half of the leads.

**Screen jobs before accepting them** (sales)
1. Pay attention to the client's vibe before agreeing to a job.
2. Check the vehicle condition rather than relying only on the client's description; Joel says some cars turn out to have trash throughout.

**Start with core equipment and add gear over time** (operations)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: A small initial equipment setup can let an operator begin before investing in a larger kit; Joel says he added equipment over time.
- *customer_acquisition*: A visible first job can lead to a local referral chain when potential customers see the work and ask about it.
- *customer_acquisition*: Reputation and proof can improve response to direct outreach; Joel and the host connect his later results with having testimonials, case studies, and before-and-after pictures.
- *operations*: Screen customers and inspect the actual job before committing, because the condition can differ from what the customer says.
- *scaling*: Repeat monthly customers and referrals can provide a steadier source of work than relying only on one-off jobs.
- *mindset_execution*: Treat low early response as a signal to build reputation and improve targeting, rather than assuming the initial result will remain the same.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Growth in a hands-on service business may require hiring; Joel says he expects to hire if he reaches his $10,000 monthly goal.
- *mindset_execution*: Joel's advice is to make steady daily progress.
