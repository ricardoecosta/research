### I1. Mobile car detailing (solo start)

- **Customer:** Car owners including owners of high-end vehicles
- **Problem:** Car owners want clean, protected vehicles without going to a shop
- **Offer:** Interior, exterior details and ceramic coatings done at the customer's location
- **Business model:** Per-job service fees with upsells to paint correction and ceramic coating
- **Pricing:** Per job, tiered packages with upsells. Early basic washes USD 50-100 per job; Online quote range for detail packages USD 270-480 per job; One-step paint correction add-on USD 399 per job; Average ceramic coating USD 1000 per car Upsell a $200 job into a ~$1,000 job by inspecting the car and offering paint correction and ceramic coating.
- **Acquisition:** google_business_profile_maps: Free Google My Business profile; get friends and family to leave reviews; 20-30 reviews start ranking. Google Local Service Ads also recommended.; organic_social_content: Instagram highlight reels of jobs, possibly AI-made commercials; paid_social: Instagram and Facebook ads showing car videos leading to a form; call leads to book; other: Radio ads: about $400 a month for eight prime-time spots, sometimes via donation to stations; paid_search: Google Local Service Ads and ads on the website 'get a free quote' form
- **Delivery:** Detailer drives to the customer in any vehicle (Alan started in a Subaru Outback), inspects the car, washes, polishes with a dual-action polisher, applies ceramic coating. Solo operator does all work.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Solo operators capped around $15K/month; About 80% profit margin if frugal and doing everything yourself; Starter kit under $500; $100 of Walmart supplies
- **Tools:** Max Shine polisher (~$150), Pressure washer, Vacuum, Drill brush, Costco towels, Walmart chemicals, Facebook Marketplace for used kit, Chinese suppliers for pads (30c vs $3), Google My Business, Google Local Service Ads
- **Startup cost:** Under $500 (polisher ~$150, ~$100 chemicals, pressure washer, vacuum); LLC and insurance about $100/month
- **Time to first revenue:** Not stated; first customers via Google profile and reviews
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Mobile detailing works in the UK. Prices would differ, LLC becomes Ltd, insurance differs; Local Service Ads availability in UK is limited so rely on Google Business Profile and ads.
- **Risks:** Physical, labour-bound work caps income (~$15K/month solo); Poor early quality and slow jobs without proper tools; Damaging paint by using a rotary polisher or pressure washer too close
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Google Business profile, local service ads and paid social can be run by agents; reviews need real customers.); onboarding 3/5 (Online quote form and booking can be automated; a salesperson currently handles calls.); delivery 0/5 (Physical cleaning and polishing of cars on site requires a human every time.); billing 4/5 (Card payments and invoicing are easily automated.); support 3/5 (Booking changes and FAQs can be handled by an agent; complaints and redo jobs need a human.)
> "Starting off at Walmart, you get a full setup for about 100 bucks." — Alan (~6:15)
> "if we upsell a ceramic coating or one of our more exclusive packages, that $200 job can turn into a thousand dollar job" — Alan (~28:37)

### I2. Go Detail service-partner (middleman) model

- **Customer:** Car owners; supply side is experienced detailers poor at marketing
- **Problem:** Experienced detailers lack marketing and consistent work; new founders lack skills and equipment
- **Offer:** Founder supplies marketing and jobs; partners supply labour and equipment
- **Business model:** Brand owner takes margin on jobs fulfilled by service partners
- **Pricing:** Job price to customer, split with service partner.  Alan says about 40% profit margin versus ~80% solo
- **Acquisition:** paid_search: Google Local Service Ads and Google Business Profile; paid_social: Instagram and Facebook ads
- **Delivery:** Service partners with their own equipment do the jobs; founder gives them consistent bookings.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Two to three partners each bringing $15K/month gives ~$45K/month revenue; Startup cost zero; Profit margin about 40%
- **Tools:** Instagram, Google ads
- **Startup cost:** Zero dollars according to Alan
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Works similarly in the UK, but subcontractor status and insurance liability need care.
- **Risks:** Quality control of subcontractors; Partners may go direct to customers; Margin halves versus solo; Alan sells the licence, so claims are promotional
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Online ads and listings drive leads; agents can manage campaigns.); onboarding 3/5 (Quote forms and job dispatch to partners can be automated.); delivery 0/5 (Partners physically do the work; quality control needs humans.); billing 4/5 (Take payment and pay partners via software.); support 2/5 (Customer complaints and partner disputes need humans with tooling.)
> "The go detail route is hiring service partners." — Alan (~12:05)
> "Over here, the startup cost is literally zero dollars." — Alan (~12:42)

### I3. Industrial ceramic coating for heavy equipment

- **Customer:** Construction, farm and fleet equipment owners
- **Problem:** Heavy equipment rusts, fades and oxidises, lowering resale value
- **Offer:** Industrial ceramic coating to protect and restore equipment

- **Pricing:** Per vehicle, priced by value not time. Industrial coating USD 5000-20000 per vehicle; Student claim USD 5000 per ambulance; Undercoating USD 3000-4000 per job Alan says no one is doing it so you set the price.
- **Acquisition:** other: Not detailed; get contractors to call you whenever they get new equipment. Methods taught inside Alan's licensing system.
- **Delivery:** Technician spends about a day on prep and coating; product applied with pad.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Product ~$400-500 per dump truck job; Labour $200-300 per technician per day; All-in cost about $2,000, profit about $3,000 on a job; Bottle costs about $800
- **Tools:** Ultra Shield industrial ceramic coating, Applicator pads
- **Startup cost:** not stated; product about $800 per bottle
- **Time to first revenue:** Students claim clients in first week
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — UK has plant, farm and fleet equipment; climate less harsh and market size and pricing unproven.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Upsell during on-site inspection** (sales)
1. Put an A-frame sign with phone and QR code on the street
2. Inspect the car and point out scratches and stains
3. Offer paint correction ($399) and ceramic coating as add-on while on site
4. Aim to turn a $200 job into ~$1,000
> "you have scratches here, you got stains here, would you like me to take care of those while I'm at the on site today?" — Alan (~28:23)

**Bootstrap first customers with Google Business Profile** (acquisition)
1. Create free Google Business Profile
2. Detail friends' and family's cars for practice and reviews
3. Get 20-30 reviews to start ranking
4. Add Local Service Ads when budget allows

**Hire a salesperson early, ideally an ex-car salesman** (hiring)
1. Be the salesperson until about the sixth hire
2. Hire an ex-car salesman to answer phones
3. Recruit A players and delegate
> "I hired an ex-car salesman. That's kind of the secret." — Alan (~26:24)

**Source consumables from China in bulk** (operations)
1. Order pads, applicators and nozzles by the thousands
2. Compare to Amazon prices to protect margin

**Hire direct ads buyer instead of an agency** (acquisition)
1. Find the freelancer agencies use behind the scenes
2. Pay about $500 a month instead of $2,000-3,000

**Set a floor price** (pricing)
1. Decide a bottom price you will not go below
2. Avoid cheapest customers who are hardest to deal with

## Lessons

- *pricing*: Go for higher-ticket jobs because they take about as much effort to book as low-ticket ones.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Hire a salesperson early to free the founder from phone calls.
- *scaling*: Using subcontractors or partners lets you start with no equipment or skill, at lower margin.
- *niche_selection*: Low-competition adjacent niches (industrial coatings) allow value-based pricing.
- *operations*: Right tools cut job time dramatically (8 hours to 2).
- *hiring_outsourcing*: One A player is worth ten B players; don't believe only you can do it best.
- *customer_acquisition*: Repeat business comes from great work and owning mistakes.
- *automation_ai*: Local home-service competitors are often unsophisticated; AI-driven content and ads can beat them.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Beginners undercharge; cheapest customers are the worst to deal with.
- Using a rotary polisher can burn through paint; beginners should use a dual-action polisher.
- Agencies charge much more than direct ad buyers and push you to the back burner.
- Avoid Armor All; some cheap films yellow over time.
