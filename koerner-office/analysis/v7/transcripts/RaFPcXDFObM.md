## Tactics and playbook steps

**Size a local grill-cleaning market** (validation_testing)
1. Estimate the number of local households with grills.
2. Test more than one willingness-to-pay assumption rather than relying on the largest estimate.
3. Divide the paying market by existing competitors to estimate a possible customer pool.
4. Treat the host's revenue and margin calculation as a hypothesis to validate with actual bookings.

**Make grill-cleaning routes efficient** (operations)
1. Group jobs geographically because the service is not urgent.
2. Consider collecting grates from several homes in one route.
3. Soak and scrub grates at a shop, then return to finish the grills.
> "You could go to all 10 houses in a day, pick up all the grates." — Chris Koerner (~3:11)

**Use local discovery channels for grill cleaning** (acquisition)
1. Try Facebook ads, flyers, direct mail, Google ads, and a Google Business Profile.
2. Check Google Trends for search interest and seasonality before setting a route or campaign.

**Cross-sell home services** (sales)
1. While at a customer's home for grill cleaning, offer adjacent work such as pressure washing, oven cleaning, or outdoor kitchen cleaning.
2. Consider driveway, sidewalk, house, roof, gutter, and window work if equipment and time allow.

**Run a pancake pop-up without a permanent shop** (operations)
1. Use a specialised mini-pancake burner.
2. Set up in a front yard or busy street corner.
3. Post the location and hours on Facebook, Nextdoor, and Instagram.
4. Offer a base serving with optional sweet or savoury toppings.

**Start a dehydrated-fruit test small** (validation_testing)
1. Find a dehydrator with a 30-day warranty and money-back guarantee.
2. Experiment with fruit, then sell at a farmers market.
3. Track which fruits sell best before buying more equipment.

**Source and sell colostrum powder through farm relationships** (operations)
1. Buy liquid colostrum from dairy farmers.
2. Use a freeze dryer, not a standard dehydrator, for the example described.
3. Sell the powder to other dairy farmers.
4. Check actual input yield and buyer demand before relying on the host's conversion math.

**Raise picnic prices as demand grows** (pricing)
1. Start by offering the service for next to free.
2. Document the setup and results on Instagram.
3. Begin charging, then raise prices as bookings make the founder too busy.
4. Rent a van or truck instead of buying one at the start.

**Turn successful picnic videos into local ads** (acquisition)
1. Post short-form, time-lapse, or edited videos organically.
2. When a video performs well, use Instagram's boost feature.
3. Target the relevant local zip codes and couples aged 25 to 55.

**Test food products before over-planning** (validation_testing)
1. Make a small batch and try selling it at a booth, cart, or farmers market.
2. Use real customer behaviour as evidence instead of relying only on opinions.
3. Treat an early failure as data and test another version.

**Operate an unattended neighbourhood bakery stand** (operations)
1. Put bakery items in a visible porch stand, shed, or table.
2. Let customers pay by QR code or Venmo.
3. Compare losses from theft with the cost of having a person attend the stand.

**Use free food to build local trust** (acquisition)
1. Give neighbours coffee and muffins.
2. Include a business card for a local service such as roofing or HVAC.
3. Use the gathering to become known locally, without assuming that a giveaway guarantees a sale.

**Compare event locations with a controlled watermelon test** (validation_testing)
1. Choose five high-foot-traffic locations.
2. Test each at the same time and on the same day of the week.
3. Repeat across five different weeks and compare sales.
> "Find five high foot traffic areas and test all five of them on the same day of the week at the same time of day across five different weeks." — Chris Koerner (~21:16)

**Start with a simple event snack test** (validation_testing)
1. Use a cooler and a small initial batch of watermelon.
2. Sell at a parade or other permitted high-foot-traffic location.
3. Record units sold and input costs before deciding whether to repeat.

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Estimate market size with conservative assumptions and then test whether customers actually pay; the grill-cleaning figures are a model, not verified demand.
- *operations*: Route density can matter for a local service because grouping nearby jobs reduces wasted travel.
- *mindset_execution*: Start small, test with customers, and use observed results rather than spending most of the time planning an ideal product.
- *validation_testing*: Do not let other people's dismissive opinions replace a small, real-world test.
- *pricing*: For a local experience business, show the work publicly, start with a low price, and increase prices as demand fills available time.
- *offer_design*: A product can gain value through packaging and positioning even when its ingredients are familiar.
- *finance_unit_economics*: An unattended sales point may be viable when avoided staffing costs exceed losses, but the trade-off needs to be measured locally.
- *validation_testing*: Test locations systematically by holding the day and time constant and comparing results across several sites.
- *finance_unit_economics*: An asset can support either a rental model or the core delivery of a service, but the asset alone does not prove demand or profitability.
- *customer_acquisition*: Local goodwill can support customer acquisition for high-ticket services, but a giveaway should be treated as a test rather than a guaranteed sale.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The host says prospective grill-cleaning customers may object that their grill cost less than the cleaning price; the market size and profit calculation are his estimates.
- Grill cleaning is seasonal in the host's Google Trends example and requires route planning to avoid excessive driving.
- Food businesses can face food-handling obstacles; the host mentions permits but does not explain applicable rules.
- Freeze dryers and dehydrators are different equipment with different moisture removal, shelf-life, and cost claims; the colostrum example uses a freeze dryer.
- An unattended bakery stand can lose products to theft; the host argues that the savings in labour may outweigh losses but offers no records.
- The Wopsicle seller spent the previous day preparing watermelon, so the low per-unit cost does not mean the business takes no labour.
- The host promotes Beehiiv with an affiliate link and discount code, and promotes TK Owners, a community he operates or sells.
