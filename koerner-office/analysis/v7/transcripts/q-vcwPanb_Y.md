## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate a live-selling niche before buying bulk** (validation_testing)
1. Install the Whatnot app and watch sellers in the product category you are considering.
2. Bookmark comparable sellers and enable alerts for their upcoming shows.
3. Use the stream analytics the guest describes to estimate average sale prices and sales pace.
4. Start by selling things you already own before buying a truckload.
> "You don't go buy a truckload of hands to start from scratch. You you sell stuff you already own." — Shannon Gene (~9:21)

**Wholesale part of a large liquidation lot** (sales)
1. Before bidding, identify smaller retailers and screen printers that may buy pieces or pallets.
2. Offer a portion of the lot to buyers who cannot take a full truckload.
3. Use those wholesale sales to recover part of the initial investment.
> "So, you should be finding other retailers that you could sell it to for $2 an item. People that want to buy it, screen printers" — Shannon Gene (~2:34)

**Calculate landed cost before bidding** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Estimate a realistic sale price using observed marketplace sales rather than the supplier's stated MSRP.
2. Add freight and handling to the per-item purchase cost.
3. Subtract platform fees and other costs before setting a maximum bid.
> "The freight's going to be the most expensive part of all these deals. I'm going to do it for a buck 83. What are the What are the fees?" — Shannon Gene (~8:57)

**Build marketplace seller credibility in small steps** (operations)
1. Register the seller accounts before purchasing inventory.
2. Sell a small batch and build feedback before scaling.
3. Keep moving after a bad transaction rather than spending the day fighting a low-value dispute.
> "You should, you know, definitely buy 10 items and and leave good feedback and they'll get view feedback so you can start building up a score" — Shannon Gene (~10:48)

**Build a relationship with the marketplace category manager** (customer_acquisition)
1. Identify the category manager relevant to the product category.
2. Connect professionally on LinkedIn.
3. Introduce yourself and offer to contribute experience without opening with a request.
> "Just say, "Hey, I'm here. I'm selling. I'm great. Just want you to give me a heads up if there's anything ever I could do for you." — Shannon Gene (~11:36)

**Use repeat volume to negotiate supplier contracts** (pricing)
1. Build a consistent purchase history with pallets before asking for truckload terms.
2. Offer a repeatable commitment, such as a regular truckload, only after you can handle that volume.
3. Use dependable demand to negotiate a lower purchase price.
> "That's how you scale it because they're going to give you better prices because you it's just repeatable for them" — Shannon Gene (~22:47)

**Make mystery offers clear and worth repeating** (offer_design)
1. Show the product category and price tier on the machine.
2. Consider a multi-buy offer to increase the number of packages purchased.
3. Seed desirable products only if the promotion is represented honestly and customers are not misled about their odds or contents.
> "I would put like electronics, clothing, shoes, just so they knew the category and then there was a price attached to each category." — Chris Koerner (~18:36)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Test a live-selling format with goods you already own before committing capital to a large inventory lot.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Use observed sales data to test a product idea and set a bid ceiling before buying.
- *operations*: Large inventory deals can work only when the resale channel removes enough preparation work or when part of the stock can be wholesaled.
- *customer_acquisition*: Build a seller reputation gradually through small transactions and feedback.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Treat occasional refunds and disputes as part of the cost of selling, and avoid spending too much time on low-value conflicts.
- *customer_acquisition*: Seller performance on a live platform depends on building an audience and a reputation, not just finding cheap stock.
- *scaling*: Supplier contracts can improve pricing when a buyer can make a dependable, repeatable commitment.
- *offer_design*: A mystery product is an entertainment offer as well as a goods sale, but customers need a fair understanding of what they are buying.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Account for platform fees, freight, handling, and refunds when judging a resale margin.
- *customer_acquisition*: Relationships with marketplace category managers may help a seller become more visible and useful to the platform.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not start live selling by buying a truckload. The guest recommends first selling items already owned and learning the format.
- Large apparel lots may require extensive sorting and shipping, and the supplier's stated MSRP may not reflect realistic resale value.
- Freight and handling can be the largest costs in a liquidation purchase; include them before bidding.
- Marketplace sellers will not win every dispute. The guest says a seller may need to refund a buyer and move on.
- A marketplace's stated no-return policy does not guarantee that a buyer will not receive a refund after complaining.
- Unmanifested customer returns can be chaotic and may contain goods with little resale value.
- Planting an Xbox in a mystery machine to create excitement could mislead buyers if the promotion does not clearly explain the odds and contents.
- The guest promotes ResellBot and reseller learning groups, and the host promotes TK Owners and a paid mastermind; recommendations and claims may have promotional motives.
