## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Hunter (Rogersville, near Springfield, Missouri), who started a parking-lot line striping and seal coating business after hearing an earlier Koerner Office episode with Sinko. In his first two months he claims about $22.9k then $46.8k in revenue (just under $50k total), roughly $20k net profit, on about $3k startup cost. Customers came mainly from a single cold email to about 300 Springfield churches (claimed 7-12% response), followed by in-person quotes. Key lessons: offer both striping and seal coating, show up in person, hire help early, and buy reliable equipment.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Cold email a niche list of organisations** (acquisition)
1. Buy or compile a list of local churches (about $6 on Upwork)
2. Send a single straightforward email offering a striping discount
3. Include licensed-and-insured claim (get insurance before first job)
4. Follow up in person for quotes
5. Plan follow-up and tested offers for spring
> "I paid someone on Upwork five or six bucks to compile a list of all the churches in Springfield." — Hunter (~21:01)

**Show up in person to quote** (sales)
1. Meet decision-makers face to face, especially in local, older-demographic organisations
2. Bring a printout if no business cards
3. Be ready to quote seal coating and crack filling as well as striping
4. A truck instead of a beat-up minivan improved close rate
> "I realized I needed to be there in person." — Hunter (~25:09)

**Practise equipment before the first job** (operations)
1. Fill the machine with water
2. Spray in a parking lot to get the feel of pushing and lining up
> "I just filled up my machine with water and I just sprayed water" — Hunter (~9:00)

**Free community job for practice and proof** (acquisition)
1. Post in the town Facebook group offering free striping
2. Let members vote on the business
3. Be upfront that you are new and want practice
4. Take before/after photos for later outreach

**Hire hourly part-timers from personal network** (hiring)
1. Recruit from church congregation, including teenagers
2. Pay $15/hr teens, $25/hr adults, $30-35 for late nights
3. Let detail-oriented staff handle chalk lines and measuring
4. Hire full-time staff for spring

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Stay flexible: the business he planned (striping) turned out to be better as seal coating plus striping.
- *mindset_execution*: Do not wait for a perfect website; an email list and in-person visits got revenue without one.
- *niche_selection*: Higher-ticket B2B services need far fewer jobs to produce meaningful income.
- *customer_acquisition*: Cold outreach to a single niche with a cheap list can have very high ROI; test one vertical before others.
- *customer_acquisition*: Outbound leads need in-person trust building, unlike inbound leads who are already in market.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Founders add more value winning jobs than doing the labour; hire detail-oriented workers early.
- *niche_selection*: Check regional fit: Texas needs striping only, Missouri benefits from seal coating.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Prove the model frugally but replace unreliable equipment once demand exists.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Presenting yourself as an experienced pro without full answers lost the first round of quotes; be upfront about being new.
- Door-to-door sales struggled to reach decision makers.
- Offering only striping lost jobs because customers wanted striping and seal coating together.
- Weed torch during crack filling started a fire near a house and a church; carry a fire extinguisher.
- Buying used equipment led to downtime; he'd buy new now.
- Church sales cycles are long because of board approvals.
