## Tactics and playbook steps

**Run ads only during hours you can answer the phone** (acquisition)
1. Set paid ads to run 5pm-9pm only when working a day job
2. Take sales calls in that window
3. Dedicate weekends to answering calls all day for about 6 months
> "Saturday and Sunday, those are your golden goose days, right? you have the whole day" — Paul (~8:55)

**Use arrival windows instead of exact arrival times** (operations)
1. Give customers a 1-hour arrival window (2 hours in afternoon LA traffic)
2. Avoids customers phoning angry about lateness
3. Better first impression improves conversion to recurring
> "just do 1 hour windows or if it's in the afternoon LA traffic, 2our windows, right?" — Paul (~16:20)

**Keep crew capacity ahead of booked demand** (operations)
1. Estimate each crew's cleans per day and monthly revenue capacity
2. Estimate expected bookings from organic, LSA, Thumbtack, Yelp and Facebook ads
3. Keep spare crews beyond current load so capacity matches bookings
4. Keep recruiting and interviewing new cleaners weekly or monthly

**Break plateaus with more crews, spend and a strong salesperson** (operations)
1. Hire more crews
2. Increase marketing spend slightly
3. Add a really good salesperson
4. Plateau lasted 3-4 months before this

**Keep crews happy to prevent client poaching** (hiring)
1. Grant reasonable pay-bump requests
2. Drop crews who repeatedly ask for more on every job
3. Happy cleaners tell customers to rebook through the company
> "when the customer asks hey um how do I book again they'll say you know call the company" — Paul (~29:10)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Saturation signals demand; do not skip a big market because it has many competitors. Ten thousand a month needs only about 33 recurring customers.
- *niche_selection*: Choose businesses on criteria: remote-capable, hard for AI to replace, and recurring revenue.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Recurring customers compound: each month starts from a higher revenue floor.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Supply quality (crews) is the product and the main bottleneck; invest continuously in recruiting and retention.
- *operations*: Small operational tweaks, like arrival windows, can have outsized effects and are often learned faster through mentorship.
- *mindset_execution*: Keep emotions level: don't get too high on great days or too low on bad ones.
- *mindset_execution*: Business exposes your weaknesses; address them (e.g. marketing) by hiring or fixing rather than avoiding.
- *mindset_execution*: Part-time founders can use evenings and weekends and time ad schedules to fit a day job.
- *scaling*: You can choose how big to scale; growth is limited by crews, sales capacity and ad spend rather than a physical cap.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Cleaners can cut the company out and strike a side deal with the client, especially if disgruntled.
- Unreliable crews make the business stressful at any revenue level; if unsure about a crew, don't use them.
- Complacency after finding a few good crews stalls growth; keep recruiting.
- Pushing marketing budget too high gives poor margins and more headaches.
