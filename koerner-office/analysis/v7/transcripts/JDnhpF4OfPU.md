## Tactics and playbook steps

**Daily contractor prospecting routine** (acquisition)
1. 7am, work from a coffee shop
2. Google contractors; vet reputation and finished product
3. Message/call every key staff member to find the decision-maker
4. Prefer calls or in-person over email
5. Set a meeting, then do a test job; first job wins ongoing work
6. Track in a spreadsheet and calendar
> "I get on Google, search, do some research on these businesses, make sure they're reputable" — Ron Butler (~29:04)

**Build a master target list from permit data** (acquisition)
1. Pull every permit in the service area and which contractors pull them
2. Identify contractors with steady volume of ideal permit types
3. Hire someone on Upwork (with AI) to compile a master list of ~1,000 targets
4. Use Apollo/ZoomInfo for contacts and run an email program
5. Visit active job sites in person when nearby
> "finding someone to help you search every permit that is pulled in the Austin area" — David Heacock (~34:13)

**Outsource delivery to 1099s and spend freed time on sales** (hiring)
1. Accept lower short-term margin by paying good 1099 crews
2. Don't hire a full-time employee before consistent work exists
3. If hiring anyone, hire a salesperson first
4. Set clear standards via sitewalks
5. Hire full-time only when cheaper than contracting
> "You're too early to be hiring a full-time employee in my opinion." — David Heacock (~40:19)

**Stay focused on one proven channel** (operations)
1. Double down on contractor network rather than Thumbtack or Google Ads now
2. Build Google Business Profile and reviews meanwhile
3. Experiment with Google local service ads only once cash flow allows

**Build the system before hiring someone to run it** (operations)
1. Owner designs the process first
2. Hire someone to manage and improve the system
3. Avoid hiring an 'expert' and expecting them to figure it out
> "I build the system, then I hire somebody to come in and to to manage that system" — David Heacock (~71:22)

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: Work for a successful company in the trade to get paid to learn before starting your own.
- *mindset_execution*: Trades are a low-risk jump: the worst case is getting another job.
- *mindset_execution*: Your job shifts from technician to salesperson and system builder; identity must change.
- *customer_acquisition*: B2B referral partners (contractors) give exclusive repeat work versus competitive lead marketplaces.
- *niche_selection*: Remodel work is evergreen and higher margin than cyclical new construction.
- *operations*: Clear communication and thoroughness win contractor loyalty.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Hire a great CPA early.
- *mindset_execution*: Good opportunities will still be there later; focus on doubling the proven model.
- *mindset_execution*: Clarity on long-term vision drives today's decisions.
- *scaling*: Every decision should maximise a repeatable future system, not today's money.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Being the bottleneck for sales, delivery and admin caps growth.
- Short-term greed (doing every job yourself to keep margin) prevents building a real business.
- Hiring a full-time employee too early can burn a valued relationship if work isn't steady.
- Scaling too fast with subcontractors can damage reputation; bad news travels fast.
- Leasing a licence or acting as remote master on record carries liability red flags.
- Lead marketplaces like Thumbtack are highly competitive and expensive.
- New construction is cyclical with layoffs after booms.
- An outsourced AI outreach test failed because the list targeted the wrong avatar and wasn't verified.
- UK: electrical work needs competent-person scheme registration; UK cold calls to sole-trader builders must be TPS/CTPS screened and cold SMS/email to sole traders needs consent under PECR.
