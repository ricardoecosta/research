## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build a niche audience before pitching a product** (customer_acquisition)
1. Publish practical content about a specific topic and make it easy for a relevant organization or event to discover it.
2. Place the blog link where the target community already looks; Deborah's blog was linked from the Mother Earth News Fair website.
3. Use speaking or other public appearances to demonstrate expertise.
> "And there was a link to my blog on the Mother Earth News Fair website and they read it." — Deborah (~1:37)

**Start an email list and use a useful lead magnet** (customer_acquisition)
1. Offer a practical free resource that solves a common problem, such as a goat food cheat sheet or mineral comparison chart.
2. Ask visitors to provide an email address to receive the resource.
3. Send relevant material that follows the audience's seasonal needs.
> "And that's where people sign up for our email list or get one of our freebies like our goat mineral comparison chart or something like that." — Deborah (~17:27)

**Keep subscribers informed while building a product** (sales)
1. Tell the list what you are building and share progress as it happens.
2. Keep communicating if production takes longer than planned.
3. Open sales to an audience that has already been following the work.
> "I read Jeff Walker's book and he says in there to get your audience invested in what you're creating by keeping them informed about your progress." — Deborah (~10:43)

**Use seasonal course promotions** (pricing)
1. Choose the course that matches a seasonal customer problem.
2. Promote that course during the relevant month and offer a 20% discount.
3. Stop repeating promotions if the sales no longer justify the email volume.
> "So like in January, the goat birthing course is 20% off. In April, the parasite course is 20% off because that's when you're gonna have parasite problems." — Deborah (~15:14)

**Turn repeated customer questions into reusable content** (operations)
1. Notice questions that would otherwise require a long email exchange.
2. Answer complex or broadly useful questions in a video or livestream.
3. Link viewers to the related articles, videos, courses, or membership resources.
> "So I'm just gonna hop on YouTube and do a live stream and say, well, if this is the case, then this is what you need to do." — Deborah (~21:20)

**Ask the audience what format they want** (validation_testing)
1. Survey the email list regularly about product ideas and preferred formats.
2. Compare interest in one-time course purchases with membership access.
3. Use the answers and actual sales trends to decide which offers to keep promoting.
> "And one of the questions that I started asking people was, do you prefer online courses with a one-time purchase price or a monthly membership" — Deborah (~28:22)

**Combine courses and community on one platform** (operations)
1. Put the courses and member discussions in the same place.
2. Make community access available at the basic membership level.
3. Use relevant course links to answer questions rather than rewriting the same explanation each time.
> "We moved the courses there two years ago and that made all the difference. Once the courses were there, then people are in that community discussion all the time." — Deborah (~33:40)

**Measure time use before hiring** (hiring_outsourcing)
1. Use a time-tracking tool to identify recurring work that takes too much of the founder's week.
2. Hire a virtual assistant for repeatable social media work.
3. Give the assistant clear routines for checking messages and linking people to existing resources.
4. Escalate complex cases to a human with the right subject expertise.
> "I discovered I was spending 15 hours a week on Facebook, and that was when I realized, oh my gosh, I need to hire somebody to do Facebook for me," — Deborah (~42:28)

**Republish original video on the website** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Compare revenue from embedded platform videos with videos hosted through the website's ad network.
2. Move original videos to the network that pays better for on-site viewing.
3. Prioritize the transfer after seeing the first month's results.
> "I immediately contacted my VA, and I'm like, okay, priority right now, we gotta start uploading our own videos to reactive" — Deborah (~41:05)

**Pre-sell a niche trip to fund deposits** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Find a knowledgeable local partner who can connect the group with suitable farms.
2. Promote the experience to an existing audience with a clear departure window.
3. Collect participant deposits before placing hotel deposits.
4. Build the itinerary around access that ordinary tourists would not have.
> "Now, in a wire, not a credit card, people got to give us money now, and they did." — Deborah (~48:58)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Build an email list early because it gives the founder a direct way to reach interested customers even when social or search platforms change.
- *niche_selection*: A focused niche can support a business when the creator keeps answering the audience's specific questions over time.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Use a book or free content to create trust and opportunities, but calculate the return on the time required to produce it.
- *customer_acquisition*: Give an audience useful updates while developing a product so customers can become invested before launch.
- *offer_design*: Make the paid offer meaningfully more detailed than the free or low-cost version; the course lets Deborah cover topics in far more depth than the book.
- *validation_testing*: Ask customers which format they want, then pay attention when their preferences and buying behavior change.
- *offer_design*: Recurring access and community can be more useful than a one-time course when customers need help over a long period.
- *operations*: Use member questions and existing archives to answer common support requests, while sending complex issues to a qualified human.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Measure where time goes before hiring; delegate repeatable work so the founder can focus on work they are good at and want to do.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Diversify how one niche earns money, since one channel can underperform while other channels grow.
- *mindset_execution*: Make space for the work that sustains the founder's motivation; Deborah returned to personal writing because it was the work she loved.
- *customer_acquisition*: A small, highly relevant audience can be enough; content need not go viral to reach the people most likely to trust and buy.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Deborah expected writing books to make her wealthy, but says the royalty income was low relative to the many hours she worked.
- Do not assume recorded farm footage will be usable; Deborah's first attempt had poor sound because she did not use a lapel mic.
- A course launch can be delayed by platform problems and production mistakes; Deborah told subscribers it was coming for months.
- Search traffic can fall sharply when Google changes how it presents answers.
- Course sales had declined over several years, and Deborah stopped sending repeated promotional emails when the sales no longer justified them.
- Animal-care advice has real consequences; Deborah says animals have died after people followed bad online advice.
- The in-person Portugal trip requires collecting deposits and arranging access to farms that do not normally offer agritourism.
- Deborah says she rarely gives a direct call to action in her videos, which may limit movement from free content to paid offers.
