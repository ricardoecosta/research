## Tactics and playbook steps

**Test billboard slogans with geo-targeted social ads** (validation_testing)
1. Use AI to generate candidate billboard slogans, keeping them short enough to read quickly.
2. Keep the podcast artwork and other ad copy the same so the slogan is the variable being tested.
3. Target people in the counties around the planned billboard and exclude people who have already interacted with the podcast.
4. Run the creative across Meta placements and compare cost per click.
5. Use the lowest-cost slogan as the billboard candidate.
> "And then I just look at the clicks. Lowest cost per click wins. And then I get because it's 950 bucks to print that billboard." — Chris Koerner (~12:01)

**Track billboard impact with a local download baseline** (validation_testing)
1. Export podcast downloads by city from Buzzsprout before the billboard goes live.
2. Define the nearby cities to include and calculate their share of all-time downloads as a baseline.
3. Repeat the export weekly and compare the local share over time.
4. Interpret the result cautiously because the data counts downloads, not unique listeners, and other episodes can affect totals.
> "I'm just going to run that export every week and see the raw number and the percentage as a ratio and see if it's climbing or not." — Chris Koerner (~17:22)

**Compare AI predictions with paid ad results** (validation_testing)
1. Give the same billboard slogans to several AI models and ask them to rank which will be most effective.
2. Run a real-world ad test with consistent creative and targeting.
3. Compare the model rankings with the actual click results to assess whether AI can shorten future testing.
> "Rank these from what you think would be most to least effective. See what the four models spit out and then compare it to the actual results from Facebook ads." — Nick (~12:49)

**Aggregate interview transcripts into a searchable dataset** (operations)
1. Keep speaker names in the transcript data so findings can be analyzed by person.
2. Clean timestamps and dead space, then combine the transcripts in Google Colab.
3. Ask ChatGPT to create a Python script, then use Claude to debug it when it fails.
4. Connect the OpenAI API directly to the notebook if a Zapier connection is unnecessary.
5. Ask ChatGPT to format the resulting spreadsheet and summarize interviews.
> "First couple times it doesn't work. Then I go to Claude. Claude fixes my Python script and it starts working." — Chris Koerner (~33:29)

**Use structured prompts to get more critical AI feedback** (operations)
1. Set a custom GPT role such as a hard-nosed content specialist.
2. Explain why direct, accurate feedback matters and ask for actionable critique.
3. Provide enough relevant context, then check whether the response reflects the information supplied.
> "I have a custom GPT where in the GPT prompt, I'm like, you're a hard-nosed, nononsense content specialist. I need you to give me direct advice" — Nick (~4:10)

**Ask AI to rank creative options, then iterate** (validation_testing)
1. Ask an AI model to generate and rank candidate titles or slogans.
2. Iterate on promising candidates rather than accepting the first output.
3. For campaigns, test the candidates with real audiences and compare the results with the AI ranking.
> "generate me the 10 best titles and rank them. And then I'll like iterate from there." — Nick (~12:37)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Build products close to revenue: customers can more easily value tools that help them generate sales and tie the tool to money earned.
- *automation_ai*: AI tools have different strengths: the speakers describe ChatGPT as a thought partner, Claude as a coding partner, Perplexity as a research tool, and Gemini as useful within Google Workspace.
- *automation_ai*: Give AI enough relevant context, but remember that it can only work with the information the user supplies.
- *validation_testing*: Interviewing active users can reveal practical workflows that are easy to miss when judging tools from brand reputation alone.
- *validation_testing*: Test advertising claims with a controlled real-world experiment and keep the creative consistent so the headline is the main variable.
- *other*: Pair software convenience with strong functionality; approachable tools can still fall short when users need to finish and ship a product.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI to speed up repeatable work, but keep human judgment in the loop for sensitive or consequential decisions.
- *customer_acquisition*: New channels can help a brand stand out, but track results with a baseline and accept that attribution may remain imperfect.

## Warnings and mistakes

- AI can give overly flattering feedback or reflect the incentive to keep users engaged; the speakers advise not treating it as automatically unbiased.
- If a user supplies one-sided or incomplete context, AI may return a one-sided answer. A shared mediation prompt would still require agreement on both the prompt and the output.
- Using AI to frame arguments in a relationship can create an unfair interaction if one person secretly uses the advice to persuade the other.
- Podcast download counts do not reveal the number of unique listeners, so a local increase may not mean a similar increase in new people.
- A local download increase may have other causes, such as a popular episode; compare the local share with overall trends and treat attribution as imperfect.
- An AI-generated app or workflow may be easy to start but difficult to finish; speakers describe incomplete Replit projects and note that some coding tools require coding experience.
- UK outbound sales automation must follow PECR rules for electronic marketing and screen cold calls against TPS/CTPS.
