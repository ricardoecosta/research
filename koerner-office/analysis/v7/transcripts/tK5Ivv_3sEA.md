## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build a small, relevant writing portfolio** (validation_testing)
1. Choose a writing format and subject area to demonstrate.
2. Create four or five samples; she suggests three blog posts, a landing page and another format as one possible mix.
3. Publish the work on a professional-looking site. She says publication by an established outlet is not required.
4. Use samples to demonstrate range and give prospects something concrete to review.
> "So, you don't need a some kind of a certification but you want to have produced I would say four maybe five pieces of writing." — Steph Spectre (~27:35)

**Use Upwork to find early work and recurring clients** (customer_acquisition)
1. Create a portfolio and apply for relevant jobs on Upwork.
2. When searching actively, choose up to 10 jobs a day that are interesting and offer a specific explanation of how you can help.
3. Look for one-off jobs that could lead to recurring needs, such as a company requesting one article that may need more.
4. Once trust is established, offer related work such as uploading the posts.
> "So, I can pitch that or they might just ask me, I can also offer to upload the blog posts myself, which is another great way to expand the contract" — Steph Spectre (~8:25)

**Write a tailored, human cold pitch** (customer_acquisition)
1. Keep the pitch to roughly four or five sentences.
2. Briefly introduce yourself and make a specific connection to the company or its work.
3. Do enough research to mention a detail that shows genuine interest; Steph gives listening to a prospect's podcast as an example.
4. Show a relevant portfolio sample and ask to connect.
5. For UK outreach, check the recipient type and consent rules before sending electronic marketing messages.
> "If you master the art of the cold pitch, right, which really I think comes down to four or five sentences where you are briefly introducing yourself." — Steph Spectre (~33:43)

**Choose a niche and make an honest personal connection** (niche_selection)
1. Review the kinds of writing work available, including books, corporate marketing, blogs, landing pages, ads and scripts.
2. Choose an area that holds your interest and develop skill in one format so clients can understand what you do.
3. Use a real connection to the prospect's product or work in the pitch.
4. Steph gives a beef protein powder brand as an example: she tried the product, sent honest feedback, then asked whether the company needed a writer.
> "I contacted the guy who ran the company. I said, "Hey, I tried this. This flavor is awesome. Like don't stop."" — Steph Spectre (~26:37)

**Quote projects after gathering scope** (pricing)
1. Ask how many pages or words the client needs and whether existing copy is available.
2. Review the current copy to estimate the amount of editing or new writing needed.
3. If the client agrees, record the discovery call with Granola and use its transcript to check details such as deadlines.
4. Set a project-based fee after clarifying scope, even if the estimate is informed by an internal hourly calculation.
> "You might even still be calculating it based on like an hourly rate, but there are no surprises. You know that like you know what the scope is." — Steph Spectre (~36:42)

**Use AI for selected steps in tutorial writing** (operations)
1. Record a screen walkthrough while narrating what is confusing or noteworthy.
2. Use Cursor to route video and audio processing to Gemini and capture the narration and difficult points.
3. Pass the notes through Claude with a template to create a first draft.
4. Edit the draft yourself, correcting inaccuracies and adding personality.
> "And then I want you to take all of those notes and I want you to take a template that I've created for this tutorial and I want you to feed it through Claude" — Steph Spectre (~5:22)

**Research software before writing about it** (operations)
1. Sign up for the products you are covering and test them yourself.
2. Read relevant Reddit threads and community discussion to see current user experiences.
3. Investigate complaints, such as confusing pricing, directly in the product or on its pricing page.
4. Capture screenshots where they help explain a tutorial or comparison.
> "A lot of it's testing the tools. A lot of it is also reading like just Reddit threads like what are people saying right now about these tools." — Steph Spectre (~13:09)

**Package college essay help** (pricing)
1. Sell the work in a package of five rather than only describing it as an hourly service.
2. Set the stated rate at $90 per hour and the package total at $450.
> "I sell it in packages of five at 90 an hour, so 450." — Steph Spectre (~37:19)

**Expand a trusted client relationship** (scaling)
1. Deliver the initial writing project well.
2. Ask what other related work would help the client.
3. Offer adjacent services, such as uploading completed posts or writing another format.
4. Look for opportunities to turn a one-off project into an ongoing contract.
> "anytime other people do like I always encourage people how else can you help this person once they like you once you have a good working relationship" — Steph Spectre (~8:39)

## Lessons

- *automation_ai*: Use AI where it saves meaningful effort, such as processing recordings or producing a first draft, and keep a human responsible for research, judgment and the final edit.
- *offer_design*: Direct experience with a product gives an article details that a generic AI draft may miss.
- *validation_testing*: A portfolio can be built without waiting for a paid client or formal certification; self-directed samples can demonstrate ability.
- *niche_selection*: A clear niche makes it easier for a client to understand why they should hire a freelancer.
- *customer_acquisition*: Tailored pitches work better when they show genuine familiarity with the prospect rather than generic flattery.
- *scaling*: Use a good first project to build trust and offer related work that can extend the contract.
- *pricing*: Clarify project scope before quoting so the client and freelancer know what is included and avoid surprises.
- *offer_design*: Distinctive human experience and personal voice can make writing more engaging than content that follows an expected formula.
- *mindset_execution*: Learning to write clearly remains useful even when AI tools are available, because people still value original thinking and articulation.
- *automation_ai*: When using an AI-generated reply for a real person, treat it as additional capacity and keep human responsibility for the relationship.
