### I2. Affinity/fraternal membership monetised with financial products

- **Customer:** Members of a shared-interest community (e.g. Catholic men, RV owners, concealed-carry holders, over-50s)
- **Problem:** Loneliness; members want belonging, discounts and tailored insurance
- **Offer:** Membership with events, chapters, magazine, discounts and preferred insurance products
- **Business model:** Membership dues plus insurance/financial products sold or white-labelled to members
- **Pricing:** Annual dues + insurance commissions/licensing. Knights of Columbus dues USD 300 per year; USCCA membership USD 300 per year Insurance is often provided by a partner (AARP licenses its name to UnitedHealthcare).
- **Acquisition:** existing_audience: Local chapters at churches (14,000 chapters); AARP mails everyone turning 50/60.; direct_mail_postcards: AARP sends a letter to people above 50 or 60.
- **Delivery:** Volunteer chapters run events and charity drives; insurance partner underwrites.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Knights of Columbus: 2-3M members, $30B assets, $5-10B annual revenue; USCCA ~ $400M/year revenue
- **Tools:** Knights of Columbus, Good Sam, USCCA, AARP
- **Startup cost:** not stated
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — Affinity membership works in the UK (e.g. Caravan and Motorhome Club, Saga), but selling insurance requires FCA authorisation or acting as an introducer/appointed representative.
- **Risks:** Insurance regulation; Community building is slow; Healthcare shares may not be legally obligated to pay

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Package a fringe trend with a killer one-line positioning** (product)
1. Watch niche subreddits (biohacking) for early signals
2. Frame the product against a huge emotional problem (divorce)
3. Use a memorable pitch line
> "I follow these weird subreddits with biohacking. And this is how I learned about GLP1s" — Sam Parr (~4:57)

**Affinity membership monetisation** (pricing)
1. Pick a deep identity community
2. Charge modest dues with discounts that justify membership
3. Partner with an insurer and license your brand (AARP/United model)
4. Monetise via financial products members trust you for

**Member-vote greenlighting** (product)
1. Show trailers/snippets to members
2. Greenlight only if >70% approve
3. Members become marketers

**Guerrilla stunt marketing** (acquisition)
1. Create an in-character social account without explaining
2. Stage an unexplained public stunt (empty Super Bowl seats, tuxedo, flowers)
3. Seat paid influencers nearby who will post about it

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Fringe health trends often go mainstream (GLP-1s, peptides, psychedelics); niche forums show early signals.
- *offer_design*: Positioning can make a strange product compelling: tie it to a huge, emotional problem.
- *offer_design*: Community memberships can monetise through trusted financial products far more than through dues.
- *niche_selection*: Loneliness is driving young men (30-40) into fraternal organisations.
- *niche_selection*: Look for the reaction to the dominant action as a source of ideas.
- *niche_selection*: Big incumbents become risk-averse, leaving ignored inventory a challenger can buy cheaply.
- *finance_unit_economics*: In movies and games, marketing spend often exceeds production cost.
- *customer_acquisition*: Strong values-based communities can greenlight and market products themselves.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Oxytocin efficacy and safety are understudied; it 'could still be bullshit'.
- Don't take substances because people discuss them on forums or podcasts.
- Healthcare shares are not legally obligated to pay claims.
- Film is a hits-driven business where even the biggest hit returns only ~2.3x and must cover many flops.
- Film-investment tax write-offs don't protect principal; you'll probably lose the money.
- Selling filtered versions of copyrighted films brought lawsuits.
