## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Ryan Schuetter, a 24-26 year old introverted Cincinnati ex-hospital project manager ($52k/yr) who now earns roughly $10-15k/month as an AI implementation consultant and educator. He started by having ~100-150 free, non-salesy video calls with people in his personal network and business groups, converting about 12 into paying clients (~$25k lifetime from the first 12). His offers are private AI coaching ($5-10k/month), month-long cohort groups ($1,000 per seat), and one-off implementation builds ($1k-$20k), organised around his DASA framework (Diagnose, Articulate, Systemize, AI). Key takeaway: relationship-led, value-first outreach plus education and free content can win clients without sales experience, though the model is human-led and network-dependent.

### I1. AI implementation consulting and education for small businesses (Ryan's overall business)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**100 free value-first calls with your existing network** (acquisition)
1. List contacts from phone, LinkedIn, ex-colleagues, family friends and business groups
2. Research each person's recent news on LinkedIn/Facebook and send a genuine rekindling message with no pitch
3. Exchange several casual messages before mentioning AI
4. Send batches of ~100 messages in the morning and reply through the day
5. Offer a free 1-hour Google Meet to provide value; aim for 100 real conversations (needed 3-4x reach-outs)
6. Do not sell on call one; send takeaways and lead magnets
7. Keep re-engaging with follow-ups and useful tidbits over months
> "I was just trying to get on the phone and provide value in any way I can. I'm not trying to pitch anything." — Ryan Schuetter (~4:13)

**Let offers emerge from conversations** (product)
1. Use free calls to learn what people value and what you're good at
2. Notice that education was valued most
3. Package into coaching, cohorts and builds
4. Start with a one-person trial cohort, then scale
> "this came from what I learned on the free calls with people." — Ryan Schuetter (~32:13)

**Lead magnet loop with unpolished YouTube content** (acquisition)
1. Record screen demos and tutorials in one take without heavy editing
2. Build prompt frameworks, SEO guides and Claude Code guides as reusable resources
3. Send the relevant resource after each call
4. Share niche-targeted videos to re-engage dormant contacts
> "a lot of these resources and guides, for instance, are like prompt frameworks, their SEO guides, their how to use clawed code." — Ryan Schuetter (~30:41)

**DASA framework** (sales)
1. Diagnose: find the real problem worth solving
2. Articulate: describe the dream outcome (time saved or money made)
3. Systemize: gather SOPs and design the working process
4. AI: only then implement AI
5. Calls 1-2 cover Diagnose and Articulate; Systemize and AI happen post-close
> "if you're automating chaos, you're scaling chaos." — Ryan Schuetter (~49:48)

**Elicit real problems with better questions** (sales)
1. Ask 'what is keeping you up at night?'
2. Ask follow-up 'why' questions
3. Research the business beforehand and ask about specific offerings
4. Use unexpected questions to get past polite answers
> "I love asking like what is the thing that is haunting you" — Ryan Schuetter (~52:30) [quote not matched verbatim]

**Daily 10 outreach to build the next cohort** (acquisition)
1. Pick a deliverable to teach
2. Text or call 10 contacts per day inviting them to join
3. Frame it as building alongside other business owners
4. Repeat continuously to avoid sales funnel stalls
> "I reach out to 10 people every day doing the same thing I've been doing for the past year." — Ryan Schuetter (~43:30)

**Give away the playbook to win repeat work** (sales)
1. Teach the exact build process in cohorts
2. Show clients results
3. They return for further projects and advice
> "I give every old thing away." — Ryan Schuetter (~46:33)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Your existing network is a large, underused sales channel; warm outreach beats cold at tiny scale (about 8% close from ~150 warm conversations).
- *mindset_execution*: Removing the selling goal ('I just want to help') lowers pressure for introverts and builds trust that converts later, often on the third conversation.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Early hourly returns can be terrible (~$2/hr); the real payoff is relationships, testimonials and offer discovery.
- *validation_testing*: Don't over-engineer tooling or CRM before you have traction; send the messages first.
- *offer_design*: Education and cohort offers let a solo operator sell many seats at once (about $250/hr effective) while building proof and follow-on implementation work.
- *customer_acquisition*: In-person networking is a differentiator versus the many online-only AI sellers and is likely to raise close rates.
- *offer_design*: Publishing low-production proof (demos, videos) gives credibility without needing a degree or audience.
- *offer_design*: Giving away know-how tends to produce more paid work rather than less because clients come back with further problems.
- *validation_testing*: Ask sharper, more emotional questions (what keeps you up at night) to surface problems owners can't articulate.
- *scaling*: Income from a personal-relationship model is lumpy; consistent daily outreach is needed to smooth it.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Rushing to give AI agent tools like OpenClaw access to business systems is risky.
- Automating a broken process just scales the chaos and makes the technology look bad.
- Mass cold email outreach produced nothing; do not rely on it.
- Pausing outreach causes income to stall; the introvert tendency to stop the funnel makes revenue spiral down.
- Don't use over-optimising CRM/tracking as procrastination before reaching out.
- Trying to solve non-existent problems with AI is a waste of time.
