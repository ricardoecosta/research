## Tactics and playbook steps

**Test price sensitivity with two landing pages** (pricing)
1. Create two otherwise identical offers with different prices.
2. Send comparable traffic to both versions.
3. Compare the number of people willing to pay at each price.
> "The second version of that message was the exact same except at $20 ununlocks.com. I just wanted to know how many more people are willing to pay $20 verse 10. It was an AB test." — Chris Koerner (~2:00)

**Use a scrape to test a business idea** (validation_testing)
1. Choose an active marketplace category with recent listings.
2. Scrape a small sample using a clear output schema such as price, URL, location, title, and date listed.
3. Export the results to a spreadsheet and check whether the fields were collected correctly.
4. Ask an AI tool to flag possible opportunities, then verify the candidates manually.
> "So, we're just going to use plain old English and some commas. So, let's do price, URL, location, title, date, and generate schema." — Chris Koerner (~9:04)

**Match marketplace supply to wanted posts** (customer_acquisition)
1. Search wanted listings to find buyers who have stated what they want.
2. Record the buyer's exact requirements.
3. Search other marketplace listings for matching sellers.
4. Send only relevant leads and test whether the buyer will pay per lead.
> "If someone is posting to a marketplace with the word wanted, they're saying, "Take my money. I want to spend money on something." So, then what you need to do is go find the seller for that thing." — Chris Koerner (~6:26)

**Look for motivated sellers and broader resale demand** (acquisition)
1. Search marketplace listings for the phrase "need gone" to find potentially motivated sellers.
2. Compare the asking price with likely prices on a marketplace with a larger audience.
3. Check condition, availability, and comparable prices before buying.
4. Sell only with clear availability and accurate item details.
> "Now, I'm going to type in one of the most legendary keywords ever for any marketplace, and that is need gone." — Chris Koerner (~6:07)

**Filter vehicle listings before reviewing them** (operations)
1. Start with a recent time window so already-sold cars are less likely to appear.
2. Filter for owner-listed vehicles rather than dealerships.
3. Collect mileage, year, title, and other vehicle fields.
4. Check descriptions for non-running or parts-only language before treating a listing as a deal.
5. Compare promising vehicles with other market listings.
> "I'm going to assume that those are unfairly priced to their benefit and I won't find any good deals there. I'm going to go to posted today only because if it was a good price posted 4 days ago, it's probably gone. Sold by owner." — Chris Koerner (~16:52)

**Use AI as a research assistant, then verify** (operations)
1. Give the AI the listing fields and ask it to explain which items appear underpriced.
2. Include titles and condition clues in the prompt.
3. For promising cars, ask an agent to cross-reference other listings.
4. Verify the listing and condition before acting on the AI's result.
> "So I'm going to redownload this as a spreadsheet. We're going to go back to Trust Deal Chad GPT. We're going to upload it here. My prompt, where are the best card deals in this sheet and why?" — Chris Koerner (~20:05)

**Use auction history to shortlist cars** (operations)
1. Scrape the auction inventory before the auction.
2. Compare each car with previous auction sale results.
3. Shortlist vehicles that appear mispriced.
4. Have a person inspect and buy the selected vehicles for the client.
> "And before the auctions, the night before, we would scrape all the auction data and find alpha, which of these cars are probably mispriced based on previous auction sale results." — Chris Koerner (~21:51)

**Build a focused job board** (product)
1. Choose a niche rather than covering every job category.
2. Collect relevant job listings from a source such as Craigslist.
3. Publish listings and build traffic to the job board.
4. Charge businesses to post jobs.
> "How about you make a niche job board? There's a lot of people out there making a lot of passive income. And you know, I don't say that very often with job boards." — Chris Koerner (~22:29)

**Connect tools already in use to a business idea** (mindset)
1. Identify a tool you already use to find or buy something.
2. Ask how the same tool could help find customers for a business.
3. Use the tool to run a small test before building a more complex system.
> "We need to connect the dots. And so, I'm over here thinking like, how can I make money with scraped Craigslist data while using ChatGpt to help me scrape the Craigslist data?" — Chris Koerner (~12:24)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Start by finding a clear gap between what a seller asks and what another marketplace's buyers may pay.
- *customer_acquisition*: A wanted listing can reveal a buyer before you have found the supply.
- *customer_acquisition*: More buyer attention can raise the chance that someone values a product or property above the typical market price.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI and software already available to help test an idea instead of waiting until you know every tool or technique.
- *validation_testing*: Use AI for screening and idea generation, then verify listing details and condition yourself.
- *pricing*: Test the economics with a small, measurable experiment before assuming a price or offer will work.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Higher-ticket opportunities may need fewer successful transactions to produce meaningful revenue, but they also carry more capital and diligence risk.
- *mindset_execution*: Do not treat an attractive data result as proof of a business; expect early tests to disappoint and keep evaluating.
- *operations*: Scraping can be a repeatable starting point for several business models, but the customer, offer, and fulfilment still need to be worked out.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The phone-unlocking growth hack eventually stopped working.
- The host's example of scraping seller contact details and sending unsolicited email or texts creates a UK compliance issue: unsolicited marketing to individuals and sole traders requires consent under the rule specified by the user.
- The Zillow low-offer experiment mostly received no response or rejection, according to the host.
- Scraped or AI-ranked listings are only leads; the host tells viewers to check descriptions and confirm whether cars are running before treating them as deals.
- The host says idea execution can disappoint at first and advises continuing through that early period rather than quitting.
- The Oxyabs segment is promotional: the host calls the company a friend of the channel, provides a tracked link, and offers a discount code.
- The host's cross-listing example does not explain ownership or seller authorization; listing a vehicle without clear authority or accurate availability could mislead buyers.
