## Tactics and playbook steps

**Automate any task you can write as a list of steps** (operations)
1. Pick a task you could hand to an intern as a point-form list
2. Avoid tasks with many conditionals and edge cases
3. Encode your expertise and context into the workflow
4. Keep a human in the loop for high-nuance outputs
> "if you can define something as a list of steps, it's kind of ripe for automation" — Max Brodeur (~8:06)

**Use categorisation buckets instead of numeric scoring** (product)
1. Use discrete buckets such as extremely high value, high value, normal, low value
2. Avoid 0-100 scores because models give mediocre scores when uncertain
> "I tend to do categorization instead of scoring" — Max Brodeur (~18:41)

**Match model to task** (product)
1. Use a cheaper fast model for simple high-volume labelling
2. Use a stronger reasoning model for synthesis over many items
3. Do not economise on models for high-importance workflows
> "use like a a cheaper model that is faster because sometimes the task is so easy" — Max Brodeur (~43:56)

**Find automation opportunities from repetitive Ctrl+F or copy-paste work** (product)
1. Notice repetitive search or copy-paste tasks
2. Ask whether an LLM could do the reasoning
3. Build a wrapper or workflow for it

**Review drafts before sending; auto-send only templated emails** (sales)
1. Save researched, personalised emails as drafts and review
2. Auto-send only simple templated emails with a calendar link
> "I always save emails as drafts and then I'm the one sending it off." — Max Brodeur (~16:33)

## Lessons

- *automation_ai*: Treat AI like a new employee: give it context, branding and examples, and encode your deep process knowledge into the workflow.
- *automation_ai*: Workflows with defined steps beat open-ended agents for near-term reliable business value.
- *niche_selection*: Niche domain knowledge plus tooling is the moat; the value is knowing what to automate, not engineering.
- *scaling*: Getting in early on a platform ecosystem (as an expert or agency) can be a way to get ahead, but it carries platform risk.
- *offer_design*: Each automation can be a productised service: look at any internal workflow and ask if it can be sold as a subscription report.
- *offer_design*: Show the customer the outcome, not the tooling: white-label workflows behind a simple form.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Use cheap models for high-volume simple tasks and keep spend on reasoning for high-value steps.
- *scaling*: Small teams of exceptional people with AI can move like large companies.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Fully autonomous agents have been unreliable and expensive; asking an AI to write a sales email without context yields slop.
- Don't spam people with AI-generated outreach; keep a human in the loop for personalised emails.
- Templates are a band-aid; screen-recording-based automation discovery may underdeliver and burn user trust.
