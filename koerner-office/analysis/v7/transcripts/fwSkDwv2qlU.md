## Tactics and playbook steps

**Start with people already in the target industries** (acquisition)
1. Write down names of people you already know in real estate, construction management, roofing, insurance, property management, solar, restoration, engineering, facilities, or schools.
2. Ask a relevant contact for an introduction or a first job.
3. Prioritize a partner who can refer repeat work over a one-off customer.

**Use outsourced drone work to practice** (operations)
1. Find companies that farm out local drone jobs.
2. Take operator work to build flight experience and learn the delivery process.
3. Use that practice before taking responsibility for a direct customer job.

**Prepare each flight before arriving** (operations)
1. Check whether the site is in regulated airspace and request approval when needed.
2. Check the weather and understand the roof or site layout using Google Maps.
3. Arrive early and check equipment.
4. Capture the data, export and upload files, run the analysis, and review the final deliverable.

**Build local search visibility** (acquisition)
1. Create a website with service-specific keywords and pages for named cities.
2. Fully complete the Google Business Profile with photos and videos.
3. Ask every customer for a review after a job.
4. Test narrower terms such as drone facade inspection against broad roof inspection terms.

**Ask for the review while the customer is present** (acquisition)
1. After the job, ask whether the customer was satisfied.
2. If they say yes, ask them to leave a review immediately.
3. Politely wait while they use their phone, rather than relying on a later reminder.
4. When a partner referred the job, ask the partner for a review as well.

**Test paid acquisition with a small budget** (validation_testing)
1. Test a service and message with a limited Google or Facebook Ads budget.
2. Measure lead cost and close rate.
3. Adjust the message or target terms before increasing spend.

**Publish repeatable drone footage** (acquisition)
1. Save short clips from normal jobs.
2. Post visually clear drone footage and before-and-after examples on social platforms and YouTube.
3. Reuse suitable clips on the website and local search pages.

**Land with an inspection and expand into adjacent services** (sales)
1. Win a core inspection or documentation job with a property or construction business.
2. Offer social-media footage from the same visit.
3. Offer recurring construction-site material documentation where it fits.
4. Use the existing relationship to identify other adjacent needs.

**Build storm relationships before a storm** (acquisition)
1. Develop relationships with roofing businesses before severe weather arrives.
2. When a storm affects an area, coordinate with those businesses on imagery or documentation needs.
3. Do not assume an aerial view proves damage; agree how leads will be verified and shared.

**Sell a physical aerial print** (sales)
1. Capture a distinctive image of a property while doing permitted work.
2. Show the owner a finished print and make a clear one-off offer.
3. Get the owner's permission before flying over or photographing a home.

**Load debris densely and follow the station's weighing process** (operations)
1. Use a trailer suited to the debris and build up its sides as described in the story.
2. Load brush densely, take the trailer to the weigh station, and follow the program's unloading and weighing process.
3. Confirm the actual rate and density calculation before relying on the example economics.

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Start with a service that solves a clear, costly problem and has repeat customers, rather than relying only on novelty.
- *mindset_execution*: A side business can be tested alongside a job; use early jobs to gain experience before making a larger commitment.
- *mindset_execution*: For an early-stage business, focus first on getting customers and delivering reliably; additional management complexity comes later.
- *niche_selection*: A modest licensing and training requirement can discourage casual competitors while remaining manageable for a determined founder.
- *offer_design*: Use a core service to earn trust, then offer adjacent work that uses information or footage already collected.
- *customer_acquisition*: Relationships with organizations that can refer recurring work may be more valuable than finding isolated one-off customers.
- *customer_acquisition*: Ask directly for customer reviews while the customer is present; a brief awkward moment may produce more reviews than a later reminder.
- *validation_testing*: Validate demand with a limited advertising test, adjust the message, and then decide whether to scale spend.
- *niche_selection*: Specialize in a harder-to-copy service first, then sell simpler adjacent work after building customer trust.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Include preparation, administration, and delivery time when estimating capacity and profit; the visible task is only part of the job.
- *customer_acquisition*: Make content from real work when the service naturally produces visual before-and-after material.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The market size, growth, vacancy, margin, and customer-acquisition figures are claims from the hosts' research or second-hand accounts; the video does not provide source citations.
- The hosts say that in some US states calling the service an inspection can trigger additional licensing requirements. Check applicable UK rules before using the term or offering regulated inspection work.
- The transcript describes flight approvals, weather checks, insurance, and safe operation as necessary parts of delivery; skipping them could create safety and licensing problems.
- Metal roofs can interfere with the drone signal, according to the hosts' account of an operator's experience.
- The hosts describe confrontational onlookers, reporting risk, and filming from the sidewalk. Operators still need to respect privacy, permissions, and the rules that apply to each flight.
- The hosts suggest manufacturing a confrontation with a friend and trying to make it go viral. Staging a misleading event could damage customer trust.
- The hosts say general drone photography has lower barriers and less differentiation than specializing in inspections.
- The hosts say a solo operator may handle only about two roofs a day after travel, preparation, analysis, file handling, and administration.
- Storm jobs can bring substantial reported revenue but may be uneven and require travel; the hosts say one operator may chase storms several hours away.
- The speakers' quoted thermal and 3D mapping price ranges are malformed in the transcript captions and should not be treated as reliable price guidance.
- The video includes a paid TK Owners community promotion and a free newsletter and podcast promotion. These create a promotional interest even though the hosts joke about not selling a course.
- The hosts describe cold messaging people in storm-affected areas. In the UK, unsolicited electronic marketing to individuals and sole traders is restricted without consent.
