## Tactics and playbook steps

**Use a local booking-and-subcontractor model** (operations)
1. Create a local service brand and website.
2. Show upfront prices and allow customers to book and pay online.
3. Send each booked job to a local subcontractor who already performs the service.
4. Share the job revenue between the operator and subcontractor; the transcript does not state a split.
> "He just gets the customers, sends the job out to small local cleaners that are maybe not the best marketers and they're looking for jobs." — Tim Richard (~1:37)

**Build pages for specific services in each city** (acquisition)
1. Choose a specific service rather than a broad category.
2. Create a page for that service in each target city.
3. Target lower-competition terms such as item removal, recycling, or replacement searches.
4. Use search visibility to bring inbound customers to online booking.
> "I build pages for each one of those in each city, and it's a lot easier to rank on Google when you have a very specific page for a very specific service" — Tim Richard (~5:10)

**Make the online quote depend on clear job inputs** (product)
1. Ask customers for the details that change the job price.
2. For cleaning, collect bedrooms, square footage, bathrooms, and add-ons.
3. For gutter cleaning, ask about house size and number of stories.
4. For dog-waste removal, ask how many dogs the customer has and how often service is wanted.
> "The customers go to his website, pick how many bedrooms they have, the square footage of their house, bathrooms, any add-ons that they want." — Tim Richard (~1:21)

**Add repeat contracts to naturally recurring services** (pricing)
1. Offer carpet cleaning on a recurring schedule, such as every six months or every year.
2. Offer discounted repeat gutter cleaning to encourage a subscription.
3. Group repeat commercial window-cleaning jobs into routes.

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Target a specific service in a specific city; Tim says narrow pages can be easier to rank than broad service pages.
- *offer_design*: Show the price and let customers book online to reduce uncertainty and friction.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Look for repeat demand so each customer can generate recurring bookings.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Use subcontractors who already know how to deliver the service instead of trying to perform every job yourself.
- *operations*: Local route density can make recurring physical services more practical.
- *offer_design*: A simple, transparent offer can be a point of differentiation in a market where competitors give unclear or high prices.
- *validation_testing*: Start by researching local demand and competition rather than assuming an idea will work.
- *automation_ai*: A digital front end can reduce the need to own vehicles, buildings, or equipment, but the physical work still has to be delivered locally.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Tim says the examples do not guarantee success or the same earnings, and advises viewers to research before starting.
- Tim says house cleaning is the most common version of the model and may face more competition.
- The large earnings, search-volume, low-competition, and workload claims are presented without supporting records or independent verification.
- Physical services still depend on subcontractor quality, availability, insurance, and safe delivery, even when sales and booking happen online.
