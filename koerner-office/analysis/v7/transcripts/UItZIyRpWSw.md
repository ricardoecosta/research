## Tactics and playbook steps

**Test the offer while the product is being built** (validation_testing)
1. Build a minimal version of the product around a problem the founder experiences directly.
2. Set up an ad campaign before the app is complete so demand can be tested while implementation continues.
3. Pause ads if checkout is broken so traffic does not continue spending without a path to purchase.
4. Return to the ad campaign after payment and signup flows work.
> "I'm going to start seeing if I can make ads for this before it's even done because what do I always say? Sell it before you have it." — Chris Koerner (~10:10)

**Start with the audience closest to the problem** (niche_selection)
1. Begin with the customer group whose problem the founder understands personally.
2. Write creative using language and examples that group recognizes.
3. Expand to adjacent customer types only after learning from the first audience.
> "I want to start with the audience that's closest in line with the problem that I'm experience and trying to solve with this product." — Chris Koerner (~14:14)

**Use specific creative to qualify paid traffic** (acquisition)
1. Record a short video describing the exact customer, situation, and pain point.
2. Put niche-specific details in the spoken creative so poor-fit viewers can skip the ad.
3. Use the landing-page pixel to measure landing page views and later purchase events.
4. Review actual delivery geography and narrow the audience if the platform is finding cheap clicks from poor-fit areas.
> "So, what that means is if I'm a 72-year-old woman who's not an active member of the LDS church, I'm going to swipe away from that so hard, which is perfect for me" — Chris Koerner (~31:05)

**Post a useful story in communities before adding a link** (acquisition)
1. Choose a relevant community and check its promotion rules and recent high-performing posts.
2. Tell a true, specific story about the problem and what was built.
3. Initially leave out the product link; answer questions in the comments and add the link only if allowed.
4. Use a screenshot or other relevant image in Facebook groups, where the founder says visual posts perform better.
> "as it starts to do well and you see the views climb and you see people are commenting, a answer comments telling people what it is cuz that's usually allowed." — Chris Koerner (~67:19)

**Track ads with a pixel and per-ad UTMs** (operations)
1. Install a Meta pixel with view-content, checkout, and purchase events.
2. Add UTM parameters to the landing-page URL to identify the source ad.
3. Compare clicks, landing page views, video plays, and purchases instead of relying on click counts alone.
4. Save a custom Ads Manager column preset containing only the metrics needed for decisions.
> "A UTM parameter is basically adding something like that to a link so you can track where that link came from." — Chris Koerner (~73:51)

**Make setup and checkout low-friction** (offer_design)
1. Allow customers to build an event and generate a link before asking them to pay.
2. Show prices clearly rather than implying the product is free.
3. Keep signers account-free and make the form mobile-first.
4. Check the real click path before claiming a specific number of clicks.
> "A CTA that lies by one click is worse than a boring one." — Chris Koerner (~25:10)

**Choose a simple price and test lifetime access** (pricing)
1. Start with one annual plan and no free plan.
2. Consider whether a low one-off lifetime option better fits customers who need the product for one event.
3. Use actual conversion and retention data before deciding whether to remove a lifetime option.
> "My bet is that people need this for onetime use. We have subscription fatigue, so I'd rather get $17 than $0 from them." — Chris Koerner (~8:42)

**Test the complete payment flow before scaling spend** (operations)
1. Run the organizer flow from event creation through payment and account setup.
2. Use test mode while validating, then confirm the live Stripe account is connected before taking real payments.
3. Verify the generated link and signed-copy workflow after checkout succeeds.
4. Pause the ad campaign while resolving live checkout errors.
> "So, for now, I'm going to pause my ad. Okay, I did the hard part. I built it. I'm going to pause it. Okay, for now so I don't burn money." — Chris Koerner (~57:00)

**Fix the product from observed user friction** (product)
1. Use screenshots and exact steps to report checkout errors to the coding agent.
2. Test the product on a real phone and complete a real payment flow.
3. Add required form fields such as emergency contact and medical information after checking the source form.
4. Add a working contact form so visitors can report problems.
> "The version of the form that was live a few days ago when I last filmed was not thorough enough. It didn't ask for allergies or emergency contact." — Chris Koerner (~65:34)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Start by solving a problem the founder personally understands, then use that knowledge to choose the first audience and the product requirements.
- *mindset_execution*: Do not let prompt preparation become a reason to delay building; a workable first prompt can be improved after the product exists.
- *operations*: The final refinement stage can take substantial time, so plan for testing and repeated fixes after the initial build appears complete.
- *validation_testing*: Test demand while the product is being built, but pause spend when there is no working path for a visitor to become a customer.
- *offer_design*: Make the first version simple and remove unnecessary steps from signup and checkout, while being clear about when payment is required.
- *customer_acquisition*: Specific creative can filter out poor-fit viewers and help the ad platform learn from people who engage.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Measure landing page views and purchases, not just link clicks, because clicking an ad does not mean the visitor reached or bought from the site.
- *customer_acquisition*: Community promotion can work when it starts with an honest, relevant story and respects the rules of the group.
- *offer_design*: Trust signals matter for a new product: show the actual founder and product, state that it is new, and offer a clear way to get in touch.
- *finance_unit_economics*: The first customer provides proof that someone will pay, but the founder's own account shows that a sale alone does not prove the economics work.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use customer and campaign data to adjust the audience instead of assuming the ad platform will automatically find the right buyer.
- *automation_ai*: Treat AI as a useful operator but verify its work, provide missing context, and push back when it declines a task or makes the wrong choice.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not copy the church's copyrighted permission form into the product. The founder says he used it as a blueprint for fields instead.
- The founder found that Meta no longer allowed the religion-based detailed targeting he initially expected, so he used audience-specific ad copy and later narrowed geography.
- Do not run paid traffic while the checkout is broken; the founder paused the ad after spending money while customers could not buy.
- Several checkout failures came from confusion between Stripe test and live modes and from the live Stripe account not being attached correctly.
- Publishing repeatedly while the agent is still changing the app can catch changes mid-build and cause a failed publish.
- Do not make false community posts or misleading landing-page claims; moderators may remove posts and trust can be damaged.
- Avoid claiming an exact click count until the real user path has been checked.
- The waiver touches legal and medical information, and a form template may not be suitable for every organization or jurisdiction. The transcript does not establish legal validity or compliance.
- A low annual price can be overwhelmed by paid acquisition costs; the founder says his spend exceeded the first annual subscription.
- The app was new and had bugs, so the founder added a contact form and a lifetime refund guarantee to reduce buyer concern.
