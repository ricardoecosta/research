## Tactics and playbook steps

**Zero-to-launch newsletter on Beehiiv** (product)
1. Sign up and name the publication
2. Pick a website template (or AI-generate from a screenshot)
3. Add a subscribe form
4. Choose an email template
5. Create a welcome automation; add a branch for non-openers after 7 days
6. Publish weekly, not daily, to avoid burnout
> "So start weekly and as you grow in scale and and find opportunities to monetize, then you can always ramp that up to to daily." — Tyler Denk (~10:05)

**Lead magnet with automated delivery** (acquisition)
1. Create a valuable asset (e.g. your fundraising seed deck)
2. Build a dedicated subscribe page
3. Promote on X, LinkedIn and ads
4. Trigger an automation that emails the asset on signup

**Free growth via recommendation swaps** (acquisition)
1. Recommend up to four similar-sized newsletters in your niche
2. They get notified and often recommend back
3. Use magic links in friends' newsletters for one-click subscribes
> "recommendations is a free way to be able to get other people in the ecosystem referring you." — Tyler Denk (~34:17)

**Boost and ads flywheel** (finance)
1. Acquire subscribers via Facebook ads (~$1.45-2) or Boost offers (~$2.50)
2. Apply to paying Boost offers in your niche and recommend them to new subscribers ($1-3 each)
3. Sell sponsorships and ad network placements
4. Reinvest income in growth, raising sponsor rates as the list grows
> "I want income in. I take that income and revenue and I put that right back into Facebook ads and boost to grow faster." — Tyler Denk (~38:35)

**Survey-driven media kit** (sales)
1. Send new subscribers a survey (role, skills, birth year, goals, revenue)
2. Segment by answers
3. Build a media kit with audience data, ad format (150 words) and case studies (41 demos, 1.7% CTR)
4. Use it to charge $5-7k per send
> "I was able to take this first party data that I've collected and turn it into a great slide to help sell my audience." — Tyler Denk (~51:03)

**Goal reminder automation** (operations)
1. Ask subscribers their 12-month goal at signup
2. Email them their own answer 12 months later
3. One of the most engaged emails

**Segment the list for permissioned extra emails** (operations)
1. Ask the whole list to opt into a 'focus group'
2. Send polls on content ideas to that segment only
3. Segment by listening frequency, investment capacity etc. to target offers
> "I just firmly believe that the more you segment your list, the more money you make." — Chris Koerner (~46:12)

**Monetise an audience with high-ticket events** (pricing)
1. Understand what the audience wants (founders want to meet founders)
2. Run a vetted $10,000 mastermind retreat
3. Need only ~8 attendees from a 120k list
> "all I need of those 120,000 readers is eight people to have interest in going to this founder mastermind" — Tyler Denk (~20:11)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: List quality beats size: 5,000 CFOs can be worth more than millions of general readers.
- *niche_selection*: Work backwards from who would pay to reach your audience before choosing a niche.
- *offer_design*: Monetise beyond ads: events, consulting and experiences can far exceed per-subscriber ad income.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Know your subscriber acquisition cost and lifetime value; when LTV is several times CAC, paid growth is a flywheel.
- *pricing*: First-party survey data turns an audience into a sellable media kit and lets you raise prices.
- *offer_design*: Design should be either deliberately ugly/ironic or genuinely beautiful, not in between.
- *mindset_execution*: Readers don't notice a missed week as much as creators fear; consistency is about not quitting.
- *scaling*: Owning an audience creates optionality for many monetisation routes.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Don't start with a daily newsletter; it's a grind and leads to quitting.
- Emailing invalid or non-opted-in addresses raises bounce rates and risks being kicked off the platform; in the UK, marketing emails also need prior consent under PECR.
- Big established newsletters rarely reciprocate recommendations to tiny new ones.
- Advice comes from Beehiiv's co-founder demonstrating Beehiiv, and Beehiiv sponsors the show, so platform claims are promotional.
- Mass-market newsletters are harder to scale than niche ones for most people.
