## Tactics and playbook steps

**Start with a warm client** (customer_acquisition)
1. List business owners you already know who have email lists.
2. Offer to help them use the list to generate repeat business.
3. Consider a no-risk fee structure where payment is due only after measurable revenue is generated.
4. Ask a satisfied client to introduce you to other business owners.
> "But most importantly, you need to manage the email list of people you know and help them make more money with it." — Chris Koerner (~14:54)

**Find and segment engaged subscribers** (operations)
1. Import the client's historical list data where available.
2. Use engagement, geography, tenure, acquisition source, and purchase status to identify useful subscriber groups.
3. Create a segment of highly engaged subscribers before sending an invitation or offer.
4. Ask the client to personally call the strongest prospects where appropriate.
> "And then I'm going to have to do number two and three because I think those are important as well and are not a very big lift." — Chris Koerner (~11:50)

**Estimate a repeat-purchase campaign** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Estimate how many subscribers are still active.
2. Apply an explicit conversion assumption to the engaged group.
3. Multiply the expected customers by the claimed average project value to estimate gross revenue.
4. Label the result as revenue, not profit, and verify assumptions with a real client campaign before promising an outcome.
> "That's $12,000 a month in revenue, not profit." — Chris Koerner (~13:50)

**Seed a community with likely participants** (operations)
1. Select 10 to 20 highly engaged people as founding members.
2. Make the community's purpose obvious at a glance.
3. Give new members a simple first assignment and provide a few low-pressure conversation starters.
4. Reply to every comment during the first 14 days.
> "Seed 10 to 20 founding members by hand. Now, those have to be very like super fans, highly engaged people." — Chris Koerner (~9:17)

**Keep community structure small and active** (operations)
1. Start with three or four channels, or test a few more if the right categories are unclear.
2. Observe which channels get activity.
3. After a month or three, reduce the structure to the two or three channels members use most.
4. Add one recurring weekly ritual with the same post on the same day.
> "So you might even want to add five to six channels and then just know in a month or three you're going to cut that down to two to three, the ones that are the most active" — Chris Koerner (~10:31)

**Use the newsletter-to-community moment** (offer_design)
1. Treat the newsletter as the lower-friction first product.
2. Invite subscribers to the community when they first join the newsletter.
3. Give the invitation a clear explanation of the community's purpose.
> "But remember, the most likely time a newsletter subscriber would ever even want to think about your community is the second they subscribe to your newsletter." — Chris Koerner (~19:30)

**Use AI to explore subscriber data and platform actions** (automation_ai)
1. Connect the email platform's MCP to the selected AI tool.
2. Ask it to explain the list import process if the client is new to the platform.
3. Ask it to rank subscribers or identify segments using available engagement and profile data.
4. Review permissions and require human approval for consequential actions such as sending campaigns.
> "If you don't know how to do it, just create a free Beehive account, connect it to Claude or whatever, and then say, "I have this email list exported from my friend's Mailchimp account." — Chris Koerner (~7:11)

**Test a local newsletter from an existing list** (validation_testing)
1. Check whether the business's subscribers are concentrated in a local area.
2. Ask whether they would value local information rather than assuming a plumbing customer wants a plumbing discussion group.
3. Test a small local newsletter concept with the list owner before changing the list's purpose.
> "So what's stopping you from helping your plumbing business owner friend from launching a local newsletter off the back of his email list?" — Chris Koerner (~11:13)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: An existing audience has little value if the owner does not connect it to a clear offer or a useful next step.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI to reduce the time and expertise needed to operate software, while checking important actions and outputs.
- *customer_acquisition*: Look for existing assets and relationships before trying to build an audience from scratch.
- *operations*: A community needs a clear identity, a simple first action for members, and active participation from its operator.
- *customer_acquisition*: Early engagement can matter more than tenure: Koerner says newly subscribed readers are often especially receptive to an invitation.
- *validation_testing*: Use actual participation to decide which community channels to keep instead of assuming every proposed category will be active.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Treat acquisition math as a hypothesis to test; a revenue projection does not establish profit or a repeatable result.
- *niche_selection*: When inviting someone to a community, make the benefit clear and avoid assuming that a customer's original reason for joining matches the new offer.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The central $12,000 revenue example is hypothetical and explicitly described as revenue, not profit; the conversion rate and project value are assumptions.
- Koerner says a community can fail when it looks inactive and emphasizes the difficulty of getting both readers and writers to participate.
- Koerner recommends replying to every comment for the first 14 days, indicating that early community operation is not hands-off.
- Koerner says a newsletter list does not automatically produce revenue; it must be used effectively.
- The AI should not be given unrestricted access without considering what it might do. Koerner says users can set permissions to require approval, block actions, or always allow them, and advises doing their own research.
- The host discloses an affiliate link for Beehiiv and promotes a discount code, creating a promotional conflict in his positive platform recommendation.
- The transcript suggests using a business's email list for new offers or a local newsletter but does not address list consent, privacy notices, or marketing permissions.
