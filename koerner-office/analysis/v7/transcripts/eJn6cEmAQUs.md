## Tactics and playbook steps

**Set a business-linked personal draw** (finance)
1. Decide how much money you are allowed to take out of the business.
2. Increase that amount as the business grows and reduce it when the business struggles.
3. To support a larger personal lifestyle, build a better business instead of taking extra money from it.
> "As the business grows, that number grows. As the business struggles, that number shrinks. If I want a bigger lifestyle, I don't pull out more from the business, I have to build a better business." — David Heacock (~4:14)

**Hire leaders who take ownership of whole functions** (hiring)
1. Look for leaders who can permanently own an entire function rather than hiring people only to fill seats.
2. Use an operations leader to turn day-to-day chaos into an operations function.
3. Use a finance leader to turn a messy back office into a finance function.
> "At Filterbuy, the most valuable hires weren't seat fillers, they were leaders who took an entire function off my plate permanently." — David Heacock (~2:48)

**Turn repeatable tasks into software processes** (automation)
1. Find repeatable work that is messy or depends on someone remembering a step.
2. Consider a one-time software or automation investment to remove the task or make it a reliable process.
3. Aim for systems that keep working when the owner is not in the room.
> "If I can spend a dollar one time and kill a task forever, that's a great investment. The business shouldn't depend on one person remembering one thing." — David Heacock (~3:33)

**Screen large purchases against personal priorities** (finance)
1. Before buying something large, ask whether it buys back time, improves family life, or helps build the business.
2. Check whether the purchase is mainly status driven.
3. Compare the benefit with leaving the money inside the business.
> "Before I buy something big, I ask myself, "Does this buy me back time? Does it make my family's life better? Does it help me build the business? Or is this just a status driven by ego?"" — David Heacock (~5:53)

**Judge private travel by time saved at business scale** (finance)
1. Compare the cost of private travel with the time it saves in airports.
2. Consider whether the saved time can be used in facilities, meetings, and time at home.
3. Treat the expense as scale-dependent; David says it would bankrupt a hypothetical $2 million company.
> "The plane lets me be in three places in a day and still get home to my family that night." — David Heacock (~2:01)

**Outsource chores that crowd out valued time** (operations)
1. Identify household chores that take time but bring little enjoyment.
2. Consider cleaning or household help to free time for family or work.
> "I don't get any joy out of spending my weekend doing laundry. I'd rather spend that time with my wife and my children." — David Heacock (~2:16)

## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: Keep personal spending from rising automatically with business success; tie withdrawals to business performance.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Spend on time when the value of the time saved justifies the cost.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: A strong hire can create lasting leverage when they take responsibility for a whole function.
- *automation_ai*: Use software and automation to turn repeatable tasks into processes that do not depend on individual memory.
- *offer_design*: Evaluate major purchases by whether they support time, family, health, or business rather than status.
- *mindset_execution*: Set a spending limit in advance, then spend within it on things you value without repeatedly second-guessing the decision.
- *operations*: Buying back time can mean outsourcing ordinary chores, not only using expensive travel.
- *finance_unit_economics*: The same purchase can make sense at one business scale and be ruinous at another.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Lifestyle costs that keep rising can make personal finances depend on the business performing perfectly, encouraging short-term decisions and less reinvestment.
- Treating the business as a personal piggy bank can starve it of the money it needs to grow.
- Status purchases may provide only temporary satisfaction and lead to wanting the next purchase.
