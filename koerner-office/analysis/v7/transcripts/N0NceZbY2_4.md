## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Sariah (27, Utah), who started Memory Magnets Co with under $1,000 in 2024 after her husband's low pay and second job left the family broke. She sells custom photo magnets online, at markets and as an on-the-spot wedding/event service, and sells magnet-making machine kits plus refill supplies and courses. She claims $1.63M online revenue in her first full year (about $600K profit), a $233K best month and a $100.5K first six-figure month after six months. Key takeaway: a simple, high-margin physical product plus a service at price-insensitive events (weddings), marketed through short-form video, grew fast, and teaching others to copy the model became a second revenue line.

### I1. Custom photo magnet shop (online and markets)

- **Customer:** Consumers who want to display photos without frames, such as families, new couples and moms
- **Problem:** People take thousands of photos and never look at them; they want to display memories without covering walls in frames.
- **Offer:** Custom printed photo magnets, e.g. a nine-pack for $25, made from customer-uploaded photos.
- **Business model:** One-off product sales (packs of magnets), repeat buyers
- **Pricing:** Per pack, bulk-style pricing. market price USD 25 per nine-pack Smallest magnets have about 91% margin; materials about 35 cents per magnet.
- **Acquisition:** other: In-person local markets (booth fees $50-$300); handing out business cards; customers return or order online for about five days after a market from that area.; organic_social_content: Short-form videos mostly showing the magnets being made; consistency stressed; followers become repeat buyers.; existing_audience: Repeat buyers from markets and weddings follow the social accounts and are reminded of the product.
- **Delivery:** Photos are printed on an Epson inkjet and pressed into magnets by hand by the owner and helpers (high school kids paid $12-15/hour); shipped for online orders.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Smallest magnets about 91% margin; Magnet materials about 35 cents each; $1.63M online revenue last year with about 30-40% net margin overall; Best market over $3,000 revenue in a two-day market; worst about $150; First month profit a little over $1,000
- **Tools:** Epson ET8500 inkjet printer, Shopify, Instagram/short-form video, Facebook groups
- **Startup cost:** Under $1,000, put on a credit card (machine and printer)
- **Time to first revenue:** First month (May-June 2024) made a little over $1,000 profit; $10K month in about 2-3 months; $100K month in about 6 months
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Photo magnets sell the same in the UK. UK markets and craft fairs exist, and Shopify and inkjet printing work the same. Prices and materials sourcing differ.
- **Risks:** Copycats and local competition; Seasonality, with fewer markets in winter; Burnout and manual production time; Physical labor limits scale
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Content and online store are inbound and can be agent-assisted; the markets that drove early growth are physical and human.); onboarding 4/5 (Online ordering with upload tools is largely self-serve.); delivery 1/5 (Physical printing, pressing and shipping require a person; mostly manual.); billing 5/5 (Shopify checkout handles payments automatically.); support 3/5 (Order questions and photo issues can be handled by agents with human checks.)
> "I sell them in nine packs for $25. So they spent it was about $225." — Sariah (~20:19)
> "Um for our smallest magnets, it's like 91%." — Sariah (~4:12)

- **Customer:** Engaged couples and event hosts (weddings, baby showers, graduations, corporate/military events)
- **Problem:** Hosts want a memorable, unique guest favor at a one-off event.
- **Offer:** A live magnet station: guests have photos taken or uploaded, magnets are printed and pressed on the spot and given free to guests.
- **Business model:** Per-event fee (flat or hourly plus bulk magnet rate)
- **Pricing:** Flat event fee or hourly rate plus bulk magnet rate; suggested per-head pricing. typical wedding range USD 1200-3000 per wedding; first wedding USD 1400 per wedding; 3-hour wedding quoted USD 2000 per wedding; hourly rate USD 50 per hour per person; discounted military baby shower USD 300 per event Host suggested per-head pricing ($20-30 per guest) to simplify quotes. ## Tactics and playbook steps

**Use markets to land first wedding customers** (acquisition)
1. Sign up for local markets (booth fees $50-$300; higher-fee markets usually have better traffic)
2. Show magnets being made live
3. Hand out business cards because rushed shoppers order online within about five days
4. Film events and post on social so couples find you
> "The ones that I pay $300 for are the ones that I make the most at" — Sariah (~33:50)

**Price weddings per head or flat** (pricing)
1. Estimate guest count
2. Charge flat or per-head (e.g. $20-30 per guest) instead of itemised hourly plus bulk
3. Bake in assumptions on magnets per guest to protect margin
> "I always try to simplify pricing as much as possible" — Chris Koerner (~16:49)

**Reduce labor with QR upload software** (operations)
1. Guests scan a QR code, upload and crop photos
2. Photos flow into a print template
3. One person prints and one presses; cut staff from four to one or two
> "you really only need one person" — Sariah (~15:38)

**Track QR codes on magnet backs** (acquisition)
1. Print a tracking-link QR code on 100 to 1,000 magnets
2. Use a URL shortener such as TinyURL for tracking
3. Review scans over a month before scaling
> "I would do like a 100 or 1,000 magnet test." — Chris Koerner (~39:36)

**Trust through warranty** (product)
1. Offer lifetime warranty on kits
2. Provide community and course support so buyers prefer you over buying direct from China
> "We have like a lifetime warranty on all of our kits." — Sariah (~41:44)

**Share openly to grow the market** (acquisition)
1. Publish how the business works
2. Become the expert and reach others who want to enter
3. Sell kits, courses and supplies to the audience
> "I don't think it will ever be oversaturated." — Sariah (~27:20)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Start with a small experiment: she began with under $1,000 after searching for a way to display photos.
- *pricing*: Events like weddings are price insensitive and let a small product earn a large fee.
- *customer_acquisition*: Selling in person at markets can be a customer-discovery and lead engine for higher-ticket services.
- *customer_acquisition*: Showing the making process in short videos works well; consistency matters more than any single trick.
- *scaling*: Selling the means to do the business (kits, supplies, courses) creates additional revenue lines beyond the product itself.
- *offer_design*: Support and warranty beat price for customers who could buy from overseas.
- *pricing*: Simplify pricing to reduce friction and make logistics easier to estimate.
- *niche_selection*: Sharing how you do it may expand the market and make you the authority rather than creating fatal competition.
- *automation_ai*: Cut labor with software: removing manual photo handling lets one person do the job and raises margin.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Doing markets alone without self-serve software was stressful; queues overwhelm one person.
- Offering a lifetime warranty may lose money.
- Burnout is real; consistency is needed but take breaks.
- Cheap local customers and copycats can undermine pricing.
- Don't assume a market is oversaturated based on listings alone.
