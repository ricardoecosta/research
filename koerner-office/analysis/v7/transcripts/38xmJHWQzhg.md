## Tactics and playbook steps

**Personalised cold opener** (sales)
1. Research the person: a recent tweet, episode, or a fact deep in their LinkedIn
2. Open with that specific reference before any pitch
3. Example: 'you said on a recent episode your favourite business is one a high schooler could run; I built one'
> "Hey, you said on a recent episode that your favorite type of business is one high school or could run. I built one, here's what it looks like" — Chris Koerner (~3:35)

**Pratfall opener** (sales)
1. Lead a pitch with one specific thing you tried that did not work
2. Admit what you got wrong in year one
> "lead with one specific thing that you tried that did not work. Here's what we got wrong in year one" — Chris Koerner (~7:21)

**Replace adjectives with numbers** (sales)
1. Strip 'really, very, incredibly'
2. Use specific numbers, dates, names: 'grew from 3 to 247 customers in December and January; 30 new customers a month with no paid marketing; need $400,000 for two engineers'
> "We add 30 new customers a month with no paid marketing. We need $400,000 to hire two engineers" — Chris Koerner (~12:25)

**End with a specific recommendation, A/B test it** (sales)
1. Replace 'let me know what you think' with one concrete next step at a real time
2. e.g. '20 minute call next Thursday at 2pm, I'll send the invite tonight'
3. Split outreach 50/50 to compare response rates
> "We get on a 20 minute call next Thursday at 2 p.m. and I'll walk you through implementation" — Chris Koerner (~17:10)

**Short, final-line negotiation email** (sales)
1. When a dominant customer threatens, write a short email with no softeners
2. Put the strongest line last (and first)
3. Go shorter, not louder
> "your continued involvement in this working relationship is a greater liability to our business than the current threats you are making" — Chris Koerner (~15:24)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Marketing is communication: be the message the customer's brain grabs at the moment of need.
- *customer_acquisition*: The first sentence does most of the work in any pitch.
- *mindset_execution*: Admitting a flaw early (pratfall effect) builds trust; honesty is profitable.
- *customer_acquisition*: Stories sell; numbers are the receipts. Flip a 90% slides pitch to mostly story.
- *offer_design*: If you can't explain your business in one short sentence you don't understand it well enough.
- *customer_acquisition*: Specific numbers, dates and names beat adjectives.
- *mindset_execution*: Vary sentence length and use silence; ramblers lose attention.
- *customer_acquisition*: End every communication with one specific recommendation and date.
- *exit_acquisition*: You don't need to be big to be acquired; Best Buy bought Geek Squad for its system and process.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Generic cold pitch openers get deleted in seconds.
- Jargon makes you seem less competent and hides weak substance (Vasa ship analogy).
- Customer concentration: one customer at 90% of revenue wielded it like a club.
- In negotiation, the person talking most usually loses.
- Ending messages with open questions signals you don't believe in your proposal.
- Personal guarantee on a five-year lease is a big risk when a store has no traffic.
