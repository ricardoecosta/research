## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Lou van der Vier, a former HR worker and SEO agency owner who fires his agency clients and builds local lead-generation websites (geography plus service, e.g. towing, carpet cleaning, concrete), which he rents to local contractors for a flat monthly fee. He claims 232 sites and $192.5K in a month, with a mostly VA-run operation and 2-3 hours a week. He walks through his niche-selection process live, and describes moving to revenue share, phantom equity (profit interest) deals, and buying a catering business with an SBA loan for tax reasons. Key takeaway: owning the ranking asset and renting the leads is more passive than agency work, but it all rests on Lou's own unverified claims, and Chris is also selling a done-for-you version of the service.

### I1. Rank-and-rent local lead-gen websites

- **Customer:** Owner-operated local service businesses (towing, tree service, pest control, carpet cleaning, concrete, landscaping design, HVAC, etc.) that already spend on ads and have a decision-maker reachable by phone.
- **Problem:** Small local businesses get inconsistent leads, have been burned by marketing agencies, and don't know how to rank in Google.
- **Offer:** Calls and quote requests routed to the business from a site ranking at the top of organic results and Maps, with call tracking, for a flat monthly fee.
- **Business model:** Flat monthly rental of lead flow to one local contractor per site (billed by card on file); sometimes converted to revenue share.
- **Pricing:** Flat monthly fee per site, roughly based on lead volume and ticket size. First Woodside towing site (30-40 calls/month) USD 700 per month; Irving carpet cleaning site (lowest paying) USD 500 per month; Bigger flat-fee clients before moving to rev share USD 5000 per month; Range from weak to well-optimised sites per Lou USD 400-4000 per month Lou runs a few days of free leads to test a buyer, then charges a flat fee. Landscaping must generate about $8,000 jobs to be worth it. Dentists would be ideal but are hard to reach.
- **Acquisition:** seo_organic_search: Customers for the rented leads come via Google organic and Maps rankings. Exact-match domains (geography+service.com), a thousand-plus words per page and a separate page per service, targeting keywords with 500+ searches. Small cities of 60K-400K, 'low competition'. Sites can rank without links.; google_business_profile_maps: Sets up Google Business profiles; the Woodside site was number one in Maps instantly because the name matched the keyword. Later GBPs are obtained via the contractor, which Chris calls a 'false competitor'. A GBP suspension tanked the towing site.; cold_call: To find paying buyers, Lou (now a VA) phones owners already advertising on Google or Meta, offering leads for a cut. He says most reply 'yeah, cool'. Where the owner is behind a gatekeeper he sometimes walks in. Buyers are tested by secret-shopper calls.
- **Delivery:** Sites are built in WordPress (Weebly originally), content drafted with AI (probably Claude per Lou) by VAs and human edited. Calls are tracked in CallRail, rank reports are sent by a VA, and the leads route straight to the business owner. Lou checks a weekly rank report and call report; most sites are left alone. ## Tactics and playbook steps

**Niche and city screening on Google Maps and organic** (acquisition)
1. Pick a trade from Thumbtack's near-me categories, favouring home improvement and high-ticket jobs
2. Search the trade plus a mid-sized city, skipping sponsored ads
3. In the Maps pack look for under 10 reviews, no website or only a social profile, and no exact-match business names
4. In organic results look for inner pages, directories, Yelp and thin content (e.g. ~400 words)
5. Compare keyword variants with Google Trends to avoid false positives
6. Skip the niche if competitors have dozens or hundreds of reviews
> "I want to see really low. So like less than 10 reviews." — Lou van der Vier (~33:44)

**Content blueprint beating competitors** (product)
1. Review competitor sites in the niche and also in top competitive cities
2. List topics, then check keyword volumes (about 500+ searches get their own page)
3. Build a home page, about, contact and separate service pages for every service
4. Write about 1,000 words per topic, including commercial and transactional keywords
5. Use ChatGPT to find common customer questions from Yelp and Reddit and add an FAQ
6. Add both a click-to-call button and a quote form
7. Use a human edit over AI-drafted copy
> "I'll go look out at all the competitors in that niche." — Lou van der Vier (~18:17)

**Vetting a lead buyer** (sales)
1. Target local owners already paying for ads (check Meta Ad Library and Google)
2. Avoid national call centres
3. Give leads for free for a few days
4. Ask how quickly they follow up; drop those who don't call back the same day
5. Secret-shop their phone answering
6. Call their customers pretending to run a satisfaction survey to check closes and prices
7. Start with commission, then switch to a flat fee once they close
> "if they tell me today, and they don't call today, they're done." — Lou van der Vier (~28:00)

**Rev share with partner agencies** (pricing)
1. Pitch top flat-fee clients on revenue share
2. Request read access to their CRM and payments
3. Bring in friends' agencies to run Facebook/Google ads and split Lou's cut
4. Ask the owner to bump 20% to 25% to cover partners

**Weekly monitoring dashboard** (operations)
1. A VA sends a Monday rank report and a call tracking report
2. Investigate only if both rankings and call volume fall
3. Use historical data to identify seasonality
4. Stripe card on file with auto-pay for billing

**Getting Google Business Profiles via the contractor** (acquisition)
1. Rank organically first and prove lead flow
2. Once the contractor is paying, ask him to help create additional profiles
> "I generally work with the contractor." — Lou van der Vier (~39:18)

## Lessons

- *scaling*: Owning the asset beats renting out your skill: Lou swapped client-controlled agency work for owned sites that he rents out.
- *scaling*: Vertical integration: use a skill you already sell (SEO) to build your own revenue-producing assets.
- *validation_testing*: Solving an everyday annoyance (a wrecked car and a tow company with no idea where its leads come from) can reveal real demand.
- *niche_selection*: Pick less competitive markets where decision-makers answer the phone and tickets are high; there are millions of service+geography combinations.
- *pricing*: Free has no value; test buyers briefly, then charge, and vet them with secret-shopper checks.
- *customer_acquisition*: Finding the right buyer is harder than generating leads; some owners refuse positive-ROI deals out of pride.
- *pricing*: Moving from flat fee to revenue share on the biggest clients can multiply income without taking on more clients.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Delegating content, outreach and reporting to VAs keeps the founder to a couple of hours per week.
- *mindset_execution*: Burning the boats (quitting all agency clients at once) forced focus but was risky.
- *automation_ai*: AI makes content creation and research cheaper, and the host believes local services are among the last to be hit by AI search.
- *customer_acquisition*: Organic rankings convert better and churn less than ads because searchers trust the top result.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Avoid niches with long, expensive contracts such as elevator repair, because customers won't switch even when ranking.
- A Google Business Profile suspension can wipe out a phone-driven niche like towing.
- Brokers and middlemen (e.g. private jets) waste leads; low-ticket trades like lawn mowing are not worth it.
- Shiny object syndrome: pick niches rigorously and follow one process.
- Link building is the hard part; follow one teacher rather than mixing sources.
- Rev share requires strong trust; do not offer it to brand-new customers.
- Don't give leads away free for long.
