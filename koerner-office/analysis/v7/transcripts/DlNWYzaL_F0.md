## Tactics and playbook steps

**Use one-to-one networking to sell a local presence audit** (acquisition)
1. Attend local business networking meetings regularly.
2. Ask owners whether they want to grow; Mike says owners who already have all the business they need are not prospects.
3. Arrange one-to-one conversations with business owners.
4. Show a concrete issue in their online presence and explain how fixing it could lead to more business.
> "The job of networking is doing one-to- ons with business owners and showing them that this is important" — Mike (~41:38)

**Turn service knowledge into search-focused content** (content)
1. List specific problems customers search for, rather than writing broad claims such as "we kill bugs."
2. Ask the operator to explain each problem aloud.
3. Record and transcribe the explanation, then publish it as a blog post and podcast episode.
4. Keep publishing regularly; Mike says a podcast should not be abandoned after only a few episodes.
> "it's easier to record and transcribe than it is to write. It's a shorter distance from your brain to your tongue than it is from your brain to your hands." — Mike (~11:12)

**Build a conversion-focused local landing page** (sales)
1. Use a clear headline that names the customer's problem and promises a specific resolution.
2. Put the headline, short video, social proof and call to action near the top of the page.
3. Make it easy for a mobile visitor to tap to call or book.
4. Create specific landing pages for the problems named in ads.
> "Headline, video, social proof, sales letter, and then everything else is if you want to or not." — Mike (~14:56)

**Use SER-term jingles in skippable local ads** (acquisition)
1. Choose a distinctive search phrase and pair it with a short positioning statement.
2. Put the phrase and positioning in the opening seconds of the jingle.
3. Keep the jingle around 15 seconds.
4. Run the ad in the target local market and let interested viewers continue while skip impressions still deliver the opening phrase.
> "So, frontload it with something that will teach your local market or your worldwide market for that." — Mike (~46:03)

**Use AI to create and repurpose client content** (operations)
1. Have the client provide a spoken explanation or script.
2. Use Gemini or another chatbot to draft local content from prompts about the service and customer problem.
3. Use ElevenLabs to clone the client's voice for a read-through when appropriate.
4. Add the jingle, publish the content and use a transcript as a blog post.
> "I take his script, I clone his voice. Now, he reads it perfectly in any language we want." — Mike (~32:05)

**Use keyword-matched domains and a single booking action** (product)
1. Research what visitors search for in the destination.
2. Buy an available domain that closely matches the local search phrase.
3. Build a simple page with one clear booking action.
4. Use organic search first and add a geographically limited ad budget once the page converts.
> "We created a simple website with one call to action. Book your boat. We send traffic here and they're one tap away from booking our boats." — Mike (~38:33)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: A local business website should quickly show who it serves, what problem it solves and how to call or book.
- *customer_acquisition*: Search visibility alone is not enough; the page must convert a visitor into an enquiry or purchase.
- *operations*: Ask service operators to explain customer problems aloud, then turn their spoken expertise into searchable content.
- *mindset_execution*: Publish consistently; do not judge a podcast or content channel after only a few posts.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use paid traffic when the landing page converts and the campaign is profitable; Mike sees paid traffic as more predictable than waiting for organic reach.
- *offer_design*: Use direct-response messages that name a problem and give the customer a clear next step.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI to reduce routine production work, while keeping a human involved in choosing prompts, facts and final output.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Recurring income can make a side business more useful than one-off work, especially for a founder who wants control over working hours.
- *customer_acquisition*: Keep explanations simple for owners who are experts in their trade but not in web design or marketing.
- *scaling*: Build an audience and publish on channels the business owns, because social platforms can change their rules.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not pay for Google reviews; Mike says businesses should ask customers for reviews and respond to existing reviews.
- A visually impressive website may still fail to convert if the useful information and call to action are hard to find.
- Podcast and content efforts often fail when operators quit before they have published consistently.
- More customer demand can become a staffing problem for physical service businesses.
- Social platforms control the distribution rules; Mike recommends publishing material on a website the business owns.
