## Summary

The Koerner Office hosts a live pitch event in Utah. Ten strangers get 2 minutes to pitch and 10 minutes of critique from Chris Koerner, Nick and Brandon. The pitches range from an operating VA-funded massage business doing about $68k/month and a $1.5M ARR home-service marketing agency to pre-revenue ideas such as a seed cup, an NFC keychain, a containment diaper, a choose-your-path kids' letter subscription, an AI relationship app and mail-in cast iron cleaning. The hosts invested in or offered to help several, and the audience voted Go Seeds first, Pick a Path Letters second and Utah Veterans Massage third. The main takeaways are that distribution (especially paid ads and short-form video) is the recurring bottleneck, and that investors want proof of a repeatable process before funding. All numbers are unverified pitch claims.

### I1. Utah Veterans Massage

- **Customer:** Veterans eligible for 12 VA-paid massage sessions; the VA refers by geographic proximity.
- **Problem:** Veterans in pain need access to medical massage that the VA funds, and new therapists struggle to find well-paid work.
- **Offer:** Medical and deep-tissue massage in one or two-room clinics, with billing, insurance back end and scheduling handled by the company.
- **Business model:** Per-session insurance reimbursement (VA via TriWest); therapists paid per session.
- **Pricing:** VA reimbursement per session; therapist paid about $50. VA reimbursement in Utah USD 90 per session; therapist pay USD 50 per session; lowest rate across states (Medicaid rule) USD 89 per session; Texas rate (approx, 'or more') USD 109 per session Rates vary by state; the founder says they never go below about $89.
- **Acquisition:** partnerships: The VA (via TriWest) calls the company based on proximity to the veteran, so many small locations get referrals. No marketing spend. Getting into the network took about 8 months.
- **Delivery:** Employed or contracted therapists treat clients in leased clinics (about $500-650/month rent each); therapists rebook clients at the end of each session; staff handle billing, insurance and scheduling.
- **Unit economics (claims):** 750 sessions/month, $68k revenue, about $20k profit; $90 collected, $50 to therapist; Leases about $500-650/month per location; Credentialing automated with AI, about 25 minutes per person
- **Tools:** TriWest/VA network, HIPAA-compliant scheduling app (subscription), AI automation for credentialing and paperwork
- **Startup cost:** not stated (rented a room one day a week to begin)
- **Time to first revenue:** about 8 months to get into the VA network; first rented room 18 months before the pitch
- **UK applicability [E]:** 1/5 — Depends on the US VA/TriWest benefit and Medicaid-style rates. The UK has no equivalent per-session funded benefit, and NHS veterans' services are commissioned differently. Massage therapy would also need UK registration and insurance.
- **Risks:** Revenue depends on a single payer (VA/TriWest); Each state differs in licensing, credentialing and pre-authorisation, so expansion may lag; Founder admits to a 'grow, expand and then figure it out' approach; Owner-operator bottleneck; needs a COO or operations manager; Valuation ask of $2M was seen as too high

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Use a payer-referred niche with guaranteed volume** (acquisition)
1. Find populations whose services are pre-paid by a third party (e.g. VA 12 free sessions)
2. Join the payer network, accept referrals by proximity
3. Open many small leased rooms near referrals instead of one big site
> "the VA will call us based on the geographic proximity to the veteran and so we have a lot of little ones" — Aean Harden (~6:49)

**Offload therapist admin** (operations)
1. Therapists only deliver and rebook the next session
2. Central staff handle billing, insurance and scheduling
3. Use scheduling software to cut admin

**Free hand-out as advertising, sell to institutions** (pricing)
1. Give tags free (30-40 cents cost)
2. Sell at $5 to institutions with minimum 5,000-unit orders
3. Optionally add a $1/month recurring option

**Automate customer choices with text and QR** (operations)
1. Text parents reminders via Go High Level
2. Accept replies or a QR code web form
3. Use choice to select the next chapter

**Pay-to-scale ads** (acquisition)
1. Treat ads as a learnable skill
2. Reinvest from $1,500 (10 customers) to larger spend
3. Study competitors in the Meta ads library
> "your next 1,500 will get 30 customers and your next will get 60" — Brandon (~45:26)

**Test with milestones before investing** (finance)
1. Reach 50 paying customers
2. Churn below 15%
3. App store approval before raising
> "call it 50 paying customers with your churn under 15%" — Nick (~53:55)

**Content-first pivot** (acquisition)
1. Film time-lapse before and after videos
2. Post consistently on short-form platforms
3. Monetise with product or sponsorship once reach is proven

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: A payer-guaranteed customer pool removes CAC risk but creates concentration risk.
- *offer_design*: Changing the form factor of an everyday product can create a standout offer.
- *mindset_execution*: Investors back the founder as much as the idea, so come prepared with specifics.
- *customer_acquisition*: Distribution is the main bottleneck for subscription and app ideas; learn paid ads yourself.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Profitable cash-flowing businesses may not need outside capital unless they have a proven funnel to scale.
- *mindset_execution*: Telling a story and involving family helps pitches resonate and attract help.
- *scaling*: Low-ticket $450/month service businesses can reach $1.5M ARR with high margins if churn is spread across many customers.
- *automation_ai*: LLM-based apps need token cost control and distribution plans because they are easy to copy.
- *mindset_execution*: Do not just consume content; start something and earn revenue.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Expanding state by state means different licensing and credentialing and payer rules; do not just hire an ops manager and figure it out.
- In-home healthcare is hard; prefer clinic-based delivery.
- Do not go to investors with an idea and no plan or numbers.
- Do not invest in your own learning with outside money; show that you have tried and burned money first.
- Mailing heavy items can cost more than the service fee.
