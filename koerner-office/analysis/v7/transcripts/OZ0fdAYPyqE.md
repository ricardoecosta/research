## Summary

Chris Koerner hosts Shannon Jean, a liquidation/auction reseller, who screen-shares live auction listings (Target, B-Stock, GovDeals, QVC, Costco, Advance Auto Parts) and explains how to buy pallets and truckloads at ~90-99% off nominal retail and resell locally on Facebook Marketplace, eBay or wholesale. Key rules: always get a manifest, buy with math and sell with emotion, list items before buying to test demand, focus on recovering your initial investment fast, buy seasonal and boring commodity goods, and buy close to home. Several side ideas are pitched (free couch flipping, Costco/IKEA arbitrage for towns without stores, Christmas tree lot, AI manifest analysis). Shannon sells a Reseller Mastermind and Koerner sells TK Owners, so claims are second-hand or promotional and many numbers are anecdotal.

### I1. Liquidation pallet auction flipping on Facebook Marketplace

- **Customer:** Local Facebook Marketplace buyers looking for cheap new goods (furniture, pools, docks, rugs, appliances)
- **Problem:** Shoppers want new items below retail; retailers must liquidate overstock
- **Offer:** Brand new, factory-sealed items (e.g. Walker Edison furniture, Target sporting goods) priced near used comparables
- **Business model:** One-off product resale margin
- **Pricing:** Resale at roughly used-comparable prices. 10x6 rigid air dock (Target retail $250), cost $3.78 USD 100 per item; Rattan king bed conservative sale, landed cost about $30 USD 350 per item; Rugs bought at about $8 each USD 100 per item Price near used comparables on Facebook Marketplace; mention 'similar to IKEA' in listings.
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: List on Facebook Marketplace with own photos, assembly time-lapse video, and keywords; use stock photos, say brand new.; facebook_groups_communities: Post in local Facebook groups such as parent and college groups.
- **Delivery:** Buyer wins pallet auction, pays freight (e.g. about $1,200 for 16 pallets in California), stores in garage or storage unit, assembles sample items, buyers collect locally.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Target sporting goods lot cost per item $3.78 vs $250 retail; 16 pallets Walker Edison furniture expected under $5,000 all in; Landed cost on rattan bed about $30 vs $350+ resale
- **Tools:** B-Stock, Facebook Marketplace, Manifest spreadsheets, Resale permit
- **Startup cost:** Roughly $1,000 to $5,000 per lot plus freight; resale permit is free
- **Time to first revenue:** Days after pallet arrives; aim to recover investment fast
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — UK equivalents exist (B-Stock UK, Amazon/Argos liquidation pallets, Facebook Marketplace, eBay, Gumtree) but US resale permit rules do not apply and pallet freight and VAT differ.
- **Risks:** Inventory that does not sell must be donated; Inflated retail values on manifests; Freight cost and distance; Capital tied up in pallets; Dependence on weak or no manifest
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Listings on Facebook Marketplace are inbound and can be drafted by agents, though FB posting has limited API support and needs human-in-the-loop.); onboarding 2/5 (Buyer enquiries and pickup scheduling can be templated but need a human to coordinate.); delivery 0/5 (Freight pickup, storage, assembly, photographing and handing over goods are physical.); billing 2/5 (Mostly cash or app payments on pickup; little to automate.); support 3/5 (Agents can handle pre-sale chat questions; returns are rare.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Reverse-engineer your bid** (finance)
1. Download the manifest
2. List what you believe each item sells for
3. Subtract freight and costs to get a maximum bid
4. Place the max bid and walk away
> "You just do it backwards." — Shannon Jean (~15:44)

**Bid on weekends** (acquisition)
1. Place auto-bid on Friday
2. Costco auctions close on weekends and sell for less

**Recover your investment first** (finance)
1. Identify high-ticket items in the manifest
2. Sell enough quickly to get money back
3. Wholesale part of the lot if needed

**Wholesale to local discount stores** (sales)
1. Walk in without product and ask if they buy from resellers
2. Collect contact info
3. Email manifest opportunities and let them name a price

**Story-based listings** (sales)
1. Add your personal reason for selling
2. Use in commodity markets

## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: Buy with math and sell with emotion.
- *niche_selection*: Boring commodities have less competition than sexy products.
- *validation_testing*: Test demand before purchase.
- *finance_unit_economics*: When starting, focus on getting your money back, not maximum profit.
- *operations*: Buy close to home to reduce freight.
- *niche_selection*: Seasonal timing gives built-in downside protection.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Larger quantities of items reduce risk.
- *customer_acquisition*: Build relationships with trade buyers.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Never buy without a manifest; you have no recourse.
- Avoid hyped brands like Stanley where bids reflect retail; you can end up upside down.
- Retail values on manifests are inflated.
- Don't buy categories you don't know (car parts, Lego, consumer electronics).
