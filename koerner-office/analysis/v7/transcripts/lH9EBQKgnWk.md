## Summary

David Heacock interviews Ian Dolberg, founder of Dolcore, a New York City (Staten Island) security guard agency started in 2018 after military service and years working for large guard firms. Revenue went from $90k in year one to about $670k, then $1.3M, then nearly $4M in 2024, dipping to $3.3M last year as client funding was cut. Ian describes cold-calling sales, shotgun hiring, regulatory barriers, thin margins (~7%) and price-driven RFP losses. He also launched a marketing agency (Black Op Digital) for other guard companies. The host pushes him on value proposition, retention and systems. Key takeaway: a people-heavy, regulated, low-margin service that grew via founder-led outbound sales and niche compliance moats, but it is not agent-automatable.

### I1. Dolcore security guard agency (NYC)

- **Customer:** NYC businesses and institutions needing licensed guards: cannabis dispensaries, supportive housing and shelters, private schools, retail, banks, co-ops/HOAs, residential buildings, event organisers, nonprofits.
- **Problem:** Businesses need legally licensed guards reliably present; a single in-house guard calling out leaves them unprotected or unable to open, and in-house forces carry insurance and licensing burdens.
- **Offer:** Licensed, military-led guards from a pool of about 200, with callout cover, 24/7 central monitoring of guards, supervisors, SOPs, training and paperwork for prevailing-wage contracts; plus bespoke tech such as a vibe-coded gate access control system.
- **Business model:** Hourly bill rate to clients for supplied guards; margin is the markup over guard wages, insurance, uniforms and equipment.
- **Pricing:** Hourly bill rate over guard wage; tiered guard experience levels; competitive bidding via RFPs. 24-hour guard service for first residential client USD 4700 per week; Access control system the incumbent charged a gated community USD 1200 per month Race to the bottom on bid rates; minimum wage rises push prices up and clients shop around.
- **Acquisition:** cold_call: Founder cold-called facilities managers, property managers and security directors; sent a letter and packet/trifold beforehand announcing a call time, then called; weekly batches of 10 LinkedIn-sourced contacts. Now a hired salesperson trained in his method (outbound calls and emails).; outbound_linkedin: Connected with property managers on LinkedIn, pulled emails into an Excel sheet and followed up.; networking_events: Paid $50 to join the Metropolitan Cemetery Association to reach cemetery directors; Staten Island Chamber of Commerce, golf committee and event sponsorships.; seo_organic_search: In-house SEO team plus Google Ads; about $135k a year on marketing; aims to respond to quote requests within 15 minutes.; referrals_word_of_mouth: Friend introductions (bank client via Statland Security owner, Snug Harbor event); guards alert him when a site gets a new security director.; marketplace_platform: Invited to many RFPs; bid about $100M in RFPs this year and won none because of price; DCAS-certified school list gives grant-funded school work.; organic_social_content: Event videos and a guard music video on website, YouTube and social, linked in proposals instead of stock photos.
- **Delivery:** Humans physically staff posts. Dispatch runs 24 hours, central monitoring watches guards, supervisors (mostly veterans) do random night inspections. Hiring via personal network, sponsored Indeed posts and social media, with interviews at coffee shops initially.
- **Unit economics (claims):** About 7% margin, which he says could be adjusted up to about 19%; $8k per week of revenue is roughly $400k a year, giving about $28k profit at 7%; 2024 revenue just under $4M, 2025 $3.3M, projected a little over $4M this year
- **Tools:** Indeed, LinkedIn, Excel, Salesforce, Perplexity, Citizen app, Vibe coding tools, Google Ads
- **Startup cost:** not stated (security agency licence obtained; first year funded by cold calling while employed as an investigator)
- **Time to first revenue:** About one month after quitting, three cemetery contracts began (April 2018 start).

The model works with changes but is labour-heavy.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Letter, packet then scheduled call** (sales)
1. Identify the decision maker (facilities manager, security director, property manager)
2. Send a typed letter and a memorable packet or trifold announcing a specific call time
3. Call at that time and ask for the person as 'expecting my call'
4. Batch ten contacts a week, calling the following week to book meetings
> "I'm going to be calling you at 10 p.m. on Wednesday and what I would do is like leave something that's memorable like a packet or a trifold" — Ian Dolberg (~20:01)

**Join the niche trade association for lead access** (acquisition)
1. Find the niche association (cemeteries)
2. Pay the membership ($50)
3. Call every member director
> "I got a hold of the Metropolitan Cemetery Association director by becoming a member for $50." — Ian Dolberg (~27:47)

**Shotgun hiring for urgent staffing** (hiring)
1. Call known managers and guards first
2. Sponsor Indeed posts and blast social media
3. Interview in coffee shops
4. Over-hire (16 for 13 posts) to cover no-shows
> "I was able to do shotgun hiring and I would blast out" — Ian Dolberg (~33:31)

**Use event video as proof in proposals** (sales)
1. Hire a camera person to film the event and guards
2. Publish on website, YouTube and social
3. Link video in every proposal instead of stock photos
> "whenever we do proposals, we have the link to like our near or like a music video I put together of the guards like working" — Ian Dolberg (~36:03)

**Spin out your marketing function as an agency** (finance)
1. Build an in-house SEO and outbound team
2. Sell the service to non-competing territories
3. Use agency profits to fund your own marketing
> "I built the marketing agency to pay for my marketing and it's worked." — Ian Dolberg (~44:46)

**New-decision-maker alert** (operations)
1. Ask guards to report any new security director at a site
2. Have the sales person or owner meet the new director quickly
3. Reassure them to reduce churn risk
> "One of my guards text me is like, "Hey, new director of security came in."" — Ian Dolberg (~65:36)

**Delegate founder sales by teaching a hire your method** (hiring)
1. Document the personal call method
2. Hire and train a salesperson
3. Track results by quarter
> "I hired someone and taught them the way I do things." — Ian Dolberg (~43:47)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Work in the industry first at large competitors to learn bill rates, P&L and sales before starting your own.
- *niche_selection*: Start in niches big competitors ignore (cemeteries) to win first clients as a startup.
- *niche_selection*: Regulatory barriers (licences, certification lists like DCAS) create moats and make some sales easy.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Founder-led sales stops scaling; teach a hire your method and use a book like Who Not How.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Commodity labour services have thin margins; diversify so no client exceeds 20% of the portfolio.
- *scaling*: Retention means revenue you can count on next year, regardless of why it leaves; bake client-funding risk into the model.
- *scaling*: A business that runs without the founder is more valuable and easier to pass on than one where the owner does everything.
- *offer_design*: A clear, sellable unique value proposition is needed in a commodity market; being military-owned and monitored guards was his differentiation but the host found it under-articulated.
- *customer_acquisition*: Reinvest in marketing and be willing to change methods each year; word-of-mouth-only competitors stayed small.
- *mindset_execution*: A big early win can come with a crash afterwards; plan for repeatability and diversify.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Margins are very thin and large PE-backed rivals can underbid; a small firm matching their rates would go bankrupt.
- Event scope can balloon, forcing rapid staffing; failing to meet required staffing can get an event shut down.
- Insurance shocks after incidents can make whole client categories (nightclubs) unprofitable.
- Dependence on funded nonprofit clients exposes revenue to funding cuts.
- Founder sales effort stagnated when it required 30 calls a week by himself.
