## Summary

Codie Sanchez explains her 'AOV to CAC' formula: judge a business by first-order average order value against full customer acquisition cost, rather than lifetime value, which she argues is usually miscalculated and leads to burning cash. She covers raising AOV through value-based pricing (10-20% of value created) and by adding tiers, subscriptions, upsells and referrals, using a plumber example and her own Boardroom ladder. She then covers lowering CAC with owned demand (organic, referrals, partnerships, email) versus rented paid ads, and gives her media business's return on content spend. The takeaway is to aim for a 3:1 AOV:CAC ratio and check it weekly.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**AOV-to-CAC ratio check** (finance)
1. Use first-order AOV, not projected LTV
2. Compute CAC = all acquisition spend incl. ads, marketing and sales labour / net new customers
3. Target AOV:CAC of 3:1; fix if CAC > half of AOV
4. Recompute weekly because CAC creeps up ~5% at a time
> "If your CAC is more than half your AOV, then your CAC is not an ratio we like" — Codie Sanchez (~18:28)

**Value-based pricing** (pricing)
1. Estimate dollar value created or disaster avoided
2. Price at 10-20% of value created
3. Answer 'too expensive' with 'compared to what?' and the avoided cost

**Increase customer surface area** (pricing)
1. Add checklist inspection upsell
2. Add premium/priority subscription tier
3. Cross-sell partner services for referral fees
4. Build three tiers: free/low cost, speed and depth, full access; add the top tier only when tier two is full

**Owned demand percentage** (acquisition)
1. Sum revenue from organic, referrals and partnerships
2. Divide by total revenue
3. Aim above 50% so growth does not depend on ad algorithms

**Return on content spend (ROCS)** (acquisition)
1. Audience value = revenue per channel / followers
2. Multiply by frequency and lifetime
3. Divide by content spend

**Channel sequencing by revenue stage** (acquisition)
1. Under $250k: master one channel
2. $250k-$1M: add a second (organic then test paid)
3. $1M-$5M: run two or three with at least one owned
4. Over $5M: any channel, keep majority of budget on proven ones

## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: Judge acquisition against first-order value, not projected lifetime value.
- *pricing*: Raise prices: small increases flow almost entirely to profit.
- *pricing*: Price to the outcome or disaster avoided, not the time taken.
- *mindset_execution*: Low prices often reflect a lack of confidence in what you sell.
- *offer_design*: Sell more to the same customer via tiers, subscriptions, upsells, cross-sells and affiliate referrals.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Include sales and marketing labour in CAC to see real survival economics.
- *customer_acquisition*: Owned attention (email, followers, SEO) has a long tail; ads stop when spending stops.
- *customer_acquisition*: Email remains the highest-ROI marketing channel.
- *scaling*: Master one channel before adding more; sequence by revenue stage.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Losing money on the first sale while banking on LTV only works with VC money; most businesses go bankrupt doing it.
- Market-based pricing (10% above/below competitors) is lazy copying of someone else's guess.
- Ad costs keep rising; an algorithm change can triple CAC.
- A 2:1 ROAS is usually unprofitable once COGS and labour are included.
- Skipping to multi-channel ad spend too early leaves small businesses with huge ad bills.
