## Tactics and playbook steps

**Weekly self-experiments** (mindset)
1. Pick a topic you are interested in and read about it.
2. Design one experiment and run it for a week.
3. Examples: enjoy what you are doing 10% more five times a day; do only what you enjoy for a week; thank five people a day.
4. Run a new experiment every few days or weekly.
> "I would read something on that topic and I would design an experiment for myself and I'd run that experiment for a week." — Joe Hudson (~51:32)

**Hard conversation method** (operations)
1. Don't solve the problem for them; ask for a plan, e.g. 'sales are off, present me a plan tomorrow'.
2. Ask open-ended questions first so they feel heard and you get the full context.
3. Be vulnerable about your own state ('I feel we've lost trust').
4. Go in from a service mindset, not fear.
> "I need a plan. Please present it to me tomorrow about how we're going to get sales back on track." — Joe Hudson (~33:41)

**Visualise worst-case outcomes** (mindset)
1. Sit quietly with eyes closed.
2. Visualise the feared outcome (company failing, divorce) and feel the emotions fully.
3. Repeat until the fear dissipates; depth of feeling matters more than repetition.
> "It's more until the fear is gone." — Joe Hudson (~21:40)

**Company health check** (operations)
1. Measure how quickly the organisation has hard conversations.
2. Measure how quickly they resolve them.
> "How quickly do they have the hard conversation? And how quickly do they resolve it?" — Joe Hudson (~31:09)

**Handle negative self-talk by changing the reaction** (mindset)
1. Don't try to change the self-talk; change how you react to it.
2. Run a different reaction experiment each day or two.
3. List the 20 things the inner voice says most and find seven ways each is untrue.
> "If you try to change the self talk, it's very inefficient. If you change the way you react to the self talk, it's far, far more efficient." — Joe Hudson (~40:08)

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: Effort is rarely why startups fail; relationships and decisions matter more than grinding harder.
- *mindset_execution*: Avoided emotions such as helplessness drive avoidance behaviours (over-grinding, refusing to delegate) that perpetuate the problem.
- *scaling*: Founders with a self-reliance pattern struggle to delegate and ask for help, which limits scaling.
- *validation_testing*: Frame growth as experiments, not failures, so you keep iterating (also applies to sales and marketing).
- *mindset_execution*: Break big goals into the next small win (the piano teacher example) rather than presenting the whole elephant.
- *operations*: Measuring enjoyment at day end is a better efficiency measure than speed because it preserves energy for tomorrow.
- *niche_selection*: Test whether a business is meaningful by asking if you would sell it for a billion dollars and never do it again.
- *exit_acquisition*: Positive emotions from wins are a muscle; practise feeling them before an exit or you may feel nothing when it comes.
- *scaling*: The happiness of the CEO is the key signal of company direction; investors should back a happy founder.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Treating self-development as a to-do list for future happiness turns it into a grind and exhausts you.
- Failing to be self-aware only works if the environment never changes; when you must change, avoiding it hurts you.
- Avoiding conflict in a business means avoiding the places where the system could be improved.
