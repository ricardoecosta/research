## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate demand before investing in product development** (validation_testing)
1. Look for existing products and evidence of repeat purchases.
2. Launch a simple first version and see whether customers pay.
3. Improve the product after demand is demonstrated.
> "if people are actually buying it yes I will take the three days and like actually try to make it good" — Sam (~16:41)

**Test Meta ad creatives with separate testing and winning campaigns** (acquisition)
1. Put new creatives into a testing campaign using ABO budgets.
2. Let the platform allocate spend across the creatives.
3. Move better performers into a winning campaign to scale them.
4. Sam's example used five creatives and $100 a day.
> "I have a testing campaign and I have a winning campaign and then I'll go launch new sets of ads on the testing campaign" — Sam (~5:49)

**Use AI to create initial product imagery** (operations)
1. Give ChatGPT a product mockup.
2. Ask for the product in different scenes, such as a person holding it or the product on a table.
3. Turn the generated images into ad creatives.
> "I give it to ChatGpt and I say Hey generate you know a woman holding this generate this laying on a table" — Sam (~4:31)

**Use multi-packs and an in-cart add-on to improve order economics** (pricing)
1. Stop offering a single pack if shipping cost makes the order uneconomic.
2. Offer multi-packs to raise average order value.
3. Add a related ebook in the cart.
4. Set a free-shipping threshold to encourage the add-on.
> "I stopped selling single packs that was the one big learning was like I got to I got to sell two of these specifically" — Sam (~8:36)

**Reduce shipping weight and compare fulfilment options** (operations)
1. Check whether a smaller product can stay below a cheaper parcel weight threshold.
2. Compare postage rates and packaging formats.
3. Ask about discounted rates or a 3PL if shipping costs are too high.
> "if I could even just cut the shipping expense in half my math was You will and so I just texted Kirk to see like what that should cost" — Sam (~10:30)

**Draft an ebook with a structured prompt** (product)
1. Ask ChatGPT for a detailed chapter outline.
2. Work through the outline one chapter at a time in the same chat.
3. Sell a first version to test demand, then improve it if customers buy.
> "write a detailed outline of 10 chapters 12 chapters whatever it is that's the key right then they write the outline" — Sam (~16:02)

**Test an offer in the simplest delivery vehicle first** (validation_testing)
1. Identify the customer outcome, such as making more money, saving time or saving money.
2. Choose the easiest vehicle to sell first, such as a service or digital product.
3. Talk to customers and learn what they actually value.
4. Use what you learn and the resulting cash flow to invest in software or automation.
> "just go sell it as a service first they'll tell you what to do they'll tell you what that like what they actually care about" — Sam (~26:05)

**Explore different product formats for an existing customer outcome** (product)
1. Start with the outcome customers want.
2. Explore whether a different format could make the offer more appealing.
3. Compare service, digital product, software and physical product formats before committing.
> "I just implore people to explore the vehicle side of the game with different price points different requirements" — Sam (~24:13)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Use visible marketplace demand as a starting signal, then test whether customers will buy your own offer.
- *validation_testing*: Sell before spending substantial time building; let customer purchases reveal what to improve.
- *finance_unit_economics*: A physical product's apparent gross margin can be misleading if shipping and labor are omitted.
- *pricing*: Price and package a physical product around shipping economics; multi-packs can raise order value and offset fulfilment costs.
- *automation_ai*: A digital product can provide a low-cost way to practise offer design and paid advertising without physical fulfilment.
- *offer_design*: Choose a delivery vehicle based on what customers need; a service or guide can validate an offer before software.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI for a first draft, then spend more effort improving the product only after buyers show interest.
- *niche_selection*: Look for different formats that deliver an existing customer outcome instead of assuming software is the only option.
- *scaling*: A software product can follow a service business after direct client work clarifies what customers value.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not assume that sales volume inferred from marketplace reviews is verified; Sam's competitor revenue estimate relies on a stated 1% review-rate assumption.
- Shipping was a major constraint for the heavy product and Sam's early economics excluded shipping and labor.
- The product is seasonal and Sam ran out of packaging inventory, which stopped the ads.
- Sam says not to invest heavily in polishing an unvalidated product; improve it after customers demonstrate demand.
- The host promotes his own newsletter and podcast during the episode, which is a promotional conflict.
