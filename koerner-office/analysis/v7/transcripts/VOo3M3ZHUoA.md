## Tactics and playbook steps

**Estimate competitor revenue from the awards page** (finance)
1. Find the competitor's public pricing page
2. Look up their SaaSpreneur tier (gold 100+, platinum 500+, diamond 1,000+)
3. Multiply price by the tier's minimum customer count
4. Weight tiers (most on cheapest, 10-15% middle, 5-10% top) for an average price

**Reverse-engineer a competitor with five free tools** (acquisition)
1. Pricing page x customer count
2. Wayback Machine to see how the niche narrowed over 5 years
3. Meta Ads Library to find long-running (profitable) ads
4. BuiltWith for the tech stack
5. LinkedIn for employee count and roles to estimate payroll

**Copy long-running and high-impression Meta ads** (acquisition)
1. Search the brand in the Meta Ads Library
2. Find ads active the longest, or sort by impressions
3. Spot recent ads with high impressions, as these are being scaled
4. Screenshot and ask an LLM to produce 35 similar ads for your business
5. Check the UK Ads Library for targeting information
> "you go to the UK version of the Meta Ads Library, and they are required, if you're running ads in the UK, the Facebook is required to show the world who you are targeting with those ads." — Chris Koerner (~24:09)

**Pick adjacent niches with an LLM** (acquisition)
1. Prompt an LLM with a proven niche and ask for 10 similar industries from first principles
2. Pick one and run it for a couple of months
3. Switch if it fails
> "Dumpster rentals, porta potty rentals, skid steer and mini excavator rentals." — Chris Koerner (~13:39)

**Missed call text back setup in GoHighLevel** (operations)
1. Create a sub-account for the customer (e.g. home services roofing)
2. Buy a local-area-code number for SMS and calls
3. Verify identity for regulatory compliance
4. Forward the owner's cell to the new number with a 20-second ring timeout, voicemail off
5. Use the Missed Call Text Back workflow template with a short delay
6. Create a roof inspection calendar and put its link in the SMS

**Use a wedge product** (sales)
1. Lead with a quick-win product (missed call text back) demo-able in 90 seconds
2. Earn trust
3. Upsell ads, websites and other services
> "I call this a wedge product. You wedge yourself into this business, earn their trust" — Chris Koerner (~28:27)

**Find hidden pricing via Google** (pricing)
1. If a site says 'book a strategy call', Google '<brand> agency pricing' to estimate retainer levels

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Public signals (customer tiers, pricing, ad longevity, headcount) let you estimate competitors' economics before building anything.
- *niche_selection*: Serving boring local service niches with a 'make you more money' offer yields durable demand and easy sales.
- *niche_selection*: Start broad, then narrow into the niche that says yes; the Wayback Machine shows a competitor's learning curve for free.
- *customer_acquisition*: Ads that stay live for months are likely profitable; copy those rather than new ones.
- *offer_design*: Use a small wedge product to open a relationship, then upsell.
- *pricing*: Lower-ticket software (CRM) scales to more customers than high-touch done-for-you services.
- *pricing*: A high-ticket vertical (dental implants) allows large retainers because one new patient pays for the service.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Margins in software resale are high, but at scale you need overseas VAs and AI; payroll became about 25% of overhead in the Event Hawk guess.
- *mindset_execution*: Copy proven competitors rather than fear saturation: they take the arrows.

## Warnings and mistakes

- If voicemail is on, calls register as answered and the missed call text back will not fire.
- Phone numbers need identity verification because of texting and calling regulations.
- Don't rely on only 1-3 ads; most profitable advertisers test far more.
