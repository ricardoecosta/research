## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build bank relationships before applying** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Keep bank accounts open and use them actively rather than letting balances sit without transactions.
2. The guest says banks may look at transaction volume, average balance, and whether more money comes in than goes out.
3. Build relationships with large banks first, then regional banks, then local credit unions.
4. Use the guest's stated Chase rules as examples of lender-specific application limits, and check current terms before applying.
> "So, how we break it down is three levels. Big banks first, regional banks second, small local credit unions last." — Brandon Elliot (~7:03)

**Prepare an auto purchase before visiting the dealership** (sales)
1. The guest suggests seeking business auto-loan pre-approval first.
2. Visit near the last day of the month, about two to three hours before closing, when sales staff may be focused on quota.
3. The guest suggests asking for a salesperson who is near the bottom of the dealership's leaderboard.
4. Treat this as a negotiation tactic, not a guarantee of a lower price.
> "And then also looking each dealership has like a uh leadership board. And so you want to look for that" — Brandon Elliot (~3:30)

**Use promotional balance transfers carefully** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Ask the issuer whether a credit limit can be transferred to a bank account at a promotional rate.
2. Confirm the rate, fee, promotional end date, minimum payments, and whether new transactions are allowed.
3. Do not assume a standard cash advance has the same rate as a promotional transfer.
4. Avoid using a card for a new transaction when the guest says that could trigger interest on the balance.
> "you want to make sure it's at a promotional rate. If you don't do that, then it's going to end up being the mafia rates of 20, 30, 40%." — Brandon Elliot (~9:33)

**Review and build a credit profile in sequence** (operations)
1. Review the credit report and separate accurate information from entries that may be wrong.
2. Address late payments, collections, charge-offs, or other issues before relying on account-building tactics.
3. The guest recommends monitoring payment timing, utilization, account age, and account mix.
4. Treat the guest's score targets and timelines as claims, not guaranteed outcomes.
> "You have to do fix otherwise it's lipstick on a pig, right? Because people do that out there, too." — Brandon Elliot (~19:27)

**Document credit-report disputes** (legal)
1. Obtain reports from the relevant bureaus and identify entries that appear inaccurate.
2. Submit a dispute with supporting documents through the bureau's stated process.
3. Keep copies, dates, certified-mail numbers, and tracking information.
4. Do not promise a specific removal or improvement timeline.
> "if they keep giving you stall tactics, you're going to want to have like clear certified mail numbers and and tracking and everything dialed in" — Brandon Elliot (~32:05)

**Screen authorized-user accounts and limit exposure** (operations)
1. The guest says an account should have a long history, low balance, and no late payments.
2. Explain that a buyer does not receive spending access but shares sensitive identity details to be added.
3. Agree on the duration and removal process in advance.
4. Monitor changes in the primary cardholder's account because a balance spike or late payment can affect the added user's profile.
> "it's got to be good. Like, it's got to be no late payments collection. like it's got to be in good status, long history, ideally large limit, and low balance." — Brandon Elliot (~21:25)

**Use a property-investment capital cycle** (finance_unit_economics)
1. The guest describes buying, renovating, renting, refinancing, and repeating.
2. Model the project around renovation cost, rental income, refinance terms, valuation, and debt repayment.
3. The guest says he uses credit funding for real estate, but provides no project-level return calculation.
> "I've always done the burr strategy. Buy, renovate, rent, refinance, repeat." — Brandon Elliot (~35:45)

**Treat aged-entity offers skeptically** (legal)
1. Do not treat an aged entity as sufficient for funding approval.
2. The guest says lenders consider multiple underwriting factors and that entity history is only one part.
3. Avoid sellers promising very large funding outcomes for a high upfront payment.
> "It's one piece of the whole pie. There's 45 rules on the checklist basically on how underwriting is judging us." — Brandon Elliot (~34:23)

## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: Learn each lender's rules and application sequence before applying; a bank's own limits can affect later approvals.
- *finance_unit_economics*: A funding offer should be judged by its full cost, including fees, promotional-period length, and the effect of new transactions.
- *operations*: Maintain accurate records and follow up when disputing a credit-report entry; the guest recommends keeping a paper trail.
- *validation_testing*: Fix substantive credit-report problems before relying on account-building tactics.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Use debt only when the repayment plan and investment case are clear; access to a large credit limit is not the same as a profitable investment.
- *legal_risk*: A legal entity is only one part of a lender's underwriting decision; avoid paying a premium for promises of guaranteed funding.
- *mindset_execution*: People may overlook opportunities because they have not learned how the underlying financial products work.
- *legal_risk*: Do not assume a paid collection disappears from a credit report; the guest says payment alone may not remove a negative entry.
- *operations*: Use marketplace or lender tactics with careful attention to downside exposure, since a change in the primary account can affect an authorized user.
- *scaling*: Build relationships and preserve lender history instead of treating each application as an isolated transaction.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not use an ordinary cash advance or balance transfer without confirming that the promotional rate and fee apply; the guest warns that standard rates can be very high.
- The guest says a new card transaction or subscription on a card with a transferred balance can trigger interest on the balance; verify issuer terms before using the card.
- Credit repair results and timing are not guaranteed. The guest explicitly says it is illegal to promise a time frame.
- Authorized-user access can backfire if the primary cardholder misses a payment or runs up a balance, and it requires sharing sensitive personal information.
- The guest warns that sellers of aged entities may charge tens of thousands while promising implausibly large funding. He says an entity is only one underwriting factor.
- High leverage can create serious repayment and credit risks. The transcript's examples do not establish that a borrower can repay safely or qualify for the described products.
- The guest's US credit-repair and credit-card claims are not evidence that the same strategy or legal process works in the UK.
- The guest promotes a credit boot camp and credit-related businesses, and the host includes a promotional segment for an entity-formation service.
