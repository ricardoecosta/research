3. Check whether the site ranks across many related keywords rather than a single phrase.

**Account for seasonal traffic before estimating revenue** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Compare traffic peaks and valleys across the year.
2. Identify the months that drive the high season.
3. Estimate revenue for the high season separately instead of treating it as a year-round monthly run rate.
> "But for a solid three or four months out of the year, this website is likely getting slammed with traffic." — Niche Pursuits host (name not stated) (~3:43)

**Consider a narrow calculator or a calculator feature on an existing site** (product)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: A calculator can be very simple and still attract substantial search and repeat traffic if it answers a specific query.
- *customer_acquisition*: Research search demand and traffic sources instead of assuming a useful tool will attract visitors on its own.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Treat seasonal traffic as a separate business condition and model its high months accordingly.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Ad revenue depends on both traffic volume and page views per visitor, while RPM is an assumption that should be stated clearly.
- *offer_design*: A broad library of calculators can serve many use cases, but it is a larger product than a single niche tool.
- *offer_design*: A calculator can be added to an existing website as well as built as a standalone niche site.
- *customer_acquisition*: A working website needs marketing and traffic planning, not only the initial tool.
