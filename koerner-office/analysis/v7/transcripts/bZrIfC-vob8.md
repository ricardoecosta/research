## Summary

Chris Koerner presents five low-capital businesses that sit around the hot tub industry rather than in hot tub manufacturing: hot tub inspection, custom spa covers, hot tub moving, hot tub rentals, and Airbnb hot tub cleaning/maintenance. Each is backed by case studies (mostly second-hand, found online or on Reddit) with revenue and pricing claims. He gives a validation playbook built on local Facebook groups, a Google Business Profile, a one-page website and cheap start-up. He closes by arguing that 'sweaty', non-AI businesses will gain demand as more people chase AI.

### I1. Airbnb hot tub cleaning and maintenance service

- **Customer:** Airbnb / short-term rental owners and property managers with hot tubs
- **Problem:** Normal Airbnb cleaners do not know how to maintain hot tubs, yet a hot tub lets hosts charge $40-$100 more per night or is needed to compete.
- **Offer:** Hot tub cleaning, chemical balancing, drain and fill, with an Airbnb-level cleanliness guarantee
- **Business model:** Recurring weekly/monthly maintenance plans plus one-off service calls
- **Pricing:** Per-visit weekly service, per-job drain and fill, monthly plans. Reddit operator USD 45-65 per weekly chemical balance; Reddit operator USD 199 per drain and fill; monthly maintenance plan USD 100 per month; Vermont spa service company USD 45-65 per week (summer); Vermont spa service company USD 65-75 per week (winter); some of Austin's one-off calls USD 900 per service call Hot tub adds roughly $1-2k/month booking income at 20 nights rented per the host's maths.
- **Acquisition:** facebook_groups_communities: Reddit operator posted once in a local Facebook group, landed three clients in month one; host recommends sifting for the best 10-20% of local groups.; google_business_profile_maps: Set up a Google Business Profile with photos, videos, five-star reviews, hours and website link.; referrals_word_of_mouth: Offer 50% off first jobs for a photo testimonial, or Amazon gift cards for referrals.; seo_organic_search: Simple one-page website; Austin got inbound demand with no paid ads.
- **Delivery:** Technicians physically visit each hot tub to balance chemicals, drain and fill and repair; Davis's firm scaled to 17 employees; chemicals bought in bulk.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Reddit operator: 40 hot tubs per week grosses $7-8k per month; Davis (Chicago): 100+ units, over $1M annualised revenue, 17 employees in 3 years; Vermont company: 30-40 hot tubs a day, 4 days a week, $9-10k per week; Austin: first month MRR over $2,000 with about $1,000 chemicals and $750 website
- **Tools:** Zapier, Slack, Google Sheets, Facebook groups, Google Business Profile, Bizzy
- **Startup cost:** Small: Austin about $750 website + a couple hundred for LLC + ~$1,000 chemicals; Reddit operator about $500 (logo $30, contract $250, hoses and chemicals $220); insurance $2-3k a year
- **Time to first revenue:** Within first month (Reddit operator landed clients in month one)
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — Short-term rental market and hot tubs exist in the UK (Airbnb, holiday lets, Lake District, Cotswolds, Scotland); prices are in USD and need adapting, and public liability insurance and chemical handling rules apply. London/Bromley has fewer hot tub rentals, so a rural area is likely needed.
- **Risks:** Seasonality in cold markets; Physical labour; hard to automate; Reliance on Airbnb host market; Insurance and chemical-handling liability
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 2/5 (Local Facebook group posting, GBP and a website can be tool-assisted, but group posting needs a human presence and cold DMs to individuals are restricted under PECR.); onboarding 3/5 (Quotes, contracts and scheduling can be handled by forms and agents.); delivery 0/5 (Physical on-site hot tub servicing every visit.); billing 4/5 (Recurring subscription invoicing is easily automated.); support 3/5 (Agents can handle scheduling and FAQ; callouts need humans.)
> "Airbnbs like this one I'm staying in today. I'm in Island Park, Idaho, are able to charge between $40 and $100 more per night if they have a hot tub." — Chris Koerner (~18:27)
> "normal Airbnb cleaners have no idea how to do it. It's not in their wheelhouse." — Chris Koerner (~18:56)

### I2. Hot tub rental business

- **Customer:** Consumers for weekend getaways, party groups, Airbnb guests and hosts without hot tubs
- **Problem:** People want a hot tub for a weekend or stay without buying one.
- **Offer:** Delivered rental hot tubs
- **Business model:** Rental fees per weekend/week/month
- **Pricing:** Rental tiers by duration. depends on model USD 200-400 per weekend; transcribed as '$2.69' (likely $269) USD 269 per week; transcribed as '$4.99' (likely $499) USD 499 per month Weekly and monthly prices appear mis-transcribed.
- **Acquisition:** seo_organic_search: Steve dominates search results for Long Island hot tub rentals as first mover.; paid_social: Facebook ads targeted at people vacationing or about to visit an area.
- **Delivery:** Owner delivers and sets up tubs and handles chemicals; spouse handles scheduling.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Started with 3 used hot tubs for $6,500; now 25+; At 50% capacity, $12-15k per month revenue; Net $7-9k per month after delivery and chemicals; One tub at 50% capacity nets over $10,000 a year
- **Tools:** Facebook ads
- **Startup cost:** About $6,500 for three used tubs, plus insurance of $2-3k a year
- **Time to first revenue:** Not stated; tubs paid off after a couple of months of rentals
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Hot tub hire already exists in the UK and demand for staycations is strong; needs insurance, electrics/GFCI (RCD) safety and storage space. Weather is less reliable.
- **Risks:** Seasonality; Damage and liability; Electrical and water safety; Delivery logistics; Capital tied up in tubs
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 4/5 (SEO and Facebook geo-targeted ads are software-driven.); onboarding 4/5 (Online booking and e-sign can be automated.); delivery 0/5 (Physically transporting, setting up and cleaning tubs.); billing 5/5 (Online payments and deposits.); support 3/5 (Agents handle enquiries; faults need humans.)
> "After delivery and chemicals, he's netting 7 to $9,000 per month while still teaching full-time." — Chris Koerner (~15:06)

### I3. Hot tub moving and relocation

- **Customer:** Hot tub retailers, spa companies, homeowners selling or moving
- **Problem:** Regular movers refuse hot tubs and wrongly moving one can cause thousands in damage.
- **Offer:** Specialist hot tub removal and delivery
- **Business model:** Per-move fees
- **Pricing:** Per-move flat fee, with per-mile surcharge. average (Charles) USD 600 per move; Big Beard Movers, Houston USD 500 per move; Utah Tub and Spa Movers USD 350-650 per move Extra per-mile rate beyond 75 miles at Big Beard Movers.
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: Post moving services on Facebook Marketplace; validate with Google and Facebook ads.; facebook_groups_communities: Organic posts in Facebook groups to test demand.; partnerships: B2B work for spa dealers and retailers who do not want to run delivery logistics.
- **Delivery:** Crews physically lift and move tubs using spa dolly, flatbed, crane or forklift.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Average move $600, 2-3 hours; 5 moves/day = $2,500/day; Net profit to owner 35-45%; Utah Tub and Spa Movers: three crews, 6-8 moves/day, 2024 revenue over $4M
- **Tools:** Spa dolly, Facebook Marketplace, Google Ads, Facebook ads
- **Startup cost:** About $10k: spa dolly $3-5k (financeable), insurance ~$3k/yr, straps/equipment ~$1k
- **Time to first revenue:** Not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Works in the UK with different equipment and prices; needs goods-in-transit and public liability insurance and possibly a van.
- **Risks:** Damage claims; Injury risk and heavy equipment; Insurance costs; Equipment capital
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Ads, marketplace listings and Google Business Profile can be run by agents.); onboarding 3/5 (Quote forms and scheduling are automatable.); delivery 0/5 (Heavy physical moving.); billing 4/5 (Online invoicing.); support 3/5 (Agents handle booking questions.)
> "most people have no idea that if you move a hot tub wrong, it can cause thousands of dollars worth of damage. And regular movers refuse to touch these things." — Chris Koerner (~9:33)

### I4. Custom hot tub covers via Shopify and Chinese print-on-demand

- **Customer:** Hot tub owners needing replacement covers
- **Problem:** Existing covers get waterlogged, fall apart and do not fit well; slow delivery from competitors.
- **Offer:** Better or custom-designed hot tub covers with faster turnaround
- **Business model:** One-off product sales with 4-5 year replacement cycle
- **Pricing:** Retail product price. retail range USD 300-1000 per cover; example sale price USD 500 per cover; example cost USD 250 per cover Target LTV:CAC of 5:1.
- **Acquisition:** paid_social: Test 10-20 AI-generated designs with Facebook ads; shift budget to lowest cost per click.
- **Delivery:** Order placed on Shopify, forwarded to Chinese manufacturer (Alibaba/AliExpress) which prints and ships directly; 3-week promised turnaround.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Example: sell $500, cost $250; Ocean Spa Covers (Babul) grew from $87.50 first order to multi-million dollar business; Spacovers.com founder shipped in two weeks vs competitors' four
- **Tools:** Canva, VO3, Sora, Grok, ChatGPT, Shopify, Facebook ads, Alibaba, AliExpress
- **Startup cost:** Low: Shopify $39/month plus ad testing budget
- **Time to first revenue:** Not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Shopify plus overseas manufacturing works in the UK, but customs duty, VAT and longer shipping times apply; UK suppliers may be better for fit and speed.
- **Risks:** Wrong sizes, misprints and defects; Long shipping from China; Polarising designs may not sell; Easy drop shipping is hard now; Ad cost may exceed margin
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 4/5 (Paid ads and creative testing are agent-operable.); onboarding 4/5 (Shopify checkout with measurement form.); delivery 3/5 (Supplier-fulfilled dropship; humans handle defects and sizing issues.); billing 5/5 (Shopify payments.); support 3/5 (Agents handle tracking and returns; misprint disputes need people.)
> "The days of easy drop shipping are long gone." — Chris Koerner (~7:19)
> "these custom hot tub covers can retail from $300 to $1,000 plus. And the thing is about these covers, they need replacing every four to 5 years." — Chris Koerner (~6:07)

- **Startup cost:** About $500

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate through local Facebook groups** (acquisition)
1. Find multiple Facebook groups in each town and ignore the roughly 80% that are spammy
2. Spend time only in the 10-20% active, moderated groups
3. Post polls and feelers about demand for cleaning, covers, repairs, moving
4. Ask Airbnb owners what is breaking in their systems
5. Be upfront that you are testing and promise responsiveness, honesty and standing behind your work
> "Find the 10 to 20% that are gold. Spend all your time in there and ignore everything else." — Chris Koerner (~19:49)

**Referral-fee partnership with home inspectors** (sales)
1. Have the home inspector tell clients the hot tub is outside their scope
2. Inspector offers a $400 add-on
3. Inspector keeps $100 and passes $300 to you
4. Aligned incentives keep referrals flowing

**Test hot tub demand with Airbnb hosts using data** (sales)
1. Ask hosts without a hot tub why competitors have them
2. Propose advertising the property with a hot tub for December
3. Compare bookings or price against last year and ask guests if the hot tub mattered
4. Use the result to sell rental or maintenance

**Test cover designs with ads before buying stock** (product)
1. Generate 10-20 designs with AI
2. List on a Shopify site
3. Run Facebook ads and push budget to lowest cost per click
4. Target LTV:CAC of 5:1
5. Order one-by-one from Chinese manufacturer shipping direct to customers
6. Capture email and phone for 4-5 year replacement reminders

**Gradual pivot by asking customers a new question** (sales)
1. On inbound moving calls, ask if they also have a hot tub
2. Offer to beat competing quote (e.g. $450 vs $500)
3. Shift niche focus gradually

**Cheap start discipline** (finance)
1. Do not incorporate until you have customers
2. Start small, under-buy chemicals and materials
3. Track all expenses in Google Sheets
4. Use a personal account at first, upgrade to QuickBooks or Xero when rolling
5. Buy insurance (about $2-3k/year)

**Get first reviews and testimonials** (acquisition)
1. Set up Google Business Profile with photos, videos and hours
2. Do first jobs free or at 50% off for photo testimonials
3. Offer Amazon gift cards for referrals
4. Build one-page website with phone, form, services and pricing

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Look for businesses around an industry, not in it; adjacent service niches are cheaper and more approachable than manufacturing.
- *niche_selection*: Specialist niches beat generalists: generalists refuse or exclude awkward items, and specialists can charge a premium.
- *offer_design*: Recurring revenue with aligned incentives (Airbnb income uplift) makes a service business sticky and valuable.
- *offer_design*: Speed of delivery can be a competitive moat even without better quality (two weeks versus four for spa covers).
- *validation_testing*: Validate demand cheaply (polls, local groups, ads) before buying equipment or incorporating.
- *mindset_execution*: Pivot gradually by changing how you talk to customers rather than burning the boats.
- *automation_ai*: As AI draws entrepreneurs, supply of non-AI 'sweaty' services may fall and prices rise.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Customer lifetime value justifies spending up on acquisition; target LTV:CAC of about 5:1.
- *customer_acquisition*: Being first mover locally (SEO, Google profile) can dominate small niche markets.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Manufacturing from China will bring headaches: wrong sizes, misprints, defects.
- Do not incorporate before finding customers.
- Do not over-buy materials or chemicals; under-buy.
- Pay for insurance; moving or servicing tubs carries liability.
- Check whether hot tubs actually exist in your climate and market before launching.
