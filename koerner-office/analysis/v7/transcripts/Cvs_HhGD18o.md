## Tactics and playbook steps

**List land on multiple relevant marketplaces** (acquisition)
1. Identify every relevant marketplace for the asset or use case.
2. Create a listing with professional pictures and a keyword-rich description.
3. Keep availability current and wait for inbound bookings.

**Test portable storage with a lease-purchase agreement** (finance)
1. Find a roadside parcel that may have storage demand.
2. Negotiate to lease the land while testing the business.
3. Agree on a future discussion about purchasing the property if the business works.
4. Use movable containers so they can be taken away if the test fails.
> "If it's working for me, I will buy your property above your asking price. If it's not going well for me, I'll pick up my portable self-storage containers, I'll take my ball and go home." — Chris Koerner (~2:23)

**Build recurring pallet supply** (operations)
1. Find businesses that regularly receive more pallets than they ship.
2. Offer free collection to firms that otherwise pay to dispose of pallets.
3. Repair usable pallets and sell them to firms that need a reliable supply.
4. Use direct contact to build a book of business.
> "You find a handful of businesses that are consistently getting rid of pallets, or you just go to homeowners that have an extra pallet or two." — Chris Koerner (~4:50)

**Stack multiple property booking channels** (acquisition)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Look for assets that buyers dislike for one use but that have another commercially useful feature, such as roadside visibility, awkward shape or storage space.
- *validation_testing*: Test demand with a reversible setup before committing to major construction or equipment purchases.
- *offer_design*: A low-cost asset can compete with a purpose-built product if it solves the same customer problem.
- *customer_acquisition*: Free collection can be valuable when it saves a business the time and cost of disposal.
- *offer_design*: Sell access to existing assets in more than one way when suitable: nightly stays, events, filming or parking can use the same property.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Check the full costs behind a margin or payback claim, including labour, transport, maintenance, delivery and time.
- *niche_selection*: Choose a niche where customers already have a recurring problem and available supply is limited, while validating the claimed economics.
- *mindset_execution*: A service can be hard or unattractive and still be worth evaluating if customers need it and alternatives are scarce.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Use patience as part of the business model when growing trees or seasoning timber, but account for the delayed cash flow.
- *customer_acquisition*: Build customer acquisition around where the buyers already search: marketplaces, local groups, keyword-rich listings and relevant directories.
- *niche_selection*: Location can determine whether a business works; physical venues need enough nearby customers.
- *finance_unit_economics*: A property or business can be made less risky by negotiating control first and preserving an option to buy later.

- Crypto mining is volatile: the host says he has had profitable years and years when he lost money, and advises buyers to beware and do their own research.
- Physical services such as pond cleaning, firewood, construction, tree relocation and shed delivery require labour, equipment and safety controls.
- Some ideas are highly location-dependent; the host says an indoor sports venue will not work if it is too rural.
- The land subdivision rules described are Texas-specific and should not be assumed to apply elsewhere.
- Septic service profitability is asserted without supporting cost detail, and the work is dirty and technically demanding.

- Food pop-ups require more than a small equipment investment; food safety, allergens, permission and insurance are not discussed in the video.
- The host says mature nursery trees can require years of waiting and some species fail early.
- The host acknowledges that the car business had breakdowns and other operational headaches.
- Private pool rentals involve safety and liability risks; the host suggests charging extra for a lifeguard but does not detail safety requirements.
- Short-term home rental, weddings, sports facilities, food businesses, telecoms and septic work may require local permits, planning or regulatory compliance.
- The video mixes first-hand operations, hypothetical calculations and second-hand stories; headline revenue and profit figures are not independently verified.
- For UK implementation, unsolicited marketing messages to individuals and sole traders require careful PECR compliance; screen cold calls against TPS/CTPS.
