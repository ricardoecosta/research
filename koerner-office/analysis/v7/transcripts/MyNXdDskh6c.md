## Summary

Dark Horse Entrepreneur host explains a weekend plan to validate a small digital product (ebook, template pack, prompt library, mini course) before building it. Evidence must cost the buyer something: existing spend, specific pain in their own words, or commitment (email, call, pre-order, payment). The plan sets go/fix/kill thresholds on Friday, researches buyers on Saturday, then writes a one-paragraph offer, builds a mock-up and landing page, and asks for a real commitment on Sunday. Key takeaway: opinions are worthless, pre-sales are the strongest evidence, and a killed idea is a cheap win.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Set go/fix/kill thresholds before testing** (mindset)
1. On Friday night spend about 10 minutes writing what pass looks like
2. Go: at least 3 strangers take a real commitment step, ideally payment or pre-order
3. Fix: pain and engagement but no commitment; change only one of promise, price or buyer and rerun
4. Kill/park: no pain found and no one paying nearby
5. Write on a sticky note as your referee
> "you're going to decide the pass mark before you start the test, not after." — Host (~1:39)

**Saturday research for existing spend and pain in buyer words** (acquisition)
1. Pick one buyer and one problem
2. Check Gumroad, Etsy, Teachable, Stan Store, Amazon for nearby products with reviews and sales counts
3. Mine Reddit, Facebook groups, forums, YouTube comments and 1-3 star competitor reviews
4. Copy exact phrases into a document without paraphrasing
5. Feed only the real quotes to ChatGPT to group by theme and rank pains
6. Target: 5+ real people describing the pain
> "Copy the exact phrases that you find into a document. Don't paraphrase because you want their own words." — Host (~7:28)

**One-paragraph offer plus mock-up landing page** (product)
1. Answer who it is for, what result, exactly what they receive, and the price
2. Be concrete (e.g. 12-page checklist and three email templates)
3. Build a cover image, sample page or table of contents in 30-90 minutes
4. Put offer, mock-up and a buy/sign-up button on a simple landing page
> "You don't need the product to test the product." — Host (~8:32)

**Past-behaviour customer conversations** (sales)
1. Message 10-20 buyer-fit people, leading with a question not a pitch
2. Ask about the last time the problem happened, what they tried, what it cost
3. Show mock-up and price at the end and ask if they would want it this week
4. If yes, give them a way to act immediately
> "Tell me about the last time this happened to you. What did you try? What did it cost you?" — Host (~12:35)

**Honest pre-sale** (sales)
1. Include a real payment option on the landing page
2. State plainly it is a pre-sale with a specific ship date
3. Offer a full refund if you decide not to build
4. Fallback: specific waitlist, not a generic newsletter
> "Payment is the strongest form of evidence you will ever get" — Host (~13:13)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Evidence counts in proportion to what it costs the buyer: opinions are zero, existing spend, specific pain and commitment/payment climb the ladder.
- *validation_testing*: Speed of building with AI is not proof of demand; validate before building.
- *mindset_execution*: Pre-commit pass/fail thresholds so you cannot grade your own idea on a curve.
- *niche_selection*: Narrow the buyer to a specific person, situation and cost rather than a crowd like 'busy people'.
- *niche_selection*: Competition signals an existing market; existing spend is good news.
- *validation_testing*: Ask about past behaviour, not future promises.
- *pricing*: Price, not free, tests real demand; pick a real price early.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI for brainstorming, sorting and drafting but keep real customers as the source of truth.
- *mindset_execution*: Write down what evidence would prove you wrong to turn hope into a test.
- *mindset_execution*: A killed idea within 48 hours is a cheap win; change only one variable when fixing.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Friend or family compliments ("I would totally buy that") are not evidence.
- Do not let ChatGPT invent quotes or stand in for real customers; never publish AI-generated reviews or testimonials.
- Small numbers are noisy and big creator results are not your forecast.
- If you find no existing products anywhere, be careful; it is often a lane nobody wants.
- Don't promote in communities that ban it; lead with a question.
