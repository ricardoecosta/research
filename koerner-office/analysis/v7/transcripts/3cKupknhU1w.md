## Summary

Chris Koerner and co-host Nick riff on offline and 'old-school' businesses: hot tub servicing for Airbnbs and hot tub moving for retailers, concierge prep for lake houses, touchless check-in SaaS for boat/kayak rentals, and buying moat-protected destination rental businesses that charge $95 for a 3-hour inner tube. They discuss a Shopify order-confirmation cold email hack, using a B2B directory (ABA autism clinics) as lead generation for an agency, and The Flower Letters, a $10/month subscription of WWII-era story letters with ~31,000 subscribers (~$310k/month) grown by word of mouth. Spin-off ideas include correspondence chess by mail, interactive AI-written kids' story letters, personalised AI story gift books, a directory of niche wedding services, and a niche white-labelled vibe-coding app. Takeaway: analog, local, and 'zig while others zag' businesses with real-world moats are underrated.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Shopify order-confirmation cold outreach** (acquisition)
1. Create a Shopify store (trial)
2. Build a CSV of dummy orders with target emails and a product name that is your pitch (e.g. 'Outsource stump grinding')
3. Import orders, mark as paid, trigger confirmation emails that land in the primary inbox
4. Include a tailored message and quote link
5. If shut down, open a new account
> "Shopify order confirmation emails almost always land in the primary inbox." — Chris Koerner (~15:38)

**Directory-to-agency funnel** (acquisition)
1. Pick a high-ticket, high-trust vertical not saturated by marketers (healthcare, CPAs, lawyers)
2. Build a directory of all businesses with AI
3. Cold email each: you're listed, want to rank higher?
4. Upsell premium placement then PPC/SEO agency services
5. Build ~10 directories expecting 2-3 winners

**Permissionless directory then charge** (pricing)
1. Scrape niche vendors into a directory in one city
2. Send them free leads
3. Return later: pay (e.g. $100-250/month) for placement or ads, or be removed
> "Hey, you're getting leads from my website. I'm going to charge you now or I'm going to take you off the website." — Chris Koerner (~52:56)

**Port features from mature industries to sub-industries** (product)
1. Find a feature new in a big industry (touchless car rental pickup)
2. Apply it as a service to a smaller adjacent industry (boat, ATV, kayak rentals)
3. Sell it to those companies as SaaS
> "find an established industry like car rentals, been around forever. Look at the features and the offerings that are just now starting to come out" — Chris Koerner (~6:21)

**Copy a proven subscription in a new genre** (product)
1. Take The Flower Letters model
2. Pick other top Amazon fiction categories for women (post-apocalyptic, space opera, Renaissance Italy)
3. Launch one or several series
4. Use paid Meta ads the incumbent isn't using

**Roll up Facebook groups for distribution** (acquisition)
1. Buy niche Facebook groups (as done for Texas Snacks with a Buc-ee's group)
2. Merge them to build a list
3. Sell a subscription to members
> "You went and bought a BIES Facebook group. You could go and probably buy some chess groups, roll them up, merge them together." — Chris Koerner (~35:48)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Most valuable local knowledge isn't on the internet, which protects offline businesses from AI.
- *pricing*: Location, domain and history create moats that let destination businesses charge huge premiums.
- *offer_design*: Zig while others zag: slow, analog experiences can win in an instant-gratification world.
- *offer_design*: Serialised products have very low churn because customers want to know how the story ends.
- *niche_selection*: A small B2B audience can be worth more than a large consumer audience for directories.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Low USPS bulk postage is an underappreciated subsidy for mail-based businesses.
- *niche_selection*: Companies that offload logistics (retailers not wanting to move hot tubs) create service niches.
- *validation_testing*: Start a marketplace as a directory in one local market, doing things that don't scale, before expanding.
- *automation_ai*: AI grows the pie and gives individuals the power institutions used to have; good content will stand out more.
- *customer_acquisition*: An incumbent with no paid ads leaves room for a well-funded copycat to grow the category.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The Shopify fake-order outreach hack risks account termination and abuses platform trust; in the UK it would also breach PECR when sent to individuals or sole traders without consent, and fake 'paid order' emails could be misleading.
- Directories monetised only by banner ads earn little; you need volume or B2B monetisation.
- Home services are over-saturated with marketers; look elsewhere for directory/agency plays.
- Directories take time to build SEO traction; don't expect immediate results.
- High gross margins can hide heavy customer acquisition spend below the line.
- Vibe-coding gets you 80-90% of the way; the last 10-20% (integrations, payments, security) needs engineers.
