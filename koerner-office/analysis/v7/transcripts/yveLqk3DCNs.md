## Tactics and playbook steps

**Calculate a buyback rate before delegating** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Divide net income by annual hours worked to estimate hourly income.
2. Halve the hourly income to set a buyback rate.
3. Compare the cost of a task with that rate before deciding whether to delegate.

**Use one premium wardrobe item and replace worn clothes** (other)

**Pay for proximity to useful people** (customer_acquisition)
1. Reduce time spent commuting where possible.

3. Treat access to a network as an input to income rather than an incidental benefit.

**Price the cost of exiting before committing** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Before signing a lease, contractor agreement, or vendor contract, ask what it costs to walk away.
2. Set aside the exit cost before signing.
3. Add the cancellation cost of recurring commitments to an exit fund.
> "Before you commit to anything, price out exactly what it costs to undo it. Not just what it costs to start it." — Codie Sanchez (~8:47)

**Use CATO to decide what to delegate** (operations)
1. List tasks that should be cut entirely.
2. Automate tasks that remain, such as inbox management.
3. Delegate tasks that software cannot handle adequately.
4. Outsource a whole job when it no longer needs to report back for each action.
> "What does this mean? Cut, automate, delegate, outsource." — Codie Sanchez (~12:02)

**Start at the bottom of the assistant ladder** (hiring_outsourcing)
1. Write down three recurring tasks that do not require the founder specifically.
2. Begin with a virtual assistant for inbox, scheduling, and repetitive work.
3. Move to an assistant for filtering daily tasks as needs grow.
4. Use a chief of staff only when the role can run projects and make decisions end to end.
> "Before the end of the day, write down the three tasks, eating your week that don't require you specifically using Keto." — Codie Sanchez (~14:40)

**Choose skills by how often they will be reused** (validation_testing)
1. Estimate how many times a skill will recur in your work.
2. Prioritize repeatable skills such as sales, negotiation, and operations.
3. Invest more in skills you expect to use repeatedly.
> "How many times if you acquire this skill, will you repeat its use?" — Codie Sanchez (~16:34)

**Use meal preparation to buy back cooking time** (operations)
1. Compare the weekly cost of prepared meals with the time spent cooking.
2. If using a chef, batch several days of food in one visit.
3. Choose the option only if its cost fits your own buyback rate.
> "For some people, this is a no brainer. Others, the buyback rate doesn't work out. Do the math, see what works for you." — Codie Sanchez (~10:44)
