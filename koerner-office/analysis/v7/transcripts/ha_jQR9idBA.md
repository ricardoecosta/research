## Tactics and playbook steps

**Start with a simple first clean** (validation_testing)
1. Use relevant prior experience to choose a service you understand.
2. Dalton filed an LLC, contacted prospects, found a customer and booked a first clean within 24 hours.
3. Perform or closely observe early jobs to learn what customers and cleaners need.
> "within 24 hours I'd filed for my LLC. got called around, created my first customer, booked my first clean" — Dalton Dry (~4:13)

**Build inbound demand before relying on lead scraping** (acquisition)
1. Create a website early.
2. Improve the site's SEO and Google presence so local customers can find the company.
3. Track which channels generate leads and reduce reliance on manual group posting when it consumes too much time.
> "my SEO sucked. Um, and uh because I was doing nothing for it." — Dalton Dry (~8:28)

**Test lead channels and monitor their return** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Test paid social and marketplace leads rather than assuming one channel works in every market.
2. Track booked revenue and ad spend by channel.
3. Keep using channels that return enough revenue to cover labour and acquisition costs; review margins as recurring work builds.
> "our return um on uh ad spend for Facebook um is is our our has been our best" — Dalton Dry (~12:15)

**Train a VA to handle warm residential sales** (hiring)
1. Recruit and vet candidates carefully.
2. Have the VA attend the company's sales calls and receive training.
3. Transfer routine warm-lead calls and booking to the VA, then monitor booked revenue.
> "he plugs into all y'all sales calls and gets training" — Dalton Dry (~10:23)

**Use service performance and communication to expand a commercial account** (operations)
1. Quote the initial sites carefully and establish who handles access, keys, supplies and escalation.
2. Prioritize reliable cleaning and proactive communication with the client's lead.
3. Record failures and clearly report what broke and what needs attention.
4. Use consistent delivery to earn additional sites from the same chain.

**Recruit locally for out-of-market contracts** (hiring)
1. Find local cleaners or small cleaning businesses in relevant Facebook groups.
2. Have an operations manager arrange interviews.
3. Personally vet candidates before assigning them to a contract.
4. Have contractors bring their supplies and provide the chemicals centrally.

**Build a repeatable outbound and vendor-portal pipeline** (acquisition)
1. Register as a federal contractor or as a vendor with the relevant state or corporate buyer.
2. Apply through corporate vendor portals and monitor for opportunities.
3. Assign a dedicated bidder to scout public and local tenders.
4. Use a pricing matrix to calculate bids.
5. Run targeted corporate email outreach and track which campaigns produce contracts.
> "I have a very heavy outbound motion like um we're tens of thousands of emails per month." — Dalton Dry (~24:26)

**Hire for trust and document the operation** (operations)
1. Review a large candidate pool and interview a smaller shortlist.
2. Put a better hiring process in place after no-shows and theft.
3. Pay enough to attract dependable cleaners.
4. Document recurring problems and communicate them to the client and operations lead.
> "We got rid of them and we started we put a better hiring process in place, started paying them better." — Dalton Dry (~19:58)

**Make owner independence a decision filter** (operations)
1. Prefer systems and roles that let the company operate without the founder doing every sale or clean.
2. Accept slower growth or missed opportunities when they would otherwise pull the owner into day-to-day delivery.
3. Keep replacing manual work with better processes and capable people.

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Choose a service where you have relevant experience or can learn the work directly; Dalton's prior housekeeping exposure helped him start quickly.
- *customer_acquisition*: A website alone does not create demand; learn and work on SEO or other channels that bring customers in.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Measure channel returns and remember that a lead's value depends on conversion and recurring customer revenue, not just its initial cost.
- *scaling*: Commercial work can expand beyond a local area when the founder can quote remotely and recruit local delivery teams.
- *operations*: Good communication and clear records can preserve client trust when operational problems occur.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Hire carefully, pay dependable cleaners and remove unreliable team members; remote delivery depends on trust.
- *operations*: Build systems and delegate routine work to become an owner rather than the person doing every job.
- *automation_ai*: Do not automate customer conversations just because the technology exists; consider whether customers want human empathy.
- *mindset_execution*: A full-time job does not automatically rule out starting a business, but the founder must be willing to invest time and effort.
- *mindset_execution*: Align with a spouse or business partner about the work, money and roles; Dalton says difficult financial conversations helped create a shared vision.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Early spending and setbacks can create relationship stress, so discuss financial decisions and avoid long commitments without evidence of results.
- *mindset_execution*: Dalton's personal view is to take a first step and learn by doing, while recognizing that financial returns may not arrive immediately.
