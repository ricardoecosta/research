## Tactics and playbook steps

**Find niche creators using average views** (acquisition)
1. Use a VA to review the creator feed in the target niche.
2. Shortlist creators averaging more than 25,000 views per video, rather than sorting by follower count.
3. Send a clear paid-promotion proposal and track replies, views, and resulting sales.
4. In the UK, do not send unsolicited marketing DMs to individual creators without the required consent; use consent-based introductions or lawful channels.
> "scroll on my for you page and outreach to everyone who has over 25,000 views on average." — George (~16:48)

**Use a VA for creator sourcing** (hiring)
1. After the first $1,000 in revenue, George recommends hiring a virtual assistant.
2. He sourced VAs through Fiverr or Upwork.
3. Show the VA the target niche, the creator feed to inspect, the view threshold, and the message script.
> "After you make your first thousand bucks, hire a VA instantly." — George (~80:44)

**Use a short creator brief and test deal structures** (pricing)
1. State that the message is a paid promotion in its opening words.
2. Explain the app briefly and say why the creator's content is a fit.
3. Test flat-fee and CPM deals, then compare the results against attributable sales.
4. Keep proven creators and stop spending on creators who do not produce engagement or sales.
> "The DM should be paid promo and the paid promo for app name. That should be the first line." — George (~80:09)

**Choose creators by content skill and audience fit** (acquisition)
1. Compare creators' average views and audience fit, not just their follower totals or athletic reputation.
2. Watch comments and engagement to judge whether the audience understands the product.
3. Start with a small test; retain creators whose content drives useful outcomes.
> "the ROI on like a a really good wrestler that's not that good at content Yeah. isn't really as big as the ROI on even though they have so much accolades." — George (~40:21)

**Turn successful organic creator videos into paid ads** (acquisition)
1. Find organic creator videos that demonstrate the product clearly.
2. Ask the creator to make a separate ad video when the organic integration is too subtle for paid placements.
3. Show the app and its result early; George advised putting the product in the first 15 seconds.
4. Study competing ads in Meta Ads Library and test creatives on Meta placements.
> "you need to show the product within the first 15 seconds" — George (~53:05)

**Lead with one clear product feature** (product)
1. Choose one visually clear feature that explains the app quickly.
2. Use that feature to bring users into the app, even if other features drive repeat use.
3. When one feature's content becomes familiar, introduce another useful feature to renew attention.
> "understand your app needs a gotcha feature, right? It needs one feature that you run advertising on." — George (~77:08)

**Measure customer quality, not just views or downloads** (validation_testing)
1. Track paid conversion, repeat use, churn, and sales alongside social views.
2. Compare creatives that generate downloads with creatives that demonstrate how the product helps.
3. Stop treating a large view count as success when users do not stay or pay.
> "Both apps got a million views in its first month. Wrestle AI the first month did 17K. Green did $35." — George (~27:33) [quote not matched verbatim]

**Make annual plans support growth, but consider monthly plans when growth is not the priority** (pricing)
1. George attached the trial to the annual subscription, encouraging customers to commit to a year.
2. He preferred front-loaded annual revenue while aggressively funding growth.
3. He suggested monthly plans may suit a business that is not trying to grow as quickly.
> "I would always try to frontload revenue this way I had more for your growth" — George (~32:22)

**Optimize ad campaigns for purchases** (acquisition)
1. Track purchases rather than only app installs.
2. Use a purchase event for campaign optimization when the app and tracking setup support it.
3. Compare cost per purchase and downstream revenue before increasing spend.
> "But if you want like like to make your ads 10 times more profitable, you optimize for purchases." — George (~18:43)

**Use first creator deals to build credibility and referrals** (acquisition)
1. Run a manageable first creator test and review comments, views, and sales.
2. Use successful collaborations as social proof when approaching other creators.
3. Make the creator relationship and the product's fit clear rather than assuming a large audience alone will convert.
> "even on your first it's okay to be unprofitable on your first few influencers because they're going to start spreading your word to other influencers." — George (~34:48)

**Keep campaign and business data in one place** (operations)
1. Track creator deals, contracts, ad metrics, and business results in an internal dashboard.
2. Connect the relevant data sources so the operator can ask consistent questions about daily results and profit and loss.
3. Use the data to make decisions, then check attribution against actual performance.
> "It tracks I run my entire business on it. my my metads, my creators, like literally dashboard contracts are sent through it." — George (~38:18)

**Treat app-store and platform relationships carefully** (legal)
1. Make subscription options and terms clear in the paywall.
2. Avoid public claims about borderline compliance that could attract review or enforcement.
3. If an account or listing is restricted, appeal through the platform and document the issue rather than assuming the first decision is final.
> "if you're doing anything that's anything close to being gray, never do that on Twitter cuz Apple's on there like a hawk." — George (~64:04)

**Protect runway and set a growth budget** (finance)
1. Keep enough operating cash available to continue growth while platform payouts are delayed.
2. Decide how much cash can be invested in ads without putting personal runway at risk.
3. George said he was more willing to scale once he had money set aside for living costs.
> "I have like this is my like per my personal runway is like six months to a year." — George (~85:01)

**Test a product's core feature before expanding the feature set** (product)
1. Build the feature that makes the product easy to explain and test whether the audience wants it.
2. Use repeated customer behavior to identify where the first feature stops being useful.
3. Add adjacent features that solve repeat-use problems, such as Wrestle AI's calorie and weight-management tools.
> "the analysis feature gets them in the door, but you can only really use the analysis feature like five times." — George (~44:51)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Choose a specific audience and a clear problem before chasing broad distribution. George said Wrestle AI had a more engaged niche than the low-intent audience for Green.
- *offer_design*: A product needs a feature people can understand quickly from a video or screenshot.
- *validation_testing*: Measure purchases, retention, and useful customer behavior rather than treating views or installs as proof of demand.
- *customer_acquisition*: Audience fit and creator skill can matter more than follower count or athletic credentials.
- *customer_acquisition*: A creator's organic video may work differently as a paid ad; make ad creative direct and show the product early.
- *offer_design*: Add features that give customers a reason to keep using the product after the initial hook has done its job.
- *niche_selection*: Seasonality can shape demand; George timed creator activity and product work around wrestling season.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: A 50/50 partnership can make sense to the founder when the partner brings essential audience access and product credibility.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Use a VA to free the founder from repeatable sourcing work once the business has early revenue.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Keep enough cash and personal runway to fund growth and survive delayed platform payouts.
- *mindset_execution*: Keep testing and learn from failed launches, but cut ideas that attract the wrong audience or fail to retain users.
- *legal_risk*: Treat app-store rules and public claims carefully; a platform relationship can affect whether customers can access the product.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The supplement brand ran paid ads that lost money, had little or no margin, and left inventory that George later abandoned.
- A large audience and high view count did not guarantee sales; the Green app received over a million views but made only $35.
- Rating-only workout ads generated downloads but caused quick churn; showing the actual workout produced better-quality interest.
- George said the app-store removal followed a public paywall discussion and a report to Apple; the app was unavailable for two weeks.
- The transcript describes buying fake followers to make an account look established. This can mislead creators and customers, damage trust, and may violate platform rules.
- Unsolicited marketing DMs to individual creators may be restricted by UK PECR; the interview's US-based outreach playbook should not be copied into the UK without a lawful basis and appropriate consent.
- The host and guest described an app-store pricing presentation that drew scrutiny. Subscription prices, trial terms, and billing intervals should be presented clearly and in the order required by platform rules.
- Weight-cutting and calorie-tracking features aimed at young wrestlers can create health and safety risks; the transcript does not describe qualified clinical oversight.
- Creator view counts can be manipulated; George said he checked comment quality and engagement, but also acknowledged that much of the sales attribution was intuition.
- George described a competitor threatening to sue and discussing an NDA. He chose not to sign an agreement he did not understand.
