The video is an interview with Flo, founder of Lindy, about building and using no-code AI agents for sales, customer support, research, recruiting, and personal assistance. Flo demonstrates agents that work across email, chat, phone, calendars, and other tools, while stressing that complex sales still needs a human closer. ## Tactics and playbook steps

**Sell agent implementation through useful public demos** (acquisition)
1. Build an agent that solves a recognizable problem for a specific audience.
2. Post demonstrations on LinkedIn and YouTube.
3. Offer a free template so prospects can try the workflow themselves.
4. Offer paid setup to viewers who want the result but do not want to implement it.
5. Charge an initial setup fee and consider a recurring maintenance fee.
> "most of them will go on to either do nothing or implement it themselves but a minority of them will say Hey just do this for me" — Flo (~38:04)

**Start with an industry you already understand** (niche_selection)
1. Choose an industry where you have experience, an audience, or a clear understanding of its challenges.
2. Look for repetitive work that businesses already assign to employees.
3. Build an agent for a concrete task before offering a broad AI transformation.
> "any industry where you've got an audience and you understands the industry and the challenges it's facing Just focus on that" — Flo (~38:37)

**Keep a human in the sales closing loop** (operations)
1. Use an agent for research, qualification, booking, reminders, meeting notes, and follow-up.
2. Have a human handle the closing conversation and relationship.
> "You still need like a human to close them That's for sure For now" — Flo (~20:24)

**Use voice agents where customers or staff are on the move** (product)
1. Look for users who spend the day in the field or cannot easily stop to answer a phone.
2. Use phone agents for repetitive enquiries or quick CRM updates.
3. Keep an escalation path for unusual requests and verify important records.
> "anytime your prospect is on the go or anytime if you're very busy and you can't pick up the phone" — Flo (~23:30)

**Use a knowledge base and branching for multiple businesses** (operations)
1. Export relevant historical email from the source inbox.
2. Add the useful material to a knowledge base for agent-assisted drafting.
3. Use a branching condition to route each business or topic to the correct knowledge base.
4. Review replies and add rules when the agent classifies a message incorrectly.
> "if this business go here if that business go there and then each of these branches would lead to a different knowledge base" — Flo (~13:28)

**Build human-feeling delays into automated replies** (operations)
1. Add a waiting step before sending a reply when an immediate response would feel unnatural.
2. Set different delays for routine messages and urgent messages.
> "Just like ZPO you can introduce like a waiting step" — Flo (~17:52)

**Use agents to search, qualify, and follow up with candidates** (hiring)
1. Give the agent target job titles and a list of relevant companies.
2. Ask it to search for multiple candidates and check prior contact in the inbox.
3. Review the candidate list before approving outreach.
4. Let the agent draft approved messages and schedule follow-ups.
5. Check that outreach complies with local marketing and data rules.
> "first it checks it searches my inbox to check if I've already reached out before because sometimes I have" — Flo (~34:49)

**Turn niche prospect research into a repeatable workflow** (customer_acquisition)
1. Define a narrow prospect profile and filter out companies that are too large or otherwise unsuitable.
2. Search a relevant marketplace or data source for prospects.
3. Find and verify business contact details.
4. Review outreach against the applicable marketing rules before sending.
> "Find independent studios like smaller than X like less than X in sales because if they're too big they've got their own in-house music person" — Flo (~40:16)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Start with a task tied directly to revenue, such as lead generation or sales follow-up, because buyers can compare the tool cost with revenue impact.
- *automation_ai*: An agent is more useful than a fixed automation when it needs to interpret context, handle conversation, or decide what to do next.
