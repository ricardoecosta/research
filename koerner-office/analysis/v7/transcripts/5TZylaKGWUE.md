## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Don, who runs a family fresh-squeezed lemonade business at the Frisco and Dallas farmers markets in Texas. It made about $250k last year over about 8 months, selling 32oz cups at $7 that cost about $1.25 to make. Don explains the recipe, equipment (about $1,500 to start), warehouse prep, staffing with his kids, a $300 booth fee, and expansion into $1,000-3,000 caterings, $500 cart rentals via Facebook Marketplace, venue concessions and a $19k frozen-banana trade-show job. His advice is to stop overthinking and just post up with a quality fresh product.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Theatrical fresh-squeeze process** (product)
1. Wedge the lemon with a Sunkist wedger
2. Smash with a pneumatic smasher customers can hear
3. Add 3oz 1:1 simple syrup, ~1lb ice, filtered water and shake
4. Squeeze a lemon in front of sceptics to prove freshness
> "you can hear it like in our markets, you can hear it and you walk in, you hear the sounds like money" — Don (~13:17)

**Batch prep at warehouse** (operations)
1. Buy 140-count lemons and 50lb sugar bags from Restaurant Depot / Ben E. Keith
2. Wash lemons centrally
3. Make 60-70 gallons of 1:1 simple syrup at a time by volume

**Anchor price** (pricing)
1. Research what others charge
2. Set a value price ($7 for 32oz) and never move it
3. Charge more ($10) where commissions apply

**Upsell rentals with product** (sales)
1. List cart rental on Facebook Marketplace at $500
2. Suggest adding lemonade to the rental

**Ask 'what else?' on catering leads** (sales)
1. When a client asks what else you do, offer a novel item (frozen bananas)
2. Freeze leftovers and sell at the next event

**Multi-market staffing** (operations)
1. Older kids run one market
2. Hired couple runs Dallas
3. Owner drops trailer Friday night and restocks Sunday
4. Owner does weekday caterings

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Overlooked 'kid' businesses can be very lucrative because people think they're too good for them.
- *mindset_execution*: Stop overthinking setup (LLC etc.); get a table, a tent and a recipe and post up.
- *offer_design*: Authenticity (real fresh product) beats gimmicky competitors and removes competition worry.
- *customer_acquisition*: Theatre and sound at the stall attract crowds.
- *pricing*: Events and catering have much higher ticket sizes than walk-up sales.
- *customer_acquisition*: A family business with kids involved looks wholesome and helps the brand.
- *offer_design*: Simple products are easier to sell walk-up; novel ones need explaining.
- *validation_testing*: Ideas from YouTube can be copied successfully with no experience.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Competitors sell Country Time or store-bought lemonade as 'fresh'; customers notice.
- Weather and seasonality hurt markets (bad April, two-month closure).
- Hand muddling at volume will wreck your hands.
- Fresh fruit add-ins add complexity and spoilage.
- Many markets require an application process.
