## Tactics and playbook steps

**Set go/fix/kill thresholds before testing** (mindset)
1. On Friday night spend about 10 minutes writing the pass mark before starting
2. Go: at least 3 strangers take a real commitment step, ideally payment or pre-order, or 1 paid pre-order plus several strong conversations
3. Fix: people have the pain and engage but nobody commits
4. Kill/park: no real people describing the pain and no spend nearby
5. Write on a sticky note as referee and adjust for audience size and price
> "you're going to write down what pass looks like before you even start your product" — Host (~4:21)

**Define a specific buyer persona** (product)
1. Define who they are, their situation, and what it costs them
2. Use ChatGPT/Claude to list 20 specific situations of wasted time for that buyer, then pick one
3. Pick one buyer and one problem for one weekend
4. Confirm pains with real people

**Two-hour evidence research on Saturday** (acquisition)
1. Check Gumroad, Etsy, Teachable, Stan Store, Amazon books for existing products with reviews, ratings and sales counts
2. Read Reddit, Facebook groups, forums and YouTube comments for pain in buyers' own words
3. Read 1-3 star competitor reviews to find the product gap
4. Copy exact phrases verbatim into a doc
5. Have ChatGPT group and rank themes using only the real quotes
6. Goal: existing spend found and 5+ real people describing the pain

**One-paragraph offer with concrete deliverables** (product)
1. Answer who it is for, what result, exactly what they receive, and the price
2. Be concrete (12-page checklist plus 3 email templates)
3. Pick a real price and avoid free
4. Use customers' exact phrases; generate 10 versions with ChatGPT and pick the one that sounds least like a marketer

**Mock-up landing page pre-sale** (sales)
1. Build a cover image, sample page, screenshot or table of contents in 30-90 minutes
2. Put offer paragraph, mock-up and action button on a simple landing page
3. State plainly it's a pre-sale with a specific ship date
4. Offer a full refund if you don't build
5. Share where buyers are, with permission, and in your profiles/audience

**Customer conversation script** (sales)
1. Message 10-20 buyer-matching people with a question not a pitch
2. Ask about the last time the problem happened, what they tried and what it cost
3. At the end show mock-up and price and ask if they would want it this week
4. If yes, give a way to act immediately

**Fix loop** (product)
1. If pain but no commitment, change exactly one of promise, price or buyer
2. Rerun a smaller test next weekend
3. Repeat until go signal

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Speed of building with AI is not proof of demand; validate before building.
- *validation_testing*: Rank evidence by what it costs the buyer: existing spend, specific pain in own words, then commitment; opinions are level zero.
- *niche_selection*: Competition showing existing spend is good news because it proves the market exists.
- *niche_selection*: Target a specific buyer with a specific costly situation, not a crowd like 'busy people'.
- *pricing*: Do not offer free when testing; price tests real demand.
- *validation_testing*: Ask about past behaviour rather than future intentions.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI to organise real customer data and draft, but not as a substitute for customers.
- *mindset_execution*: Name in advance what evidence would prove you wrong to turn hope into a test.
- *mindset_execution*: A killed idea within 48 hours is a win that saves weeks of building.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Friends saying they would buy is worthless; compliments are free and future predictions unreliable.
- Deciding pass criteria after the test makes you grade on a curve.
- Never publish AI-generated reviews or testimonials and do not let AI invent customer quotes.
- Small numbers are noisy; three buyers this weekend does not guarantee 300 next month, and big creator results are not your forecast.
- Finding no existing products at all is often a lane nobody wants to be in.
- Most communities ban promotion, so lead with questions.
