## Tactics and playbook steps

**Ask-reframe loop to handle 'too expensive'** (sales)
1. When the buyer says it's too expensive, ask what it costs them each month to not solve the problem
2. Get them to state a number and total it over the delay period
3. Reframe their own words back and compare to your price
4. Ask the next question and repeat until they close themselves; don't defend or attack
> "you're going to redo their words into your next words and then ask the question again." — Codie Sanchez (~6:15)

**Current state / future state / bridge discovery** (sales)
1. Ask questions to get the prospect to reveal the current pain: who is involved, where time and money are lost, how much
2. Get them to say a number; write it down and show it to them
3. Ask about the future state without pitching
4. Only then present your product as the bridge, restating their numbers
> "the bridge starts to write itself because you're just restating their own numbers back to them" — Codie Sanchez (~11:06)

**Build specific, named proof** (sales)
1. Use testimonials with name, photo and screenshot
2. Add before/after photos, payment links showing amounts, or video
3. Turn testimonials into stories with numbers
4. Link to the customer's real social profile for verification

**Use anti-sales and avatar filtering** (sales)
1. State openly who should not buy
2. Target people already in pain with a budget
3. Score prospects as financially ready, reliable, right fit, energising
4. Ask whether your offer is oxygen (something breaks if no) or dessert
> "You don't sell anyone anything. You find the people already predisposed to what the thing that you are selling." — Codie Sanchez (~19:01)

**Replace needy phrases with confident ones** (sales)
1. Instead of 'just checking in', offer something valuable and say to let you know if interested
2. Instead of handing off the work, ask 'Does option A or B work better for you?'
3. Instead of discounting, show the ROI or offer a more expensive option

## Lessons

- *pricing*: Price against the cost of the problem the customer has, not against your own sense of what is affordable.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Small price rises have outsized profit impact, so under-pricing is costly (claim attributed to McKinsey, unverified here).
- *offer_design*: Get the buyer to articulate the gap and the numbers; self-generated arguments persuade more than being told.
- *niche_selection*: Prioritise problems that are urgent and painful (oxygen) because those buyers find budget.
- *customer_acquisition*: Good proof is specific and verifiable (name, face, numbers, before/after); stack enough that persuasion is unnecessary.
- *niche_selection*: Selecting good customers (able to pay, reliable, fit, energising) matters more than pitch polish.
- *customer_acquisition*: Consistent follow-up cadence and referral partnerships are a key part of pipeline; most stalled deals are asking/follow-up problems.
- *mindset_execution*: Neediness and 'circling back' language leaks a weak mindset; lead the buyer with choices and value instead.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Selling from your own wallet and limited beliefs kills deals before the buyer objects; underpricing leaves the highest-leverage growth move unused.
- Selling dessert to someone with an oxygen-level need elsewhere leads to deals that go cold; nothing is on fire so no budget.
- Vague, anonymous testimonials with no name or photo count as failed proof.
- Deals die from lack of follow-up rather than a bad pitch.
