## Tactics and playbook steps

**Find listings that make a useful before-and-after sample** (acquisition)
1. Search local property listings for poor-quality or unstaged photos.
2. Save a listing photo and use a simple prompt to improve lighting, tidy a room, or add furniture.
3. Identify the listing agent and prepare a sample edit for that specific listing.

**Build a narrow photo-editing app before adding extra features** (product)
1. Prompt Lindy to make a simple web app for uploading photos and an editing prompt, then downloading the results.
2. Connect the Gemini Nano Banana API and enable billing.
3. Test the app on real listing photos and verify that the images actually change before using it with prospects.
> "It just appears that it's not actually using the API cuz nothing changed." — Chris Koerner (~8:51)

**Clean and import local leads into a CRM** (operations)
1. Scrape local agent contact listings using Outscraper and Google Maps.
2. Keep mobile numbers, discard unclassified numbers, remove duplicates, and standardize phone formats in Google Sheets.
3. Export a CSV and import it into HighLevel, mapping the name, phone, and address fields.
4. Apply a dedicated tag and add the contacts to the relevant pipeline or workflow.

**Use a sample upload to trigger CRM follow-up** (operations)
1. Add a custom field for the edited real estate image to the agent contact record.
2. Set a workflow trigger for a change to that field.
3. Use the trigger to create an opportunity, send a text with a sample-image link, and move the lead through response and follow-up stages.
> "That is also a trigger. When I upload a photo to this real estate save staging section, it will trigger an automation to text that person." — Chris Koerner (~14:28)

**Start manually and automate after launch** (mindset)
1. Handle the first listing search and image upload manually.
2. Use the manual process to find friction and learn what customers value.
3. Automate repetitive steps after the offer is live instead of delaying launch to perfect the system.
> "Now, this is a little manual, but we can automate this later. As much as I love showing videos how to automate everything, it's really not important in the early stages" — Chris Koerner (~13:16)

**Use a low per-photo price to test willingness to pay** (pricing)
1. Offer an initial price of $1 per photo as a proposed test.
2. Use a free sample to start the discussion with the listing agent.
3. Consider a monthly plan only after confirming that agents will pay for the service.
> "Hey, what if I could charge you a buck per photo to make your home look staged or to make the photos look like they were taken with a DSLR camera?" — Chris Koerner (~2:30)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: A personalized free sample can make a service concrete for a prospect and open a sales conversation.
- *validation_testing*: Test whether customers will pay before assuming a proposed service or price has demand; the video does not show a customer accepting the offer.
- *mindset_execution*: Work through early friction with a basic manual process, then automate steps once the service is moving.
- *automation_ai*: Do not let automation work delay a launch; the speaker recommends doing some early work manually.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Check unit economics carefully: the speaker's stated cost, price, and margin do not agree with each other.
- *niche_selection*: A narrow service can be built around one recurring task for one customer group, but broad claims about market capacity are not evidence of demand.
- *operations*: Connect CRM tags, custom fields, and workflow triggers so lead status and follow-up can be tracked in one pipeline.
- *legal_risk*: Keep customer-facing images accurate and clearly handled; the host recognizes the possibility of a bait and switch but dismisses it.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The demonstrated lead-generation workflow scrapes mobile numbers and sends marketing texts without permission. Under the stated UK PECR constraint, software cannot automate unsolicited marketing SMS to individuals or sole traders without consent.
- The host acknowledges that virtually staging an unstaged property may mislead buyers, then says not to worry about it. Altered listing images should be handled transparently and approved by the agent.
- The first app test did not apply edits because API billing was not enabled. Confirm that the app is using the API and producing real edits before relying on it.
- The host acknowledges that placing the API key in a public app is unsafe, but proceeds for the video demonstration.
- The stated unit economics do not support the claimed margin: the stated image cost is about $3.90 while the proposed selling price is $1 per photo; the later 'paying 4' figure is also unclear.
