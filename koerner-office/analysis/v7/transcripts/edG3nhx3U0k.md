## Tactics and playbook steps

**Sell portable sanitation to two customer types** (sales)
1. Target active construction sites with longer rental and servicing contracts.
2. Also rent units to weddings, festivals, and outdoor events for shorter periods.
3. Set a service schedule that includes pumping and restocking.
> "And then you've got two very different customers paying for the same exact asset." — Host (name not stated) (~1:54)

**Compete on small-engine repair reliability** (operations)
1. Offer an accessible shop where contractors can drop off equipment.
2. Build a reputation for fast turnaround and same-day callbacks.
3. Plan for seasonal demand if outdoor contractors are the main customers.
> "with fast turnaround, with, you know, an actual shop you can pull up to, drop your machines off" — Host (name not stated) (~4:14)

**Build a recurring fire inspection route** (operations)
1. Win commercial building accounts and record their inspection schedules.
2. Return on the annual schedule and inspect each building's extinguishers.
3. Expand the route account by account.
> "once you get in with, you know, a building's annual inspection schedule, you're back every single year, tag after tag." — Host (name not stated) (~6:18)

**Sell preventive maintenance to warehouse operators** (sales)
1. Offer quarterly checks of loading dock doors and levers.
2. Explain that preventive work may reduce the chance of a loading bay failing during busy shipping periods.
3. Offer emergency repairs to existing accounts when equipment breaks.
> "Specifically so they don't get caught with a dead loading bay during their busiest shipping weeks." — Host (name not stated) (~8:21)

**Use one waste collection route for two revenue streams** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Sign businesses that generate used oil or grease to a recurring pickup schedule.
2. Charge for collection.
3. Arrange resale of collected oil to re-refiners.
> "So you can charge the pickup fee, right? But on top of this, you can also take that oil and sell it to re-refiners." — Host (name not stated) (~10:20)

**Recruit drivers with paid social ads** (acquisition)
1. Run Facebook ads targeting CDL holders and owner-operators.
2. Frame the opportunity around better freight, earnings, or routes closer to home.
3. Check each applicant's CDL, driving record, and experience before referral.
4. Work with multiple carriers to avoid depending on a single company's hiring pace.
> "All you need to learn how to do is run Facebook ads targeting CDL holders and owner-operators who are looking for better freight" — Host (name not stated) (~12:22)

**Coordinate rides through independent drivers** (operations)
1. Set up the required payer billing and compliance processes.
2. Schedule rides and assign them to independent drivers with suitable vehicles.
3. Prioritize driver reliability because repeat bookings depend on patients trusting the service.
> "And you can handle all of the scheduling, the insurance billing, the compliance, and route the ride straight to them." — Host (name not stated) (~14:55)

**Partner with specialist foundation crews** (partnerships)
1. Find licensed foundation and waterproofing crews that want more consistent lead flow.
2. Build a Google Business Profile and collect before-and-after photos.
3. Answer the phone, schedule inspections, and handle the sales conversation.
4. Agree how the lead operator will be paid for jobs completed by the partner.
> "All they have to do is show up and do the work." — Host (name not stated) (~16:54)

**Keep the cleaning owner out of the cleaning work** (hiring_outsourcing)
1. Recruit independent cleaners who already have supplies and transport.
2. Send cleaners to booked jobs and pay the stated 50% share.
3. Focus the owner's time on marketing, sales, scheduling, and recruiting rather than doing the cleans.
4. Use one-off cleans to build recurring weekly, fortnightly, or monthly clients.
> "The end of the day, guys, you are the owner. That's the whole point of everything I just showed you." — Host (name not stated) (~20:48)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: A market can have unmet demand even when competitors exist if few providers are professional, insured, and reliable.
- *operations*: Judge a business by who performs the work, not only by its apparent demand or revenue potential.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Recurring contracts can reduce the pressure to find new revenue from zero every month.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: A physical service can be structured so the owner coordinates demand while trained contractors perform the field work.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Model seasonality, sales cycles, and commodity exposure before treating recurring demand as dependable income.
- *customer_acquisition*: In service businesses, reliable delivery and prompt responses can be a competitive advantage.
- *operations*: Recurring service depends on trust and reliable people, especially when customers are vulnerable or the service is important.
- *mindset_execution*: Keep the owner focused on recruiting and operating the business instead of taking on the delivery work by default.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The host says the examples are educational and that results are not typical or guaranteed.
- Portable sanitation requires upfront asset capital and recurring physical service work.
- Small-engine repair is a skilled trade with seasonal demand.
- Commercial fire inspection sales can take time because the service is not described as high-ticket.
- Dock and door repair requires skilled work and can be dangerous.
- Waste oil collection needs a truck and permits, and resale value can fluctuate with oil prices.
- Truck recruiting has an uneven pipeline and no subscription revenue, so the recruiter may need to keep selling for new placements.
- Medical transportation depends on paperwork, payment timing, and dependable drivers.
- Foundation repair is mostly a one-time job, so the operator needs a continuing flow of new calls.
- The host says owners often undermine remote cleaning by doing the cleaning themselves instead of recruiting cleaners.
