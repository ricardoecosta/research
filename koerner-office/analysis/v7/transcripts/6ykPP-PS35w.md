## Summary

Johnny Robinson (Johnny & Sergio, Home Service Academy) ranks 10 business types to buy from F to S tier using one test: would the business survive if the owner walked away tomorrow? Ecommerce/FBA, founder-brand businesses and first-timer franchises rank worst; laundromats mid; lawn care, fence/deck, pest control, waste routes and HVAC/plumbing rank higher; small ageing-owner cleaning companies rank S tier. The key takeaway is to buy sticky recurring customer lists from owners with no exit plan, using seller financing, then add systems. The video ends in a pitch for his Home Service Academy coaching, so the ranking is promotional.

### I1. Buy a small local cleaning company (residential/commercial)

- **Customer:** Households, offices, gyms, medical suites, universities
- **Problem:** Owners are ageing out with no succession plan; businesses run on word of mouth with no systems
- **Offer:** Cleaning services backed by a bought customer list, run via a system rather than the owner
- **Business model:** Recurring residential and commercial cleaning contracts; acquired via seller note/SBA loan
- **Pricing:** Purchase price typically 2-4x annual owner cash flow, low end or 1x or less for these. down payment on the author's own acquisition USD 1000 one-off Seller financing, SBA loan ~10% down, sometimes zero down
- **Acquisition:** referrals_word_of_mouth: Find deals locally, off-market: ask ageing owners respectfully what happens to the business when they want to slow down; deals not on BizBuySell or brokers; networking_events: Pound the pavement and get to know local owners
- **Delivery:** Cleaners kept as independent contractors rather than W-2 employees; online booking and dispatch system; recurring weekly/fortnightly/monthly client structure; owner runs it from a laptop
- **Unit economics (claims):** Author acquired a cleaning business for $1,000 down plus a revenue share (30% then 15% then 10%) over 3 years; ~$250k/yr revenue
- **Tools:** BizBuySell, SBA loan, booking system
- **Startup cost:** Low: potentially zero to ~10% down with seller financing; one example $1,000 down
- **Time to first revenue:** Immediate, as the business has existing customers
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Cleaning firms and seller-financed deals exist in the UK; SBA is US-only (use UK bank/Start Up Loans), contractor vs employee status (IR35/worker status) is stricter in the UK, TUPE may apply to staff on acquisition.
- **Risks:** Owner may be the only scheduler/cleaner so business may not survive without them; Contractor classification risk; Customer churn after ownership change; Needs operational skills before buying; Physical delivery limits automation
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 1/5 (Finding off-market owners is relationship-driven; agents could build lists of local firms, but outreach to sole traders cannot be cold-messaged under PECR and deal negotiation is human); onboarding 3/5 (Online booking forms, quotes and contractor onboarding can be mostly automated); delivery 0/5 (Physical cleaning is done by people; agents can only schedule and dispatch); billing 4/5 (Recurring card billing and invoicing by software); support 3/5 (Agents handle rebooking and queries with human escalation for quality complaints)
> "You're not really going to buy a cleaning business. You're really looking at, "Hey, I can buy a loyal customer list."" — Johnny Robinson (~23:32)
> "we paid him $1,000 down and every time we completed a service for one of his customers, we would pay him a percentage." — Johnny Robinson (~25:15)

### I2. Waste and recycling route

- **Customer:** Households and businesses
- **Problem:** Poor service from large (e.g. PE-owned) haulers
- **Offer:** Reliable local trash collection
- **Business model:** Monthly subscription for trash pickup
- **Pricing:** Monthly subscription per customer. Spencer's price USD 35 per month per customer 
- **Acquisition:** facebook_groups_communities: Spencer posted in neighbourhood Facebook group: if 200 people signed up and prepaid, he'd start the route; got ~150 in 10 days and $15,000
- **Delivery:** Drivers run trucks on routes; owner need not drive
- **Unit economics (claims):** Spencer ~$18k/month revenue at ~20% margin, ~40% if he drives himself; Almost zero churn
- **Tools:** Facebook groups
- **Startup cost:** High: trucks and permits cost more up front than anything else on the list
- **Time to first revenue:** About 10 days in Spencer's case
- **UK applicability [E]:** 2/5 — UK waste collection is largely council-run; commercial waste needs a waste carrier licence and domestic collection is statutory, so household subscription model is limited.
- **Risks:** High capex; Municipal contracts and permits required; Entrenched big-city contracts
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Community posts and local ads can be automated; permits and municipal contracts need humans); onboarding 4/5 (Online sign-up and prepayment); delivery 0/5 (Trucks and drivers physically collect waste); billing 5/5 (Recurring card billing); support 3/5 (Missed-pickup queries handled by agents with human dispatch)
> "he had about 150 people signed up and $15,000 raised charging around 35 bucks per month per customer" — Johnny Robinson (~19:38)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Off-market owner outreach** (acquisition)
1. Identify local firms run by ageing owners
2. Ask respectfully what happens to the business when they want to slow down
3. Get real owner cash flow numbers
> "Have you thought about what happens to this business when you're ready to slow down?" — Johnny Robinson (~26:29)

**Structure deal to self-fund** (finance)
1. Small down payment
2. Seller note paid from revenue share
3. SBA loan if needed

**Prepaid neighbourhood demand test** (acquisition)
1. Post in neighbourhood Facebook group
2. Ask for prepaid sign-ups at a threshold
3. Start only after commitments

## Lessons

- *exit_acquisition*: Test any acquisition by asking whether it survives if the owner leaves.
- *niche_selection*: Platform-dependent revenue is rented, not owned.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Hyped boring businesses become overpriced.
- *niche_selection*: Sticky recurring revenue with barriers to entry is the best acquisition target.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Seller financing lets the business pay for itself.
- *scaling*: Unsystemised businesses are upside, as improvements are bought at a discount.
- *mindset_execution*: Get operating skills before taking on debt service.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Buying a founder-brand business means the audience's trust doesn't transfer
- Franchise buyer lost his 401k after being sold the dream
- Get real owner cash flow, not gross, before pricing
