## Tactics and playbook steps

**Keep the seller financially invested** (acquisition)
1. Ask the seller to finance at least part of the acquisition.
2. Use seller financing as one signal that the seller retains a stake in a successful handoff.
3. Treat the seller's willingness to train and introduce the buyer to customers, suppliers, and vendors as part of transition diligence.
> "One of the things that I would say to anybody is do not ever buy a company, to the seller's not willing to finance the least portion of it, right?" — Andrea Palacio (~8:12)

**Stress-test an acquisition for people and handoff risk** (validation_testing)
1. Identify which manager or employee holds customer, sales, and operating knowledge.
2. Confirm who owns sales before the sale and who will perform it afterward.
3. Get a specific transition plan for training and introductions to clients, suppliers, and vendors.
4. Model what happens if the manager or most of the crew leaves soon after closing.
> "You can never expect that the manager's going to quit, that the seller is going to completely leave." — Andrea Palacio (~8:06)

**Rebuild demand while restoring service capacity** (operations)
1. Use Google Ads to bring in leads and try to recover lost customers.
2. Do not oversell capacity while crews are understaffed; overwork led to poor work and more client losses.
3. Balance customer acquisition with the number of workers available to fulfill jobs.
4. Prioritize recurring work while rebuilding the customer base.
> "So for us, it was like, okay, let's start running Google ads. Let's try to get back the people that we have lost the site, increasing the revenue again" — Andrea Palacio (~9:02)

**Automate recurring billing and set clear customer terms** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Bill recurring customers on the first of the month before service.
2. Give customers a portal where they can see service information and timing.
3. Set payment terms before taking over a customer book, since late payments can make collections burdensome.
4. Consider likely churn before changing an acquired company's rates or terms.
> "They are paying on the first of each month before we even mow their lawn." — Andrea Palacio (~45:51)

**Send a service report and collect feedback automatically** (operations)
1. Trigger an email after a crew completes a property.
2. Pull the job photo from the CRM and include it in the message.
3. Send a satisfaction text after service.
4. Route satisfied customers to a Google review and dissatisfied customers to a complaint form.
5. Review complaints before they grow into a larger retention problem.

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: A business that looks stable before sale can depend heavily on one manager or seller; map that dependency and the actual handoff before buying.
- *niche_selection*: Choose a business that fits the owners' actual strengths, but verify that both partners can cover the skills the business needs.
- *operations*: Local service businesses have a labor-supply problem as well as a customer-acquisition problem; adding demand without reliable fulfillment can harm reputation.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Recurring revenue is valuable, but the service quality and staffing required to retain it matter as much as the contract book.
- *automation_ai*: AI works better when the owner writes down explicit steps, exceptions, and escalation rules instead of expecting the system to infer an informal process.
- *automation_ai*: Treat an AI agent like a junior employee: define its role, train it with examples, listen to its work, and route sensitive situations to a person.
- *automation_ai*: Automate repeatable admin tasks first when they remove a predictable burden, such as routine calls, booking, service photos, and satisfaction checks.
- *operations*: Track a small set of operational KPIs closely enough to notice problems before cash, customers, or equipment disappear.
- *pricing*: Set clear prices and payment terms before taking on a customer book; raising prices and changing terms at once can increase churn.
- *scaling*: A founder can turn hard-won operating experience into a consulting or educational offer, but one-to-one consulting remains limited by founder time.
- *mindset_execution*: When a setback occurs, identify the missing knowledge or system and use it to improve the next decision rather than treating it only as personal failure.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not assume a business acquisition will be a passive investment. The manager and most employees may leave, and the owner may need to take over labor, sales, marketing, admin, and customer service.
- Do not buy a company unless the seller will finance at least part of it, according to the guest; also establish a real handoff and training plan.
- Do not assume a manager who handled operations also knows how to sell; confirm who owned sales and how the role will transfer.
- Uncontrolled ad spending can waste cash while the owner learns campaign management.
- Understaffing can create a negative cycle: crews become overworked, quality falls, and more customers leave.
- Changing both prices and payment terms after acquiring a customer base can drive customers away; delayed payment also creates collection work.
- Do not rely on trust in a manager without controls, access limits, and KPI oversight.
- AI agents need detailed process instructions and ongoing review; the guest listened to calls and updated prompts when agents failed.
- The guest says an AI receptionist is easy to assemble but needs careful training for the situations it will face.
- Acquisition videos can omit the experience and safeguards that make a deal appear easy to repeat; consider ways a deal can fail before closing.
