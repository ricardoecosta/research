## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build a quick first version to test an idea** (validation_testing)
1. Use an AI coding tool to turn a written proposal or problem description into a working prototype.
2. Set a short build window and test whether the core experience works before committing to a large development budget.
3. Cheney says his own initial build took about three days; treat his timing and cost as a personal claim, not a universal estimate.
> "I just dragged it into Replet and said, "Build this." And like 20 minutes later, it was built." — John Cheney (~4:50)

**Find prospects and call them** (acquisition)
1. Use a research assistant to identify 20 businesses in a target area and gather decision-maker details.
2. Call prospects and keep doing outreach after the first sale.
3. For UK prospecting, screen cold calls against TPS/CTPS and do not use unsolicited marketing texts or DMs to individuals or sole traders without consent.
> "Really what you can do is you can go on to something like Manis right and just say hey find me 20 customers" — John Cheney (~35:27) [quote not matched verbatim]

**Use a concise AI readiness pitch to start conversations** (sales)
1. Contact business owners you know and explain that you can measure employee AI readiness.
2. Offer to build a plan to help the company adopt AI.
3. Ask for a call, then scope the customer's needs and quote a paid package.
> "I'm measuring, you know, I want I can measure all your employees, see how good they are at AI and, you know, then kind of build a plan" — John Cheney (~17:53)

**Show progress publicly to create trust and inbound interest** (acquisition)
1. Post what you are building and what you are learning on LinkedIn or a personal Facebook page.
2. Do not wait for a large audience; explain your work in plain language.
3. Use personal interests as well as work topics to give potential customers a way to connect.
> "The best way to get no customers, the best way is to make sure nobody knows what you're doing." — John Cheney (~36:07)

**Convert one-off training into an ongoing retainer** (pricing)
1. Listen for a customer who wants continuing help after an initial training engagement.
2. Offer a recurring fractional chief AI officer service with follow-up and ongoing business impact work.
3. Explain the fee against the customer's potential cost savings or revenue impact.
> "I don't like the idea of you just coming in and teaching us and then being gone. Like do you have a model where you'd kind of stick around?" — John Cheney (~20:29)

**Structure the sales conversation around strategy, transformation, and education** (sales)
1. Discuss how AI could affect the customer's industry and competitors.
2. Ask which tasks or roles the customer wants to improve or hire for, then show a practical automation example and its possible ROI.
3. Explain how employee training could improve productivity after process changes are in place.
> "we really help in three ways, right? We want to help you with strategy, right? To figure out how your industry is going to be affected" — John Cheney (~28:12)

**Coach clients to build their own solutions** (delivery)
1. Work alongside the employee who owns the problem instead of selling only custom code.
2. Help the client get an early, satisfying result so they feel capable of building more.
3. Let the client do the implementation work and provide support when they get stuck.
> "The best customers we have, they're vibe coding their own solutions, right? They're they're director of operations" — John Cheney (~31:12)

**Create an early win in training** (operations)
1. Start a class with a fun, simple build such as a small game.
2. Let learners change the output and see that plain-language instructions can create something usable.
3. Use the early success to build confidence before introducing more work-focused applications.
> "The first thing we're gonna do here is we're gonna open up Ched PT or Grock or something and we're gonna vibe code a game." — John Cheney (~34:30)

**Use referrals to shorten the sales process** (sales)
1. Ask referred prospects about their business and where they see room for help.
2. Connect the offer to the trusted referrer's experience and move to a close sooner when fit is already established.
> "A lot of my deals at this point are coming from referrals. And so they're kind of already sold by the time I get on the phone with them." — John Cheney (~39:36)

**Use an executive dashboard as an entry point to broader automation** (product)
1. Begin with a CEO's need to see the business clearly.
2. Connect relevant business systems through APIs and build a dashboard that can answer questions and generate reports.
3. Use the work to identify further process improvements and repeatable tasks.
> "we usually go in and one of the first things we'll do is we'll build him a dashboard that helps him see his business better." — John Cheney (~26:57)

**Test the full payment path before promoting a funnel** (operations)
1. Run a test purchase through the same checkout customers will use.
2. Check the payment processor configuration before sending traffic.
3. If people sign up but do not buy, inspect the funnel and payment path before assuming there is no demand.
> "I went through the funnel and I found out that, you know, I'm a vibe coder and I was a young vibe coder and I had messed something up with my stripe configuration." — John Cheney (~17:09)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Talk directly to potential customers early; the first enterprise sale came from personal outreach after the automated funnel failed to convert.
- *customer_acquisition*: A small audience can still contain relevant buyers, so share useful progress before trying to become a large creator.
- *niche_selection*: Do not assume prospects understand AI well; a modest practical advantage can be enough to start helping them.
- *offer_design*: Sell the business outcome and connect the fee to a customer's real costs or opportunities.
- *pricing*: Recurring revenue can come from staying involved after a one-off project and tracking whether changes affect the business.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Coach people to build and own their solutions rather than taking responsibility for every custom build and its maintenance.
- *operations*: Give learners a quick, enjoyable early success to make a new technical skill feel approachable.
- *validation_testing*: Test the whole checkout and delivery path; a broken payment configuration can make a working funnel look like there is no demand.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Cheney prefers the control and lower obligations of a self-funded business over raising capital when the business can grow through cash flow.
- *mindset_execution*: Stay curious about unfamiliar technical ideas and ask for explanations; the founder says this helped him speak credibly with technical customers.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use referrals and existing relationships to build trust, and move toward a close when the prospect already has context and intent.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Cheney says the first version of the AIQ sales funnel had a Stripe configuration error, so sign-ups could not complete purchases. Test the payment path before driving traffic.
- Cheney cautions against selling custom builds as the whole service because a dev-shop model creates implementation and long-term maintenance obligations.
- The host warns that venture investors may have liquidation preferences, so a sale below the amount raised can leave founders with nothing.
- The interview includes a Zapier sponsor read and the host promotes his PlaymakersAI community; treat recommendations and performance claims in those segments as promotional.
- Cheney's examples about savings, productivity, and customer results are sales hypotheticals or self-reported claims, not independently verified outcomes.
