## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Eli, a former Connecticut real estate agent and truck dispatcher who built a trailer-subleasing business. He leased 53ft semi-trailers from a big lessor for about $350/month, rented them to truckers for about $180/week, and found customers through Facebook Marketplace and then Facebook ads. He claims about 300 trailers, $250k/month in billings and $40-50k/month net profit with two virtual employees, run from his phone. Key takeaways: net-30 supplier terms plus upfront deposits and weekly billing give immediate positive cash flow; GPS tracking and insurance structure matter; paid ads were the scaling lever.

### I1. Semi-trailer subleasing to truckers

- **Customer:** Owner-operator truckers and small trucking companies, often new companies or with poor credit, including Spanish-speaking clients
- **Problem:** Truckers need trailers to work; many big rental companies will not rent to new companies or people with weak credit.
- **Offer:** Brand-new or DOT-inspected 53ft dry van, food-grade and flatbed trailers available quickly with flexible credit requirements and weekly billing.
- **Business model:** Recurring weekly rental (arbitrage between lessor cost and sublease price) plus deposits and early-exit penalties
- **Pricing:** Weekly rental with a deposit and a 3-month minimum term. Trailer rental to truckers (about $750/month) USD 180 per week; Deposit USD 750 one-off; Cost of leasing a trailer from supplier USD 350 per month Aim to at least double cost; thirds rule: a third covers the trailer cost, a third expenses/taxes/payroll, a third profit. Prices were held low at first to get customers in, planning to raise later. Deposit forfeited and sometimes a penalty if the client leaves before 3 months.
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: Basic Facebook Marketplace posts on his personal page with photos of the trailer inside, outside and tyres, plus year, make, location and 'flexible with new companies'. Boosted posts and multiple posts in different areas. About 10 trailers rented in the first 2 months.; paid_social: Business Facebook page with still-image ad campaigns run nationwide because truckers travel for deals. Spent about $5,000/month at the end for roughly 25 new clients per month.; referrals_word_of_mouth: By the end, existing truckers referred other truckers.
- **Delivery:** Trailers are leased from a big supplier (Penske, Ryder, Premier Leasing type companies) and handed to renters at pickup locations across several states (CT, NJ, NY, MA, PA, FL). Trailers are DOT inspected yearly. The supplier handles maintenance on a triple-net basis. One virtual employee ran ads, followed up with leads, sent documents, coordinated billing and pickup; a second virtual employee also worked in the business.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Lease cost about $350/month vs rental about $750/month; Roughly 10 trailers in first 2 months, about $7,500/month recurring; About 100 trailers and $75k/month end of 2020 with $10-15k net; About 300 trailers and $250k/month with $40-50k net profit; $5,000/month ad spend for about 25 new clients, about 10x return on ad spend by interviewer's maths; Startup cost was only the roughly $4,000/year insurance; 10-15 of 300 trailers never came back, costing $15-25k each
- **Tools:** Facebook Marketplace, Facebook Ads, Upwork, PaySimple, Stripe (dropped), GPS trackers, Grok bot (AI test), DAT load board
- **Startup cost:** About $4,000 for the first year's insurance policy; no deposit needed with the supplier
- **Time to first revenue:** Immediate; the first trailer was rented to his uncle in Nov 2019 and about 10 trailers were rented within 2 months

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Net-30 supplier terms with upfront deposit and weekly billing** (finance)
1. Negotiate net-30 payment with the supplier and no deposit
2. Take a deposit from the customer upfront
3. Bill the customer weekly from day one so cash flow is positive immediately
4. Seek triple-net terms where the supplier covers maintenance
> "you want something called triple net, which is that you take care of the maintenance so that your cost on a monthly basis is the lowest possible" — Eli (~26:19)

**Facebook Marketplace listing to validate demand** (acquisition)
1. Post simple photos of the asset inside and outside, with year, make and location
2. Say you are flexible with new companies
3. Post multiple listings in different areas
4. Boost the posts
5. Match inquiries to inventory wherever the driver needs it

**Price at 2-3x cost using the thirds rule** (pricing)
1. Aim to at least double the lease cost, ideally triple
2. Allocate one third to cost, one third to expenses and taxes, one third to profit
3. Start with a lower price to get customers into a sticky asset, then raise later
> "You're always trying to double at least double if you can triple because you know you have to think about taxes and insurance fees" — Eli (~10:33)

**Minimum term plus deposit to reduce churn** (operations)
1. Require a 3-month minimum
2. Take a $750 deposit forfeited if they leave early
3. Charge a penalty in some cases

**Delegate ads and lead closing to a virtual assistant** (hiring)
1. Hire bilingual VAs via Upwork, then move them off the platform
2. One VA runs Facebook ads and calls leads
3. She sends documents, coordinates billing and pickup

**Test the idea before forming the LLC** (mindset)
1. Get at least one paying customer first
2. Then form the LLC, bank account and scale the supplier relationship
> "You always have to test your ideas before creating the LLC, creating a bank account and things like that." — Eli (~11:25)

## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: Ask suppliers what they can do for you (net-30, no deposit, GPS, maintenance); terms that make you cash-flow positive from day one are a huge advantage.
- *validation_testing*: Follow customer requests; the business started when his uncle asked for a trailer, and a small test with one client validated demand.
- *offer_design*: Sticky assets that customers need to earn a living give low churn and tolerance for pricing.
- *customer_acquisition*: Paid ads were his consistent answer to growth; once unit economics are proven, spend more.
- *pricing*: Weekly billing feels smaller to customers and speeds cash collection.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Hire low-cost bilingual virtual staff to cover ads and sales so the owner can work remotely.
- *niche_selection*: Serve underserved segments (new companies, poor credit) that big incumbents decline.
- *automation_ai*: AI agents can now set up much of such a business (contracts, sourcing, ad management), which Eli says would have saved him two years.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Put GPS trackers on every trailer from day one; without them trailers went missing and some were never recovered.
- Structure contracts and additional-insured requirements so you are actually covered, not just theoretically covered.
- Stripe froze his funds for no apparent reason, a risk for high-volume businesses.
- Running listings on a personal Facebook page was not scalable or delegatable; use a business page.
- He did not tell the supplier he was subleasing at first; it worked out but is a contractual risk.
