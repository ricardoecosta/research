## Tactics and playbook steps

**Lawn quote pricing logic** (pricing)
1. Acre = ~43,500 sq ft; subtract house footprint and 10% of lot for driveway
2. Multiply net sq ft by 1.6 cents (~$45-50 for a 0.1 acre lot)
3. Discount 20% per quarter acre for big lots
4. Frequency discounts (2x/month 20%, 3+ 40%)
5. Minimum $40 per mow; weed eating +20%
> "I just said multiply the the square footage by 1.6 cents because that's about how you get to about 40 50 bucks" — Chris Koerner (~18:49)

**Speed-to-lead follow-up** (sales)
1. Get a text (not just email) for each form submission
2. Call/text immediately
3. Offer a small discount to start this week (quote $80 -> $75)
> "the quote says it would be 80 bucks uh a week, but actually if if we start this week, I'll do it for 75" — Brandon Doyle (~23:18)

**Vibe coding without prompt engineering** (product)
1. Write a short plain prompt; include your math example
2. Answer the agent's clarifying questions
3. Ask the same chat how to deploy
4. Reference a known design (Airbnb style) for good UI
5. Skip integrations (Google Sheets over SendGrid) to launch fast
> "Like don't get hung up on how perfect your prompt needs to be" — Chris Koerner (~2:43)

**Decide lead-capture friction** (acquisition)
1. Ask for name and phone before showing price for more qualified leads
2. Or show price first for more volume and fewer contacts
3. Choose based on industry
> "The more friction you implement, the fewer leads you get, but the higher quality those leads will be" — Chris Koerner (~31:05)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Small businesses pay for bloated SaaS while using a few features; cheap custom tools are an opening.
- *customer_acquisition*: Free niche tools can be a foot in the door to sell bigger services.
- *customer_acquisition*: Transparent upfront pricing plus speed of reply makes a small trade look like a national brand.
- *automation_ai*: Non-coders can ship a working app in under an hour for about $10.
- *automation_ai*: Use the building agent itself as support (how do I deploy?) instead of Googling.
- *customer_acquisition*: Every question added to a form trades response rate for accuracy.

## Warnings and mistakes

- AI-built calculators can output absurd prices ($0, $2,450 per acre); test edge cases and add minimums.
- Showing price before capturing contact loses leads who dislike the price.
- Elaborate 'prompt hacks' are unnecessary.
