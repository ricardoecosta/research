## Tactics and playbook steps

**Find overlooked properties through public listing signals** (acquisition)
1. Review Realtor.com features of unusually popular homes and look for recurring property characteristics.
2. Check public property records to learn whether an interesting listing sold or was withdrawn.
3. Use property-owner records to identify a contact, then ask whether the owner is still interested in selling.
4. If the owner agrees, arrange a prompt site visit and make the case in person.
> "I was going through every single week's list just to look for consistencies of what types of homes were going the most viral on realtor.com when they became for sale." — Kyle McLash (~6:12)

**Test demand with a small, targeted social campaign before opening** (validation_testing)
1. Create a brand account while the property is under construction.
2. Use clear images of the actual property and disclose which amenity images are renderings.
3. Promote one reel to selected zip codes in nearby feeder markets rather than targeting everyone.
4. Track booking inquiries and reservations against the campaign spend.
> "we put about $500 into pushing the real into, you know, you can select your market and demographic and whatnot." — Kyle McLash (~19:41)

**Use a high-capacity, distinctive guest experience to stand out** (offer_design)
1. Study competing listings and look for amenities guests want that local competitors lack.
2. Use internal booking and guest data to identify recurring preferences such as pools and quality playgrounds.
3. Choose a property type and capacity that are difficult to reproduce nearby.
4. Compete on the experience rather than cutting nightly rates.
> "One of the biggest pivotal moves that we made as a strategy from a company perspective was competing on experience instead of price" — Kyle McLash (~14:41)

**Research a market from its metro outward** (validation_testing)
1. Start with a growing major metro, not only the operator's hometown.
2. Compare economic growth, population growth, crime, and employers moving into the metro.
3. Map roughly an hour's drive around the metro.
4. Look for rural locations with more acreage, fewer neighbours, and distinctive structures.
> "What does population growth look like? What does crime look like? Just a bigger picture of that major metro" — Kyle McLash (~31:23)

**Use dynamic pricing rather than fixed weekend and weekday rates** (pricing)
1. Set baseline prices in PriceLabs.
2. Adjust for booking lead time, seasonality, and demonstrated demand.
3. Let prices fall as dates approach when demand is weak and rise when many guests are clicking the same dates.
4. Review price and occupancy together so the property has time for maintenance.
> "we had a flammings for weekends and weeknights before we got set up on price labs, which is our dynamic pricing software" — Kyle McLash (~22:31)

**Build co-host credibility with a tailored performance report** (sales)
1. Learn the local market and property type before approaching an owner.
2. Prepare a customized B&B Calc performance report that can be shared with owners, realtors, bankers, or investors.
3. Explain the likely guest profile and specific improvements that could support the property.
4. Offer a clear management scope and commission, then follow up persistently.
> "if you can go to a property owner that has never been showed anything like a performer or a software tool that shows what they can make" — Kyle McLash (~50:43)

**Build knowledge before cold outreach** (mindset)
1. Use free podcasts and other public material to learn how co-hosting works.
2. Study operators who share their processes before contacting owners.
3. Prepare enough to present yourself as someone who understands the owner's property and market.
> "I think just like all the free knowledge that you can just gather out there first from John Bianchi, Jeremy Worden, Bill Faith." — Kyle McLash (~53:46)

**Position the property for the intended guest** (operations)
1. Define the target guest, such as a family group, before choosing amenities and writing marketing.
2. Keep listing descriptions, social content, and guest screening consistent with that audience.
3. Use guest vetting and platform protections to reduce party and fake-account risks.
> "So we set them up to be family, family friendly, and then all of our marketing is directed towards families." — Kyle McLash (~41:59)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Give guests a reason to travel for the property itself; amenities and a memorable setting can differentiate a stay better than competing only on nightly price.
- *niche_selection*: Look for a property or operating strategy competitors cannot easily copy, including a distinctive building, market, or guest experience.
- *validation_testing*: Use actual market and guest data to challenge assumptions, but treat personal intuition as a hypothesis rather than proof.
- *niche_selection*: Avoid the crowded middle if the market is commoditized; either offer a small, clear use case or a large group destination.
- *pricing*: Build pricing around demand and booking lead time; fixed weekday and weekend rates can leave money on the table.
- *customer_acquisition*: Choose a guest profile and align the property, marketing, and screening with it.
- *customer_acquisition*: A prospective co-host needs to show property-specific knowledge and a credible performance analysis before asking an owner to trust them.
- *mindset_execution*: A no-property route such as co-hosting can build operating experience and cash flow before buying assets, but it still requires persistence and local execution.
- *niche_selection*: A property can be a strong fit outside an operator's immediate area if market selection is grounded in demand and economic data.
- *operations*: High occupancy is not automatically optimal when maintenance and wear require downtime; evaluate occupancy alongside price and property condition.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The first property involved substantial leverage across commercial financing, a business credit line, another property's equity, a home equity line, and savings. Kyle says failure could have led to bankruptcy.
- Kyle says the business made a pricing mistake by using fixed weekend and weeknight prices before adopting dynamic pricing.
- Kyle says rental arbitrage is much harder to succeed with than it used to be; a landlord must also permit the arrangement.
- The host cautions that the guest's results are not typical or guaranteed.
- Large-group properties can attract destructive parties or fake accounts; guest vetting and platform safeguards do not eliminate the risk.
- Kyle advises avoiding generic mid-sized rentals and mentions zoning and neighbour issues in urban centres.
