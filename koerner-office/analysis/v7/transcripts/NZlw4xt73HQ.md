## Tactics and playbook steps

**Find low-competition high-ticket trades with keyword data** (acquisition)
1. Look up service keywords in Ahrefs for monthly volume and keyword difficulty (look for KD 0-10)
2. Check the search trend for rising demand
3. Search the service + a big city and inspect the map pack: few listings and <30 reviews mean an opening
4. Build a site with pages dedicated to the exact service term
> "If we look at the Google business listing showing up" — Tim Richard (~6:22)

**Be the marketing hub, subcontract the work** (operations)
1. Build one national brand website targeting a service in many areas
2. Recruit expert subcontractors per area
3. Send each job to a subcontractor and keep the customer relationship
> "I basically built a nationwide website, and I have subcontractors who do junk removal that I get a job, I send it out to them" — Tim Richard (~3:02)

**Anchor rental revenue with repeat B2B renters** (acquisition)
1. Target wedding planners, construction firms, schools and golf courses
2. Offer monthly rentals that need only regular cleaning

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Low keyword difficulty plus weak map packs signal local service niches where a dedicated site can rank fast.
- *niche_selection*: Specialise and name the exact service; generalist contractors often do it but don't advertise it.
- *niche_selection*: Demographic and climate trends (ageing, outages) create growing service demand you can ride early.
- *niche_selection*: Scarce skilled trades are booked out; skill scarcity is a moat.
- *scaling*: Being the brand and lead source while subcontracting delivery is a common scalable home-service model.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Ideas are opinions; starting one does not guarantee money.
- Artisan trades (Venetian plaster, stucco) need real skill and apprenticeship before launching.
- Licensing is required for roofing and waterproofing in many US areas.
- Keyword volumes are US figures; UK demand for several of these (generators, attic fans, crawl spaces) is much lower.
