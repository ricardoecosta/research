## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build a voice-cloned voicemail pack** (operations)
1. Clone the realtor's voice in Fish Audio from a 10-60 second clip, or from existing Instagram/YouTube video.
2. Ask an LLM for 10 generic voicemail scripts (new listing, price reduction, showing confirmation, offer accepted, counter offer, inspection, closing reminder, anniversary, referral request, holiday check-in), each under 30 seconds.
3. Translate into target languages (Spanish, Vietnamese, Mandarin, Portuguese; also Hindi, Italian, German, French).
4. Generate audio per message and per language one at a time, since batch download merges them.
5. Organise in Google Drive folders per language and send.
> "write 10 common real estate voicemail messages a realtor would leave for clients." — Chris Koerner (~4:24)

**Proactive demo before pitching** (sales)
1. Clone the prospect's voice before the meeting, ideally from a public video.
2. Generate sample voicemails in several languages.
3. Offer to share the files, or generate them only after they say yes.

**Free or one-time package as loss leader** (pricing)
1. Offer a one-time package or do it free, costing a few dollars in credits.
2. Use it to start a conversation about other AI help (more leads, faster response, more listings).
3. Convert to recurring services.
> "Why don't I do this for you for free? It might cost you like a couple bucks in credits." — Chris Koerner (~8:47)

**First 30-day launch plan** (acquisition)
1. Day 1: sign up, clone your own voice, make three demo voicemails in English, Spanish and Chinese.
2. Day 2: message friends who are real estate agents, take them to lunch and show a cloned demo.
3. Days 3-7: follow up with every office and friend.
4. Weeks 2-4: land 3-5 clients, over-deliver and deliver faster than promised, collect five-star reviews and list on Google Business Profile.
5. Month 2: expand to audiobooks, insurance agencies and immigration attorneys.
> "Follow-up, follow-up, follow-up. That's where the magic is." — Chris Koerner (~14:02)

**Teach-for-free at broker offices** (acquisition)
1. Bring lunch to a broker office and offer to teach agents something new with AI.
2. Show them how to do it themselves for free.
3. Leave a one-page flyer with QR code, pricing and URL.
4. Expect them to ask you to do it for them: give away the secrets, sell the implementation.
> "Give away the secrets. Sell the implementation." — Chris Koerner (~13:50)

**Niche down by channel size** (acquisition)
1. Pick a segment, such as YouTube channels with 100k-500k subscribers.
2. Offer voice cloning and dubbing only to them.

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Reduce friction for the customer: scrape public video for the voice sample and do the work for them instead of asking them to record.
- *customer_acquisition*: A cheap proactive demo made with the prospect's own assets (voice, listings) is a strong opener compared with a generic pitch.
- *niche_selection*: Productise one capability (voice cloning) and re-skin it for adjacent verticals by changing scripts, rather than building new products.
- *offer_design*: Give away the know-how and sell implementation; most professionals know it is possible but won't do it themselves.
- *pricing*: Recurring revenue is attractive but buyers resist it, so a one-time package can be the door-opener before a retainer.
- *mindset_execution*: Aggressive follow-up after initial contact is where conversions happen.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Very low marginal cost (cents per asset) with a $200-500 monthly price means margins are high, but defensibility is low since tools are public.
- *validation_testing*: Start with warm contacts to obtain testimonials, then use them to close strangers.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The cloned voice speaking Spanish sounds like an English speaker with a foreign accent, not a native speaker, so quality and authenticity for real leads are unverified.
- Batch downloading in Fish Audio merges the messages, so each file must be downloaded individually.
- Using friends' unpaid reviews as 'testimonials' without disclosure is ethically and legally questionable (UK CMA rules on fake and incentivised reviews).
- The speaker admits that translated verb conjugations and Mandarin could not be verified by him, so a native-speaker check is needed.
