## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Hector, who built an online, grant-funded trade school in Texas (about $2.4M in year one) and now consults for people starting schools. The core idea is that US workforce grants (WIOA-style, called 'WA' in the transcript) pay $5k-$15k per student, so a school can white-label a course and have the government pay the tuition. Variants include embedding a school in an existing business that struggles to hire (e.g. CNAs), on-the-job-training wage subsidies, and a school plus staffing placement combo. The key takeaway is that the arbitrage is navigating grant bureaucracy and enrolling students, but it is heavily US-state-specific.

### I1. Grant-funded online trade school (white-label courses)

- **Customer:** Laid-off workers, people on food stamps/benefits, warehouse, retail and fast-food workers, nonprofit and shelter clients who qualify for the grant.
- **Problem:** People qualify for free training grants but find the process bureaucratic and do not know about it; there is a shortage of tradespeople and technicians.
- **Offer:** Free training (plus free laptop or VR goggles) toward a certificate in a technician or remote-capable field, paid for by the state grant.
- **Business model:** Tuition paid by government workforce grant per enrolled student; margin over white-labelled course cost.
- **Pricing:** Grant pays per student, varying by state, region and field. Grant payout range USD 5000-15000 per student; Central Texas max USD 7500 per student White-label course cost $800-$1,000 (later $500 example); equipment such as goggles $200-$300 or a laptop.
- **Acquisition:** partnerships: Pitched the grant to churches, church groups, women's shelters, nonprofits, workforce development centres and chambers of commerce as community/referral partners; spent $0 on marketing.; organic_social_content: Got on Twitter, which produced his first consulting call (a flight attendant school in Minnesota).
- **Delivery:** Online courses licensed from a white-label provider, delivered remotely; some students receive laptops or VR goggles. Hands-on trades like welding or phlebotomy need in-person components or employer externships.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Grant pays $5k-$15k per student depending on region, state and field; White-label course costs $800-$1,000, rest is margin; Scaled to about $2.4M in year one on grants in Texas; Average graduate earns $25-$30 an hour
- **Tools:** White-label course providers, Laptops, VR goggles
- **Startup cost:** Low: white-label course about $500-$1,000 plus $200-$300 goggles or a laptop per student; state licensing fees and waits not stated.
- **Time to first revenue:** Varies by state: easy states quick, tough states wait 6 months to 2 years for approval (Florida, California, New York, Washington).
- **UK applicability [E]:** 1/5 — Depends on US state/federal workforce grants; the UK has different funded-training systems (e.g. apprenticeship levy, Skills Bootcamps, Adult Education Budget) with approved-provider registers and Ofqual/OFS rules, so the model would need a full rebuild.
- **Risks:** State licensing delays (up to two years in some states); Grant rules vary by region and can change; Hard to get trust from skeptical students ('what's the catch'); Compliance and reimbursement-timing risk; Some trades cannot be taught online
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 1/5 (Relied on in-person church and community outreach; enrolment is sales-driven. Online ads or SEO could be added but the grant message needs trust-building.); onboarding 2/5 (Grant paperwork, eligibility checks and student enrolment can be partly handled by forms and CRM but require human government liaison.); delivery 4/5 (Pre-built white-label online courses are largely software-delivered; some hybrid hands-on components need humans.); billing 2/5 (Reimbursement from state or workforce boards needs compliance documentation and human follow-up.); support 3/5 (Student support and job placement help can be mostly agent-handled with human escalation.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Church and nonprofit community partner outreach** (acquisition)
1. Identify nonprofits, shelters and church groups whose members already qualify for the grant
2. Pitch the free training grant as awareness, not just your school
3. Offer a free laptop to fix low login rates
4. Build a network of community partners as referrers
> "I just went knocking on church doors and getting involved with church groups and, you know, helping nonprofits" — Hector (~4:33)

**Reprice using the grant and bundle courses with placement** (pricing)
1. Find the grant amount in your region
2. Add courses to bundle
3. Offer job placement through your network
4. Raise the price to the grant level (e.g. $9,999) instead of charging the public $2,000

**Start with private pay while awaiting grant approval** (finance)
1. Sell white-label course to the general market
2. Use buy-now-pay-later or instalments
3. Switch to grant billing when approved

**Capture and follow up leads with a CRM and scripts** (sales)
1. Install a CRM so every call is captured
2. Use enrolment sales scripts
3. Run email campaigns and funnels

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Government-funded demand removes customer payment friction; the real edge is simplifying a bureaucratic process others find too complicated.
- *pricing*: Tradespeople-turned-school-owners often undercharge and skip grants; the opportunity is in sales, pricing and process, not the craft.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Turning a cost centre (recruiting) into a profit centre (paid training) is a powerful framing for employers.
- *mindset_execution*: Curiosity on a routine call (an insurance enquiry) led to discovering a whole industry.
- *offer_design*: Find the real blocker to adoption (students had no laptops) and fix it; adding a free laptop increased usage.
- *scaling*: Online-only delivery lets a school market statewide rather than being limited to a local area.
- *customer_acquisition*: Cold outreach to small employers about subsidies had very low response; 'too good to be true' offers need trust and warm intros.
- *legal_risk*: Regulatory friction (slow state approval) is a moat for those willing to wait.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Some states (Florida, California, New York, Washington) can take about two years to approve a school; others take six months.
- Grant payouts and rules vary by region, state and field; each region treats the grant independently.
- Employers and students distrust 'free' offers, which sound like a scam, so conversion is hard.
- Some trades such as welding cannot be taught online yet, and hybrid ones need externships.
- Putting students on long payment plans is risky; about half paid back.

## Tools mentioned

WIOA-style workforce grant (called 'WA') (Government funding that pays tuition per student; Pays $5k-$15k per student), White-label course providers (Buy courses and put your school name on them; $500-$1,000 per course), VR goggles (Remote hands-on training; $200-$300), Twitter (Found first consulting clients), Stripe buy now pay later / Klarna (Instalment payments for private-pay students), Go High Level (CRM/marketing platform (sponsor); 30-day free trial)
