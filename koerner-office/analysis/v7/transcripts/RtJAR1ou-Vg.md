## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate product demand before listing** (validation_testing)
1. Search Etsy for comparable products and inspect their sales history.
2. Use Everbee to estimate recent sales for individual listings.
3. Cross-check demand on Amazon by looking at the sold counts shown on listings.
4. Treat these figures as demand signals, not as proof of your own likely sales.
> "Now, before you sell or launch any product, you always need to make sure and validate that it actually does sell." — George McConnell (~0:31)

**Use listing variants and reviews to select a product size** (product)
1. Review the sizes buyers selected in the competitor listing's reviews.
2. Look for a size that appears more often; the host identifies 15 inches as the apparent top seller.
3. Compare that size's retail price with the supplier product and shipping prices.
> "So 15 seems like it is really the top seller. So I come up here, go to the 15 in, and as you can see right there, it's $26." — George McConnell (~2:49)

**Create AI-assisted product advertising** (acquisition)
1. Provide ChatGPT with a reference image and a description of the product.
2. Specify the desired model, pose and audience, and include negative prompts for unwanted appearance details.
3. Use Higgsfield with a reference, spoken script, behaviour prompt and voice settings to create a short video.
4. Review the output before using it; the host's example mispronounces the pet's name.
> "I said, "Hey, I want a handsome athletic man in his late 20s holding a custom pet shape pillow."" — George McConnell (~4:07)

**Make the product listing visually informative** (product)
1. Create an infographic showing the uploaded photo, customised product, name and colour choices.
2. Show what arrives in the box and how the product hangs.
3. Explain details such as the one-sided print so buyers know what to expect.
4. Keep the visuals easy to scan instead of filling the image with text.
> "So, jumping in the ChatGPT, you can see I've already made a couple here, all right?" — George McConnell (~8:55)

**Connect POD fulfilment to an online store** (operations)
1. Choose a product in InkJoy and add the customer's image.
2. Use the supplier's automatic background removal.
3. Add the item to the cart or push it to a connected store.
4. Consider Shopify or WooCommerce if you want to own the customer relationship and run your own ads.
> "Either one of these work, as well as Etsy. I feel like Etsy is kind of going downhill, so definitely probably lean towards one of these right here." — George McConnell (~11:43)

**Use supplier education materials** (operations)
1. Open the InkJoy blog section for its guides on selling.
2. Use the guides alongside the supplier's product catalogue when setting up listings.
> "Now one last thing I do want to show really quick on Inkjoy, if you click on the blog section, they have a ton of blogs teaching you how to sell, which is awesome." — George McConnell (~13:23)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Check whether buyers are already purchasing comparable products before investing time in a new listing.
- *validation_testing*: Cross-check demand signals across more than one marketplace, while remembering that competitor sales do not prove your own profitability.
- *niche_selection*: Use listing reviews and selected variants to look for products and sizes buyers prefer.
- *offer_design*: Show the product clearly, including what customers upload, choose and receive.
- *automation_ai*: A supplier workflow that handles customisation and fulfilment can reduce routine work for a small store.
- *customer_acquisition*: An independent store can give a seller more control over the customer relationship and advertising, but the host does not quantify the extra work or cost.
- *automation_ai*: AI-generated creative still needs a human check for names and other customer-specific details.
- *finance_unit_economics*: The host's example compares retail prices with supplier charges, but a sound margin check also needs fees, advertising, refunds and other costs.
