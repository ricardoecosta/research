### I1. AI Spanish lip-sync video dubbing for local businesses

- **Customer:** Local US businesses with video content: home services, dentists/med spas, real estate agents, food vendors and small restaurants
- **Problem:** About one in seven US people speak Spanish at home but businesses have no Spanish versions of their videos; traditional dubbing or re-shoots are too costly and lips don't match.
- **Offer:** Spanish-dubbed version of the business's existing video with matched lip movement, keeping the speaker's voice and pacing, delivered fast.
- **Business model:** Per-video fee or monthly retainer for dubbed videos
- **Pricing:** Per video (about $300) or monthly package for two videos. suggested per-video price USD 300 per video; two videos a month USD 500-600 per month per client Do first ones free for testimonials, then low-to-mid hundreds per video, or charge by minute/second for longer videos. Test pricing to find equilibrium.
- **Acquisition:** cold_sms_dm: Text/DM business owners with a free pre-made Spanish version of their video: 'I made a Spanish version of one of your videos for free. No catch. Want me to send it over?' then offer two a month for $500. Generation takes about 1-2 minutes.
- **Unit economics (claims):** $300 per video, two videos a month gives $500-600 per client per month; 10 clients at $500 a month is $5,000 a month; Task takes about 20 minutes or an afternoon; Token costs are a tiny fraction of the price charged
- **Tools:** OpenArt, OpenArt Arena, Seedance 2.5, Minimax H3, Meta glasses
- **Startup cost:** A few tokens/credits on OpenArt; no other cost stated

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Pick the lip-sync model via the OpenArt Arena leaderboard** (product)
1. Open the OpenArt Arena lip-sync leaderboard
2. Choose the top-ranked model (Seedance 2.5)
3. Upload the client video
4. Use the simple dub prompt keeping voice, pacing and lip match
5. Compare against a lower model only if needed
> "Look at the board. The leaderboard. Run with the top model." — Chris Koerner (~5:34)

**Free sample DM to get the first yes** (sales)
1. Find a local vendor's existing video
2. Dub it into Spanish for free
3. DM: made a Spanish version, no catch, want me to send it?
4. Offer two videos a month for $500 if it brings customers
5. If no reply, send the video proactively
6. Repeat across 100+ messages
> "the magic of this DM is the word free. No one's going to say no to a free thing that's already made." — Chris Koerner (~11:32)

**Choose a clip that shows lips and logo** (product)
1. Trim the source video to about 30 seconds
2. Pick a segment where the mouth and logo are clearly visible
3. Prefer talking about the business over hard selling

**Start with free work for testimonials, then test pricing** (pricing)
1. Do first videos free for testimonials
2. Move to low-to-mid hundreds per video
3. Consider per-minute or per-second pricing for long videos

**Niche down to an industry you know** (acquisition)
1. Pick an industry you have worked in (pizza, restaurants, oil change)
2. Pitch businesses in it using your insider knowledge

## Lessons

- *automation_ai*: Use a blind-test leaderboard to choose AI tools instead of testing every model yourself; it saves time and credits.
- *customer_acquisition*: Do a small piece of work for free first so the prospect can see the result; free removes the objection.
- *validation_testing*: With no testimonials, doing early work free to get social proof is worth it.
- *pricing*: Test pricing until you find what people will pay; don't set it once.
- *niche_selection*: Pick a niche you know, since expertise helps with pitching and credibility.
- *niche_selection*: Spotting an underserved segment (Spanish speakers) in a market where competitors have nothing gives an easy wedge.
- *mindset_execution*: Outreach is a numbers game; expect low response rates and keep sending.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Cheap tokens versus high-value deliverables make an asymmetric bet with low downside.
- *scaling*: A skill that opens new markets for your own content can be a revenue source before selling it as a service.

## Warnings and mistakes

- A single DM won't build the business; the host's one DM got no reply, so volume outreach is needed.
- Poor lip sync looks bad and clients won't want it on their website; model choice matters.
- YouTube's audio-track dubs don't match lips, which limits performance.
