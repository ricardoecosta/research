### I1. Denver Mobile Sauna (mobile sauna and cold plunge rental)

- **Customer:** Consumers and groups around Denver booking for private use; wellness studios for pop-ups
- **Problem:** People want sauna and cold plunge experiences without owning equipment
- **Offer:** Delivered mobile sauna and cold plunge units rented for hours, overnight or the weekend
- **Business model:** Per-rental fees by duration, plus event partnerships
- **Pricing:** Tiered by duration with a weekend upsell. 2 hours USD 300 per 2-hour rental; 4 hours USD 450 per 4-hour rental; 8 hours (transcript says $5.25, read as $525) USD 525 per 8-hour rental; overnight USD 600 per overnight rental; weekend add-on USD 200 extra for whole weekend Host says price anchoring pushes about 70% of renters to the weekend option (unsourced).
- **Acquisition:** partnerships: Pop-up events with wellness studios give recurring revenue without a physical site.; seo_organic_search: Website bookings; the host implies customers arrive at the site intending to book hourly.
- **Delivery:** Owner drives units to the customer, drops off, maintains the equipment and collects it afterwards.
- **Unit economics (claims):** 10 rentals/month at about $450 average = $4,500/month revenue; 2-3 weekend rentals = extra $1-2k/month
- **Tools:** 
- **Startup cost:** not stated
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Mobile sauna hire works in the UK. Check insurance, towing licence limits and venue permissions. Prices will differ.
- **Risks:** Competition is high per the host; Insurance and equipment maintenance; Physical logistics cap scalability
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Website SEO and ads could be run mostly by agents; studio partnerships need human outreach.); onboarding 4/5 (Online booking and waiver flow is software-driven.); delivery 0/5 (Physical towing, drop-off, set-up and pick-up every time.); billing 5/5 (Online payment at booking.); support 2/5 (Customer questions and issues mostly need a human or an agent with a human on call.)

### I4. Drip Sauna (contrast therapy inside a gym, Texas)

- **Customer:** Gym members and walk-ins in Cleburne, Texas
- **Problem:** Contrast therapy elsewhere requires a separate trip and costs $150-175/month
- **Offer:** Unlimited 45-minute sessions at under half the competitor price, inside the gym
- **Business model:** Monthly membership plus walk-in sessions
- **Pricing:** Membership plus pay-per-session. unlimited membership USD 75 per month; walk-in USD 30 per session First session free. Competitors charge $150-175/month.
- **Acquisition:** partnerships: Gym foot traffic and the gym's shared email list; the space sits on the route through the gym.; paid_social: Facebook ads bring almost all customers for the stock tank installs.; other: Free first session and public/private plunges for viral sharing.
- **Delivery:** Four infrared saunas ($7-8k each, financeable) and two cold plunges ($10k each) in about two bedrooms of rented gym space at $2,000/month.
- **Unit economics (claims):** 130 members at $75 = over $10k MRR; Broke even in under a year including equipment
- **Tools:** Facebook Ads
- **Startup cost:** About $50-60k equipment (4 saunas at $7-8k plus 2 plunges at $10k) plus $2k/month rent
- **Time to first revenue:** Within a few months they had 130 members (after 2 years looking for a site)
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Gym-based contrast therapy works in the UK; UK gyms and studios are open to such tie-ins. Check business rates, water and electrical safety.
- **Risks:** Depends on one gym relationship; Competitors can undercut; Large capex
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Gym partnership gives built-in traffic; ads can be agent-run.); onboarding 4/5 (Membership signup and access can be software-run.); delivery 1/5 (Equipment self-serve for members, but cleaning, water changes and maintenance need humans.); billing 5/5 (Recurring subscription.); support 3/5 (Members' queries can be handled by agents.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Price anchoring with a weekend upsell** (pricing)
1. Offer hourly, overnight and weekend rates.
2. Set the weekend rate only slightly above the short-rental rate.
3. Delivery effort is the same for 2 hours or 2 days, so the longer rental adds margin.
> "you might as well juice a little more revenue out of it" — Chris Koerner (~2:43)

**Piggyback on an existing business's customer base** (acquisition)
1. Find a gym or wellness studio with unused space.
2. Negotiate rent and cross-promotion, such as an email list share.
3. Position the unit on the route through the gym so foot traffic is the marketing.
> "He's got a built-in customer base on day one." — Chris Koerner (~14:42)

**Free first session** (acquisition)
1. Give the first session free.
2. Convert to $75/month membership or $30 walk-ins.
> "He makes this first session free. This creates an addiction." — Chris Koerner (~15:06)

**Price off local competitors' calendars** (pricing)
1. Watch competitors' booking calendars through the previous season.
2. Estimate how many weekends out they are booked.
3. Set similar prices.

**Facebook Marketplace funnel** (acquisition)
1. Post the unit with day price in title.
2. Put weekend pricing and a website link in the description.
3. Website uses Squarespace with Acuity scheduling for booking.
4. If it works, boost the post, then move to proper Facebook ads.

**Check local competition before entering** (acquisition)
1. Search Google Maps for competitors within an hour.
2. Use an ad scraper to see competitors' ads.
3. Either many competitors (market awareness) or none (first mover) can work.

**Build Google presence early** (acquisition)
1. Set up Google Business Profile.
2. Collect reviews and photos.
3. Launch the website in the first couple of weeks.

**Stack services and branded events** (product)
1. Create a branded event (workout, plunge, sauna, coffee).
2. Co-market with gyms.
3. Add services to raise revenue per customer.

**Counter seasonality** (operations)
1. Pair a seasonal business with one for the other season.
2. Partner with Airbnbs and lake rentals for off-season add-ons.

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Location, physical or digital, is the marketing; piggybacking on existing foot traffic cuts acquisition cost.
- *offer_design*: Stack complementary services and events to raise revenue per customer.
- *offer_design*: Community and expertise differentiate a commodity rental.
- *pricing*: Tiered pricing nudges buyers to larger packages.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Offset recurring revenue with high-ticket one-offs to smooth lumpy income.
- *validation_testing*: Start small, test with a free Marketplace post, then scale ads as demand proves out.
- *mindset_execution*: Harder tasks like partnerships and events create differentiation.
- *operations*: Buy lighter equipment first for easier logistics.
- *niche_selection*: Either competitors or none can be a good signal; competition shows awareness, none lets you be first.
- *pricing*: Local presence signals permanence and allows higher prices.
- *mindset_execution*: A genuine user of the product sustains a side business longer.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Not installing drains under cold plunges makes water changes painful.
- Don't let customers use their own wood, since you can't control what they burn.
- Inflatable cold plunges are hard to keep cold in summer without a chiller.
- Summer demand for saunas is weak; a Minnesota operator couldn't generate enough bookings in the first two years.
- The industry is still in the education phase, so patience is needed.
- Danny would start with the lighter, cheaper unit because it is easier to tow.
