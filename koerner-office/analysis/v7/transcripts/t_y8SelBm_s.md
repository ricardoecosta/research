**Start with a small paid-social test** (acquisition)
1. Launch the product page and initial social presence.
2. Test Facebook and Instagram ads with small spends.
3. Track customer acquisition cost and bring in someone with advertising skills when the early tests show promise.

**Capture email addresses from paid traffic** (acquisition)

3. Use the list for customer communication and win-back campaigns, and treat consent and UK direct-marketing rules as part of the setup.
> "That's the first sort of on-site funnel is you ask for their email address for a discount." — Chris Koerner (~8:09)

**Make prepaid annual plans attractive** (pricing)
1. Offer a prepaid annual story alongside monthly billing.
2. Set the annual price so buyers see a lower monthly equivalent.
3. Use the prepaid option to fund fulfillment earlier and reduce cancellations during the story.

**Expand the story catalogue to serve existing customers** (product)
1. Develop additional stories for customers who already enjoy the product.
2. Give a customer options to choose another story rather than relying only on one story renewing.

**Use win-back campaigns for completed or inactive customers** (acquisition)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Validate with customers who fit the product rather than treating supportive family purchases as proof of demand.
- *customer_acquisition*: An owned email audience can reduce dependence on social platforms and can drive a substantial share of sales.
- *pricing*: A prepaid plan can fit a story product better than a conventional monthly subscription when the customer needs the full sequence.
- *niche_selection*: Know the specific customer and how to speak to them; a broad demographic label does not provide a useful marketing plan.
- *offer_design*: A story delivered in sequence creates a stronger continuity promise but makes writing, fulfillment, and customer expectations more demanding.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Pair the creative product owner with someone who can focus on sales, marketing, and business operations.
- *operations*: Plan capacity and cash reinvestment around predictable seasonal peaks.
- *customer_acquisition*: Small conversion improvements can matter at high traffic volumes, but keep experimentation tied to what customers actually do.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI for analysis and decision support where it helps, while judging carefully whether generated creative content fits the product.
- *validation_testing*: Test a product concept with a simple website and email sign-up before committing heavily to production.
- *mindset_execution*: A product that serves a narrow interest can still have a market; lack of early sales does not by itself prove there is no demand.
- *offer_design*: Creative quality is central to a creative business, but it still needs a workable way to reach customers and sell.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Family and friends can give a false positive when testing demand; seek customers who match the real target market.
- A serialized letter story is operationally harder than a standalone monthly letter because the story must connect and arrive in order.
- At least one insert was removed after the team learned it was offensive.
- Gift recipients may dislike a product even when someone else purchased it, leading the purchaser to cancel.
- Seasonal peaks create staffing and fulfillment pressure; Christmas can be hard because staff want to be with their families.
- The company became more expensive to acquire customers through Meta than it was in its early days.
- The host says broad marketing such as “anybody with a mom” does not identify the actual customer; The Flower Letters found that marketing to men did not work for its product.
- Writers and other creatives can struggle if they focus only on making the product and do not handle the business and sales side.
- The host says adding recipe videos could create a substantial production burden; he suggests testing whether the target customer would actually use them.
- The episode contains a Quo sponsor read with a discount link and also promotes the host's paid business community.
