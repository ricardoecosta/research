## Tactics and playbook steps

**Sell branded upcycled products back to the source business** (acquisition)
1. Approach wineries and breweries
2. Collect their empty bottles
3. Melt into cheese boards with their logo
4. Sell back for gift shops and tasting rooms
> "take their empty bottles, melt it down, put their logo on it, and sell it back to them" — Tim Richard (~1:09)

**Florist referral partnership for keepsake preservation** (acquisition)
1. Visit flower shops that do weddings and special occasions
2. Pitch preservation as an upsell they cannot offer
3. Show resin and keepsake box options
4. Agree a referral arrangement
> "So if you can go there and partner with a flower shop who is constantly doing weddings and special occasions" — Tim Richard (~6:47)

**Win recurring B2B accounts rather than one-off retail** (sales)
1. Pick a product that businesses reorder weekly (tortillas, coffee, boba)
2. Target accounts like taquerias, cafes and bubble tea shops
3. Win a standing order route

**Find an under-served product niche before buying a machine** (product)
1. Look for categories where few people sell the item (fresh boba, toothpaste tablets)
2. Offer a local, fresh or unique angle vs imports

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Recurring B2B accounts (cafes, restaurants, shops) are more valuable than scattered retail sales because they reorder regularly.
- *customer_acquisition*: Partnering with an adjacent business that wants an upsell (florists, funeral services) gives a ready referral channel.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Free or very cheap raw materials, such as waste bottles, give high margins on a custom product.
- *offer_design*: Branding a product with the customer's own logo turns commodities into B2B merchandise sales.
- *niche_selection*: Choose niches where few competitors serve a local, fresh angle versus imports.
- *automation_ai*: Machine-based physical production caps automation: delivery stays manual, so choose the model that best fits agent-run operations.
- *validation_testing*: Treat single anecdotes like the $500k-a-year pillow maker as unverified; validate demand before buying equipment.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Buying a machine does not guarantee income, and results will differ from the examples shown; do your own research.
- Maple syrup depends on climate and trees; it won't work in places without maple trees.
- Bath bombs are a crowded market; differentiation is needed.
