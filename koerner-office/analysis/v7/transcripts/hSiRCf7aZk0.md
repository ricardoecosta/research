## Tactics and playbook steps

**Start listing searches broad, then narrow** (acquisition)
1. Set a maximum budget and choose a broad business type filter.
2. Keep the initial search wide if the buyer is unsure what they want.
3. Narrow by industry, revenue, profit, traffic or age after learning which listings are relevant.
> "I'd probably try to keep it quite broad to begin with, especially if you're not sure what you're looking for." — Joe (~2:50)

**Verify listing claims before making an offer** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Ask for Google Analytics access and the profit and loss statement.
2. Ask the seller to walk through supporting records on a video call or share screenshots.
3. Check the site and compare claims against its traffic and financial history.
> "Can I have access to Google Analytics? Can I have the P&L? And anything else that's required to verify that P&L." — Nick (~9:14)

**Check what assets transfer with the business** (operations)
1. Read the sale description for the domain, website files, email address, social accounts and brand assets.
2. Ask the seller to confirm that any important connected asset not listed is included.
> "if you find something that's connected to the business, and it's not stated to be included, you need to make sure that it is" — Joe (~12:33)

**Identify growth opportunities that fit the buyer's skills** (product)
1. Look for concrete changes such as moving from AdSense to another ad network, if eligible.
2. Assess whether existing skills could grow traffic or improve affiliate conversions.
3. Estimate how those changes might shorten the break-even period.
> "if you can apply some of the existing skills you have, all of a sudden, your break even window becomes much, much faster." — Joe (~8:29)

**Meet the seller and build rapport** (sales)
1. Get on a call or video call with the seller.
2. Ask how the business started and use the conversation to assess whether the seller is open and trustworthy.
3. Treat unresolved concerns during the conversation as a reason to reassess the deal.
> "So get on a call, get on a vehicle and just talk to them, how they started it, try and build some rapport." — Joe (~34:30)

**Write a transition and growth plan before buying** (operations)
1. Decide what to inspect and change after the sale.
2. Set a plan for transferring assets and continuing monetization.
3. Identify concrete opportunities to improve profit, traffic or the business.
> "make sure that you've got a plan after the fact on what you're going to look at this one." — Joe (~36:33)

**Use AI as a diligence assistant, then verify its output** (validation_testing)
1. Give an LLM the listing information and the buyer's situation to generate diligence questions or possible opportunities.
2. Use it to organize information into reports or SWOT analysis.
3. Check its conclusions against source records; do not accept its advice without verification.
> "It essentially gives you information, but just be really careful not to believe everything it says" — Joe (~25:32)

**Look for diversification and uniqueness** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Check whether revenue comes from several sources and traffic comes from several channels.
2. Find out whether a small number of pages or one platform account for most results.
3. Assess how easy the business would be to copy and what makes it distinctive.
> "More money does not necessarily mean that the business is secure. It means you wanna look for things like diversity, uniqueness" — Joe (~43:01)

**Negotiate and evaluate seller financing carefully** (finance_unit_economics)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Verify a listing through source records, analytics and a direct seller walkthrough before trusting its financial claims.
- *operations*: Buy with a clear plan for transition and improvement; an acquired business can deteriorate if the owner does nothing.
- *niche_selection*: Look for diversified traffic and revenue, and ask what makes the business difficult to copy.
- *validation_testing*: A buyer should assess whether an acquisition fits their skills and goals rather than assuming every attractive listing is a good fit.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI to organize diligence and generate questions, but verify its conclusions against evidence.
- *scaling*: Consider a bolt-on acquisition when an existing business can use the acquired site's content, audience or traffic.
- *customer_acquisition*: Build rapport with a seller; a direct conversation can reveal concerns that listing data does not.
- *mindset_execution*: Consistency and continued execution matter; keep working at a chosen path until it is shown not to work.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Start with the end in mind: define the cash flow goal, transition plan and growth plan before committing capital.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not buy a business and leave it untouched; the guest says a buyer should have a clear plan to maintain or improve it.
- Seller descriptions present the business in its best light; buyers should check claims and investigate possible problems.
- Do not treat the pest site walkthrough as a recommendation or as completed due diligence.
- A stale revenue graph may make an ecommerce listing misleading about current performance.
- Seller financing can create complicated terms and default remedies; Joe advises caution, especially for a first purchase.
- Avoid SaaS lifetime sales unless they are necessary at an early stage; users may retain lifetime access without generating ongoing payments.
- A faceless YouTube channel depends on YouTube and its changing rules for traffic and revenue.
- AI advice can be wrong; use it to support diligence and verify important claims independently.
- Large businesses are not automatically secure; traffic concentration can put even a high-profit business at risk.
- Traditional informational content sites may lose search traffic to AI summaries and algorithm changes.
