## Tactics and playbook steps

**Start with a nucleus colony or attract a swarm** (validation_testing)
1. Hang a swarm trap to invite a colony, or buy a nucleus colony from a local beekeeper.
2. Slobo recommends buying a nucleus colony because the beekeeper checks that it is established and ready to grow.
3. Begin with two or three hives so colonies can be balanced if one is struggling.
> "You can do a beer removal. You can hang a swarm trap, swarm traps look like this. So you can simply hang one on a tree and it can invite a new colony straight to the swarm trap." — Slobo (~7:34)

**Use a self-serve roadside stand to make local honey easy to discover** (customer_acquisition)
1. Place honey by the street where passing drivers can see it.
2. Let customers choose products and pay through a cash drop or the named peer-to-peer payment services.
3. Use local visibility and customer referrals to build awareness.
> "people can drive by, and they see the farm stand, and they pick out whatever honey they like, and they can drop their cash in there." — Slobo (~14:34)

**Add seasonal revenue alongside honey** (offer_design)
1. Notice when honey sales slow and removals pick up.
2. Add bee removals and education tours as complementary services.
3. Treat the different revenue streams as a way to reduce dependence on honey sales alone.
> "we're always trying to find different ways to make money because honey sales can slow down on the summer and the beer moveals pick up in the summer." — Slobo (~2:02)

**Build a local service reputation from honey customers** (customer_acquisition)
1. Let honey customers know the business also rescues bees.
2. Respond to property owners who find colonies on their land.
3. Use referrals generated by the existing honey business to bring in removal jobs.
> "People know that you do honey so they're like, okay, I have bees, this guy, he can maybe rescue these bees from my properties." — Slobo (~2:34)

**Stabilize rescued colonies before moving them** (operations)
1. Bring rescued colonies to a holding apiary.
2. Check that the colony has a queen and that she is laying.
3. Feed colonies, treat for mites, and let them establish for one or two weeks before moving them to other apiaries.
> "So we bring them here so we can make sure they're okay, make sure they have a queen, make sure they have enough food, and once they're well established, then we can move them out to our other apiaries." — Slobo (~5:16)

**Choose a retail location for existing foot traffic** (customer_acquisition)
1. The founders moved to a shopping plaza near a popular restaurant after the previous building's new owners planned to triple the rent.
2. They say restaurant visitors began coming into the new shop and formed a new customer base.
3. Evaluate nearby customer traffic before committing to a retail location.
> "a lot of the people from the restaurant started coming in. So that was a new customer base for us is people that were just going to the restaurant." — Slobo (~32:34)

**Expand the store assortment beyond own honey** (offer_design)
1. Stock honey from other beekeepers as well as the founders' own local honey.
2. Add infused and monofloral varieties, books, children's products, candles, and beauty products to give different shoppers options.
3. The founders say they wanted the store to feel exciting for families and children.
> "We want to make the store exciting for everyone including kids. So we had all this artwork done in the store and we have a lot of children's books." — Alejandra (~32:57)

**Avoid harvesting honey before cells are capped** (operations)
1. Inspect whether honey cells are capped before harvesting.
2. Do not rush to extract open cells containing nectar.
3. The founders say honey with excessive moisture may ferment.
> "I think the biggest mistake is if you harvest honey too soon. So if they haven't capped off the cells, like if you see some open cells where it's just nectar, like if you just see nectar in there and they haven't capped it off, then the moisture content might be too high." — Slobo (~24:11)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Look for adjacent services that complement the main product and can earn money in a different season.
- *mindset_execution*: Build the business around a real customer need and an existing skill or resource; the first colony came from bees nesting in the founder's garage and his father's beekeeping knowledge.
- *validation_testing*: A business can start with a small initial setup, but growth still requires hands-on skill, equipment, and more space.
