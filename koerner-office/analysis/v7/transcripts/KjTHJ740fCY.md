## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews two college-age resellers, Lucas and John, from rural North Carolina/Virginia, who started on eBay selling childhood Legos and thrift-store finds, then moved into bulk consumer electronics. They describe buying 500-unit lots from a European supplier, a bad batch that lost about $4-5k, and diversifying into US suppliers via about 1,000 cold calls and 12,000 emails. They claim six figures of revenue last year and say anyone can make $1,000-5,000 a month with 2-5 hours a week. Key takeaway: reselling starts with low risk (sell your own stuff, thrift, Facebook Marketplace to eBay), but sourcing is the bottleneck and supplier concentration is dangerous.

### I1. Bulk vintage consumer electronics reselling on eBay

- **Customer:** eBay consumers buying used electronics and cameras; a New York flea-market vendor who bought cameras in bulk
- **Problem:** Used electronics are cheap at source but sell for much more to buyers who value them; sellers can't test or list them
- **Offer:** Tested/working used consumer electronics and cameras at around half the price of new
- **Business model:** Buy low in bulk, resell individually on eBay (and some wholesale to a bulk buyer) at a markup
- **Pricing:** Market price per unit on eBay; bulk buyer paid roughly eBay prices. average sale price now USD 100-190 per unit (current average sale); first lot working range USD 50-200 per working unit; Kodak cameras USD 25-30 per unit; Canon cameras USD 100-200 per unit; first European lot cost USD 10-10 per unit; current US supplier cost USD 15-40 per unit Sold-listing checks on eBay determine price; eBay fees about 12% mentioned
- **Acquisition:** marketplace_platform: Sell on eBay via app listings; listing takes about a minute per item. Buyers come inbound.; partnerships: Sell about 60% of cameras directly to a New York flea-market vendor who found them via their eBay store, paid via PayPal/Stripe avoiding eBay fees.
- **Delivery:** Founders receive lots, test items (using YouTube guides), photograph, list and ship themselves; bulk camera buyer gets packaged lots with batteries added
- **Unit economics (claims):** First 500-unit lot cost about $5k, returned $8-10k; About 15 hours combined work for roughly $5k profit; Margins about 50% early, about 30% now with US suppliers; Bad lot lost about $4-5k
- **Tools:** eBay, Google Lens, WhatsApp, PayPal, Stripe, YouTube, AI for finding emails
- **Startup cost:** Scaled from selling household items; first bulk lot about $5,000
- **Time to first revenue:** About 2 years from first resale to first bulk lot; items sell within days
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — UK has eBay UK, car boot sales, charity shops and WEEE/liquidation suppliers. Tax/VAT and import duty differ, and trading income is reportable to HMRC.
- **Risks:** Single supplier dependence; Untested lots can be mostly broken (lost $4-5k); Suppliers realising eBay value and raising prices; High return/defect rate in electronics; Tariffs and slow overseas shipping; Wire transfers to unknown overseas supplier; Cash flow is tight so can't match bigger buyers
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (eBay listings are inbound marketplace demand; agents could generate listings and handle bulk-buyer messages); onboarding 2/5 (Supplier onboarding is by phone/WhatsApp relationships; no customer onboarding on eBay); delivery 0/5 (Physical receiving, testing, photographing, packaging and shipping require a human every time); billing 5/5 (eBay and PayPal/Stripe handle payment automatically); support 3/5 (Returns and buyer messages can be agent-drafted but returns need physical handling)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Staged reselling progression** (operations)
1. Sell items from around the house first
2. Use proceeds at thrift stores, yard sales and flea markets
3. Sell 100+ items in a category to learn it
4. Then buy bulk lots from eBay, Marketplace, Mercari or Poshmark
> "you need to sell 100 plus items in a category to really know it then you can start looking in bulk" — Lucas (~13:34)

**Cold call and email suppliers** (acquisition)
1. Find liquidators, junk removal firms and eBay sellers with large untested inventory
2. Look up phone/email from company names
3. Pitch 'can I buy in bulk off eBay'
4. Expect about 0.5-1% conversion; used AI to scrape emails

**Cross-check sold listings for floor price** (pricing)
1. Check eBay sold listings
2. Subtract about 12% fee
3. Buy only if worst-case piecemeal sale is profitable

**Sell in bulk to flea-market buyer off-platform** (sales)
1. Wait for buyer to approach via eBay store
2. Text photos of lots
3. Accept PayPal/Stripe and avoid eBay fees

**Wanted listing on Marketplace** (acquisition)
1. Post 'I will buy your cameras / old electronics'
2. Respond to inbound offers (about 20 messages a day)

**Build standing relationships with sellers** (sales)
1. Give your number to anyone you buy from
2. Ask them to message you when they get more
3. Do the same with eBay sellers

**Split AirPods for sale** (pricing)
1. Sell left and right individually for premium

## Lessons

- *operations*: Sourcing, not selling, is the bottleneck in reselling; demand on eBay is effectively assured for electronics.
- *validation_testing*: Start with zero-cost inventory and reinvest proceeds before taking on bulk lot risk.
- *scaling*: Diversify suppliers early; relying on one source nearly broke the business.
- *customer_acquisition*: A cold call that offers to give someone money is an easier ask than one selling a product.
- *niche_selection*: Scarcity of discontinued products (VCRs, camcorders) pushes prices up.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Margins shrink as you operationalise; early flips return much higher percentages than bulk.
- *niche_selection*: Hot categories rotate; stay tapped into the eBay culture.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Cash flow constrains scale; lacking cash cost them bigger lots.
- *customer_acquisition*: Reselling is a people business; relationships bring inbound supply offers daily.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Relying on a single supplier is dangerous; tariffs, licensing and quality shifts can break the business
- Untested lots from unknown or newer sellers can be mostly broken; one lot of 500 had nothing working
- Don't confuse e-waste sources with resellers selling their leftover junk; two middlemen increases fraud risk
- Medical devices and supplements sell well but require care on what you can legally sell
- As vintage electronics gain attention, suppliers realise eBay value and stop selling cheaply
- VHS movies are hit or miss, not reliably valuable
