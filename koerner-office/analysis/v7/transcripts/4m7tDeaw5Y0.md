## Tactics and playbook steps

**Pallet selection by size and value** (operations)
1. Walk the warehouse and pick pallets with visible mid-sized boxes of small appliances (e.g. air fryers)
2. Avoid extremely large or heavy items that are hard to ship
3. Remove top layers of pallets to fit on the trailer
> "I like the medium sized boxes. I'm really worried about the extremely large boxes." — Host (~3:12)

**Item valuation by Amazon lookup** (operations)
1. Unbox each item and find barcode or product name
2. Search Amazon for comparable listing price
3. Record a rough retail value, discount heavily for unknowns
4. Check packaging condition to judge if new
> "Let's see if Amazon can find anything close." — Host (~12:50)

## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: Retail value on a pallet is not resale value; assume 50-60% of retail before fees, and expect some losses.
- *operations*: Bulky items drive shipping and storage costs, so favour small to medium, high-value, easily shipped goods.
- *scaling*: Time spent on research and handling per item is a hidden cost that makes this model hard to scale.
- *validation_testing*: Check the manifest or source: pallets labelled Amazon returns can contain other marketplace goods like Temu items.
- *validation_testing*: Profit figures shown before items are sold are estimates; wait for realised sales numbers before treating the model as proven.
- *automation_ai*: Physical-inventory resale is largely manual work, so it fits poorly with a business that agents should run almost entirely on their own.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Pallets are mystery gambles; many items are broken, bulky or low value, and mixed Temu goods lower value.
- Valuing and unboxing a pallet takes far longer than expected, so labour time eats into profit.
