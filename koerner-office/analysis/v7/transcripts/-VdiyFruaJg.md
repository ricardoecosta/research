### I1. Buy a retiring exterminator's pest control route

- **Customer:** Homeowners on recurring pest plans
- **Problem:** Retiring solo exterminator has no successor and customers who need continued service
- **Offer:** Continuation of recurring pest treatments under the original owner's name
- **Business model:** Recurring quarterly pest control plans paid by homeowners
- **Pricing:** Recurring plan per home. Recurring pest plan USD 400-700 per home per year Route of about 120 homes gives roughly $60,000 a year revenue
- **Acquisition:** referrals_word_of_mouth: Seller Hal writes a letter introducing the buyer to each customer and rides along on the first round; business keeps the original name.
- **Delivery:** Owner-operator physically visits homes (garages, porches, back steps) every 3 months to treat them.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Route about 120 homes, ~$60,000/yr revenue; Solo operator keeps around half of revenue; Routes sell for 1-2x yearly recurring revenue; $20,000 down, remainder in seller monthly payments; About a dozen homes (1 in 10) churned in first 3 months
- **Tools:** State pest control applicator exam/license
- **Startup cost:** About $20,000 down plus exam fee of roughly $25 to a couple hundred dollars
- **Time to first revenue:** Immediate on takeover (existing recurring customers)
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — Pest control routes exist in the UK, but there is no SBA loan, US licensing exam differs, and UK pest control is typically commercial-heavy; seller finance still possible.
- **Risks:** Customers loyal to the old owner cancel (about 1 in 10 did); Physical solo labour limits scale; Low income from a single route
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 1/5 (Deal sourcing from retiring owners is relationship-led; customers come with the route.); onboarding 2/5 (Customer intro letters and scheduling can be templated, but the seller handover is personal.); delivery 0/5 (Physical pest treatments at homes require a licensed human every visit.); billing 4/5 (Recurring plan billing can be automated with card on file/invoicing software.); support 3/5 (Scheduling and basic queries can be handled by agents; complaints need a human.)
> "Homes on a recurring pest plan commonly pay somewhere between four and $700 a year." — Narrator (~2:52)
> "Customers don't buy a company. They buy the person they trust." — Narrator (~4:40)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Seller introduction letter and ride-along** (operations)
1. Ask the retiring owner to write a short personal letter to every customer introducing you
2. Have the owner ride along on your first round
3. Keep the original business name on trucks and branding
> "He writes a short letter to every customer, in his own words, introducing you." — Narrator (~4:21)

**Retain key technicians with their wants, not just money** (hiring)
1. Sit with the tech and ask what he actually wants
2. Offer a four-day schedule, lead trainer title and a two-year retention bonus
3. Keep senior tech pay unchanged on acquisition

**Fund acquisitions with SBA 7(a) plus seller standby note** (finance)
1. Put 10% down
2. Have the seller carry half of the 10% as a standby note
3. Keep seller on for up to a year for transition
4. Use existing company cash flow to fund later down payments

**Win sellers by letting them meet your people** (sales)
1. Offer same name, same crew, same phone number
2. Bring your technicians to meet the seller's crew
3. Do not pitch; talk shop

**Consolidate routes and move scheduling to software** (operations)
1. Fold nearby acquired routes into the same trucks
2. Move paper schedule into routing software over a weekend
3. Reduce cross-town driving

**Prioritise members during peak demand** (operations)
1. Text maintenance-plan members first each day
2. Give non-members honest arrival windows and callbacks
3. Owner answers phones during crunch

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Succession gap creates deal flow: many boomer owners lack a plan and want the right successor, not only the highest bid.
- *operations*: Keeping the original brand and people reduces churn in acquisitions of trust-based businesses.
- *scaling*: Start small (a solo route) to learn the trade before buying larger companies.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Recurring-revenue service businesses are valued on multiples of owner earnings, usually 1x to 2.75x in the story.
- *scaling*: Cash flow from acquired companies can fund the down payments for the next acquisitions.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Retention of key employees is cheaper than replacing them; ask what they actually want.
- *legal_risk*: Holding a licence yourself means the company does not depend on one employee's credentials.
- *mindset_execution*: Building this takes years (11 in the story); the quiet approach beats the fast, loud one for most people.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not buy a business that lives entirely in the owner's head (phone, customers, techs); get the seller to stay through the handoff and plan for key people inside the deal.
- Private equity platforms are acquiring and poaching techs; you cannot outbid them, so retention must rely on non-price factors.
- Customers may leave after an ownership change; about 1 in 10 left in the first 3 months of the pest route.
