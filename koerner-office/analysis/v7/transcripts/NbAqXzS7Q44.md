## Summary

Chris Koerner argues 'follow your passion' is poor advice and presents an eight-step 'obsession audit' for finding what you are obsessed with: review YouTube, Google, Amazon and bank histories with AI; ask five friends what you never shut up about; recall childhood interests; keep a two-week energy log; map envy; write 100 questions; use a teach-it-for-free filter; and run many cheap experiments. He says passion comes from the Latin for suffering, that obsession is built by getting good at things, and that you should sell before you build and post publicly for accountability. A few small business ideas are mentioned in passing, and he promotes his Playmakers AI agency community.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**AI analysis of YouTube history, searches and spending** (mindset)
1. Export your YouTube watch history from takeout.google.com (free) or screenshot a few pages
2. Export your Amazon order history as a spreadsheet and gather bank statements
3. Upload to ChatGPT, Claude or Grok
4. Prompt: 'Here's 90 days of my YouTube watch history, ignore the random junk, show me the three obsessions hiding in here that I'd never admit out loud'
5. Ask AI to categorise discretionary spending and say what you clearly care about
> "Show me the three obsessions hiding in here that I'd probably never want to admit to myself out loud." — Chris Koerner (~10:52)

**Hijacked test** (mindset)
1. Text five people who know you best: 'What do I not shut up about?'
2. Look for answers that cluster into about three similar themes
3. Optionally feed six or more months of texts, notes and voice memos to AI and ask which topics you keep circling back to
> "text five people who know you the best right now and ask them one question, what do I not shut up about?" — Chris Koerner (~11:40)

**Childhood callback** (mindset)
1. Recall what you did for hours between ages 8 and 15 without being asked
2. Recall what you got in trouble for (e.g. talking in class could point to sales or content, taking things apart to building, bossing kids to leadership)
3. Identify which of these could be monetised today

**Two-week energy log** (mindset)
1. After each activity for two weeks, rate 1-10 how you feel or how much energy it gave or took
2. Also note activities where hours vanished (time distortion/flow)
3. Keep it in a notes app
4. Paste the log into AI and ask it to find what charges you up and what drains you

**Envy map** (mindset)
1. Write down five people who give you a sting of jealousy (not mere admiration)
2. Study their lives, work and businesses
3. Look for the pattern across all five

**100 questions dump** (mindset)
1. Write or voice-memo 100 questions you want answered before you die
2. Push past the first 20 surface-level ones
3. Transcribe and have AI cluster them into themes
4. The biggest cluster is what your brain keeps chewing on

**Teach-it-for-free filter** (mindset)
1. Identify what you'd explain to a stranger on a plane for free
2. Treat it as a possible obsession and potential paid offer

**Sell before you have it / many small experiments** (acquisition)
1. Try two or three new things a month, with permission to quit fast
2. Run the ad or post the offer before buying equipment or building the product
3. Post on Facebook or Nextdoor and see if the phone rings
4. Post experiments publicly for accountability and free lottery tickets
> "run the ad first, post on Facebook or replace next door, see if your phone rings" — Chris Koerner (~20:38)

**Financial stakes for accountability** (mindset)
1. Set a deadline for a task you are avoiding
2. Promise friends or a group chat a payment (e.g. $100 each) if you miss it
3. Result: he filmed three videos in a five-hour session the same day

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: 'Follow your passion' fails because most people don't know their passion and have several; use evidence from your own behaviour to find it.
- *mindset_execution*: Obsession is built, not found: passion follows progress, so pick the direction that gives the smallest, fastest win and stack two or three wins.
- *automation_ai*: With AI, the cost of testing an idea has dropped to near zero (site, copy, video and offer in an afternoon), so do many cheap experiments.
- *validation_testing*: Validate demand before spending: post the offer first, and let the market respond faster than your feelings.
- *mindset_execution*: Ask 'what am I willing to suffer for?' rather than 'what do I love?'; the real signal is that bad days doing it beat good days doing anything else.
- *mindset_execution*: Attach stakes and public accountability to commitments; public posting also lets customers and partners find you.
- *scaling*: Recognise your own operating style: the host loves going from zero to one and prefers handing off to operators, so he picks businesses to fit that.
- *niche_selection*: The industry is just a costume; a durable through-line (e.g. commerce, the first customer) matters more than any niche.
- *customer_acquisition*: Getting past the cringe of posting publicly is necessary; free repeated 'lottery tickets' of content can eventually hit.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Don't buy expensive equipment or finance a business before talking to a single customer; validate demand first.
- You cannot think or journal your way to your obsession; gather evidence from behaviour and try things instead.
- Dopamine from research and information gathering without action leads to procrastination.
- If the first 30 posts hadn't gone viral he might have quit, so expect many attempts before traction.
