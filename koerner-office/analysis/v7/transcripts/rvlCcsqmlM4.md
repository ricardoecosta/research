## Tactics and playbook steps

**Score an idea for friction** (validation_testing)
1. Choose one idea.
2. Score startup cost, skill barrier, weekly hours, time to first sale, and risk from one to five, with one meaning less friction and five meaning more.
3. Add the scores, then inspect each factor so a low total does not hide a constraint.
> "Take one idea, score five things from one to five: startup cost, skill barrier, weekly hours, time it takes to get to that first sale, and risk." — Unnamed narrator (~0:38)

**Test buyer demand before spending on subscriptions** (validation_testing)
1. If you do not have buyers, talk to a prospective customer before buying another subscription.
2. Ask what problem they have and whether they would pay for the proposed result.
> "You have no buyers? Okay, that's fine. Talk to a prospective customer before buying yet another subscription." — Unnamed narrator (~2:13)

**Make a sample before promising delivery** (product)
1. If you are missing skills, make one sample of the work.
2. Use the sample to check whether you can deliver before making a promise to a customer.
> "Missing some skills? No problem. Make one sample before promising delivery." — Unnamed narrator (~2:20)

**Write a specific service offer** (offer_design)
1. Name a specific buyer and a specific result.
2. Describe a deliverable the buyer can preview, such as three useful clips with accurate captions from a long video.
> "Your offer should be something like, "I'll turn this into three useful clips with accurate captions, all ready for your preview."" — Unnamed narrator (~1:44)

**Test the biggest obstacle within available time** (mindset_execution)
1. Compare the idea's weekly hours with the time you actually have.
2. Circle the biggest obstacle and choose one action to test it.
> "Tonight, write down one offer and score those five factors I mentioned earlier. Then circle the biggest obstacle and choose one action to test it." — Unnamed narrator (~2:04)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Judge an idea by the obstacles between the idea and its first paying customer, not by an income screenshot.
- *niche_selection*: Check each friction factor separately; a low total can hide one constraint that makes the idea impractical.
- *offer_design*: A specific buyer and result make an offer easier to understand and demonstrate.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI to assist the work, while retaining human responsibility for permission and quality control.
- *customer_acquisition*: Talk to a prospective buyer before spending on another subscription.
- *mindset_execution*: Treat the first sale as evidence that an offer can work, not as a promise of financial independence.

## Warnings and mistakes

- A free-to-start idea can still consume evenings and personal time.
- A time-intensive idea can become a second job that has not paid yet if it exceeds the founder's available hours.
- Do not treat an impressive income screenshot as proof that an idea will work for you.
- The example video service still requires permission, quality control, and a paying customer.
- Using a robot does not remove the work involved.
- Do not assume a low friction score guarantees revenue; the narrator says the scores are judgment calls, not lab results.
