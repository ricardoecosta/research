### I1. Remote-run residential cleaning business (subcontracted cleaners)

- **Customer:** Busy two-income families and households who outsource cleaning (also mentions buildings generally).
- **Problem:** Households lack time to deep clean; independent cleaners dislike marketing, bidding and client management.
- **Offer:** Booked house cleaning (first-time deep clean then weekly, biweekly or monthly recurring) delivered by vetted independent cleaners with their own equipment and vehicles.
- **Business model:** Per-job and recurring cleaning bookings; owner keeps the spread between customer price and contractor payout.
- **Pricing:** Per-job first deep clean converting to recurring bookings; contractor paid a percentage of job value. average first-time clean USD 300-350 per first-time clean; example recurring customer value USD 200 per month Contractor gets 40-50% of the job (also stated as roughly 25-35% an hour, garbled); owner keeps the rest, e.g. $150-180 on a $300 job.
- **Acquisition:** google_business_profile_maps: Customers search 'house cleaning near me' and find the business through Google Business Profile and reviews.; paid_search: Local ads to capture existing search demand; ad cost is paid back from the job margin.
- **Delivery:** Independent contractor cleaners use their own equipment, vehicle and gas, and bear damage liability; owner handles marketing, bookings, scheduling and keeping cleaners accountable from a laptop, never entering the home.
- **Unit economics (claims):** 30-40% net margins; 30-35 clients gives about $10k/month revenue and $3-4k/month profit; about 1 in 3 one-time cleans converts to recurring; lifetime value per customer $2-4k; 10-15 hours per week
- **Tools:** Google Business Profile, Local ads, Laptop/phone scheduling system (unnamed)
- **Startup cost:** not stated
- **Time to first revenue:** Launch kit claims zero to first sale in 30 days; Luis took a year to a year and a half to reach $25k/month.
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Domestic cleaning exists and is fragmented in the UK, and Google Business Profile works the same. Changes: UK cleaners are often self-employed, but IR35/employment-status risk and HMRC worker-status rules, public liability insurance, DBS checks and GDPR need care; pay rates and prices differ in GBP; US stats (800,000 cleaners, $16/hr) don't apply.
- **Risks:** Worker misclassification risk if contractors are treated like staff; Contractor quality, no-shows and damage disputes; Owner doing cleans themselves and burning out; Ad cost may eat margin on one-off cleans; Results claimed are unverified and promotional
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 4/5 (Google Business Profile, reviews, SEO and paid ads are inbound channels that agents can run with occasional approval.); onboarding 4/5 (Online booking, quote and scheduling flows plus agent-sent confirmations can be automated.); delivery 1/5 (Physical cleaning is done by human contractors; agents can dispatch and chase them, but recruiting, vetting and quality complaints need a human.); billing 4/5 (Card payments, recurring invoices and contractor payouts can be automated via payment software.); support 3/5 (Agents can handle rescheduling and routine queries; damage, no-shows and quality disputes need a human.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Lead-gen plus subcontractor arbitrage** (operations)
1. Capture local demand through Google Business Profile, reviews and local ads
2. Sell a first-time deep clean at about $300-350
3. Dispatch to an independent cleaner who supplies own equipment and vehicle
4. Pay the cleaner 40-50% and keep the rest to cover ads and profit
5. Push first-time customers into weekly, biweekly or monthly recurring bookings
6. Never do a clean yourself
> "You're essentially just the one who books the job, sends someone else to do it, and then you keep the difference." — Johnny Robinson (~4:33)

**Recruit cleaners by offering better pay and no business hassle** (hiring)
1. Target cleaners who want to clean but not market or bid for jobs
2. Pitch higher effective pay than W-2 rates, steady work and no client hassle
3. Handle scheduling and customer communication yourself

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Choose boring, existing-demand services with recurring revenue rather than inventing new products; the unglamorous image reduces competition.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Prioritise recurring revenue so you are not restarting from zero every month.
- *scaling*: Run a service business as owner, focused on sales and management, not labour, so it scales without more of your hours.
- *customer_acquisition*: Target existing search demand so the job is getting in front of buyers rather than persuading them.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Using independent contractors who bear their own costs reduces payroll and management burden, but check employment-status rules locally.
- *validation_testing*: Treat headline income stories from course sellers as marketing; ask for the margin and ad-cost detail they omit.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Most failures come from the owner doing a cleaning themselves to save money, hating it and quitting; stay in the owner role.
- Results are individual, not typical, and the video is not financial advice.
