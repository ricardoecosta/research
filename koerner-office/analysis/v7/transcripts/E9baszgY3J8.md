## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Braden, a former territory sales manager from central Arkansas who started an estate sale company in 2017 with almost no capital. He explains the 35% commission model, three-day markdowns, lead sources (EstateSales.net, overflow from established companies, realtor meetings) and a warehouse model that combined up to 30 small estates into $20-30k weekend sales, peaking at $30-40k gross per month. The estate sale business became a funnel for off-market real estate: about 60 houses since 2018 via wholesaling, flips and mid-term rentals on Furnish Finder. Key takeaway: a low-capital, unsexy service that gives first access to distressed sellers can feed a much more profitable business.

### I1. Estate sale company

- **Customer:** Heirs of the deceased, divorcing couples, people in debt/foreclosure, downsizers (the 'four Ds'), often referred by realtors, elder-law attorneys and investors
- **Problem:** Families must empty a house fast so it can be sold
- **Offer:** Full-service on-site sale: price every item, market the sale, staff it, collect payments, send leftover to auction, pay client ~2 weeks later
- **Business model:** commission on gross sales (typically 35%, up to 45-50% for small homes) with optional minimum guarantee; marketing fees passed to client
- **Pricing:** percentage of gross. industry average commission  35 percent of gross; small homes  45-50 percent of gross; example minimum guarantee USD 2000 per sale Offers like 39% instead of 40% ('nine numbers'). Day 1 full price, day 2 20-25% off, day 3 50% off.
- **Acquisition:** inbound_listing_sites: Listed as a company on EstateSales.net; sales posted there and emailed weekly to buyers; posting fee $100-200.; partnerships: Worked for and got overflow/small leads from established estate sale companies.; networking_events: Brought breakfast/donuts to real estate brokerage meetings for a 10-minute pitch.; referrals_word_of_mouth: Real estate investors/flippers, elder-law attorneys and realtors refer clients.; paid_social: Facebook Marketplace posts and Facebook ads using keywords estate sales, estate liquidation, yard sales, garage sales.
- **Delivery:** Owner walks the house, estimates gross room by room, prices every item with a price gun or painter's tape, family/staff run cashier, floor and loading roles; leftovers go to auction (which takes 35%).
- **Unit economics (claims):** First sale grossed $5-6k with no cost; $10k gross sale nets about $2.5-3k after staff; First year ~$5k/month gross, year 2 $10k, year 3 $20-30k, peak $30-40k/month; Part-timer can make an extra $1-4k a month on weekends
- **Tools:** EstateSales.net, Facebook Marketplace, Facebook ads, Price gun, Cash register with lot numbers, Painter's tape, Costco folding tables
- **Startup cost:** Almost nothing: tape, price gun, phone/POS, borrowed tables (~$50 or $2 rental each)
- **Time to first revenue:** First week; first sale within two to three weeks
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — UK equivalent is 'house clearance' and on-site 'estate sales' are culturally rare; selling firearms requires licensing under UK firearms law; removing waste needs an Environment Agency waste carrier licence; probate timelines differ.
- **Risks:** Mispricing valuable items; Theft at sales; Neighbour/HOA conflict, parking; Clients disappointed with gross; Firearms and drugs found in houses
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 2/5 (Inbound via listing sites and referrals automates partly; in-person relationship building with realtors caps it.); onboarding 1/5 (In-home walkthrough and contract.); delivery 0/5 (Physical pricing, staffing and selling on site.); billing 3/5 (POS and reconciliation software can automate.); support 1/5 (Human client handling.)

### I2. Multi-estate warehouse sales

- **Customer:** Small estates other companies turn down; clients with HOA or timing limits
- **Problem:** Small estates ($1-2k of items) are unprofitable as individual sales
- **Offer:** Pickup or drop-off of items to a warehouse, combined weekend sale with cross-pollinated buyers; leftovers from house sales re-sold here at lower prices
- **Business model:** commission on each estate's lot plus buyouts
- **Pricing:** percentage of each lot.  Clients wait longer to be paid but houses empty faster.
- **Acquisition:** paid_social: Facebook/social ads as a flyer with address/date, no specific items.; partnerships: Leads that other estate sale companies don't want.
- **Delivery:** Mon-Thu pricing lots, each estate tagged with a lot number; cash register supports up to 50 lots; spreadsheet reconciliation per family.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Warehouse sales of $20-30k per weekend with ~30 estates; Warehouse rented for about $400 a weekend; One sale every 3-4 weeks
- **Tools:** Cash register with lot numbers, Spreadsheet, Facebook ads
- **Startup cost:** Warehouse rental (his was ~$400/weekend from a relative)
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — House clearance and auction rooms exist in the UK; warehouse sales plausible with business rates and waste licensing.
- **Risks:** Item-to-buyer ratio dilution vs in-home sales; No air conditioning, lower perceived value; Warehouse costs at market rent
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 2/5 (Inbound and partner referrals.); onboarding 1/5 (Logistics of pickups.); delivery 0/5 (Physical pricing and selling.); billing 3/5 (Lot-based reconciliation can be automated.); support 1/5 (Human.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Work for the incumbent to learn and get overflow leads** (acquisition)
1. Build a relationship by shopping at the best local company's sales
2. Ask to work for them (offer to volunteer)
3. Learn staffing, pricing and paperwork
4. Ask them to send small leads they don't want
5. Become the go-to 'XYZ company' they refer
> "would you consider letting me work for you for a while? And or actually I volunteered to volunteer" — Braden (~12:32)

**Donut pitch at real estate brokerages** (acquisition)
1. Offer to bring breakfast/coffee/donuts to a brokerage's regular broker meeting
2. Get 10 minutes to explain the service
3. Repeat across brokerages
> "nine times out of 10, those brokerages were like, "Sure."" — Braden (~17:56)

**Room-by-room gross estimate and offer** (pricing)
1. Walk the house with the client taking pictures
2. Walk again alone, estimating each room's gross in a notebook
3. If gross exceeds ~$10k and house is clean, make an offer
4. Quote 39% instead of 40% ('nine numbers')
5. Set a minimum guarantee when gross is uncertain
> "We'll offer them, you know, 39% to make it sound a little sexier than 40%" — Braden (~31:00)

**Three-day markdown structure** (pricing)
1. Day 1 full price
2. Day 2 20-25% off
3. Day 3 50% off
4. For two-day sales: 25% off 9-noon, 50% off noon-3
5. Price so the day-3 50% price is still acceptable
> "the first day is full price. Second day is typically 20 to 25% off if if there's any items left. And then the remaining items are 50% off on Sunday" — Braden (~14:55)

**Bid sheet to stop haggling** (sales)
1. When a shopper asks for a discount, hand them a bid sheet
2. They write their offer and contact details
3. Call the winning bidder at end of the sale if the item remains
4. Use the captured contacts for a mailing list
> "The remedy to that, which I love, is a bid sheet" — Braden (~61:42)

**Lot-number cash register reconciliation** (operations)
1. Assign each estate a lot number
2. Write lot number on every tag
3. Ring up price under the lot on a multi-department register
4. Reconcile per lot and per payment type in a spreadsheet
5. Pay clients about two weeks after the sale
> "The customer still pays the same price, but your your cash register separates everything out" — Braden (~48:54)

**Capture buyer emails and sell VIP early access** (acquisition)
1. Collect emails and phone numbers at sales
2. Email upcoming sale announcements
3. Offer paid ($50) night-before early access
> "for 50 bucks you can you can come to the night before estate sale and have first dibs" — Braden (~63:14)

**Trade estate sale commission for the house listing/purchase** (acquisition)
1. Offer a lower commission (25% or free) if you can list or buy the house
2. Make cash offers for house plus contents when contents are too small for a sale
3. Ask other estate sale companies to refer houses needing work
> "if you let me list the house, I can do the estate sale for instead of 35%, I can do it for 25%" — Braden (~52:32)

**Use AI to spot valuable items for marketing** (acquisition)
1. Photograph everything in the house
2. Upload to Claude or ChatGPT and ask what is valuable or click-worthy
3. Feature niche/collectible items in listing descriptions to attract specific buyer personas
> "today you just take pictures of everything, upload it to Claude or Chad GPT and say, I'm totally ignorant to this" — Chris Koerner (~38:06)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Life transitions (death, divorce, moves) are when people spend money; build offers around them.
- *finance_unit_economics*: A service paid out of gross sales collected by you gives positive cash conversion and needs no capital.
- *customer_acquisition*: Competitors are often happy to refer overflow; become their trusted alternative.
- *customer_acquisition*: Surface area: each unique item is a separate marketing angle that pulls in a different buyer persona.
- *scaling*: A low-margin service can be a front-door lead source for a far more profitable business.
- *pricing*: Price like occupancy: selling 95%+ on day one means you underpriced.
- *pricing*: Items sell for more in context (staged in a nice home) than in a warehouse.
- *mindset_execution*: Analog, unsexy, hard businesses stay uncrowded because most people take the path of least resistance.
- *validation_testing*: Test the market before spending on renovation; listing as-is can work.
- *validation_testing*: Low-commitment businesses let you quit cheaply compared to leases and restaurants.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Mispricing a valuable item is every liquidator's nightmare; consult other companies or appraisers.
- Allowing haggling once means doing it a thousand times as others overhear.
- Bad pictures, low-quality items or weather can drop attendance to 30-50 people a day.
- Interest rate rises blindsided his buy-and-hold real estate.
- Going full throttle into real estate and leaving estate sales was a mistake in hindsight.
- Don't promise clients a line-by-line list of sold items; only for high-value items.
- Guns, drugs, cash and IDs turn up in houses; regulations vary (UK firearms law is strict; extractor note).
- Wholesaling requires constantly refilling the deal funnel.
