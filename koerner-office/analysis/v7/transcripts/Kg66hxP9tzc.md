## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Alan Doan of the Missouri Star Quilt Company about turning Hamilton, Missouri (pop. 1,500) into a quilting destination with 16 quilt shops, restaurants, a retreat hotel, an e-commerce site and YouTube tutorials. Growth came from free YouTube content, a newsletter, a daily-deal site and a bi-monthly magazine that repurposes tutorials. He then pitches how others could build niche 'destination towns' (sourdough, fish, cheese), and riffs on side ideas: a home-services membership, a home-maintenance subscription, unstaffed small-town bodegas, small-town micro apartment roll-ups, and a tiny-town Airbnb network. Key takeaway: build an audience and a company first, then add physical experiences in undervalued small-town real estate.

### I1. Missouri Star Quilt Company / quilting destination town

- **Customer:** Quilters, largely older women (his mom's demographic)
- **Problem:** Quilters were ignored by retail; fabric is hard to sell online without touch and feel; a small town had no reason to visit.
- **Offer:** Online fabric/quilting store plus themed physical shops, retreat hotel (sleeps 40), restaurants and tutorials; 'Disneyland for quilters'.
- **Business model:** E-commerce product sales plus retail, hotel retreats, restaurants, and tourism
- **Pricing:** Product sales; loss-leader deals; retreat rentals. intended price of crazy eights charm pack (accidentally listed at 0.88) USD 3.88 per charm pack Fabric shop prices otherwise not stated.
- **Acquisition:** youtube: Weekly quilting tutorial videos starting about 2008-2009, posted Fridays, filmed with his mom; drove traffic and built the list.; existing_audience: Weekly newsletter with about 60-70% open rates, always leading with free valuable content.; other: Press coverage (WSJ, CBS Sunday Morning, NBC Nightly News, NPR) from the unusual quilt-town story; claims a couple billion media impressions.; other: Early comment spamming on blogs and Craigslist/Kijiji got him blocked; did not work.
- **Delivery:** Online orders fulfilled from retail shop buildings in Hamilton (later also a warehouse built with an SBA loan); about 12+ employees cutting and shipping; physical themed shops, retreat hotel, restaurants, staff run.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Charm pack loss leader: cost about $4 plus $2 shipping, sold at 0.88 plus $5 shipping, most buyers added items; Buildings bought for about $20k each, $100k+ to restore, roughly $200-300k all in each; About 150,000 visitors a year, claimed 98% due to Missouri Star; Company reached about $20M a year at one inflection point
- **Tools:** Shopify, YouTube, Windows Movie Maker, Canon digital camera, SBA loan
- **Startup cost:** Started with one small quilt shop; later buildings about $20k purchase plus restoration. Initial cost not stated.
- **Time to first revenue:** About three weeks to first sale on the daily-deal site (from his cousin); slow growth after.
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — Online craft retail works in the UK; cheap small-town buildings and tourism draw are more US-specific. UK has a strong quilting and craft scene but property costs and planning differ.
- **Risks:** Heavy capital and staffing for physical buildings; Local government resistance; Processes break at employee-count inflection points; Fabric is hard to sell online without touch
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Video and newsletter content can be heavily AI-assisted, but authentic personality-led content and PR needs human presence.); onboarding 4/5 (E-commerce checkout is automated.); delivery 0/5 (Cutting fabric, picking, packing, hotel and shop operations are physical labour.); billing 5/5 (Shopify payments.); support 3/5 (Standard e-commerce support can be agent-handled; hotel/retreat guests need humans.)
> "we've created a Disneyland for quilters and that's what we've turned Hamilton into now through our work at the Missouri Star Quilt Company." — Alan Doan (~2:46)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build audience with free niche content first** (acquisition)
1. Pick an ignored demographic
2. Film weekly tutorials with a simple camera
3. Post on a fixed weekday (Fridays)
4. Send a weekly newsletter leading with free value
5. Use the list to sell product
> "We'd just upload like five of them in a day and then a ton of people would come to the site and then it would all die down." — Alan Doan (~11:08)

**Repurpose content into a paid ad-free magazine** (product)
1. Take the last 10 tutorials
2. Add featured product collections
3. Print bi-monthly at about $6 per issue with no ad sales team
4. Sell to existing audience as subscription
5. Use it to surface new products

**Loss-leader pricing** (pricing)
1. Sell a staple item below cost with shipping charged
2. Rely on customers adding other products to the order

**Manufacture energy and visible activity** (operations)
1. Fulfil online orders from the visible retail location so visitors see busy staff
2. Create the impression of momentum

**Cluster 2-3 themed shops to own a niche title** (acquisition)
1. Build a company and audience first
2. Buy 2-3 cheap undervalued buildings
3. Theme each (supplies, tools, finished goods)
4. Put up a 'capital of' billboard
5. Run classes and document the build as content
6. Hedge with online sales

**Re-engineer processes at headcount inflection points** (operations)
1. Expect breakage around 10, 28, 75 employees
2. Voluntarily declare processes broken
3. Redesign for the new headcount

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Serve an underserved, ignored demographic with real attention and content; loyalty and word of mouth follow.
- *customer_acquisition*: Content and audience come before physical build-outs; destination ventures need an existing company and audience.
- *validation_testing*: A price mistake revealed that loss leaders work, a reminder to test aggressive offers.
- *offer_design*: Repurposing existing content into a new product format is high margin with no extra audience cost.
- *operations*: Processes break predictably at headcount thresholds; rebuild them deliberately before they fail.
- *operations*: Don't DIY work outside your expertise when pros cost relatively little.
- *customer_acquisition*: Unusual stories earn free press; media wants novelty.
- *mindset_execution*: You don't need permission to build; act despite local obstruction.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Cheap, ignored real estate in low-value towns can be leveraged with long-term financing, but it is capital heavy and not agent-runnable.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Commenting on blogs and posting on Craigslist/Kijiji to promote the site got him banned as spam.
- DIY-ing specialist jobs (pallet racking, network cabling) turned the warehouse build into a disaster; paying pros was better value.
- Building a town without an audience fails (Humboldt, Kansas).
- Local government can be hostile to a successful business investing in town.
- Wrote off the Waco visitor figure as unverified hearsay.
