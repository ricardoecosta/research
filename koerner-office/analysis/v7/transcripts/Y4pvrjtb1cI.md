## Tactics and playbook steps

**Choose a mailing area by household fit** (acquisition)
1. Enter a target ZIP code in the USPS EDDM tool.
2. Sort routes by higher income when the advertisers are home-service businesses that need single-family homeowners.
3. Match advertisers to the area they serve and avoid routes dominated by apartments or condos if those households are a poor fit.
> "And usually I'll sort by higher income, just 'cause they're mostly single-family homes" — Josh (~3:24)

**Pre-sell enough space to cover the mailing** (finance)
1. Calculate print and postage costs before promising a mailing.
2. Treat the front half of the card as the break-even target for those costs.
3. Treat additional filled space, including the back, as potential revenue above break-even.
> "So it's like I gotta fill the front half of my card to cover printing and postage." — Josh (~3:50)

**Post a short, specific offer in local business groups** (acquisition)
1. Describe the area, approximate household reach, and category exclusivity.
2. Say that there is one business per category and spaces are first come, first served.
3. Post about once a week in relevant business networking groups.
4. Keep the post short enough that the key offer appears without making readers click to expand it.
> "I've put it together, a little co-op of local businesses as a way to work together to reduce crazy marketing costs." — Josh (~28:05)

**Use early engagement to increase group-post visibility** (acquisition)
1. Post at a time Josh says local business owners are likely to be checking Facebook.
2. Like the post and add an invitation to send a direct message.
3. Have the business page comment, then reply from the personal account to create early engagement.
> "So now I have proof, it's social proof, you're engineering your own social proof." — Josh (~30:28)

**Target businesses already advertising** (acquisition)
1. Look for yard signs and for businesses actively posting in local business groups.
2. Contact businesses that already spend effort promoting themselves.
3. Explain the card and offer details; Josh says one early sale followed roughly five messages.
> "I was like, well, he's already open to getting his business name out there and he's spending this time posting." — Josh (~32:13)

**Qualify advertisers for the audience** (sales)
1. Prioritize home services and local brick-and-mortar businesses that serve the mailing area.
2. Tell businesses when the audience is a poor fit, such as B2B firms advertising to homeowners.
3. Reserve one slot per business category to avoid placing direct competitors together.
> "So I will turn away businesses or like, I get some B2B type businesses and I'm just like, look, this is going to homeowners." — Josh (~29:01)

**Offer design and response tracking as part of the sale** (offer_design)
1. Design each advertiser's ad and provide revisions.
2. Help the advertiser develop a stronger, simpler offer.
3. Add a dynamic QR code and track scans.
4. Encourage a unique offer or phone number so the advertiser can identify responses that do not use the QR code.
> "So I design all of the ads for free for the businesses. It's part of the purchase price at all throw a dynamic QR code on there for them to keep track of scans." — Josh (~15:46)

**Check print quality and postal preparation** (operations)
1. Ask a new printer for a sample before ordering a large run.
2. Confirm card stock and colour quality.
3. Arrange for cards to be bundled in hundreds and labelled correctly for USPS acceptance.

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Validate that customers will pay before spending heavily on branding or a polished website.
- *offer_design*: A strong, simple offer can make a local promotion easier to redeem and easier to measure.
- *legal_risk*: Sell the outcome you can control, such as exposure, and be clear when you cannot promise a customer's return.
- *niche_selection*: Choose channels and audiences that fit the customer's service area and customer type.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use early customer outcomes as sales evidence, but follow up consistently so useful results are not lost.
- *pricing*: Test pricing against demand and local purchasing power rather than assuming one rate works everywhere.
- *customer_acquisition*: Look for customers already trying to promote themselves; they are more likely to understand a marketing offer.
- *scaling*: A product that already reaches a customer can become a channel for learning about and selling adjacent services.
- *validation_testing*: Use repeat direct-mail pieces when the customer benefits from repeated exposure, and test routes and offers over time.
- *customer_acquisition*: For B2B customer acquisition, Chris describes building a defined prospect list and using a sequence of direct-mail pieces rather than relying on a single touch.
- *mindset_execution*: A business can be started as a side project and grow beyond the founder's original goal, but the transcript does not establish that this outcome is typical.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The guest says he sells exposure and does not guarantee advertiser ROI; some businesses see results while others report none.
- Response measurement can miss customers who call instead of scanning a QR code; unique offers or numbers can help attribution.
- A weak or unsuitable offer can reduce response, and the advertiser's fit with the household audience matters.
- Get a print sample before ordering thousands of cards from an unfamiliar supplier.
- Cards must be bundled and labelled correctly for USPS acceptance.
- Do not mail to an audience that is a poor fit for the advertisers; the guest gives apartments and condos as examples for some home services.
- Josh says he initially spent time worrying about a polished website and logo instead of validating whether the business could make money.
- The transcript's casual statement that Josh began without an LLC is not UK legal or tax guidance; UK business structure and obligations need separate assessment.
- UK operators must account for PECR before using unsolicited email, SMS, WhatsApp, or social DMs to market to individuals and sole traders, and must screen cold calls against TPS/CTPS.
- Facebook group posting and staged early engagement depend on group rules and platform behaviour; excessive posting can look like spam.
- The transcript's $9.99 space figure is unclear and may be a transcription error; confirm the intended price before relying on it.
