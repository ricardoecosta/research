## Tactics and playbook steps

**Test demand by listing a consumer item on Marketplace** (validation_testing)
1. Buy a cheap consumer version (~$300 inflatable screen)
2. List it on Facebook Marketplace to see if anyone books
3. Take the first booking even if unprepared
4. Reinvest in commercial-grade gear once demand is proven
> "If you are prepared, you've waited too long" — Chris Koerner (~2:57)

**Package-based pricing** (pricing)
1. Offer a base package (screen + speakers, $375)
2. Add tiers by chair count and type (6, 10, 20; cinema or LED)
3. Top tier at $1,500
4. Most customers pick a package rather than a la carte
> "people just typically nine times out of 10 they're going to choose a package" — Derek (~7:01)

**Competitor research by attending events** (product)
1. Attend school movie events run by others
2. Note what they do and how to improve
3. Add upgrades (themed/LED chairs, coming attractions, post-movie music)

**Spot the next upsell by watching customers** (product)
1. Notice kids dancing to music after the film
2. Source an LED dance floor
3. Buy triple power kit so you can run three bookings a day
> "how can I monetize these kids dancing" — Derek (~15:29)

**Ask out-of-state operators for advice** (operations)
1. Contact operators in other states (not local competitors)
2. Be transparent about what you're doing
3. Ask a couple of questions
> "typically I'll reach out to people out of state cuz someone local might be like, I don't want you infringing on my business" — Derek (~19:46)

**Negotiate with overseas manufacturers by asking what to ask** (operations)
1. Spend time with the manufacturer to get the best price
2. Ask them which questions you should be asking
3. Plan for lead times of several months
> "give me the questions that I don't know to ask you" — Derek (~15:56)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Start before you're ready; the first messy job teaches what to buy.
- *offer_design*: Solve a real constraint (late sunsets) to create a category with no competitors.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Equipment rentals have high margins once gear is paid off; the main cost is your time.
- *pricing*: Customers who haggle aren't the target for premium offers; affluent clients on acreage pay upfront.
- *pricing*: Bundle add-ons into packages to raise average order value.
- *offer_design*: Watch how customers behave at your service to find adjacent upsells.
- *operations*: Obsess over the experience details (coming attractions, cold AC, hidden wiring).

## Warnings and mistakes

- Consumer-grade inflatables fail in wind; his first event flopped and he had to comp it.
- Cheapest manufacturer is not always best long term; research suppliers.
- No repeatable lead system: reliance on Facebook Marketplace and referrals.
- LED floors' music sync is poor unless the speaker is close.
