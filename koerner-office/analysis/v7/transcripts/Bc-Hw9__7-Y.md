## Summary

The Dark Horse Entrepreneur host argues that AI makes building apps fast, but unvalidated apps still don't sell. Copying proven apps proves a market, not that anyone will switch to yours. He gives a five-step test: a micro-problem filter, a proof checklist of evidence that costs someone something, 'switch conversations' with 5-10 real users about past behaviour, a 'smallest paid promise' tested by pre-sale, manual delivery or a specific waitlist, and a build-or-don't-build decision against a pass mark written in advance. The worked example is a $5/month game-day confirmation text tool for youth cheer coaches.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Micro-problem filter (pass 4 of 5)** (validation)
1. Who exactly has it (job title/situation)?
2. How often (weekly or better)?
3. What does it cost them (time, money, embarrassment, missed sales)?
4. Are they already paying to deal with it?
5. Can one feature fix the core?
6. Fill 'I want to build an app that does X to solve Y for Z'

**Proof checklist (get 3+)** (validation)
1. Paid competitors exist (app stores, Product Hunt) with many reviews
2. People complaining in their own words on Reddit, Facebook groups, forums
3. 1-star and 3-star reviews of competitors
4. Searches for '<competitor> alternative'
5. Homemade workarounds (spreadsheets, templates)
6. Copy exact quotes into a doc for sales copy
> "One-star reviews tell you what people hate. Three-star reviews are going to tell you what they liked" — Dark Horse Entrepreneur host (~9:24)

**Switch conversation** (validation)
1. Find 5-10 persona matches where complaints live; ask questions, don't pitch
2. Ask about the past: walk me through the last time
3. What do you use now? What do you hate? What have you tried? What would make you switch?
4. Never ask 'would you use my app?'
5. Listen for heat and existing spend
> "Please never ask that. Everyone is going to say yes" — Dark Horse Entrepreneur host (~13:09)

**Smallest paid promise** (validation)
1. One sentence: I help [person] do [one thing] without [pain] for [price]
2. Test via Stripe pre-sale page with ship date and full refund guarantee, do-it-by-hand service, or specific founding-price waitlist
3. Write the pass mark first (e.g. 3 strangers pay within 2 weeks)
4. Build if hit; else change one variable (price, person, feature) and retest
> "If three coaches that I do not personally know pay or commit within the next 2 weeks, I'm going to build" — Dark Horse Entrepreneur host (~16:40)

**15-minute review mining** (validation)
1. Pick the biggest competitor
2. Read 20 one-star and three-star reviews
3. Copy lines that make you think 'I can fix that'

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Validate demand before building; code is cheap but your evenings aren't.
- *niche_selection*: Copying proven apps is fine if you have a specific reason customers would switch.
- *validation_testing*: Evidence must cost someone something: money, time, workarounds, complaints.
- *validation_testing*: Ask about past behaviour, not future intentions.
- *validation_testing*: Pre-sell with refunds, or deliver by hand, before building.
- *mindset_execution*: Set the pass mark before testing to avoid moving goalposts.
- *offer_design*: Micro apps should solve one weekly-or-more pain with one feature.
- *mindset_execution*: Walking away after a failed two-week test is a win, not failure.

## Warnings and mistakes

- AI makes building fast but doesn't make unwanted apps sell.
- A proven market doesn't prove anyone will buy from you; you need a reason to switch.
- Zero competitors and zero complaints usually means an empty road for a reason.
- Don't buy upvotes or game communities; it burns reputation with your customers.
- YouTubers' income claims are their story, not your forecast.
- Don't let AI pretend to be a customer.
