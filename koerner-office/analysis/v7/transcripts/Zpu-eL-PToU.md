## Tactics and playbook steps

**Define a narrow ideal customer profile** (customer_acquisition)
1. Specify a narrow company type and size rather than saying the service is for anyone.
2. Add relevant qualifiers such as employee count, outbound sales team, and software used.
3. Identify the role of the owner or manager who can make the decision.
> "I sell to SAS companies that have 50 to 500 employees that have outbound sales teams that use XYZ software." — Will Cannon (~13:49)

**Start with a small, relevant prospect list** (customer_acquisition)
1. Build a list of 100–200 people to begin with rather than trying to send tens of thousands of emails.
2. Filter prospects by the defined customer profile and relevant buying signals.
3. Prioritize the right person, message, and timing.
> "You can build a list of 100, 200 people to start with and if you send emails at the right time with the right message." — Will Cannon (~23:08)

**Use a multi-email sequence and test its length** (customer_acquisition)
1. Create a sequence rather than sending only one email.
2. Try short early gaps, such as three to five days after the first email and two to three days after that.
3. Space later emails farther apart; the guest says the sequence may eventually move to weekly.
4. Test a single email against a multi-touch campaign with comparable prospect groups.
5. Use the sequence length that performs better for the particular segment.

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Define the ideal customer narrowly enough to identify the company type, size, sales setup, and relevant software.
- *validation_testing*: Test a multi-email sequence against a single email using comparable groups; do not assume the best follow-up length.
- *customer_acquisition*: A follow-up sequence can produce more revenue than the first email, so plan follow-ups rather than treating outreach as a one-message task.
- *offer_design*: Keep cold emails short, plain, relevant, and clear about how the sender found the prospect and what value is offered.
- *offer_design*: A low-friction sample, trial, or free leads can give a prospect a way to assess the product before subscribing.
- *customer_acquisition*: Target a segment that is not heavily contacted and make the message specific to a real relationship or buying context.
- *scaling*: A channel that works today can fail after platform or market changes; build additional channels over time.
- *validation_testing*: A free trial is more persuasive when it reaches a buyer who is already looking for the service and has a clear way to judge the result.
- *operations*: High email volume does not replace a relevant audience, a useful offer, and deliverability controls.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI as a way to do work, but recognize that business owners may still pay someone to complete a job for them.
- *customer_acquisition*: A lead magnet can build an email list, but make the exchange clear: Will says people provide their email to download his free guides.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not rely on a single acquisition channel. Will says a Google algorithm update once caused revenue to disappear overnight and recommends building additional channels.
- High-volume cold email can harm deliverability if domains are not warmed up and monitored; requirements change over time.
- Long or generic follow-ups may not be read, and recipients may regard repeated outreach as unwanted.
- The fake publisher email uses fabricated identities and a false going-out-of-business story; the speakers explicitly identify it as fake and discuss it as an unethical tactic.
