## Tactics and playbook steps

**Personalised priced flyer** (acquisition)
1. Make a flyer with a simple logo, your face photo and the services offered.
2. Leave blank lines at the bottom.
3. Walk the neighbourhood, assess each fence, deck or gutter, and write a specific price and a short note.
4. Put it on the front door and invite the customer to text you to book for the weekend.
> "I would write there, "Hey, my name's Tim. I was looking at your fence. I could restain it for you for $500." — Tim Richard (~2:06)

**Leave flyers at pet businesses** (acquisition)
1. Bring a box of donuts and a stack of flyers to doggy daycares and vet centres.
2. Ask to leave flyers on the counter, where every visitor has a pet.

**Walk-in quote for visible problems** (sales)
1. Drive around and spot graffiti on commercial buildings.
2. Walk in and offer removal at a set price, e.g. $200.
3. Leave a card for future incidents.
4. Also offer the city a standing arrangement.

**Grow organically from own and friends' work** (acquisition)
1. Start by doing the service on your own items.
2. Do it for a few friends.
3. Post on the local Facebook community page.
4. Raise prices as demand exceeds capacity.

**Target mid-sized commercial properties** (sales)
1. Choose medium strip malls with 5-10 businesses, not too small or too large.
2. Call owners at volume.
3. Work after hours alongside a day job.

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: A personalised, priced offer for a specific visible job (a named price for this fence or gutter) lowers friction compared with a generic flyer.
- *offer_design*: Recurring-service models (weekly pickup, scooping, parking lots) need fewer customers to reach solid revenue.
- *niche_selection*: Boring, low-skill local services with low competition can be started with minimal capital.
- *mindset_execution*: Selling to commercial property owners and working off-hours allows starting alongside a full-time job.
- *customer_acquisition*: Low-competition local keywords like couch cleaning give a long-term SEO route after initial grassroots outreach.
- *pricing*: Raise prices when demand exceeds capacity, as the photo organiser did.
- *validation_testing*: Most income figures here are second-hand anecdotes and should be validated locally before relying on them.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Starting one of these businesses doesn't guarantee income or the results of the examples; do your own research.
