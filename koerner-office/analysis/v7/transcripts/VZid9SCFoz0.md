## Summary

Chris Koerner and Sam Thompson brainstorm business ideas enabled by ChatGPT's new image generation. They argue standalone novelty products (custom coloring books, print-on-demand art) are real but short-lived, and that the bigger opportunity is selling AI-generated personalised before/after mockups to home-service businesses (landscaping, painting, tree trimming, pool, remodel) for direct-mail and sales use. The pitch is a ~$500/month mockup tool or service sold to local contractors, found via Valpak mailers, plus tracked-number revenue-share deals. Key takeaway: the best marketer and channel wins, and the B2B application is less obvious than consumer novelty products. All numbers are speculative and untested.

### I1. AI before/after mockup service for landscaping and home-service companies

- **Customer:** Local landscaping, painting, pool, tree-trimming, remodel and home-builder companies, especially those already spending on direct mail.
- **Problem:** Homeowners cannot visualise the end result and are not designers, so friction stops them spending money; contractors' generic mailers have low response rates.
- **Offer:** Unlimited custom AI mockups, graphics and quotes for each prospect, integrated into the contractor's CRM or via a simple form (upload photo, budget, location, wants like pool or koi pond); or personalised postcards showing the prospect's own house.
- **Business model:** Monthly subscription (~$500/month per contractor), or a percentage of jobs won via tracked phone number, or done-for-you mailers per postcard.
- **Pricing:** Flat monthly subscription, or revenue share on jobs, or per-postcard. mockup tool per landscaping company USD 500 per month; hypothetical pitch for personalised postcards USD 5 per postcard; consumer-facing version of Sam's trip planner style tool USD 20 one-off Revenue share via tracked phone number as an alternative to fixed fee.
- **Acquisition:** cold_call: Find local businesses already buying direct mail via Valpak (valpac.com zip code lookup), then phone the owner, whose number is probably his cell, and show a mockup of his own yard.; cold_sms_dm: Text the owner a mockup of his own front yard, found using deep research for his address.; direct_mail_postcards: Personalised postcards with the prospect's house mockup, sent to homeowners to drive calls to the contractor.; door_to_door_in_person: Contractor sales reps generate a mockup live at the door in about five minutes.
- **Delivery:** AI image generation (ChatGPT/Sora) from Google Earth or Zillow photos produces mockups; direct-mail APIs and tools such as Banner Bear produce and send postcards; optional chat query to estimate job cost and home value uplift. Human sales closes the physical job.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Direct mail conversion could rise from 0.5% to 5% with targeted mockups; Pool example: comps listed at 830k vs 700k; Garage conversion example: cost 24k, value 323k to 428k
- **Tools:** ChatGPT image generation, Sora, Google Earth API, Zillow, Valpak, Banner Bear, direct mail API, tracked phone number, deep research
- **Startup cost:** Low; mainly image generation credits and postage (not stated precisely).
- **Time to first revenue:** Claimed testable same day with a mock mailer; not otherwise stated.
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — Concept works in the UK, but Valpak, Zillow and Google Earth workflows are US-centric; UK needs Rightmove/OS data, Royal Mail Door Drop and compliance with GDPR/PECR for addressed mail; cold texting owners is restricted.
- **Risks:** Image realism and accuracy of cost or value estimates could mislead customers; Fear-based fake damage mockups (fallen tree) are ethically and legally questionable; Contractor adoption is slow (host guesses ~10 years to reach 10% of landscapers); GDPR/PECR and TPS rules in the UK; Everyone will copy once API is available
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 2/5 (Valpak lookup, deep research and mockup generation are scriptable, but cold calls/texts to sole-trader owners face UK PECR/TPS limits and still need human sales.); onboarding 3/5 (A simple intake form (photo, budget, location) can be automated; CRM integration needs setup.); delivery 3/5 (Image generation, mailer creation and cost estimates are largely agent-driven; accuracy checks needed and contractor does physical work.); billing 4/5 (Stripe subscriptions are routine.); support 3/5 (Chat or email support is agent-handled; edge cases need a human.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Find contractors already buying direct mail via Valpak** (acquisition)
1. Go to valpac.com and enter a zip code to see local advertisers
2. Shortlist businesses already spending on direct mail
3. Phone the owner and ask why they are wasting money on Valpak
4. Send a personalised mockup of the owner's own house or yard as proof
5. Offer to do it for all their customers, priced as a fee or a percentage of tracked jobs
> "you can find the local businesses that already find direct mail effective. They're already spending on it. That's your lowest hanging fruit." — Chris Koerner (~30:39)

**Test the mailer before having a client** (acquisition)
1. Act as if you run a home remodeling company
2. Send AI mockup mailers to a target neighbourhood
3. Count the leads you get
4. Take the data to a real remodeler and offer to repeat it for them

**Filter then personalise to raise direct-mail ROI** (acquisition)
1. Scrape house images for a city
2. Use AI to remove homes that do not fit (for example no trees in the front yard)
3. Generate improved versions for the remaining ones
4. Mail only the filtered set, for example about 100 of 10,000 homes

**Put the maths on the picture** (sales)
1. Pull the home's current value from Zillow
2. Add estimated value after the project and the project cost
3. Print the profit on the mockup
4. Close with a clear call to action

**Test niches with one general store** (validation)
1. Run one Shopify store with many products and test ads
2. Find the winning market (for example weddings)
3. Double down on it after testing about eight things

**Brainstorm from Sora's most-liked feed** (product)
1. Open Sora and sort by most liked
2. Read the prompt behind each design
3. Pick a simple wrapper idea based on one prompt

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: When a technology lowers the barrier to entry, the best marketer who finds the right channel, angle and price first wins.
- *pricing*: Test price points: conversion rate times price decides which version earns more.
- *niche_selection*: Selling B2B applications of new tools to existing businesses is likely more durable than novelty consumer products.
- *offer_design*: Remove customer friction by showing the end state; people don't know what they want or can afford until they see it.
- *validation_testing*: Validate cheaply before approaching a client by running a small test campaign and bringing results to the business.
- *automation_ai*: Use the business's real cost data (hourly rates, distances) with AI to get accurate quotes.
- *customer_acquisition*: Target businesses already spending on a channel as the lowest-hanging fruit.
- *mindset_execution*: Prompting ChatGPT with memory about yourself for self-reflection can give language to define your strengths and weaknesses.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Standalone AI image products will be short-lived as awareness spreads and anyone can do it free in ChatGPT; the window is roughly 3-4 years.
- Adoption by contractors is slow; the host estimates about 10 years before 10% of landscapers use this in marketing.
- Fake damage imagery plays on fear and vanity, which is ethically questionable.
