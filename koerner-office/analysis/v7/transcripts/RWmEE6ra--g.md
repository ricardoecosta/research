## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate by speaking with potential customers before building** (validation_testing)
1. Choose a specific customer group and a broad problem area.
2. Run a simple ad that invites people to schedule a call.
3. Ask what they are struggling with and use probing questions tied to the area you can serve.
4. Use the answers to decide what to sell, rather than assuming the first offer is correct.
> "get on sales calls with nothing to sell just ask them what they're struggling with they just tell you the problem" — Chris Koerner (~0:27)

**Use ad results to refine the promise** (customer_acquisition)
1. Start with a broad message connected to a clear customer problem.
2. Book calls and listen for the outcome customers ask for most.
3. Update the ad and landing page to reflect that outcome.
4. Compare the cost per booked call before and after the change.
> "So then he changed his ads to speak to that of like we'll help you rank on Google Maps better" — Chris Koerner (~4:26)

**Use discovery calls to set an initial price** (pricing)
1. Know the minimum price that covers software and delivery time.
2. Ask the prospect what the outcome is worth to them and what they can afford.
3. Treat early price conversations as customer research.
4. Record differences between customer types and test price points over more calls.
> "you can AB test pricing live on the phone as long as you know what your floor is especially early" — Chris Koerner (~9:09)

**Use customer language to explain the value** (sales)
1. Ask what one customer lead or job is worth in profit.
2. Estimate the value of the additional leads the service could generate.
3. Compare that value with the proposed service fee.
4. State a guarantee only if you can honour it and the terms are clear.
> "so are you telling me that every call you get organically from Google is worth $60 is that accurate" — Chris Koerner (~14:12)

**Get a first customer who does not know you** (validation_testing)
1. Use friends and family if they help you learn how to sell and deliver.
2. Then test whether someone without a personal connection will pay.
3. Treat a stranger's purchase and feedback as a stronger test of demand than warm support.
> "every business that's going to reach a meaningful size comes from getting people to pay you that do not know you" — Chris Koerner (~6:47)

**Treat a first product offer as a test** (product)
1. Check whether pack size and shipping cost work before scaling inventory.
2. Test a starter pack and bundle discount.
3. Track whether buyers repeat orders and whether the bundle raises average order value.
4. Resolve supply-chain questions before committing to a new liquid product.
> "I think I did my math wrong on the size of the bag that I needed and so I'm going to change my top offunnel offer to sample pack" — Sam Thompson (~23:26)

**Use AI video to test creative concepts before hiring a creator** (operations)
1. Generate several short versions with different hooks, characters, or scripts.
2. Run the versions and identify which hook performs best.
3. Invest in a human creator for the stronger concept.
4. Check the final video for inaccurate text, odd background details, and character inconsistencies.
> "what we end up doing is we run those and then we say Oh this hook is our highest performer let's go have like an actual UGC creator" — Sam Thompson (~39:29)

**Structure a video prompt in layers** (operations)
1. Describe the setting and time of day.
2. Specify the visual style and camera behaviour.
3. Define each character's appearance and personality.
4. Write the dialogue and describe how the characters interact.
5. Remove quotation marks or platform names if they cause unwanted subtitles.
> "most of the time I start with it's like setting right and so like a busy sidewalk in a urban neighborhood at sunset" — Sam Thompson (~42:45)

**Commit to a test period before switching ideas** (mindset)
1. Choose one business idea.
2. Keep doing the actions that are meant to produce results.
3. Allow enough time for the test to produce evidence before switching.
> "pick something commit to it for 60 days and just actually do the things that drive results" — Chris Koerner (~18:39)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Start customer conversations before the offer is polished; the calls may reveal what the customer actually wants to buy.
- *validation_testing*: Use first-party customer evidence to complement market research, rather than relying only on search trends or an LLM's estimate.
- *pricing*: Use real customer conversations to understand willingness to pay, and know your minimum viable price before negotiating.
- *validation_testing*: A stranger buying is a stronger demand signal than a friend buying to support the founder.
- *mindset_execution*: Take action quickly, then be patient while you learn and wait for results.
- *mindset_execution*: Practice in the real world can teach faster than extended preparation in a low-stakes setting.
- *offer_design*: Use customer language and customer outcomes to make an offer easy to understand.
- *pricing*: When the customer outcome is valuable, connect the fee to the value they say they receive.
- *finance_unit_economics*: A one-time software sale can be attractive when customers dislike subscriptions or churn quickly.
- *automation_ai*: AI tools reduce production effort, but a creator still needs judgement about what is funny, useful, and worth distributing.
- *mindset_execution*: Keep a promising idea long enough to run the actions that could produce results before switching to another idea.
- *offer_design*: Making content is not the same as having a business; decide how the audience will be monetised.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Do not treat research, pitch decks, or a perfect website as substitutes for talking to customers and launching a test.
- Warm customers may buy to support the founder and may not give candid feedback, so a stranger's purchase is a stronger demand test.
- Sam had shipping delays and realised he may have chosen the wrong bag size.
- Sam had not worked out the supply chain for the liquid extender product.
- Hummingbird nectar demand is seasonal, so timing inventory and marketplace preparation matters.
- AI-generated testimonial-style videos could mislead customers if they are presented as genuine customer reviews.
- AI video output can contain incorrect subtitles, unexpected text, or inconsistent details that need human review.
- Switching ideas after only a few days does not give a business enough time to produce useful evidence.
- A product with a strong theoretical return can still be overpriced if the founder focuses only on capturing more value instead of making the customer outcome exceptional.
