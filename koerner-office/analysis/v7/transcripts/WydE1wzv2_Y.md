## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Wesley, owner of a Grounds Guys franchise in Keller, Texas (DFW), about adding a Christmas lights installation line to a lawn business. They cover pricing by linear foot, a leasing model where the operator keeps and stores the lights, rising margins in repeat years, labour and time per job, insurance, marketing (door hangers, Facebook, yard signs) and low startup costs. Wesley claims one Grounds Guys owner does $1.25M in Christmas lights alone. Key takeaway: recurring, route-dense, leased holiday lighting can have high repeat-year margins, but it is physical, risky work that agents cannot do.

### I1. Residential Christmas light installation (leased lights, recurring annual)

- **Customer:** Homeowners in larger homes (example 3-4,000 sq ft house) wanting roofline lights without doing it themselves.
- **Problem:** Hanging lights is risky, time-consuming and fiddly; homeowners must store and fix lights and replace bulbs.
- **Offer:** Professional measured, custom-cut roofline lighting, timer included, installed, serviced if bulbs fail, taken down and stored by the company.
- **Business model:** Annual per-job install fee priced per linear foot; lights leased and owned by the operator; yearly recurring with 10% discounts in years 2 and 3.
- **Pricing:** Per linear foot of roofline, with a minimum job; 10% discount in year 2 and again in year 3, then flat.. first year USD 1500-2000 per install, average large home; small single-level home, 150-200 linear ft USD 800 minimum job Permanent lighting is 2-3x the price. Customer-owned lights model priced differently. No extra charge for colour changes (Wesley says they could charge).
- **Acquisition:** direct_mail_postcards: Door hangers delivered by install crews to a 'box nine' of nearby homes (2 each side plus 5 opposite) while working, creating two touch points.; paid_social: Facebook posts with before and afters plus Facebook marketing are the main lead generation.; other: Yard signs (now technically illegal in Texas this year per Wesley); another franchisee put out about 80 signs a month and accepted complaints for the leads.; existing_audience: Marketing to the internal CRM of existing lawn customers and taking over commercial customers from another Grounds Guys; campaigns start in July (early-bird discounts) and again late September/early October.; referrals_word_of_mouth: Repeat customers rarely reshop if communication is good; idea of knocking on doors of new owners of homes where lights are already cut to fit (not yet tried).
- **Delivery:** Measure with a measuring wheel, pre-bulb and pre-clip socket wire in the shop (30-60 min), two-person crew (one on ladder/roof, one on ground) installs in 1-1.5 hours for a large house; takedown takes about 10 minutes with painter's pole; strands labelled and stored indoors; service calls for failures. Employees do installs.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Year-one margin about 20-25%, year three about 50-60%; Material about $200 on a $1,500 house; Insurance about $12,000 per year; Takedown 10 minutes, install 1-1.5 hours
- **Tools:** Measuring wheel, Socket wire rolls, Duck bill clips, Wire clippers, Zip ties, Timers, Cougar Paws boots, Pitch wedge, 30ft ladder, Painters pole, Lightream colour-changing lights, CRM
- **Startup cost:** $400-500 minimum for materials plus safety gear/ladders; $2-3k typical first year; Wesley spent $4.5-5k on material for about 15 homes.
- **Time to first revenue:** Same season; marketing starts July, installs in Oct-Nov (supply ships around September).
- **UK applicability [E]:** 2/5 — Demand is strong in the US where big house Christmas displays are a norm; UK is much smaller market and lower display culture, houses often smaller with fewer large rooflines, shorter ladders work and Work at Height Regulations, public liability insurance and PAT/electrical safety apply. ## Tactics and playbook steps

**Lease-and-store lights for repeat revenue** (pricing)
1. Cut lights to fit the house and keep ownership
2. Store lights indoors between seasons
3. Discount 10% in year 2 and 3, then hold flat
4. Reuse material for new customers if one cancels
5. Margin rises from about 20% to 50-60% by year 3
> "We cut the lights and we keep the lights and we're essentially leasing them to them." — Wesley (~2:56)

**Price by linear foot after wheel measurement** (pricing)
1. Measure roof lines from the ground with a measuring wheel
2. Account for roof pitch with simple maths
3. Apply a per-linear-foot price
4. Apply minimum job around $800
> "We use a measuring wheel. So we'll just basically walk it off from here." — Wesley (~3:50)

**Pre-bulb in the shop and design for easy takedown** (operations)
1. Pre-bulb and pre-clip wire on spools in the shop
2. Use minimal time on site
3. Install so takedown can use a painter's pole without ladders
4. Label strands and keep a sketch of runs
5. Mark peaks with coloured zip ties

**Box nine door hanger marketing** (acquisition)
1. When installing at one home, hang door hangers on 2 homes each side and 5 opposite
2. Neighbours see the crew and get a second touch point

**Seasonal marketing calendar** (acquisition)
1. Run campaigns in July with early-booking discounts
2. Restart marketing late September/early October
3. Take down from second week of January

**Route density scheduling** (operations)
1. Give customers a one-week install window
2. Route areas together for install and takedown
3. Cluster to avoid driving time exceeding job time

**Proactive communication on mistakes** (operations)
1. Crews call immediately after incidents like hitting a mailbox
2. Manager calls the customer first
3. Be available any time of day

**Target new owners of homes with pre-cut lights** (acquisition)
1. When a customer moves, knock on the new owner's door
2. Offer lights already fit to the house at a deeper discount

## Lessons

- *finance_unit_economics*: Recurring, repeat-year work increases margin because material is already paid for and install time drops.
- *operations*: Route density determines profit: drive time can exceed on-site time.
- *customer_acquisition*: Communication and proactive honesty keep customers from reshopping.
- *niche_selection*: Seasonal add-on lines can diversify a thin-margin base business like mowing.
- *offer_design*: Owning the asset (leasing lights) creates switching costs and lets you resell to new owners.
- *validation_testing*: Start small and test on your own house; the downside is a ladder.
- *scaling*: Commercial customers can be worth far more than residential ones.
- *pricing*: Price in risk and insurance cost; the liability is part of what the customer pays for.
- *automation_ai*: Physical risky field work is a poor fit for agent-run businesses, but pricing, scheduling and marketing parts can be automated.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Falling from roofs is a serious risk; prioritise safety training and equipment.
- Make sure staff will actually go on roofs before hiring; several hires backed out.
- Never hire uninsured roof workers; insurance classes this as higher risk.
- Texas recently made yard signs technically illegal; neighbours may complain.
- Avoid installing high up where adjustments or timers need a ladder; each service call for one bulb is costly.
