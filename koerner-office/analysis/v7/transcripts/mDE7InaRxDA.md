## Tactics and playbook steps

**Choose a service and market using search and growth signals** (validation_testing)
1. Use Google Trends to check whether searches for the service, such as pool service near me, are trending upward in the target country.
2. Check whether the service has a demand pattern that fits the business; the speaker treats seasonality as acceptable.
3. Choose a growing city and check whether local demand appears plausible before building the site.
> "But before we build the website, we gotta know what website to build, what niche are we building in, and what markets are we building in?" — Chris Koerner (~2:18)

**Use a service-and-city domain and repeatable site template** (product)
1. Choose a domain combining the service and city, such as the pool-service example in the transcript.
2. Use an AI site builder to make a clean, mobile-first local lead-generation site with service, area, contact and SEO content.
3. Give the AI the business intent and explain that the site will be adapted for other locations and niches.
4. Reuse the structure for another service and market, then review the generated content before publishing.
> "It's important to tell any AI what your intent is, right? Give it context and tell it your intent because it's going to know more about what you're wanting than what you even know because we've never done this before, right?" — Chris Koerner (~4:29)

**Reduce lead-form friction according to service value** (offer_design)
1. For a low-ticket service, test a short form to reduce barriers to submission.
2. For a higher-ticket service, add questions that qualify the prospect, such as project size and property details.
3. Compare lead volume with close rate rather than optimizing only for the number of form submissions.
4. Treat the right balance of form friction and prospect intent as specific to the niche.
> "There is a perfect trade-off between friction and intent, okay, and conversion rate. Don't ever forget that. The trick is to find the balance for you and your niche." — Chris Koerner (~7:03)

**Publish, connect the domain and set up payment infrastructure** (operations)
1. Publish the generated site on its temporary domain to review it.
2. Register or connect the chosen custom domain and publish the site again.
3. If buyers will pay through the site, ask the builder to create an invoice area and connect Stripe using an API key.
4. Keep the API key private; the speaker says he blurred it out in the demonstration.
> "Boom, just like that, I didn't even ask for it, but now I have an admin portal, where I can send invoices, see invoices, and others can come and pay invoices, all because I just gave it my one Stripe API key, awesome." — Chris Koerner (~10:04)

**Add local SEO foundations and links** (acquisition)
1. Include local keywords, meta tags, meta descriptions, headings and location-relevant content.
2. Add blog articles and mention nearby cities where appropriate.
3. Create a Google Business Profile.
4. Seek backlinks through local chambers of commerce.
> "It's got local keywords mentioned throughout all of the content, a few blog articles in there, the names of other outlying cities that aren't been built but are close by" — Chris Koerner (~11:03)

**Prove lead quality before pitching a buyer** (sales)
1. Wait until the site produces leads before approaching a local service business.
2. Offer the first leads for free so the business can verify that they are real and useful.
3. After demonstrating lead quality, offer ongoing or exclusive access to the leads.
4. Use exclusivity to support a higher price, while confirming the buyer's terms directly.
> "You need to give them the first leads for free so that they can see that A, they are real and B that they are good and then you'll be like their drug dealer, they'll wanna buy all of your leads" — Chris Koerner (~11:33)

**Test multiple sites because results will vary** (scaling)
1. Treat each site as a test rather than assuming every site will generate leads.
2. Build across more than one niche or market only if the work and platform costs remain manageable.
3. Track lead flow and revenue by site to identify which markets deserve more effort.
> "Not all of them will have fish, but the more hooks we set out, AKA the more websites we build, the more money we'll make." — Chris Koerner (~2:07)

**Monetize through ads as an optional second method** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Create a Google AdSense account and obtain a publisher ID.
2. Provide the publisher ID to the site builder for integration.
3. Choose where ads appear on the site and review whether this fits the lead-generation strategy.
> "So, to integrate AdSense, this is literally all you do. You go to this website right here, Google AdSense, create an account, just like the Stripe API key, you get a key, it's called a publisher ID" — Chris Koerner (~10:15)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Validate a niche and location before building: check whether relevant searches are growing and whether the market appears promising.
- *offer_design*: A form should be designed around the value of the service and the intent needed to close a sale. More submissions are not necessarily better if the leads do not buy.
- *customer_acquisition*: Give a prospective lead buyer proof they can evaluate. The speaker recommends supplying initial leads free, then discussing paid and exclusive access.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI tools with clear context and intent, then review and refine what they produce.
- *scaling*: A repeatable template can make it easier to test another service or location, but each site's results can differ.
- *niche_selection*: Local businesses may underinvest in internet marketing even when their existing website still produces leads, creating room for a marketer to improve it.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Treat optional monetization methods as strategic choices. Ads may add revenue, but the speaker chooses to focus on selling leads for this site.
- *operations*: Use a customer-facing process that makes payment and invoicing easier; the speaker demonstrates generating an invoice portal through the site builder.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Not every site will rank or produce a useful volume of leads; some may be inactive and some may take weeks or months to bring in leads.
- The speaker advises against approaching a business before there are leads to show; without proof, the operator may appear to be making an unsupported promise.
- A high form conversion rate can still produce poor customers; optimize for qualified leads and closed sales, not only submissions.
- The tutorial's SEO claims do not guarantee ranking or revenue. The demonstration is promoted through a Hostinger affiliate link and discount code.
- For UK operations, collect and share lead data appropriately, and do not use unsolicited marketing texts, WhatsApp messages, DMs or emails to individuals and sole traders without consent.
