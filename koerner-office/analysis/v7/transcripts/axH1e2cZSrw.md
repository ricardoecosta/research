## Tactics and playbook steps

**Use Reddit posts to get early players and feedback** (acquisition)
1. Post screenshots and explain what the game is, including its free-to-play approach.
2. Share major updates every few months when subreddit rules restrict developer promotion.
3. Treat critical discussion as feedback and engage with the conversation.
4. Track whether posts lead to new players and revenue.
> "The very first post that I made just showed some screenshots of the game and kind of just explaining a little bit about what the game is and a few metrics about, you know, how to free to play it is" — Ken (~8:28)

**Launch early and learn from potential customers** (validation_testing)
1. Set a launch deadline; Ken chose to launch after six months regardless of whether the larger vision was complete.
2. Put the product in front of potential customers or playtesters, not only friends and family.
3. Use feedback to shape the product and improve later versions.
> "One of the best decisions I made was forcing myself to launch the game after six months, no matter what." — Ken (~12:44)

**Prioritize retention before monetization** (product)
1. Build a game people want to keep playing before focusing on monetization.
2. Use early player feedback to improve the experience.
> "I also didn't worry much about monetization in the beginning. My focus was retention first and building a game that people wanted to keep playing." — Ken (~13:08)

**Experiment with in-app purchase prices** (pricing)
1. Test different purchase prices rather than assuming a price will convert.
2. Ken says players were generally comfortable around $4.99 to $9.99 and that higher prices were harder to convert.
> "What I've learned through a lot of monetization experimentation is anything around $4.99 to $9.99. Players will generally be okay to spend." — Ken (~2:43)

**Use asset stores and freelancers to save build time** (operations)
1. Buy pre-packaged game assets from Unity Asset Store or GameDev Market.
2. Hire freelancers through Fiverr or Upwork for screenshots, icons, and videos.
3. Keep the founder focused on building the game.
> "For purchasing assets, I use Unity Asset Store or Game Dev Market, which have a lot of pre-packaged assets that you can purchase and save a lot of time on making part." — Ken (~12:11)

**Start with a small project to learn the full shipping process** (validation_testing)
1. Choose a project with a small enough scope to finish quickly.
2. Use the first build to learn AI-assisted development, product delivery, store approvals, and launch operations.
3. Apply those lessons to the next project.
> "I also wanted to make sure that the scope was small enough for me to complete in a short amount of time to gain experience of actually shipping something." — Ken (~4:12)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Build in a niche you understand well so you can recognize the audience and make something you care enough to keep improving.
- *validation_testing*: Ship an early version on a deadline and use real user feedback to decide what to improve.
- *customer_acquisition*: Ask potential customers and playtesters for feedback rather than relying only on friends and family.
- *niche_selection*: A small, engaged audience can support a business; a mass-market hit is not the only viable outcome.
- *offer_design*: Build something people want to keep using before optimizing monetization.
- *mindset_execution*: A smaller first project can teach the full product cycle, including development, approvals, and shipping.
- *pricing*: Test prices with actual buyers; Ken's own experiments suggested a more comfortable in-app purchase range and weaker conversion above it.
- *mindset_execution*: Set personal decision thresholds before making a major career change; Ken chose an organic revenue target before leaving his job.
