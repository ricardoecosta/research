## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build a customer profile before setting the brand** (validation_testing)
1. Photograph all sides of the product; he suggests six views for a three-dimensional item.
2. Give Astra the product description and its current cost.
3. Specify the target customer and ask for their routine, purchase motivations, aesthetic preferences, buying occasions, adjacent products, and emotional motivations.
4. Use the profile to draft positioning and compare the proposed price with what the target customer already buys.
5. Treat the fictional profile as a hypothesis; the video does not show customer interviews or purchase tests.
> "You need to know how much money they're spending and what they're spending their money on." — George McConnell (~5:27)

**Anchor the price to the customer's broader category** (pricing)
1. Ask the model to estimate price against direct product-category competitors.
2. If the product is intended as a lifestyle accessory, compare it with the target customer's activewear or beauty spending instead.
3. Ask the model to explain what brand positioning and product presentation would need to support the new price.
4. Test willingness to pay with real prospective customers before setting the retail price.
> "This premise is what leaves the bottle category. It sells a plain glass bottle for $52 in the presentation alone" — George McConnell (~7:38)

**Keep the product and brand cues consistent in generated creative** (product)
1. Create a concise product description and a brand statement.
2. Reuse those details with reference photos whenever generating a new image or video.
3. Keep a consistent fictional model across scenes and show the product in several relevant customer settings.
4. Check generated images against the real product and regenerate them when they change its design.
> "This is important that you're adding this into each and every single line because it's going to create the consistency every time that we're producing this." — George McConnell (~14:51)

**Review whether the images justify the price** (validation_testing)
1. Ask Astra what each image communicates about the customer and product.
2. Ask which visual details support the price.
3. Review the images as a sequence and check whether they tell a coherent lifestyle story.
4. Revise scenes or creative where the product still looks like an ordinary low-priced item.
> "What does this image justify about the price? Right? All three of these." — George McConnell (~22:02)

**Use a constrained creator launch brief** (acquisition)
1. Ask Astra to plan a launch around a stated budget and timeline.
2. Set a follower cap and prioritize micro-creators.
3. Request creator briefs, outreach email sequences, launch funnels, and a first-month content plan.
4. Review the budget and contact strategy before spending or contacting creators.
> "We want micro creators and give us ideas of all this." — George McConnell (~23:42)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Define the customer and the role a product plays in their life before deciding how to present it.
- *offer_design*: A premium price depends on a coherent brand and lifestyle presentation, not only on the physical item.
- *pricing*: Compare a product with the spending category it is meant to join, but treat that comparison as a pricing hypothesis to test.
- *automation_ai*: Consistent brand details and reference images help keep AI-generated creative aligned across scenes.
- *offer_design*: Use ordinary customer settings to make the product fit a lifestyle story instead of making every ad a direct product close-up.
- *operations*: Review AI outputs critically and rerun or revise creative when the product is inaccurate or the scene feels unnatural.
- *validation_testing*: A detailed launch plan is not evidence that the offer will sell; test demand, economics, and fulfillment before committing the budget.
