## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Trevin, an Amazon Influencer Program creator and co-founder of the tool Viral View, about filming short product review videos with a phone and uploading them to Amazon product pages for commission. They cover approval steps (public social profile, then three human-reviewed videos for onsite placement), the Creator Connections boosted commissions (10-50%) that drive most earnings, getting free products from brands, cross-posting to YouTube with deep links, and scaling by outsourcing. Earnings claims are large (GBP-equivalent $1-3K/month typical, $25-40K top) but are second-hand or from the guest who sells a tool in this space. Key takeaway: a near-zero-cost, volume-driven affiliate video model, but filming is a human task and results are self-reported.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Get approved for Amazon onsite video placement** (acquisition)
1. Apply at the Amazon Influencer program with a public social profile (TikTok easiest; needs some post history)
2. Upload three videos: horizontal, good lighting and sound, no claims, price, CTA, license plates, labels or personal info
3. Pick simple products (tripod, backpack, soccer ball); avoid health or baby products
4. Tag the exact product, brand and colour
> "the first three videos that you post are going to need to be better than the videos after that." — Trevin (~53:47)

**Prioritise boosted-commission products** (pricing)
1. Make 20-30 videos on decently selling products to unlock the Bronze tier
2. Browse Creator Connections for 10-50% campaigns
3. Only review products with boosted rates
4. Check number of existing influencer videos (a beginner should avoid listings with a thousand)

**Source products at zero cost** (operations)
1. Scan order history (own or family) for products still selling
2. Film items at home, Airbnbs or public places
3. Message brands with address to receive free products
4. Resell the products at a discount to recoup cost

**Video format best practices** (product)
1. Film horizontal for desktop
2. Aim for 2+ minutes
3. Use hook stacking: tease demo at end
4. Use a simple lav mic
5. Talk as if to a friend; use script or voiceover if shy
6. Add b-roll to boost retention

**Duplicate winners** (product)
1. When a product sells well, review similar products
2. Comparison videos (e.g., lav mics) perform well

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Piggybacking on an existing high-traffic platform removes the need to generate your own traffic.
- *scaling*: Earnings are heavily skewed; volume of attempts matters because a small share of videos produces most income.
- *mindset_execution*: Asymmetric bets with near-zero cost reward consistent output.
- *mindset_execution*: Platform rate cuts weed out casual entrants and favour those who persist and adapt.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Boosted-commission campaigns, not standard rates, drive most income; incentives set by brands matter more than platform defaults.
- *niche_selection*: A two-sided marketplace can still be early on the supply side even years in, as brands slowly learn.
- *mindset_execution*: Momentum beats focus: moving between ideas led the host to a better business.
- *offer_design*: Build tools for problems you personally have (the guest's origin story for Viral View).

## Warnings and mistakes

- Amazon rejects videos with price mentions, claims, buy-now calls, license plates, labels or loud noise; failing the third approval attempt means starting again.
- Buying then returning products is unethical and risks Amazon account closure.
- Amazon can cut rates at any time.
- Social profile must stay public once linked.
