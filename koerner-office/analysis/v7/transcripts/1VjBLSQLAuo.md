## Summary

Chris Koerner interviews Tim Mloud ('The Full-Time Flipper'), a former nurse in Muskoka, Ontario, who flips premium used couches bought on Kijiji/Facebook Marketplace in dense city neighbourhoods and resold from five storage units. He did $183k sales and ~$120k gross profit in 2023 working part-time, targeting ~$1,000 profit per couch, with video inspections, keyboard-shortcut scripts and deadline-driven sourcing. He also does $100 dump runs and sells a course. Key takeaway: position yourself to be first when sellers hit deadlines, relocate rather than rehab, and focus on high-value items.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Position to be first when impatience strikes** (acquisition)
1. Do one big weekly shop, start ~30 conversations on couches you want regardless of price
2. Watch notifications for price drops, description changes, pending-then-available
3. Ask about deadlines (move-out, closing, new couch arrival)
4. Look for impatience, indifference and ignorance (gifted expensive sectionals)
5. Green flags: couch moved to garage/foyer, new couch visible
> "it's positioning myself to be first in line when impatience strikes" — Tim Mloud (~9:55)

**Keyboard-shortcut messaging scripts** (sales)
1. Text expansion for 'Yep, here's a quick video inspection'
2. Batch questions: anyone scheduled? repairs/stains? help to lift? weekday morning pickup?
3. Pump the brakes: 'just starting to peek at options, might get back to you'
4. Return with a polite lowball: out-of-towner with trailer
> "Keyboard shortcuts having everything so that you're not typing all day long" — Tim Mloud (~33:35)

**Video inspection selling** (sales)
1. Film a 59-second inspection showing every flaw
2. Send on 'is this available?'
3. Buyers decide remotely and take delivery without surprises

**Seller pitch: reliable easy pickup** (acquisition)
1. Offer full-price e-transfer immediately
2. Lift solo; husband needn't be home; pop the garage
3. Remind them to call you if their deadline hits

**Inventory par levels** (operations)
1. Keep five storage units as showrooms
2. Drop prices when at capacity; buy when space frees
3. Target whales ($1,000+ profit), skip low-value items

**Pre-sell from waitlist** (sales)
1. Keep contacts like 'W wants brown leather sectional'
2. Send cropped screenshot of a lined-up couch
3. Send driveway video inspection once acquired

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Look for the middleman who gets paid when a transaction stalls (U-Haul got paid because he lacked a truck).
- *validation_testing*: Start with free/cheap inventory to learn values safely, then take bigger bets.
- *customer_acquisition*: Deadlines create deals; extract seller timelines.
- *niche_selection*: Geographic arbitrage: buy where supply is dense and nobody has trucks, sell where supply is scarce.
- *operations*: A business differs from a hobby by finding and smashing bottlenecks.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Focus on high-value items (80/20): whales over volume.
- *customer_acquisition*: Be open that you're a reseller so sellers and buyers become repeat sources.
- *mindset_execution*: A side gig can replace overtime without overhauling your career.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Hiring a VA for sourcing messages is the obvious next scaling step.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Don't start in debt; borrow a trailer instead of buying an $8k truck.
- Inspect thoroughly (smell for cat urine); prefer an empty trailer to a bad couch.
- Comfort and unhumble pricing slowed sales; extra storage made him lazy on prices.
- Once you post a price you can't raise it.
- Don't lie in negotiations; he regretted a fake 'little sister' story.
- Fragile/heavy items (china cabinets, washers, tables) caused losses and injuries.
- Rehabbing couches was a bottleneck; relocate instead.
