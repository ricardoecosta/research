## Tactics and playbook steps

**Package agent setup around a solved problem** (offer_design)
1. Choose a clear customer group such as individuals, executives, or business owners.
2. Connect the agent to the channels and apps the customer already uses.
3. Lock down permissions before enabling actions.
4. Configure the agent to solve a specific problem rather than stopping after installation.
5. Offer maintenance and service after the initial setup.
> "Make sure their permissions are locked down. Make sure it's tied together to all the right channels and make sure it's actually doing things for them." — Chris Koerner (~2:04)

**Test an agent on your own work, then talk about the result** (customer_acquisition)
1. Install the agent on a computer you already have; the host says a Mac Mini is not required.
2. Tell ChatGPT what you do during a typical day and ask for agent use cases.
3. Try one useful workflow yourself.
4. Talk about the result with friends and people you meet in person, including at church, and offer help to people who ask.
> "And then just start talking about it on the internet to your friends, in person, at church, wherever. And people will want help with it. And you can charge them." — Chris Koerner (~5:04)

**Build a low-friction hosted version** (product)
1. Host and manage the agent on your own server.
2. Choose a customer group such as real estate agents or lawyers.
3. Sell managed AI assistance as a software-as-a-service product so customers do not have to manage their own server or computer.
> "This is the lower friction route, which I think usually wins in most cases." — Chris Koerner (~2:50)

**Connect support agents to the existing knowledge and channels** (operations)
1. Connect the agent to business email, support tickets, and FAQs.
2. Make it available as a voice agent, text agent, chatbot, or a combination.
3. Treat it as a customer support system rather than just a website widget.
> "But instead of just being one widget on the website, it can be multimodal. It can be a voice agent, a text agent, a chatbot, whatever you want it to be." — Chris Koerner (~3:05)

**Turn competitor monitoring into a recurring report** (product)
1. Choose a specific e-commerce customer and product category.
2. Identify the customer's direct competitors.
3. Collect and review competitor ads on a regular schedule.
4. Send a recurring report summarizing what competitors are doing.
> "You want to spy on what ads other e-commerce businesses are running, right? It will automatically go scrape and find and spy and look at your direct competitors and tell you on a regular recurring basis what they're doing" — Chris Koerner (~3:21)

**Use search demand to plan a niche news site** (validation_testing)
1. Use Google Trends to compare phrases people might search for around a new topic.
2. Buy a domain name related to the topic and search phrase.
3. Write separate articles for distinct queries, such as an explainer and a news article.
4. Use readable writing, relevant tags, and internal links to keep readers on the site.
5. Review traffic and search queries in Google Analytics and Google Search Console.
> "And then I wanted to do research. So, I went to Google Trends and I typed in some common searches that people are probably searching for around Moltbook, right?" — Chris Koerner (~7:37)

**Build and publish a site through iterative prompts** (operations)
1. Find a design reference and provide a screenshot and link to the AI coding tool.
2. Prompt the tool to create the site and describe its topic, domain, content schedule, and signup form.
3. Publish the site, connect the domain through DNS settings, and add Google Analytics.
4. Make one focused change at a time; the host advises against asking for multiple changes at once.
5. Republish after making changes.
> "One vibe coding tip is you don't want to ask it to do multiple things at once. I kind of did there. I'm kind of breaking my own rule." — Chris Koerner (~13:08)

**Capture subscribers and automate the welcome email** (customer_acquisition)
1. Add a newsletter signup form to the site.
2. Create an API key in Beehive and connect the form.
3. Configure Beehive to record where signups came from and send an automated welcome email.

## Lessons

- *automation_ai*: Configure permissions before giving an agent access to accounts, messaging tools, or payment information.
- *offer_design*: Sell a working outcome rather than an installation; show that the agent is solving a real customer problem.
- *offer_design*: A managed hosted service may be easier for customers than asking them to install and maintain software themselves.
- *automation_ai*: Look for recurring tasks with clear inputs and outputs, such as weekly competitor monitoring, that an agent can repeat.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use search trends to find the questions people ask, then publish distinct, useful answers for those queries.
- *customer_acquisition*: Write for the visitor's question and keep the page useful; the host says engagement and relevance affect whether a page retains its search position.
- *customer_acquisition*: Collect an audience you can contact again; the host adds a newsletter signup instead of relying only on website visits.
- *operations*: Keep prompts focused when making changes to an AI-built product, so the result is easier to review and correct.
- *validation_testing*: A new trend can create a fast test of interest, but early visitors do not prove revenue or lasting demand.
- *customer_acquisition*: Talk about a useful tool in everyday settings to find people who want help, but expect this kind of customer acquisition to require personal effort.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Restrict agent permissions. The host gives an example of an agent using stored payment information to register a phone number and then repeatedly calling its owner.
- The host says the agent behavior is not evidence of AGI and that he cannot explain the science behind that view.
- Repeatedly requesting Google indexing may annoy Google and push a page back in the queue, according to the host.
- The host warns against asking an AI coding tool to make multiple substantial changes in one prompt.
- SEO rankings depend on users finding the page relevant and staying on it; the host warns that a bait-and-switch page will not rank highly for long.
- The host promotes his paid AI consulting community, Playmakers, which is a promotional conflict for his recommendations about becoming an AI consultant.
