## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate product-market fit through retention** (validation_testing)
1. Do not treat initial orders as proof of product-market fit.
2. Measure whether customers continue to use the product and how much they use it.
3. Check whether the product solves a pressing, repeat problem rather than a nice-to-have.
> "Just because you have orders doesn't mean that you have product market fit, though. Retention is the real indicator of product market fit." — Codie Sanchez (~1:43) [quote not matched verbatim]

**Dominate a narrow market before expanding** (niche_selection)
1. Choose the smallest market you can dominate.
2. Win that market before moving to a slightly larger one.
3. Repeat the expansion as the business proves it can serve more customers.
> "Instead of trying to capture 1% of a gigantic market, you want to find the smallest market you can dominate." — Codie Sanchez (~3:18)

**Build a team that solves problems without the founder** (hiring)
1. Start with a small, capable team that is committed to the mission.
2. Hire operators who investigate problems, try solutions, and bring back a recommendation.
3. Add infrastructure and people after the offer has traction.
> "You want to have somebody that says, hey, I found a problem, I tried a few things. I found the solution for you." — Codie Sanchez (~4:20)

**Reinvest to increase delivery capacity** (finance_unit_economics)
1. After finding product-market fit, plan delivery capacity beyond current demand.
2. Reinvest profits into the team and infrastructure needed to serve more customers.
3. Treat long-term scale as a choice to forgo some near-term owner income.
> "The only way this works is if you reinvest nearly everything in the business to grow as fast as humanly possible." — Codie Sanchez (~6:22)

**Use Amazon's assortment and speed progression as a scaling example** (product)
1. Start with a focused product range.
2. Expand the range after learning what customers want.
3. Improve speed and fulfilment so the offer addresses a more frequent need.
> "But then he started selling more things on Amazon. He started making it faster to get delivery until eventually Amazon became an oxygen business because everybody needed every single thing, sent immediately to them." — Codie Sanchez (~2:29)

**Review prices, expenses, and vendor terms** (pricing)
1. Compare product prices with current costs and revisit prices when rent, materials, labour, software, or other costs rise.
2. Review subscriptions, vendors, and payment-processing fees to identify unnecessary expense.
3. Call the top five vendors and negotiate terms and prices.
4. Use a profit-and-loss view to identify where cash is leaking.
> "If you pick up the phone and really go shape with your top five vendors, one of the easiest ways to make more money right now." — Codie Sanchez (~10:08)

**Delegate through a three-question handoff** (operations)
1. Ask whether someone else can do the task; if not, keep it with the owner for now.
2. If someone else could do it, ask whether they have been taught how.
3. If they have been taught, ask whether the owner has let them do it without supervision.
4. Set a handover date within 14 days when the task is ready to transfer.
> "The first question is, could somebody else do this? If no, keep it for now. Second question is, have I taught someone how to do this?" — Codie Sanchez (~11:29)

**Measure lifestyle-business health by profit per owner hour** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Track profit alongside the owner's time.
2. Prioritize work that moves profit without consuming disproportionate owner hours.
3. Do not stop working on the business without checking how the change may affect profit.
> "So I usually measure the health of a lifestyle business by profit per hour of your time." — Codie Sanchez (~11:54)

**Prepare a business for a transferable sale** (exit_acquisition)
1. Calculate SDE from net income, owner salary, company-provided benefits, and eligible one-time expenses.
2. Reduce owner dependence by building systems and assigning day-to-day work to a team.
3. Develop recurring revenue and diversify customer revenue.
4. Track profitability and revenue growth, then prepare to reach buyers through a business-sale marketplace.
> "There are basically four areas I want you to focus on to increase the multiple that you can sell your business for and make a ton of money." — Codie Sanchez (~14:54)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Choose the business path that fits whether you value long-term scale, current profit and time, or a future sale; the operating choices differ.
- *validation_testing*: Use retention and product usage to check whether customers keep receiving value, rather than treating initial orders as proof.
- *niche_selection*: A smaller market with strong customer love can be a better starting point than a tiny share of a huge market.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Scaling requires people who can identify and solve problems without waiting for the founder to direct each step.
- *pricing*: Regularly review prices against costs because small pricing changes can have a large effect on profit.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Track business finances and negotiate with suppliers to find avoidable costs and improve margins.
- *operations*: Delegate work in stages: decide whether someone else can do it, teach them, then let them do it without the owner.
- *exit_acquisition*: A business's sale value depends on more than current profit; transferability, recurring revenue, customer concentration, and growth also matter.
- *scaling*: Recurring revenue can make a company more attractive to buyers than one-off revenue, according to Sanchez's rule of thumb.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Orders do not establish product-market fit; Sanchez says retention is the real indicator.
- A scaling business can require major reinvestment and sacrifice, with little work-life balance in the early years.
- Margin drift can reduce profit when costs rise but prices stay unchanged.
- Failing to track subscriptions, vendors, and payment fees can leave avoidable expenses in the business.
- Skipping negotiation can leave money on the table.
- A business that relies on the owner may be difficult to sell, and the seller may struggle to find serious buyers.
- Customer concentration can make a business less attractive to a buyer.
- The video promotes Busy, Sanchez's advisory services and resources, and BizScout; these commercial interests may affect how the services and claims are presented.
- For UK outreach, software should not send unsolicited marketing SMS, WhatsApp messages, DMs, or emails to individuals and sole traders without consent. Cold email to limited companies and cold calls screened against TPS/CTPS have different rules.
