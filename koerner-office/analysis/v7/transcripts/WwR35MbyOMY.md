## Tactics and playbook steps

**Pilot the physician workflow with loaner devices** (validation_testing)
1. Buy a small set of devices and approach a physician or clinic in person.
2. Offer a short trial and explain what is recorded, where it is stored and how to turn it off.
3. Show the physician a sample summary, then map any approved note workflow to the clinic's systems.
4. Confirm privacy, consent and EHR constraints before promising automated record updates.
> "wear it for two days and then I'm going to show you what it does and if you like it, we can implement it" (~9:53)

**Start with a workflow the customer already understands** (offer_design)
1. Target businesses that already use scribes or routinely capture conversations.
2. Position the device as a way to improve an existing process rather than asking staff to invent a new one.
3. Begin with a summary or transcript before attempting deeper software integration.
4. Expand into structured record updates only after the first workflow is accepted.

**Turn field sales recordings into repeatable coaching** (operations)
1. Get the sales manager's approval and explain the recording indicator and controls to staff.
2. Collect recordings and transcripts from the team.
3. Ask AI to identify strong interactions and quote specific examples verbatim.
4. Use the examples in the next team meeting and let a manager verify recommendations.
> "I want you to pull out the five things he did best, like specific conversations, word for word, verbatim." (~17:44)

**Test a pay-per-use research price** (pricing)
1. Offer an occasional-use option for customers who do not need a monthly plan.
2. Compare a $1 prompt price with the stated estimated API cost of 30 to 50 cents.
3. Measure actual usage, processing costs, payment fees and support before offering unlimited plans.
> "And my cost on that based on token usage would be 30 to 50 cents. So not like a huge margin, but that's fine." (~27:25)

**Use deep research to locate hard-to-find business owners** (customer_acquisition)
1. Start with the facts the buyer knows about the person or target business.
2. Ask the research tool to identify public records and related businesses.
3. Cross-check names and details against primary public records before making contact.
4. Use the result to guide the buyer's own outreach and due diligence.

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Look for business processes where AI can improve a customer's existing workflow, especially when the customer already pays people to perform the task.
- *validation_testing*: Start with a concrete use case and verify the customer's software supports it before promising an automation.
- *validation_testing*: Use a small pilot to let a buyer experience the product and judge its value before asking for a full implementation.
- *automation_ai*: Ongoing AI implementation may create repeat work because tools and workflows keep changing.
- *operations*: For AI analysis to be useful, collect enough context and compare outputs with real business outcomes rather than relying on generic summaries.
- *pricing*: Recurring pricing is not automatically suitable for occasional-use software; pay per use may better fit customers with infrequent needs.
- *customer_acquisition*: AI research can speed up finding leads and public information, but names and records still need checking.
- *legal_risk*: Recording people creates consent and privacy obligations that must be designed into the offer.
- *mindset_execution*: A business built around useful work can give an owner more control over schedule and working life, but the transcript does not show that the proposed agency has achieved this.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The speakers raise consent and recording-law questions for people captured by a pendant, and say the rules vary by state. UK implementations need clear notice, a lawful basis and safeguards for personal data.
- The optometrist's EHR workflow may not accept an automatically generated note because it requires clicking individual findings and diagnoses.
- The speakers describe using an OSINT prompt after Deep Research refused a request. That raises privacy and platform-policy concerns; public information can still be sensitive or inaccurate.
- The pay-per-use research site depends on an API that the speaker says is not available yet.
- The sales-coaching proposals rely on recordings and AI interpretation; recordings may capture customers or employees who have not agreed, and analysis may be misleading.
