## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate demand before buying equipment** (validation_testing)
1. Identify who already buys the service locally.
2. Ask what they currently pay.
3. List what could make the work fail.
4. Call one relevant property manager, youth coach, or contractor and ask about current prices before buying.
> "First, who already buys this near me? Second, what are they paying for it right now? Third, what would make it fail?" — Narrator (~12:50)

**Reinvest in equipment in stages** (finance)
1. Start with the least expensive machine that has a fast payback.
2. Use earnings from each machine to fund the next purchase.
3. Rent the seasonal aerator before buying one.
4. Leave the highest-cost line striper until the earlier services have generated enough cash.
> "The cheapest machine with the fastest payback goes first. Whatever it earns buys the next one." — Narrator (~0:34)

**Test heat-transfer quality before accepting shirt orders** (operations)
1. Press a test shirt using the suggested temperature and time range.
2. Wash the test shirt.
3. Take the customer job only after the transfer survives the wash.
> "Press one test shirt at around 305 to 320° for 10 to 15 seconds. Wash it and only then take the job." — Narrator (~4:00)

**Make mirroring the first step for shirt artwork** (operations)
1. Mirror the design before cutting heat-transfer vinyl.
2. Make the mirror setting the first click in the shirt job workflow.
> "Make the mirror button the first click on every shirt job, and it never happens again." — Narrator (~5:30)

**Reduce fence-staining overspray** (operations)
1. Check wind conditions before spraying.
2. Cover nearby items that must not be stained.
3. Brush the first coat into the wood directly behind the spray.
> "Check the wind. Cover what you don't want stained, and brush the first coat into the wood right behind the spray." — Narrator (~7:02)

**Protect sprinkler heads during aeration** (operations)
1. Walk the yard before starting.
2. Ask the owner where the sprinkler heads are.
3. Mark every head with a flag before using the aerator.
> "Walk the yard first, ask the owner, and put a little flag on every head." — Narrator (~10:16)

**Lay out parking stalls before spraying** (operations)
1. Measure and chalk the full lot.
2. Include the stated line width in the layout.
3. Check the diagonal before spraying.
> "The fix is a tape measure, a chalk line, and checking the diagonal before you spray a single stripe." — Narrator (~11:48)

**Cross-sell multiple products to the same contractor crew** (sales)
1. Start with truck lettering for a contractor.
2. Offer matching crew shirts using the heat press.
3. Offer branded hats or flat embroidered patches after the relationship is established.
> "Same customer, third product. That's when a little garage setup starts to feel like a real shop." — Narrator (~8:10)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Check local buyers, current prices, and failure points before spending on equipment.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Buy equipment in stages and let earlier jobs fund later purchases.
- *customer_acquisition*: Start with a customer need that already exists; equipment alone does not create demand.
- *niche_selection*: A small supplier can target jobs or order sizes that large providers may not prefer.
- *offer_design*: Cross-sell related services to the same business customer to deepen the relationship.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Rent expensive or seasonal equipment first, then buy it only after repeat demand appears.
- *operations*: Use simple pre-job checks and test runs to prevent avoidable damage and rework.
- *mindset_execution*: Customer discovery can be valuable even when the answer is disappointing because it can prevent an unwanted purchase.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The narrator says none of the machines are a sure thing and that there is no guarantee.
- Using too much water on upholstery can cause browning, slow drying, and odour.
- Heat transfers may peel if the press uses too little pressure or heat.
- Forgetting to mirror heat-transfer vinyl can make the shirt design read backwards.
- Sprayer overspray can reach nearby property, including a neighbour's car.
- Trying to stitch a stiff structured cap on a flat home machine can pucker and ruin it.
- Unmarked sprinkler heads can be hit by aerator tines and cause a repair bill.
- Rushing parking-lot layout can make stalls drift and vary in size.
- The narrator cautions that a machine does not find customers, correct bad pricing, or knock on doors.
- The transcript's lawn aeration price range is stated as '$75 to $25', which is descending and may be a caption or source error.
- The standard fence example says 'at $300 or $4 a foot' before giving a $450 to $600 total, so the price wording is unclear.
