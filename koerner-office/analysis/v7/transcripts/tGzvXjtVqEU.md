## Tactics and playbook steps

**Test demand before committing to a storefront** (validation_testing)
1. Start by repairing phones on the side.
2. Use the small-scale work to prove that local demand exists.
3. Try paid ads before committing to a full store; the transcript does not specify an ad platform.
> "if it were today, I would have taken my mentor's advice and started fixing phones on the side, proved the demand, tried some paid ads." — Chris Koerner (~9:42)

**Plan for a business downturn** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Keep some savings or a job opportunity available as a fallback.
2. Discuss what the fallback would be before starting, so the risk is shared.
> "I think it's important to have a backup plan, you know, like make sure there's some savings or a job opportunity or or something else that they can fall back on." — Name not stated (~16:07)

**Make business decisions together** (operations)
1. Acknowledge when a business decision feels scary to a partner.
2. Talk through the concern and reconsider the plan together.
3. Do not start a venture behind a partner's back.
> "I feel like that's something that we have done like I don't you've never done anything behind my back or like started anything that we haven't talked about before which I think is is huge." — Name not stated (~23:30)

**Protect family time from work** (operations)
1. Schedule one-on-one time with children and attend important events.
2. Leave the laptop in the office rather than carrying it around the house.
3. Set a no-phone rule at dinner.
> "So, if your schedule is so busy that it requires penciling in time to like spend one-on-one time with a kid or make it to an athletic event or whatever, like make sure your family sees that you prioritize that." — Name not stated (~24:34)

**Choose work that fits the founder** (niche_selection)
1. Consider whether the day-to-day work matches the founder's interests.
2. Chris says he disliked the car buying business partly because he was not a car person.
> "And I hated it because I'm not a car guy." — Chris Koerner (~17:42)

**Keep perspective through setbacks and good periods** (finance_unit_economics)
1. Treat setbacks as possible steps toward a later opportunity.
2. When income is strong, put some aside because prosperity may be temporary.
> "So, not being as upset by the setbacks, but also like recognizing that the prosperity was great, but short-lived and we could be more prepared for later." — Name not stated (~31:49)

## Lessons

- *validation_testing*: Test demand on a small scale before taking on the cost and commitment of a storefront.
- *mindset_execution*: A partner's trust is built through consistent work and follow-through, not excitement about ideas alone.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Have savings, a job option, or another fallback when starting a risky venture.
- *other*: Discuss major business risks with a partner and make decisions together.
- *niche_selection*: Consider whether the daily work suits the founder, not only whether the business could make money.
- *operations*: Set clear boundaries so work does not crowd out time with a spouse and children.
- *finance_unit_economics*: When income is strong, save for the periods when it may fall.
- *mindset_execution*: Long delays or unanswered emails may matter less than making family memories and continuing to work toward goals.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The phone repair shop was opened without testing demand first, and it took months for the location to take off.
- Business income can be irregular; the family describes selling rental homes to cover bills during a period without income from a venture.
- A business can be a poor fit even if it seems viable; Chris says he disliked car buying because he was not a car person.
- Working from home can make it easy to work at all hours, so the couple recommends clear boundaries and family time.
