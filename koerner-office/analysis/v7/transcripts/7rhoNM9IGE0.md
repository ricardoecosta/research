## Tactics and playbook steps

**Let the prospect define the offer** (sales)
1. Run ads that get prospects on the phone
2. Ask what they actually need
3. Package that on the spot (e.g. backlinks-only at $500/month)
4. Keep selling the new offer if others want it
> "I didn't like wake up three weeks ago saying, dude, you know what would be an interesting offer is backlinks only" — Sam (~12:59)

**Copy competitors' iterated final offer via the Wayback Machine** (sales)
1. Find 5-10 businesses in your niche charging what you want
2. Look them up in the web archive to see how long they've been in business
3. Compare how their homepage/offer evolved
4. Copy their current offer, which is the most optimised
> "go find five to 10 people in that niche that's doing exactly what you're doing or want to be doing and charging what you want to be charging" (~14:23)

**Swipe competitor ads from the Facebook Ads Library** (acquisition)
1. List your biggest competitors
2. Check what ads they run in the Facebook Ads Library
3. Adapt their creative for your offer
> "I just always go to like, oh wait, these are our biggest competitors. What are they running?" — Sam (~15:36)

**Sell a stalled asset for a free roll** (finance)
1. When you can't give a project time, offer it to someone who can
2. Sell at cost and keep a % of revenue off the top
3. Contribute strategy (e.g. ads) while they handle operations

**90-minute question-led sales call** (sales)
1. Only ask questions about what the prospect wants and needs
2. Build the pitch from their answers
3. Replace 2-3 minute price-only calls

**Batch short-form video while rucking** (acquisition)
1. Walk in zone 2 with a weighted vest
2. Record shorts while walking (37 in 2 hours)

**Vibe-code a personal tool with Lovable** (product)
1. Define the data fields first (name, description, due date, priority, estimated time numeric)
2. Describe the UI in plain language
3. Click fix on errors
4. Restore a previous version rather than repeatedly patching a broken feature
> "don't be scared to restore previous versions" — Sam (~35:26)

**Daily time-boxed task queue** (operations)
1. Brain dump tasks with estimated time, priority, company tag, due date
2. Each evening check tomorrow's calendar blocks and fill them from the queue
3. Use the timer to record actual time and compare against estimate
4. Plan: add Stripe to compute revenue per hour per company

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Customers tell you what offer to sell; have more conversations with people who have problems.
- *mindset_execution*: Momentum is the most underrated asset; launch in 12 hours, not 12 months.
- *customer_acquisition*: The one main piece of advice: go get a sale first.
- *niche_selection*: People buy the result/shortcut from point A to point B, not the paper; find a big enough A-to-B market.
- *offer_design*: Content quality decides whether info products get refunds and repeat buyers.
- *pricing*: Make-money-online style products should be one-time/lifetime purchases rather than monthly.
- *validation_testing*: Copy what competitors have already iterated to; you don't need to be a genius.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Selling an asset you can't focus on for cost plus a revenue share is a free roll.
- *automation_ai*: When vibe coding, design the data model first (e.g. numeric fields to sum) before the UI.
- *mindset_execution*: Pick your addiction: momentum can be as addictive as Netflix.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Monthly subscriptions for make-money-online style apps will churn heavily because most users never do the work.
- Most course buyers never act; buyers who do succeed would likely have succeeded anyway.
- Don't spend months building; people spend years on ideas instead of testing fast.
- A health influencer built a tens-of-millions app and cookbook on faked cancer claims; deceptive influencer marketing collapses.
- When vibe coding, don't keep patching a broken feature; restore and re-prompt.
