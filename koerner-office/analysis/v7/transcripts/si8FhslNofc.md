## Tactics and playbook steps

**Use the slice method to find a software product** (product)
1. Find a multi-million- or multi-billion-dollar software company.
2. Review its features and identify one that is underserved or feels like an add-on.
3. Check that a market exists for the feature before building.
4. Make the standalone feature better than the larger company's version.
5. Offer it as a niche product, potentially on a monthly subscription.
> "You take this underserved feature, make sure there's a market for it, and you slice it off into its own niche product." — Niche Pursuits host (name not stated) (~8:29)

**Talk directly with customers** (validation)
1. Speak directly with customers to learn their real needs.
2. Do not avoid support conversations; pick up the phone and ask questions.
3. Use what customers say to find product ideas and understand the end customer.
> "Pick up the phone and talk to people. Get inside the mind and truly understand your end customer." — Niche Pursuits host (name not stated) (~9:02)

**Build and engage an email list** (customer_acquisition)
1. Build an email list that the business can reach directly.
2. Stay engaged with subscribers.
3. Use the list to communicate new offers rather than depending entirely on search algorithms.
> "Build an email list and stay engaged with your audience." — Niche Pursuits host (name not stated) (~9:19)

**Reduce dependence on a single algorithm** (customer_acquisition)
1. Use channels such as SEO and social platforms, but do not make most of the business depend on one.
2. Add other channels such as webinars, podcasts, paid ads, or partnership outreach.
3. Build an email list and focus on marketing the business can control.
> "You need to take the multi-prong approach, find other marketing strategies like webinars, podcasts, paid ads, or outreach to find partnerships." — Niche Pursuits host (name not stated) (~10:12)

**Recruit affiliates to promote a real product** (customer_acquisition)
1. Create a product that solves a real problem first.
2. Recruit affiliates to promote it.
3. Give affiliates a commission; the rate is not stated.
4. Support promotion through affiliate-created YouTube videos, blog posts, social posts, and emails.
> "Then you can start recruiting an army of affiliates to help you grow your business." — Niche Pursuits host (name not stated) (~11:10)

**Target a narrow keyword with an exact-match domain** (customer_acquisition)
1. Choose a low-competition keyword.
2. Use an exact-match domain.
3. Write an article targeting that specific keyword.
> "Just targeting low competition keyword by the exact match domain, write an article targeting that one very specific keyword and voila." — Niche Pursuits host (name not stated) (~1:39)

## Lessons

- *offer_design*: Start with a customer problem and build a product that solves it instead of searching for a quick loophole.
- *validation_testing*: Customer conversations and support can reveal needs that are hard to see from a distance.
- *customer_acquisition*: An email list gives a business a direct way to reach its audience without relying entirely on platform algorithms.
- *scaling*: Diversify marketing across channels so one algorithm does not control most of the business.
- *customer_acquisition*: A product can grow through affiliates when it solves a real problem and gives promoters a reason to recommend it.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Treat platform-dependent side projects as risky income rather than the foundation of the business.
- *niche_selection*: A focused feature from a larger product can become a niche product if a market exists and the feature is improved.
- *mindset_execution*: A real product and customer support may be less troublesome than repeatedly adapting to fragile loopholes and algorithms.
