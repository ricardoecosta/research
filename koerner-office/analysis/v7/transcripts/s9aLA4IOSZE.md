## Summary

Starter Story host Gus interviews Frank Michael Smith, a non-technical sports creator who built Geogames (Geosports, Geohistory and others), a daily five-question map-tap trivia web game, in about eight hours with Claude. ## Tactics and playbook steps

**Build shareability into the product** (product)
1. Make results a shareable score (e.g. colour-coded dot and score out of 100)
2. Keep the game playable with no signup or install
3. Make it playable inside the social platform where it spreads (X)
4. Keep the game so simple that nobody needs teaching
> "make your product shareable. This sounds obvious, but it's not so geosports is only a success because of word amount." — Frank Michael Smith (~9:31)

**Guest 'host' programme with influencers** (acquisition)
1. Watch for large-following accounts who like or play the game
2. DM them offering a 'host' role
3. Host writes the five daily questions
4. When their questions go live, post announcing them as host
5. They retweet, exposing the game to their audience
> "I DM people with large followings who have been liking the post about the game." — Frank Michael Smith (~12:21)

**Stealth launch then amplify** (acquisition)
1. Send to family and friends first and check whether they share it
2. If they share, post on social stories
3. Attach the pitch as a reply to an existing post
4. Prepare for retention once traffic spikes
> "I sent it to a couple family members, friends, and I noticed that they were sharing it." — Frank Michael Smith (~11:03)

**Build a non-technical app with an LLM** (product)
1. Have Claude research a reference product and write a spec
2. Block eight uninterrupted hours
3. Refer back to the spec whenever stuck
4. Screenshot confusing screens (e.g. Vercel) and ask the LLM to explain and verify
5. Ask the LLM to recommend tools to install
> "I relied on screenshots and tons of questions and recommend everyone else do the same." — Frank Michael Smith (~10:23)

**Remix a successful existing product with a niche twist** (product)
1. Find a game or app you love (Map Tap)
2. Identify what it lacks (no trivia, no sports)
3. Build your own version for your niche audience
4. Spin off the same engine to other niches
> "I thought I could do my own thing and make a remix of sorts" — Frank Michael Smith (~7:23)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Build sharing into the core loop (score cards, stats, one-tap share); word of mouth carried about 98% of traffic.
- *offer_design*: Zero-friction web products that work inside the platform where they spread (no app install, no signup) maximise virality.
- *niche_selection*: Remixing a proven product with a niche twist is a fast route to an idea with built-in demand.
- *automation_ai*: LLMs let non-technical founders ship a working product in a weekend; block out a full day and use the LLM for every blocker, including screenshots of confusing UIs.
- *scaling*: Daily habitual products compound: retention from daily play drives both ad inventory and subscription conversion.
- *customer_acquisition*: Partner with people who already have audiences (hosts) by giving them a creative role and exposure, rather than paying for ads.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Ads can dominate revenue for high-traffic consumer games (about 75-85% here), with subscriptions as a smaller add-on.
- *mindset_execution*: Long-run persistence and skill stacking (13+ years in content before success) underpin 'overnight' wins; survivorship bias applies to such stories.
- *customer_acquisition*: Founders with distribution skills have an edge now that building is cheap; building an audience, even a small one in public, is still advised.

## Warnings and mistakes

- The launch was unprepared on retention; a viral spike on a five-day-old game forced rapid work on getting players to return.
- The LLM was not always correct; verify outputs, especially setup steps like hosting and domains.
- Objection that success is only due to a big audience: the founder says audience helps, but the game's growth is mostly word of mouth. The audience advantage should still be discounted when copying this.
