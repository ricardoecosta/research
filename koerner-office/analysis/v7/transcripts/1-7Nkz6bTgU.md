## Summary

Koerner Office host Chris Koerner interviews Tyler Deng, co-founder of Beehive (newsletter platform, ex-Morning Brew growth lead), about monetising small email lists and growing newsletters. Topics include niche audiences monetised through cohorts, recruiting fees and events, paid acquisition and co-registration (Boost), local newsletters, content-led brand building, a referral-program 80/20 and turning a company's customer email list into a daily ad-supported newsletter. Key takeaway: niche, high-intent audiences can out-earn larger lists, and quality content plus cheap, trackable acquisition matters more than growth hacks. Both speakers have affiliate or product interests in Beehive.

### I1. Local newsletter sponsored by local businesses

- **Customer:** Local residents (readers) and local small businesses (advertisers)
- **Problem:** Small local businesses need customers; large newsletters cannot target geography precisely, whereas a local newsletter guarantees local readers.
- **Offer:** A local guide (restaurants, events, local news) emailed to residents, with sponsor placements for local businesses.
- **Business model:** Sponsorships/ads sold to local businesses, with potential to extend into other revenue lines
- **Pricing:** Ad/sponsorship CPMs, claimed about twice those of non-local newsletters.  No specific sponsorship prices given; only a relative CPM claim.
- **Acquisition:** paid_social: Facebook ads targeting residents of a specific city at roughly 50 cents per subscriber, versus about $1.50 for business subscribers.; direct_mail_postcards: Targeted postcard to every household in a handful of zip codes, estimated about $5,000 for an Annapolis-sized newsletter.; partnerships: Give popular local pizza places cards to hand to customers, or run affiliate-style deals with local establishments for acquisition only.; referrals_word_of_mouth: Start with 50-75 friends and acquaintances, ask each to share with one person.
- **Delivery:** The founder writes or curates the newsletter (restaurants, events, news) and sells sponsorships; AI tools could help produce content.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Subscriber cost about 50 cents locally versus about $1.50 for other newsletters; Local CPMs roughly double; Postcard campaign about $5,000 for several zip codes; Newsletters reached 5,000-15,000 local readers
- **Tools:** Beehive, Facebook ads, Direct mail
- **Startup cost:** Not stated for a local newsletter; paid acquisition of a few thousand subscribers at about 50 cents each implied.
- **Time to first revenue:** Not stated; Tyler cites newsletters going from 100 friends to 5,000 in a quarter and selling out Q1 sponsor inventory.
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Works in UK towns and boroughs; Facebook geo-targeting works similarly. Subscriber costs differ and consent and GDPR apply to list building. Local newsletter tooling is available in the UK.
- **Risks:** Subscriber and CPM numbers are speaker estimates for the US; Local sponsor sales can be slow; Audience size is capped by local population
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Paid social ads and referral programs run largely by software; postcards and partnerships need human effort. Cold DMs to businesses would risk PECR issues.); onboarding 4/5 (Subscriber signup is automated via the platform; sponsor onboarding is a simple intake that agents can handle.); delivery 3/5 (Agents can research local events and draft issues, but local knowledge and taste need regular human review.); billing 4/5 (Sponsor invoicing and platform ad payments can be automated.); support 4/5 (Mostly reader replies and sponsor queries, which agents can handle.)
> "very bullish on local newsletters, and I think it makes perfect sense." — Tyler Deng (~24:18)
> "you can acquire subscribers for local newsletters at like half or a third of the cost of a any other newsletter." — Chris Koerner (~26:20)

### I2. Turn a company's customer email list into a daily ad-supported newsletter

- **Customer:** Ecommerce and other businesses sitting on large customer email lists
- **Problem:** Businesses use their email lists only for promotions and miss a new revenue line and brand-goodwill benefit.
- **Offer:** Create and run a topical daily newsletter on the business's list, selling ads or affiliate placements as a new revenue stream.
- **Business model:** Agency fee or revenue share on net-new newsletter revenue (ads, sponsorships, affiliates)
- **Pricing:** Not stated.  Chris estimates revenue of about $60,000 per month from a 150,000-subscriber list; agency pricing not given.
- **Acquisition:** other: Pitch directly to businesses with large email databases, with no specific method stated.
- **Delivery:** Writer or AI-assisted content team produces the newsletter and sells ads; the main difficulty is filling ad space.
- **Unit economics (claims):** Chris: 100,000 subscribers daily could earn about $50,000 per month; Chris: 150,000 list at decent CPM could be about $60,000 per month
- **Tools:** Beehive, AI writing tools
- **Startup cost:** Not stated
- **Time to first revenue:** Tyler says it could bring in money from day one via ads or affiliates.
- **UK applicability [E]:** 3/5 — UK PECR/GDPR require that customers consented to receive different content; repurposing a customer list for a new unrelated newsletter may need fresh opt-in. Otherwise the same.
- **Risks:** Filling ad inventory is described as the hardest part; Subscribers may not engage with a new, different newsletter; Consent and data protection when repurposing lists; Revenue figures are rough back-of-envelope estimates

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Seed a newsletter with 50-75 friends then multiply via sharing** (acquisition)
1. Ask friends, coworkers and community members to subscribe for free
2. Provide real value so they stay
3. Ask each to share with one person to reach 150-200 quickly
4. Only then add paid growth
> "you have a base of like 50 to 75" — Tyler Deng (~7:35)

**Break-even paid acquisition via co-registration** (acquisition)
1. Recommend 3 partner newsletters in your signup flow
2. Spend about $2 per subscriber on Facebook or Instagram
3. Receive about $2 per partner subscription
4. Reinvest surplus into more ads
> "if I'm spending $2 to acquire an email on Facebook or Instagram they come in through my flow and they subscribe to one of those three newsletters I'm break even right off the bat" — Tyler Deng (~8:31)

**Normalise acquisition cost by engagement** (finance)
1. Tag each source with UTM parameters and segments
2. Compare open and click rates by source
3. Judge cost per engaged subscriber, not cost per signup
4. Dial spend up or down on the best sources
> "You need to normalize it for opens." — Tyler Deng (~11:04)

**Advertise in other newsletters** (acquisition)
1. Place ads or recommendations in newsletters with similar readers
2. Track quality of resulting subscribers
3. Double down on best sources

**One-referral reward to bootstrap referrals** (acquisition)
1. Create a high-value free asset (a one-page expert summary)
2. Offer it for just one referral
3. Automate delivery in the platform
4. Push referrers toward further tiers afterwards
> "what is enticing enough to incentivize someone to share with just one person" — Tyler Deng (~37:30)

**Test new ad channels early** (acquisition)
1. Try new ad formats while competition is low
2. Make sure you can track quality
3. Scale what works, stop what fails

**Content-first brand nurturing for businesses** (acquisition)
1. Spend 30 minutes brainstorming what customers want more of
2. Publish useful content unrelated to selling
3. Mix in a weekly transactional email

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: A small, niche, high-intent audience can earn more than a larger generic one by selling events, cohorts and placements instead of only ads.
- *scaling*: Treat an audience as an asset with many possible monetisation routes; start with one and expand.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Measure acquisition quality, not just cost per signup.
- *validation_testing*: Leaders should be open to testing unconventional channels, provided you can track results.
- *customer_acquisition*: Early adoption of new ad placements gives low CPMs before the window closes.
- *customer_acquisition*: Content-led brand building wins purchase decisions later even if it cannot be attributed directly.
- *niche_selection*: Founders should have expertise or real interest in the niche they build an audience around.
- *offer_design*: Make the first referral the easiest and most rewarding step; low barriers beat large far-away rewards.
- *other*: Existing business assets such as customer email lists can be repurposed into separate revenue streams.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Growth hacking alone does not build a successful newsletter; quality content comes first.
- Cheap subscribers can be low quality; judge by engagement not signup cost.
- People who enter a niche without expertise or passion tend to fail.
- Large multi-tier referral rewards rarely get completed; most people will not refer 5-10 people.
