### I1. AI avatar UGC ad creative agency for supplement brands

- **Customer:** Supplement e-commerce brands that already spend heavily on Facebook/Meta ads (often ~$10M/yr revenue with ~5 staff)
- **Problem:** Brands need to test hundreds of ad creatives monthly; human UGC creators are slow, costly (~$100/video), give one script variation and require shipping product.
- **Offer:** 25 AI-avatar UGC videos per week, with hook research, scripts and edits, informed by a performance dashboard; next batch based on top performers. Does not run the ads.
- **Business model:** Monthly retainer for a fixed weekly volume of ad creatives
- **Pricing:** Monthly retainer. suggested retainer (25 ads/week) USD 5000 per month; what some agencies reportedly charge for 10 videos USD 8000 per month Claimed that 16 clients at $5k/month gives about $80k/month.
- **Acquisition:** cold_email: Find supplement e-com brands, identify the founders, email all ~5 staff with a Google Drive folder of 5 sample ads made for them, backed by pain-point research.
- **Delivery:** Operator uses AI tools (e.g. MakeUGC.ai, n8n/make.com workflows) to research pain points, write scripts, generate about 50 videos, curates the best 25 plus ~5 bonus. Client's own creators can remake winners.
- **Unit economics (claims):** $5k/month x 16 clients = $80k/month; 25 ads can be made in ~2 hours, ~32 hours/week for 16 clients; Search volume for AI UGC rose from 1,600 to 4,300/month in a year
- **Tools:** MakeUGC.ai, n8n, make.com, Perplexity, Reddit, Google Drive, Apollo
- **Startup cost:** not stated
- **Time to first revenue:** not stated
- **UK applicability [E]:** 4/5 — Works in UK; cold email to Ltd companies is OK. Supplements carry ASA/CAP health-claim rules and AI avatar disclosure considerations. Target UK or US brands remotely.
- **Risks:** Platform-compliance risk: Meta and ASA rules on health claims in supplement ads; AI avatar tool quality, cost and policy changes; commoditisation as more agencies copy; Revenue depends on client creative performance and churn when ads go stale; Hours claim is optimistic for 16 clients
- **Automation [E]:** acquisition 3/5 (Cold email to limited companies is PECR-legal; agents can find brands, generate sample ads and send; human handles replies and sales calls.); onboarding 2/5 (Needs brand brief, product photos and dashboard access; mostly templated but needs human input.); delivery 3/5 (Agents can research, script and generate videos; human curates winners weekly.); billing 4/5 (Stripe subscriptions/invoices.); support 2/5 (Weekly client feedback and performance-based iteration needs human judgement.)

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Sample-first cold email with ready-made ads** (acquisition)
1. List supplement e-commerce brands
2. Identify all ~5 staff/founders
3. Research customer pain points
4. Create 5 sample ads in a Google Drive folder
5. Email the folder link offering hundreds of ads a month
> "You just open this Google Drive folder. You're going to see five ads of the of the hundred that I can make." — Cody (~7:24)

**Reddit-quote scripting for niche products** (product)
1. Pick a very narrow niche (e.g. creatine gummies for women long-distance runners)
2. Ask Perplexity/ChatGPT for pain points and Reddit quotes
3. Build hooks and scripts from the quotes
4. Generate videos

**Pain-point mining via Reddit deep research** (acquisition)
1. Prompt Perplexity: main pain points for [industry] Reddit
2. Pick a pain point you can solve
3. Drill down with ChatGPT deep research on complaints across Reddit, Instagram, Facebook groups
4. Craft cold outreach that names the pain
5. Check demand on Google Trends

**Copy the leading competitor via layered deep research** (operations)
1. Find 5 businesses crushing it in the niche
2. Pick the best
3. Run new deep research prompts on its growth, pricing history and fulfilment
4. Copy the playbook

**Local SEO via consistent directory listings** (acquisition)
1. Build Google Business Profile with keywords, services and interior photos
2. Submit identical name/address/phone to Yelp, Foursquare, MapQuest
3. Aim for Google Maps pack top 3

**Find prospects via low-view YouTube webinars** (acquisition)
1. Search YouTube for webinar, filter this week and over 20 minutes
2. Ignore view counts
3. Find company marketers on Apollo
4. Cold email with a free ebook from their webinar

**Pricing hack: half price for list access** (pricing)
1. Quote e.g. $2,000/month
2. Offer $1,000/month if you may pitch their webinar signups
3. Gain customers through customers

**Annual plan with a refund guarantee** (pricing)
1. Offer yearly membership (~$72)
2. Guarantee a refund if they don't fly with you within a year
3. Few will claim

**Niche down within an industry** (acquisition)
1. Pick a vertical (biotech, cybersecurity)
2. Reference case-study names known in that category in outreach

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: Sell to buyers who already spend heavily on the thing you improve instead of convincing non-buyers.
- *niche_selection*: Unsexy, boring B2B niches (webinars) often have budgets and little competition.
- *offer_design*: Sell a narrow done-for-you deliverable on a recurring cadence rather than running the client's whole channel.
- *automation_ai*: Use AI for production and keep the human as curator who picks winners.
- *pricing*: Copy proven pricing models and unbundle big players by going local or niche.
- *validation_testing*: Do the work upfront (free samples) so outreach shows value and validates demand.
- *validation_testing*: Check market growth with Google Trends before launching a lead-gen business.
- *offer_design*: Clients who can do it themselves still pay for time saved; the DIY risk is small.
- *mindset_execution*: Do the simple thing fast, e.g. buy the domain, instead of over-planning.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Ad creatives go stale in about two months, so winners need constant replacement.
- Don't pitch businesses that don't run ads; target those already addicted to ads.
- Posting before/after jobs you didn't do is dishonest; do it ethically.
