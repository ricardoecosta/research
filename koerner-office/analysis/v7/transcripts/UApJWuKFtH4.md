## Summary

Nick Huber and a guest (Corey) discuss breaking a phone addiction. Nick turned his phone off from 7pm to 7am, cut screen time to about 2.5 hours a day, batches texts and email into a 4:30-5pm calendar slot, and uses a notepad instead of his phone. The video contains no business idea; the takeaway is that managing attention and being hard to reach improves sleep, relationships and focus for a founder running several companies.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Phone off 7pm to 7am** (mindset)
1. Turn the phone off at 7pm and back on at 7am
2. If a full phone fast is too hard, start with a shorter window such as 7 to 7 or 8 to 8
3. Expect discomfort and anxiety at first
> "I'm going to turn my phone off at seven p.m. I'm going to turn it back on at seven a.m." — Nick Huber (~0:16)

**Batch messages into one daily slot** (operations)
1. Block 4:30 to 5pm on the calendar to clear all inboxes and texts
2. Don't reply outside that window; let people wait until the next day
3. Accept that people close to you will understand
> "I have on my calendar from 4 30 to 5 to clear all my inboxes." — Nick Huber (~1:55)

**Notepad and pencil instead of phone** (mindset)
1. Carry a notepad and pencil at all times
2. Write down anything that makes you want to reach for the phone, such as Amazon orders or messages to send
3. Copy the notes into the phone later, e.g. after a walk
> "keep a note pad and a pencil with you at all times." — Nick Huber (~2:12)

**Swap phones with spouse** (mindset)
1. Trade phones with your partner so you can't run your usual check routine
2. Move app-only tasks such as ordering food or the daycare swipe to a shared arrangement first
> "Me and my wife can trade phones now." — Nick Huber (~3:44)

**Phone-free morning walk** (mindset)
1. Walk about 30 minutes each morning without the phone
2. Use the time to think about long-term goals
3. Write down ideas on a notepad

## Lessons

- *mindset_execution*: Being slow to respond is acceptable; you don't have to reply to every message or say yes to every request.
- *operations*: Batching communications into a fixed daily window protects focus time for a founder running several companies.
- *operations*: Text messaging has become the new email and needs the same triage discipline.
- *scaling*: Sharing a live Slack feed of leads and closed deals is highly motivating for a team, but the founder may not want to receive it personally.
- *mindset_execution*: Leading by example matters for parenting; reducing phone use improved Nick's sleep, relationships and engagement with his kids.
- *mindset_execution*: Best ideas and long-term thinking come from unplugged time, so protect it with offline note-taking.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Constant notifications on leads, closed deals and revenue, plus social media, create a dopamine loop that makes it very hard to stay off the phone.
- Even carrying the phone on a thinking walk leads to checking Slack, email and messages.
- Real-time deal and lead feeds are motivating for a team but a slippery slope for the founder.
