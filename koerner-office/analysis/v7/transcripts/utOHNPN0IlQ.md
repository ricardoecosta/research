## Tactics and playbook steps

**Choose a niche that suits both monetization and AI visuals** (niche_selection)
1. Choose a niche with potentially strong revenue per view.
2. Check whether AI-generated visuals suit the subject.
3. Use a focused topic such as dark business history, mythology, AI tools, weird science, or true crime.
4. For local business-history stories, use an agent to find local news articles the speaker says may not reach large documentary outlets.
> "We want to pick a niche that does two things. Number one, it pays well. And number two, it will look good as an AI video" — Chris Koerner (~3:29)

**Set up Claude’s Higgsfield connector** (operations)
1. In Claude settings, open Connectors and choose Customize.
2. Add a custom connector, name it Higgsfield, enter the connector URL, and connect it.
3. Allow the connector, then enable it from the chat window’s connector menu.
> "Then, we're going to click customize right here. Then click the plus button right here. Add custom connector. Okay. We're going to call it Higsfield." — Chris Koerner (~2:28)

**Prompt Claude for the channel package and script** (product)
1. Ask for a channel name and a 10-word description.
2. Request 10 click-oriented video titles and a full script for the first video.
3. Specify a cinematic tone, an eight-minute target, and retention as the goal.
4. Tell Claude to use the Higgsfield MCP connector.
> "Give me a channel name, a 10-word description, 10 video title ideas built to get clicks, then write the full script for video one. cinematic tone, eight minutes long, built for retention." — Chris Koerner (~5:47)

**Edit the AI draft through follow-up prompts** (operations)
1. Review the generated script rather than accepting it automatically.
2. Ask for a punchier opening or for the boring middle to be cut.
3. Treat Claude as the writer and yourself as the editor.
> "You can tell it to make the first paragraph punchier or to cut the boring middle out. You're the editor. Claude is the writer." — Chris Koerner (~7:07)

**Generate consistent scenes and animate them** (operations)
1. Ask Claude to divide the script into 12 cinematic shots and write a visual prompt for each.
2. Ask Higgsfield to generate the scenes in a consistent visual style.
3. Use Face Lock if a recurring character should have the same face across scenes.
4. Animate a reference image with a prompt describing the motion or scene.
> "For each shot, write a detailed visual prompt. Then use Higsfield to generate every shot. Keep the visual style consistent, dark, cinematic, slow camera movement." — Chris Koerner (~8:50)

**Create narration and assemble the video** (operations)
1. Generate narration in Higgsfield Speak 2.0 or use ElevenLabs, especially if cloning a voice.
2. In CapCut, add the generated clips and narration to a project.
3. Add royalty-free background music if desired.
> "And then within Cap Cut itself, you choose background music should you want to include that, which I would. Just use royalty-free." — Chris Koerner (~15:41) [quote not matched verbatim]

**Upload and test titles and thumbnails** (acquisition)
1. Create a YouTube Studio account and upload the video.
2. Use YouTube Studio’s A/B testing option with three title and thumbnail versions.
3. Review which version gets more clicks, then let YouTube choose a winner or choose one yourself.
> "And then you would just click AB testing and you would put three different titles, three different thumbnails, titles and thumbnails." — Chris Koerner (~14:36)

**Treat publishing as iterative testing** (validation_testing)
1. Publish enough videos to test what works; the speaker suggests 24 as a useful volume for finding winners.
2. Keep learning and improving with each video.
3. If the channel does not take off, offer the production workflow as a service to other people.
> "If you throw enough volume at it and you keep learning and improving with each video, it will happen eventually." — Chris Koerner (~14:07)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Choose a niche that has both plausible monetization and subject matter that works visually with AI-generated media.
- *offer_design*: Viewers may accept AI-generated content when it answers a question or entertains them well; quality matters more than whether it was made by AI.
- *automation_ai*: Use the model as a draft writer and keep a human in the editor role, giving precise follow-up directions when the output needs work.
- *finance_unit_economics*: The speaker recommends videos of at least eight minutes because he says that length permits midroll ads.
- *validation_testing*: Use YouTube’s title and thumbnail tests to compare alternatives and select the version that gets more clicks.
- *mindset_execution*: Make repeated videos, review performance, and improve each one; a hit may not arrive immediately.
- *finance_unit_economics*: A creator can diversify a channel’s income through advertising, affiliate links, their own products, and eventually a channel sale.
- *scaling*: A creator who cannot get their own channel to take off can offer the production workflow as a service.
- *legal_risk*: Use background music only when it is royalty-free.
