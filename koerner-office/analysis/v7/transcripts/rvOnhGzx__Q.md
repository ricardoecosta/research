## Tactics and playbook steps

**Validate demand by doing and sharing a real award-search example** (validation_testing)
1. Choose a specific points deal and research how it can be booked.
2. Call the airline if needed; Colin says he spent about five hours on a Turkish Airlines-to-Hawaii example and was on hold for about an hour.
3. Get as far as confirming the booking path without spending money if you are practising.
4. Post the research and what you learned on LinkedIn, accurately saying whether you booked the trip.
5. Use audience response as evidence of interest; Colin says his post received almost 100,000 impressions with about 1,000 followers.
> "Not for a real client just for me. Didn't book it. I got on the phone with the agent. I was on the hold like an hour." — Colin Stroud (~23:44) [quote not matched verbatim]

**Use LinkedIn content for inbound demand** (acquisition)
1. Publish useful points and rewards content one or two times a day, six days a week, as Colin says he did.
2. Share practical research and examples rather than relying on outbound sales.
3. Build an email list from the audience so there is a channel to reach people if social distribution changes.
> "I've almost never done any kind of outbound marketing that's been 100% inbound organic, just from content." — Colin Stroud (~7:19)

**Start with spending and earning rate, not card perks** (offer_design)
1. Ask how much the customer spends and where that spending occurs.
2. Compare the points earned on those categories before weighing lounge access or insurance benefits.
3. Recommend a simple card strategy that fits the spending pattern and explain how to implement it.
> "stop focusing on the benefits of your card and start thinking about how much it's earning." — Colin Stroud (~16:31)

**Scope trip research with a form, deposit and complexity-based fee** (pricing)
1. Collect the traveller's points balance, destination, cabin and trip requirements in a website form.
2. Take a deposit to start work.
3. Quote a flat fee based on trip complexity, with multi-city and larger-family trips costing more.
4. Collect the remaining fee once the trip is booked.
> "Anybody on the internet in the world could come to my website, fill out a form. And it's free to fill out the form." — Colin Stroud (~4:51)

**Systematize research and delegate trip work** (operations)
1. Use an award-search tool to search multiple programmes rather than checking airline sites one at a time.
2. Track trips through a custom CRM and dashboard built with Lovable.
3. Hire experienced points researchers to handle most of the research while retaining oversight and client contact.
> "So custom CRM, custom dashboard, really, really cool. So in terms of delivering that service, it's all done through AI by humans." — Colin Stroud (~14:55)

**Learn through free resources and practise before taking clients** (validation_testing)
1. Study points blogs and free courses, including The Daily Drop resources Colin recommends.
2. Practise researching an itinerary using your own points or by stopping before payment.
3. Build a truthful sample case study from the research and distinguish a researched example from a completed booking.
> "Luckily, you can practice on yourself. Like you can go book fake trips, basically. You can get all the way to the booking stage and stop." — Colin Stroud (~22:23)

**Keep a simple fixed-fee core offer** (pricing)
1. State the package price clearly before work begins.
2. Keep the scope easy to understand: consultation, written strategy, implementation help and one trip plan.
3. Track the later trip work because it can vary substantially and affect the effective hourly return.
> "I charge flat fees. So I say it, yeah, it's what you want. This is exactly how much it costs. I keep it really simple." — Colin Stroud (~3:39)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Look for a large group of people who value an outcome but find the underlying system confusing; Colin saw people with many points who did not know how to use them.
- *offer_design*: A useful service can come from solving a neglected task for busy owners, even when the task is not technically difficult.
- *validation_testing*: Validate interest by doing the work, sharing what you learned and observing whether people respond before building a large operation.
- *validation_testing*: Practise with sample research before taking clients; stop before payment and be clear about what was and was not booked.
- *offer_design*: For rewards advice, compare the points earned on actual spending rather than being distracted by card perks.
- *pricing*: Keep the core price and deliverables simple, then track follow-up work that can make a fixed-fee offer less profitable.
- *automation_ai*: AI can speed up search and organize a service, but clients may still value a person who can set expectations and answer questions.
- *customer_acquisition*: Build an audience asset you can contact directly, because dependence on one social platform can make growth fragile.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Treat points as a changing asset: reward programmes can devalue them, so delaying redemption has a cost.
- *mindset_execution*: A part-time service can start with manageable work around a job, then expand when consistent demand and revenue justify it.
- *hiring_outsourcing*: Delegate repeatable research after documenting the workflow, while keeping oversight of the customer experience.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Award-search AI may return best-case availability that exists on only one date, giving customers false expectations.
- Leaving points unused can expose them to programme devaluation; Colin recommends using them rather than hoarding them indefinitely.
- Premium card annual fees may not be worthwhile for a new or smaller business owner.
- Using the word 'hacking' can conflict with card-bank affiliate relationships; Colin says he avoids that word and uses 'strategy'.
- The time required for included trip help can vary widely, lowering the effective hourly rate on a fixed-fee package.
- Colin's customer acquisition depends heavily on LinkedIn, and he says he has no outbound-sales experience.
- A beginner should avoid claiming that a researched trip was booked if it was only practised or priced out.
- The host's sponsor read promotes Beehiiv with a discount, which is a promotional conflict in the video; it is separate from Colin's business discussion.
- In the UK, credit-card advice and affiliate links may be subject to financial-promotion, consumer-credit and disclosure rules; check requirements before launching.
