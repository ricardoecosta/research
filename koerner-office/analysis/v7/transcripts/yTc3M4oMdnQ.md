## Tactics and playbook steps

**Start with a focused set of units and learn from rental demand** (validation_testing)
1. Track which units go out most often and use observed demand to guide purchases.
2. Consider a bounce house with a detachable slide, since Martina recommends it to new operators.
3. Add a different unit type for variety rather than buying two identical units.
4. When the requested unit is booked, offer a suitable alternative.
> "i always tell them to get a bounce house with a slide okay our bounce houses with slides are detachable everything is detachable" — Martina (~17:15)

**Build inbound discovery with listings and visible proof of work** (acquisition)
1. Create a website and make sure customers can find the business through Google.
2. List inventory on Facebook Marketplace.
3. Photograph each setup and post recent rentals on social and personal profiles.
4. Use photos and videos to show the equipment in real event settings.
> "make videos take pictures i take pictures every time I set up even though I've have probably a million pictures of the same one" — Martina (~16:10)

**Use a visible location to attract walk-in customers** (acquisition)
1. Choose a location with useful drive-by traffic rather than considering rent alone.
2. Inflate a unit outside during the week so passing customers notice it.
3. Make it easy for local customers to pick up and return tables and chairs.

**Bundle related event items and expand from customer requests** (offer_design)
1. Listen for requests for tables, chairs, tents, heaters, or air conditioners.
2. Offer a table-and-chair package alongside other rentals.

**Inspect and maintain equipment regularly** (operations)
1. Set up units during the week for cleaning and inspection.

**Use local warranty and repair support as a product differentiator** (product)

2. Offer local repairs so customers do not have to ship a large inflatable back to a distant seller.

**Prioritise punctuality and answer the phone** (operations)

**Evaluate new equipment against used inventory** (finance_unit_economics)

## Lessons

- *niche_selection*: Choose inventory based on which products customers actually rent, then use alternatives to retain customers when a popular item is booked.
- *customer_acquisition*: Use public listings, search visibility, and frequent real-world photos to create inbound demand.
- *customer_acquisition*: A visible location can bring walk-in demand and may reduce reliance on advertising, though the host's claimed savings are only an estimate.
- *offer_design*: Add complementary products when customers request them, but account for the extra inventory and operational complexity.
- *operations*: Maintenance and inspection protect useful life; the operator says inflatables can last years when cleaned and repaired.
- *offer_design*: Warranty and local repairs can differentiate a product when importing a bulky item makes returns difficult.
- *operations*: Reliability matters in event services: answer calls and arrive on time because customers have fixed schedules.
- *legal_risk*: Insurance can be a cost and an entry requirement for venue work, rather than a reason to avoid starting.
- *exit_acquisition*: Existing employees may be attractive successors when an owner wants to retire because they already know the operation.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Simple rental payback calculations should be treated as assumptions until they include operating costs and realistic utilisation.

## Warnings and mistakes

- A late delivery can ruin an event booking; punctuality and answering calls are essential.
- Martina says insurance is necessary, particularly for venues that require proof of coverage; lacking it can mean losing the booking.
- Used equipment may have unknown condition and remaining life; Martina recommends new units with warranty for a new operator.
- The host's revenue and profit examples rely on full utilisation and simplify costs. The interview itself mentions gas, time, insurance, repairs, and physical work.
- A single imported inflatable can have substantial shipping costs, and warranty returns may be difficult.
- Adding product categories can make a one-stop shop harder to manage, even if it lets the business say yes to more requests.
- The interview reports increased competition and a decline in rentals after the COVID period.
