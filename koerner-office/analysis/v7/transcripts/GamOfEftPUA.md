## Summary

Short clip in which Nick Huber tells a story from his book: in 2015, with about $600k of cash saved and a self-storage development underway, his mentor Jim, a DC wealth manager, gave him a free one-hour review of his entire financial life. Jim flagged bonus depreciation and cost segregation (a $600-700k tax write-off), key man insurance and buy-sell planning, and C Corp vs S Corp tax choices, then refused to invoice. Jim explained he does this free for all prospective clients, who are high-value business owners, and 30-40% sign up on the spot while the rest return later. Takeaway: lead with free expert value to build trust with high-value prospects, but this does not suit every sales environment.

## Tactics and playbook steps

**Free high-value first meeting for high-value prospects** (sales)
1. Spend one hour breaking down the prospect's whole situation
2. Unload as much expert value as possible on a whiteboard
3. Give handouts and a photo of the action list
4. Don't invoice, talk money or pitch
5. Expect 30-40% to sign immediately and many others to return when the next problem arises
> "I build trust with them" — Nick Huber (~3:31)

## Lessons

- *customer_acquisition*: With high-value prospects, giving expert value first builds trust that converts later.
- *customer_acquisition*: Prospects with business problems signal money and influence, so they are worth investing time in.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Real estate builders should learn cost segregation and bonus depreciation for big tax savings.
- *legal_risk*: Plan for partner death or exit with key man insurance and buy-sell funding.
- *finance_unit_economics*: Entity choice (C Corp vs S Corp) affects tax efficiency; get expert review once cash builds up.

## Warnings and mistakes

- Giving free value doesn't fit every sales environment; people will walk all over you and waste your time.
- Business partners need key man insurance and a buy-out plan in case a partner dies.
