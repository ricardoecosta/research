## UK acquisition rules used throughout

The plans below do not use unsolicited SMS, WhatsApp, or social DMs to individuals, sole traders, or ordinary partnerships. The ICO’s PECR rule for email is broader than this shortlist’s operating rule; to follow Ricardo’s constraint, cold email is limited to verified UK limited companies only. Identify the sender and provide a working opt-out. PECR treats sole traders and some partnerships as individual subscribers, while UK GDPR still applies to named contacts at companies. See the [ICO’s B2B marketing guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/) and [electronic mail rules](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/).

Before live cold calls, screen against TPS and CTPS, refresh the screening frequently, identify the caller, and honour objections. Do not use automated marketing calls. The [ICO live-call guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-live-calls/how-do-we-comply-with-the-rules-on-live-marketing-calls/) says numbers registered on TPS/CTPS generally cannot be called without the subscriber’s specific agreement. If personal data is used for marketing, document the lawful basis and legitimate-interests assessment where relevant, give privacy information, and maintain suppression records; people have an absolute right to object to direct marketing ([ICO planning guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/plan-direct-marketing/)).

For customer data handled on a client’s behalf, agree controller/processor roles, a written Article 28 contract, retention/deletion terms, security controls, and subprocessor terms ([ICO contracts guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/contracts-and-liabilities-between-controllers-and-processors-multi/)). Apply the current PECR rules to storage/access technologies: consent is needed where required, while DUAA exemptions for statistical or functional uses have conditions and may require an opt-out ([ICO DUAA guidance](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/what-we-do/legislation-we-cover/data-use-and-access-act-2025/the-data-use-and-access-act-2025-what-does-it-mean-for-organisations/); [ICO storage and access guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/cookies-and-similar-technologies/)).

## 1. Shopify-first niche add-on app

**Clusters and ranking.** C0266 (add-on apps for platform marketplaces: success 66, automation 78) plus C0106 (Link Whisper / niche WordPress SEO software: 69, automation 75); eight distinct videos. This is the most technical, self-serve option: the marketplace can bring buyers, and app delivery, billing, and much support are software. C0266 has no first-hand claims of its own; the stronger outcome evidence is from the adjacent plugin cluster C0106.

**Concept.** Build one narrow Shopify app that fixes a repeated merchant workflow complaint, such as an unreliable UK carrier or accounting integration. Start with Shopify, not a portfolio across Shopify, WordPress, and Chrome. Charge a modest monthly fee through Shopify’s app billing and keep the first version small enough for Ricardo to maintain.

**Evidence.**

- C0106 founder on Link Whisper’s sale: “in early January 2025, I was wired $2.7 million and the deal on Link Whisper was done.” (`eVldld15cXo`)
- On the early MVP investment: “I invested only a few thousand dollars in an MVP and I made that back quickly.” (`czzv3o0PCmI`)

That is one experienced founder with a large audience, not a likely first-year result. The launch reached an email list of 10,000 non-customers (`eVldld15cXo`), which Ricardo does not have. The stricter validation rates this 4/10 for reaching roughly £500 MRR by month 12 and estimates realistic automation around 55% (still above the relaxed floor), £0–£6k first-year revenue, with a central case of £500–£2k and month-12 MRR most likely £0–£500 (`TOP10_validation_opus_high.md`, §2).

**Who does what.** Agents inspect marketplace reviews, draft the app, test integrations, maintain docs, triage tickets, and prepare releases. Ricardo spends 3–5 hours a week choosing the niche, reviewing code and data handling, testing releases, and handling app-review or emergency escalations. A VA can handle 2–4 hours a week of ticket triage and test-account checks once customers arrive. A subcontractor is only needed for a specialist integration or security review.

**UK legal and acquisition plan.** Acquire through marketplace search, documentation, and opt-in partner referrals; no cold outreach is needed. Start with merchants as business customers. Minimise merchant/customer personal data, publish a privacy notice, and use a DPA if the app processes store-customer data. If using WordPress instead, follow its GPL/plugin-directory terms; Shopify is the simpler first test. Use the platform’s billing and privacy-webhook requirements, and do not buy or seed reviews.

**12-month outcome.** Use the validated estimate above: £0–£6k total, central case £500–£2k; no basis to expect the Link Whisper outcome.

**Top risks.** No marketplace visibility; an incumbent fixes the gap; app-review friction; release or API changes create support spikes; customer data or app permissions expand beyond what can be safely maintained in five hours.

**First-week test.** Review at least 100 one- and two-star reviews across 3 Shopify apps in one UK-relevant workflow. Pass if at least 15 reviews describe the same concrete failure, at least 5 merchants independently confirm it in public forums/review responses, and a testable MVP can be scoped to two weekends and under £500 cash. Fail if no repeated problem or the platform API does not expose the required data.

## 2. UK niche directory with paid listings and leads

**Clusters and ranking.** C0066 (niche SEO directory sites: 75 / 80) plus C0080 (local service-provider directory: 66 / 72); nine distinct videos. C0066 supplies stronger automation and general directory evidence; C0080 adds a direct advertiser-paid local listing model. This ranks highly on success, automation, and a clear path to business revenue without a founder audience.

**Concept.** Pick one UK niche with a legally usable, valuable dataset and buyer intent, then build a directory with filters, verified details, and a small number of paid featured listings. Do not start with an undifferentiated “all local businesses” directory. A niche could be a specific type of accessible venue or specialist supplier across London and the South East. Add lead requests only when the consumer understands which provider will receive their details.

**Evidence.**

- One directory operator: “completely passively it's probably around like 2300 to $3,000 a month” (`1fTGX4sLVqA`)
- Local directory operator: “I have about 60 advertisers. It makes a little over 50 grand a year” (`LZ_bjRV6xx0`)
- The latter also says: “I spend about a an hour a month on it, maybe” (`LZ_bjRV6xx0`)

The second example is an established US site whose owner had visited 50 ranches; the domain alone cost nearly $18,000 (`LZ_bjRV6xx0`). It is evidence that listings can sell, not that a £2k UK launch will match it. The stricter validation estimates realistic automation around 70%, £0–£2k total revenue in year one, most likely low hundreds, for the SEO-directory version (`TOP10_validation_opus_high.md`, §3).

**Who does what.** Agents find and normalise licensed data, generate pages, check links, update listings, and handle routine correction requests. Ricardo spends 2–4 hours weekly on niche choice, licence checks, data quality, and the few difficult corrections. A VA can spend 3–5 hours on calls to listed businesses, corrections, and renewal follow-up. Agents can draft sales material; the VA or Ricardo closes paid placements.

**UK legal and acquisition plan.** Build from an official API or dataset whose licence permits commercial reuse; record source and attribution, and avoid scraping platform pages without checking terms. A listed sole trader’s details can be personal data: minimise fields, state the source and purpose, keep information accurate, and offer objection/removal. Acquire providers through inbound “claim listing” requests, permission-based referrals, or screened calls. Cold email only verified corporate subscribers. Put clear sponsorship labels on paid placements and disclose the destination before collecting a consumer lead. Attribute OGL data under the [National Archives licence](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/) where applicable.

**12-month outcome.** £0–£2k total is the relevant validation estimate; low hundreds is more defensible than £1k/month. Revenue from local paid listings in the UK remains unknown.

**Top risks.** Slow SEO; unlicensed or stale data; no local search demand; a weak directory has no reason to beat official results; owner-led sales takes longer than five hours unless a VA handles follow-up.

**First-week test.** Identify 3 niches with at least 500 records in a commercial-use-permitted source. Verify 30 records manually, check 20 UK searches for weak/outdated results, and contact 10 limited-company providers through compliant channels. Pass if one niche has clean licences, at least 90% accurate sampled records, and 3 providers willing to pay a refundable £50 founding-listing deposit. Fail if no dataset passes rights/accuracy checks or nobody will pay.

**Who does what.** Agents mine keywords and reviews, produce code and screenshots, run test suites, prepare metadata, and triage support. **Who does what.** Agents research search terms, build/maintain pages, answer inbound enquiries, score lead completeness, and send reports. **First-week test.** Build a single transparent page and a call/form route, then spend at most £100 on tightly geofenced search ads. Pass if it produces at least 3 valid enquiries at less than £35 each and one local limited-company provider signs a written £25-per-qualified-lead pilot. Fail if spend reaches £100 with fewer than 2 valid enquiries or no provider will pay.

**Clusters and ranking.** C0004 (AI voice receptionist: 67 / 51) plus C0139 (speed-to-lead AI: 65 / 58); 18 distinct videos. **Who does what.** Agents answer approved questions, book requested appointments, send permitted confirmations, summarise calls, flag low confidence, and monitor uptime. Ricardo spends 3–5 hours weekly on sales demos, workflow design, QA samples, and escalations. A VA can use 2–4 hours for onboarding, weekly call-log review, and billing. Tell callers when AI is answering, set recording/retention rules, use a DPA and approved subprocessors, and keep a human escalation route. Avoid health, financial, legal, and emergency-response clients at launch.

**12-month outcome.** Unknown. The source supports a premium B2B willingness-to-pay example, but not a UK customer-acquisition rate or a part-time service’s monthly revenue. Require paid pilots before hiring a VA beyond a few hours.

**Top risks.** Bad bookings or hallucinated answers; customers expect human-level handling; telephony/integration faults; client lead data is used outside its original purpose; a 51–58% automation business grows into a support agency.

**First-week test.** Make a demo for one local trade using a test number and synthetic call data. Contact 15 Bromley-area limited companies using compliant channels. Pass if 3 agree to a live demo, one signs a £150–£300 paid two-week pilot, and the agent correctly handles 19/20 scripted cases with safe escalation on the 20th. Fail if nobody pays or any test call produces a fabricated price, emergency assurance, or unapproved appointment.

**First-week test.** Choose one product and one audience, order a physical sample, and calculate landed UK margin including VAT, shipping, and refunds. Pass if contribution margin is at least 35%, sample quality passes, and a £100 test produces 5 add-to-carts or 2 paid orders. Fail if the sample is poor, rights are unclear, or 100 qualified page visits produce no add-to-cart.
