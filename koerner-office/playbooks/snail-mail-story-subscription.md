> **Second source (added 21 Oct 2026).** *She Makes $1k/Day Sending Letters* (`KuQtxDCmyO4`, published 14 Feb 2026) was merged into this idea. Hannah in Austin runs a **standalone monthly letter club** (the simpler variant in §3): art, a recipe card, two or three stickers and a paper surprise in a one-ounce envelope at **$11/month**. Guest's claims: hard cost **~$2–4 per letter including the stamp**, **~70% gross margin before labour**, churn planned at 10% but **closer to ~5%/month**. Growth was **organic TikTok with no paid media**: 50 sign-ups in month 1, 107 in month 2, then 1,200 after a video with 1.1M views. Now **4,100 subscribers, almost $50,000/month recurring and about $30,000/month profit after six months**. Her lesson: her first Google Form didn't say it cost money, which brought low-intent sign-ups. This supports the standalone-letter niche and an organic-first launch next to the Meta-ads route in §5–§9.

## 1. ICP and buyer

- **Product:** "We tell stories through letters sent through the mail. You get two letters a month for a year and it tells a story."
- **Buyer:** mostly gift buyers. "It's a great Mother's Day gift", and Mother's Day is "our Super Bowl… daughters for the mothers." Customers also buy for themselves and gift it on ("I'm going to gift this to my mom and to my sister").
- **What not to do:** the guest warns that "anybody with a mom" is not a customer definition. "Know who your customer is… just start with a customer of one."
- **Why it works:** it's "something to look forward to", it's very giftable, and gifts mean "they can't cancel for themselves", which keeps churn low.

## 2. Offer and pricing

| Item | Figure (guest's claim) |
|---|---|
| Launch price | **$12/month for two letters** |
| Prepaid | **~$100** for a story up front. "The key to our growth has been the prepaid option." Prepaid customers churn much less. |
| Early cost per shipment | "a dollar, stamp, envelope, paper"; in practice **$2–3** because "we put a lot inside" (stationery, postcards, stickers) |
| Shipping | Extras such as a tin are charged ~$8–9 shipping; free shipping in peak seasons "to remove any barriers to entry" |
| Lifetime value | **~$180** across 12 months |
| Churn | **~5%** |
| Upsells | Each story is a separate subscription. There are six concurrent stories and nine subscription options, plus "other products that help… from a profitability standpoint" |

**UK note (not from the video):** postage is the biggest unknown. Price Royal Mail letter rates for your format before you set a price in pounds.

## 3. Niche-selection scorecard

The guest's ideas for other niches, and his warning:
- **Serialised story** (their model): historical fiction, then fantasy. He calls it "much harder" because "you have to get it right" and "you have to write a story".
- **Standalone month-to-month letters:** "way simpler than getting into the story business."
- Ideas raised in the interview: **recipe letters** (a listener started one around the book *Bread and Wine*, aimed at groups for referrals, with an idea to add QR-code cooking videos), **true crime**, and **puzzles or "something to decode".**

Score each from 1 to 5 on these criteria from the interview:

| Criterion | Why |
|---|---|
| Giftable to a defined person | Gifting drives sales and lowers churn |
| Wants every issue in order | Serial curiosity keeps people subscribed ("they want to know what happens at the end") |
| You have a creator | "She can really focus on the creative and I can really focus on the business" |
| Has a seasonal gifting peak | Mother's Day is "10 out of 10" for them |
| Cheap to fulfil per letter | Early cost $2–3 |

## 4. First 30 customers

What they did:
- They launched with an Instagram account, "not like from a big business page", and **33 people signed up**.
- **Don't launch to family and friends:** "They'll give you a false positive… your mom's gonna buy 10 of them, but she's not your customer."
- They sent the first letters at the end of August, started "dabbling in ads" in October ("just spending a few bucks here and there and people started showing up"), and had about 3,000 customers by year end.

UK approach: post the first chapter as content on Instagram and TikTok and point it at a waitlist. The guest says organic is "an art" and suggests studying the TikTok style of Hannah's snail-mail business, a different Hannah who appeared in an earlier episode. Then run small Meta ads to the same waitlist (§6).

## 5. Outreach scripts (ad and landing copy angles)

These are taken from how the guest describes the product. Rewrite them in your own voice:
- **Hook:** "Something you've never heard of, but your mother's going to love."
- **Promise:** "A story told through letters, two a month, delivered to her door." / "Something to look forward to."
- **Gift angle:** in the run-up to Mother's Day, "free shipping" to remove barriers.
- **On-site:** "you ask for their email address for a discount" — the first step of the funnel.
- **Win-back emails:** when a new story launches, email past customers. "We'll do email campaigns to try to win them back."

## 6. Demo / validation workflow

This is the guest's own test, and what he says he'd do if he started again:
> "I would develop a product… build a website… put up a form, coming soon. Here's my product… Give me your email address, stay up to date. If I can just run some ads… and I can find a pretty good mass of people that are willing to transact with me through their email address, I'll say, 'Okay, I think I got something going here.'"

Steps:
1. Write a sample first letter and photograph it with the stationery and extras.
2. Build a coming-soon page: product, price and an email form offering a launch discount.
3. Run small Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) to the page, and post organic content.
4. Track the cost per email sign-up. Before building further, convert the waitlist with a pre-sale of the prepaid option.

## 7. Delivery SOP

From the interview:
- **Content:** write the whole story arc before launch. Each envelope is planned ("in this envelope… a letter, and this is three pages"), with extras such as postcards (12 per story), telegrams, period ads and stickers.
- **Fulfilment:** every piece is hand-packed ("you're not going to get AI to stuff your envelopes anytime soon"). They now run a warehouse in Orem, Utah, with up to 25 people at peak. Start at home.
- **Billing:** monthly runs through subscription software. They handle prepaid annual orders outside it ("we don't have to pay 1% to the subscription [software] for the prepaid").
- **Email from day one:** "Meta can break." Email drives 38–50% of revenue.
- **AI use:** analysing email threads, Facebook ad efficiency and financials. Not packing.

## 8. QA / acceptance gate (before each dispatch)

- [ ] Letters are in the right order for each subscriber's start date. "You have to get every letter… in order."
- [ ] Every insert promised for that issue is in the envelope.
- [ ] Postage for the final weight and size has been checked.
- [ ] Gift orders: the recipient's address is used and a gift note is included.
- [ ] Cancellation and support requests are answered. Gift recipients may contact you to cancel.

## 9. 21-day launch plan

| Days | Action |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Pick a niche with the §3 scorecard. Decide story vs standalone. Choose a creator/writer. |
| 4–7 | Write the first 2–3 letters and the full outline. Source stationery. Weigh a sample and price the postage. |
| 8–9 | Build the coming-soon page with email capture and discount (§6). Set up email. |
| 10–16 | Organic posts plus a small Meta ad test to the waitlist. Don't push to family and friends. |
| 17–19 | Open pre-sales: monthly and prepaid. Track conversion from the waitlist. |
| 20–21 | Hand-pack and post the first letters to paying customers. Ask for referrals. |

## 10. Metrics and kill criteria

**Track** (the guest's own metrics): cost per acquisition (early $15–20, now $40–50), payback ("profitable within three months"), churn (~5%), LTV (~$180), email share of revenue (38–50%), prepaid mix, and cost per email sign-up on the coming-soon test.

**Kill or rethink if** _(proposed thresholds, not from the video; set your own)_:
- the waitlist test can't get email sign-ups at a cost that, with your own conversion rate, gives a cost per acquisition below about one-third of LTV. The guest's spending was roughly a third on goods and postage and a third on Meta;
- pre-sales from the waitlist are close to zero after the test;
- postage plus inserts make the cost per letter too high for your price.

## Risks (from the card)

- Needs a strong writer, and serialised stories are hard to get right.
- Depends on Meta ads, and acquisition costs rise over time ($15–20 to $40–50).
- Fulfilment is manual and labour-heavy at scale.
- UK postage costs differ from the US.
