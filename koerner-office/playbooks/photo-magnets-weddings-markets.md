## 1. ICP and buyer

- **Market shoppers:** people who keep coming back "with more pictures". At her second market, customers "probably bought 40 magnets".
- **Wedding couples:** the couple pays and the guests get magnets free at the reception. "People go into weddings knowing like I'm gonna be broke after this… flowers 3,000… videographer 9,000."
- **Online buyers:** the website (the guest found the idea from photo magnets "being sold like on Etsy").

## 2. Offer and pricing

| Item | Figure (guest's claim) |
|---|---|
| Wedding package | "$1,200 to $3,000" for a 2–4 hour reception. Example flat rate "2500 for 4 hours", including setup, backdrop, labour and drive time |
| Pricing levers | Bulk rate ("the more the magnets, the cheaper they are"), plus a per-person hourly rate ("$50 an hour"), mileage and travel time |
| Hard cost | "about 35 cents a magnet" (paper, ink, metal mylar); 300 magnets ≈ $100 |
| Margin | "for our smallest magnets, it's like 91%" |
| Market booth fees | "$50 to 300"; the $300 markets are "the ones that I make the most at" |

**UK note (not from the video):** UK wedding budgets and market fees differ. Price a 3-hour package in pounds against UK photo-booth hire rates.

## 3. Niche-selection scorecard

Score each channel from 1 to 5:

| Criterion | What to check | Video basis |
|---|---|---|
| Foot traffic | Visitors per market day | Higher fee markets earned more |
| Repeat buying | Do buyers come back with more photos? | "They just kept coming back" |
| Event budget | Couples or companies with an entertainment budget | Wedding package prices |
| Travel time | Within an hour of home | Mileage "can make a huge difference" |
| Social follow-on | Buyers follow and reorder online | "so many repeat buyers from markets or from weddings" |

Start with markets (cheap to test), then weddings.

## 4. First 30 customers

Follow the guest's order: markets first. She first dismissed weddings ("nobody would want this at a wedding… I just threw it out the window") and started with markets. For the first 30:
1. Book 2–3 local markets with different fees.
2. Sell on the spot from people's phones. Take each buyer's email or social handle for reorders.
3. Show a "book us for your wedding" sign and collect enquiries.

## 5. Outreach scripts

- **Market sign:** "Your phone photos → fridge magnets in 2 minutes. [Price] each, [bulk price] for 9."
- **Wedding enquiry reply:** "We set up a magnet station at your reception. Guests scan a code, upload a photo and leave with a magnet: their favour and your keepsake. [Hours], [price], setup and backdrop included."
- **Wedding suppliers (not from the video):** venues and planners can refer couples; offer them a sample magnet set.

## 6. Demo / validation workflow

- The market stall is the demo: people watch magnets being made.
- For weddings, bring a sample board of magnets from earlier events.
- **Validation test:** two or three market days. Count magnets sold per hour and repeat buyers per day.

## 7. Delivery SOP (event)

The guest's "version 2.0" setup: "one person at a table with the machine and a printer and some photo paper and a QR code and a MacBook."
1. The guest takes a photo, scans the QR code and uploads it.
2. The operator places it in the template and prints it.
3. Press it on the magnet machine and hand it over.
4. The guest says the earlier text-and-download process "was a long process", which is why they were building QR-upload software.

## 8. QA / acceptance gate

- [ ] Test print and press before doors open; spare paper, ink and magnet blanks.
- [ ] Faces are centred and not cropped in the template.
- [ ] The wedding contract states hours, number of staff, mileage and the magnet cap or bulk rate.
- [ ] A queue plan for peak times (two operators for large weddings).

## 9. 21-day launch plan

| Days | Action |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Research magnet machines and suppliers. Price a starter kit under ~£1,000. Book 2–3 markets. |
| 4–7 | Practise. Make a sample board and the QR upload page. Set market and bulk prices. |
| 8–14 | Run the first markets. Track magnets per hour, average order and repeat buyers. Collect wedding enquiries. |
| 15–18 | Make a wedding package and price list. Contact 10 venues/planners with samples. |
| 19–21 | Review the numbers (§10). Decide whether to add weddings or stop. |

## 10. Metrics and kill criteria

**Track:** revenue per market day net of booth fee · magnets per hour · average order · repeat buyers · wedding enquiries → bookings · cost per magnet.

**Kill or rethink if** _(proposed thresholds, not from the video; set your own)_:
- after three markets, average profit per market day is below the booth fee plus your hourly target;
- no wedding enquiries after three markets and 10 planner contacts;
- cost per magnet is well above the guest's ~35 cents at your volumes.

## Risks (from the card)

- In-person events and labour; low async fit.
- Seasonal wedding demand.
- Headline revenue includes machine sales, so the event-only business is smaller than $1.63M suggests.
- A low barrier to entry invites local copycats.
